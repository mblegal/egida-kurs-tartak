## Content

### 1. Yield as the key to sawmill economics

**Yield** (raw material recovery) is the ratio of output volume to input volume expressed as a percentage. In a sawmill, yield is **measured at each stage** of the process:

**Stage 1: cutting yield** (roundwood log → green lumber). Depends on:
- Log diameter (>35 cm gives 55-60%, <25 cm gives 35-40%)
- Log taper (the lower, the higher the yield)
- Defects (heart cracks, curvature, knots reduce recovery by 3-8%)
- Cutting scheme (cant sawing gives 55-60% for 28 mm boards, quarter sawing 48-52% but higher strength class)
- Kerf thickness (blade 1.07 mm gives 99% yield, blade 1.5 mm only 97%)

For standard EGIDA pine 28 mm boards the yield is **55-60%**. For oak 28 mm boards **45-50%**. For thicker boards (50 mm) yield rises (fewer kerfs, less sawdust), for thinner (20 mm) falls.

**Stage 2: drying yield** (green lumber → dry lumber). Shrinkage depends on species and moisture:
- Fresh pine 32% → 12%: volumetric shrinkage **13-15%** (typically 14%)
- Fresh oak 38% → 14%: shrinkage **18-22%** (typically 20%)
- Fresh beech 32% → 10%: shrinkage **16-19%** (typically 18%)

**Stage 3: final processing yield** (dry lumber → final product). Planing removes 2-3 mm from each surface, cross-cutting shortens length by 40-60 mm. Volumetric shrinkage **2-4%** depending on allowances.

**Final yield** = stage 1 × stage 2 × stage 3 = **0.58 × 0.86 × 0.97 = 0.484** ≈ **48% for standard pine**. For Mykoła's ZLE-077: 0.441 × 0.85 × 0.97 = **0.364 = 36.4%**, **below average by 12 percentage points**.

### 3. Production cost structure

**Percentage shares of own costs** (without Drew-Sus) for pine flooring boards:
- Raw material (roundwood log): **34%** (380 zł / 1135 zł)
- Sawmill labor: **19%** (220 zł)
- Own drying: **7.5%** (85 zł)
- Final processing: **17.5%** (200 zł)
- Machine depreciation: **16%** (180 zł)
- Consumables: **3%** (35 zł)
- Other: **3%** (35 zł)

**Largest items**: raw material (34%) and labor (19%) are **53% of costs**. Optimizations in these items have the greatest impact. 10% saving on raw material = 38 zł/m³ (2.3% of margin). 10% saving on labor = 22 zł/m³ (1.3% of margin).

**With Drew-Sus** external drying takes up **22%** of costs (297 zł / 1347 zł), pushing other shares down. Raw material drops to **28%**, labor to **16%**.

**Why is Drew-Sus more expensive than own despite the same technology**? Drew-Sus must:
1. Cover round-trip transport cost (350 zł/trip, distributed over a 3-5 m³ charge)
2. Cover administration and profit margin cost (sawmill operator, accounting, insurance)
3. Cover commercial risk (doesn't always have a full charge)

EGIDA **gains on Drew-Sus** only when the customer's deadline is critical and its own kiln is not ready. In other situations Drew-Sus is a financial loss.

### 4. Waste in numbers and in logistics

**Typical volume distribution** in sawing pine 28 mm boards with cant sawing:
- Green lumber: **55-60%** of roundwood log
- Slab: **20-25%** (4 slabs × log, each ~7 cm × 8 cm × 3.5 m)
- End offcuts: **3-5%** (cut off at first cut for normal kerf)
- Sawdust (kerf + micro-rejects): **15-17%** (blade 1.07 mm × cutting length)

**Values** (EGIDA 2026 prices):
- Green lumber: no sale at this stage (goes to drying)
- Slab: **50 zł/m³** sale to fuel-neighbors
- Offcuts: **80 zł/m³** sale to Baltic Wood (biomass chips)
- Sawdust: **30 zł/m³** briquette or fuel sale

**For 1.32 m³ roundwood log of ZLE-077**:
- Green lumber: 0.582 m³ (44% of roundwood log)
- Slab: ~0.28 m³ (21%)
- Offcuts: ~0.04 m³ (3%)
- Sawdust: ~0.195 m³ (15%)
- Weight/moisture loss during process: ~0.22 m³ (17%, not physical waste)

**Total waste value**: 0.28 × 50 + 0.04 × 80 + 0.195 × 30 = **14 + 3 + 6 = 23 zł**.
**Lumber value (before processing) 0.582 m³** × ~1500 zł/m³ (average pre-processing sales): ~870 zł.

### 6. Operator decisions affecting margin

**Impact of parametric decisions on margin** (for a 5 m³ pine batch):

**Decision 1: choice of the right blade for the species.** Alloy 7/8 for fresh pine (correct) vs stellite 9/11 (wrong, resin clogs stellite). Difference: stellite blade dulls faster, 2 changes during the batch instead of 1, cost 800 zł (blade 405 zł + 40 min change × 2 = 1.3 h × 55 zł = 72 zł + 2 × 165 zł sharpening = 330 zł). Error cost: **~800 zł for a 5 m³ batch**.

**Decision 2: blade tension.** 2200 PSI pine (correct) vs 2400 PSI (too high). Difference: blade breaks faster (about 60% of life), 1.6 blades per 5 m³ instead of 1. Cost: additional blade 145 zł + sharpening 165 zł + 40 min change × 55 zł = 60 zł. **~370 zł**.

**Decision 3: feed speed.** 32 st/min pine 28 mm (correct) vs 45 st/min (too fast). Difference: surface scaling, 5% of boards drop from C24 to C16. For a 5 m³ batch: 0.25 m³ × (1700 − 1100) = **150 zł classification loss**.

**Decision 4: cutting scheme.** Scheme A cant sawing (correct for C24) vs scheme B quarter sawing (wrong for C24 because more expensive). Difference: working time 2× longer (7.5 h vs 15 h), labor cost about 400 zł more. Yield 5-8% lower (scheme B). For a 5 m³ batch: labor cost +400 zł + lower yield 0.4 m³ × 1700 zł = +1080 zł. **Total error cost: ~1500 zł**.

**Decision 5: log order.** Hardest first (Mykoła in l1, correct strategy) vs easiest first. Difference: parameters set for the first cut, a difficult log at the end without parameter correction. Potentially 1 board falls from C24 to C16 or to reject. **For a 5 m³ batch: ~60 zł loss per board**.

**Sum of potential operator decision impact on a 5 m³ batch: up to ~2800 zł** (difference between an experienced M3 operator and a beginner). For the 0.5 m³ ZLE-077 batch proportionally: **~280 zł**. Mykoła in l1 made all decisions correctly, so this potential was fully used. The −529 zł loss of ZLE-077 **does not stem from operator decisions**, but from raw material conditions and external drying strategy.

### 7. Typical errors in understanding economics

**Error 1. "Order loss = operator error"**. Mykoła sees −529 zł and thinks "I did something wrong". In reality, operator decisions were good, the loss resulted from raw material and strategy. **Correctly**: **separate the impact of operator decisions from the impact of external factors**. The shift report shows both components, a good operator sees the difference.

**Error 2. "Drew-Sus is wasteful"**. The operator sees +212 zł/m³ premium and thinks "why not dry in our own kiln". He forgets that **the own kiln requires a 30 m³ full charge**, and the ZLE-077 batch is 0.58 m³. Waiting 13 days for a full charge = customer delay of 3-13 days = loss of relationship. **Correctly**: **Drew-Sus is a cost of timeliness, not waste**. When the deadline is critical, it is a rational decision.

**Error 3. "Better raw material = bigger margin"**. The operator thinks "if we bought logs >35 cm, yield would be higher, margin larger". He forgets that **logs >35 cm are 20-25% more expensive** (450-480 zł/m³ vs 380). The raw material cost difference eats up the yield gain. **Correctly**: **optimal raw material = best price/quality ratio**, not "best logs". EGIDA buys I-II class from Nadleśnictwo, that is a sensible compromise.

**Error 4. "Waste must be reduced at all costs"**. The operator sees 44% waste (slab + sawdust + offcuts) and thinks "must be reduced". He forgets that **waste is a physical consequence of cutting**, blade 1.07 mm = 1.07 mm of sawdust from each kerf. Attempting to reduce waste = thinner blade = less stiff = worse kerf quality = higher classification reject. **Correctly**: **optimal waste**, not minimal. 44% is the norm for scheme A cant sawing.

**Error 5. "The operator should maximize yield"**. The operator thinks "my task is high yield". In reality the operator's task is **high classification class**, yield is a side effect. An operator maximizing yield at the cost of class (e.g. risking cracks for thicker boards) generates a loss in classification. **Correctly**: **class > yield**. 0.4 m³ C24 is better than 0.5 m³ C16.

**Error 6. "Operator labor is 19% of cost, insignificant"**. The operator thinks "since labor is only 19%, my efforts don't have much impact". He forgets that **operator decisions affect 30-70% of margin depending on batch** (not just 19% of labor cost). A parametric decision can change yield by 5-10%, classes by 3-5%, working time by 15-20%, together changes on the order of **15-30% of margin**. **Correctly**: **the operator is key to margin, even though cost-wise he is 19% of the batch**.

### 8. Maturing of the M3 operator in economics

**Beginner M3 operator** (first month after passing M3, Mykoła in April-May 2026): parametric decisions good, but **does not understand the economics of his decisions**. Looks at the shift report and sees only yield, not value.

**Developing M3 operator** (3-6 month, Mykoła currently, June 2026): **sees the shift report** and **understands the causes** of losses/profits. ## Key terms

**Yield (raw material recovery)** (*yield / wydajność*, EN *yield / lumber recovery*, ES *rendimiento / aprovechamiento*, UK *вихід / коефіцієнт виходу*): ratio of output volume to input volume, measured at each stage of production; for pine 28 mm boards the cutting yield is 55-60%, final yield roundwood log to product 33-38%.

**Drying shrinkage** (*skurcz suszarniczy*, EN *drying shrinkage*, ES *contracción por secado*, UK *сушильна усадка*): reduction of wood volume upon water evaporation, 13-17% for fresh pine 32% → 12%, 18-22% for fresh oak 38% → 14%, 16-19% for beech.

**Processing shrinkage** (*skurcz obróbki*, EN *finishing shrinkage*, ES *contracción por mecanizado*, UK *усадка обробки*): volume reduction during planing and cross-cutting of dry lumber to final product, typically 2-4% depending on thickness and length allowances.

**Shift report** (*raport zmianowy*, EN *shift report*, ES *informe de turno*, UK *змінний звіт*): EGIDA Excel file with 4 tabs (order balance, waste balance, order comparison, monthly aggregation), written by the foreman, read by M2 operators (one line) and M3 (full report), and by the manager (aggregation).

**Order margin** (*marża zlecenia*, EN *order margin*, ES *margen del pedido*, UK *маржа замовлення*): difference between sales revenue and full production cost for a specific order, typical EGIDA pine margin 550-600 zł/m³ of final product (~33% margin) with own drying.

**Cost of timeliness** (*koszt terminowości*, EN *cost of timeliness*, ES *coste de puntualidad*, UK *вартість своєчасності*): additional cost arising from a conscious decision to use a more expensive production method (Drew-Sus external drying +212 zł/m³) to meet the customer's deadline, strategic investment in long-term relationship.

**Drew-Sus premium** (*premium Drew-Sus*, EN *Drew-Sus premium*, ES *sobrecoste Drew-Sus*, UK *доплата Drew-Sus*): difference between the price of Drew-Sus Mrągowo external drying (297 zł/m³ including transport) and EGIDA own drying (85 zł/m³), amounts to 212 zł/m³ of final product.

**Production cost structure** (*struktura kosztów*, EN *cost breakdown*, ES *estructura de costes*, UK *структура витрат*): division of full lumber production cost into 7 items: raw material 34%, sawmill labor 19%, processing 17.5%, machine depreciation 16%, own drying 7.5%, materials 3%, other 3%.

**Slab** (*oblina*, EN *slab / edging*, ES *costero / canto*, UK *горбиль*): rounded bark-bearing edge removed in the first four cuts of scheme A cant sawing, ~0.028 m³ per slab, 4 slabs per log, fuel value 50 zł/m³.

**Offcuts** (*zrzyny*, EN *offcuts / trims*, ES *recortes / trozos*, UK *обрізки*): short board pieces cut off during cross-cutting or defects inside a board removed after sawing, ~3-5% of roundwood log volume, chip value 80 zł/m³ (Baltic Wood biomass chips).

**Sawdust** (*trociny*, EN *sawdust*, ES *serrín*, UK *тирса*): powder arising from blade cutting, kerf width × length × number of kerfs, ~15-17% of roundwood log volume for scheme A cant sawing, value 30 zł/m³ (fuel or briquette).

**C24 strength class** (*C24*, EN *C24 strength class*, ES *clase de resistencia C24*, UK *клас міцності C24*): European class of structural wood according to PN-EN 338, bending strength 24 N/mm², used for flooring, residential rafters, EGIDA market standard, sale price 1700 zł/m³ pine 25×150×4000 mm.

**Single-batch vs long-term economics** (*ekonomika partii vs długoterminowa*, EN *batch vs long-term economics*, ES *economía de lote vs largo plazo*, UK *економіка партії vs довгострокова*): distinction between margin of a single order (measurable immediately) and value of the customer relationship (chain of subsequent orders, measurable over 6-12 months); a conscious loss on a single order can be an investment in long-term relationship.

**M3 operator maturity** (*dojrzałość operatora*, EN *operator maturity*, ES *madurez del operario*, UK *зрілість оператора*): the operator's ability to understand the economics of his decisions, to separate own impact from external factors, to think economically before cutting (not after), typically 6-12 month after passing M3.

**Operator personnel file (foreman's evaluation book)** (*akta osobowe operatora*, EN *operator personnel file*, ES *expediente del operario*, UK *особова справа оператора*): a notebook kept by the foreman with entries on orders, margins, training attendance, reflections and evaluations, the basis for promotion decisions (M3 → advanced, advanced → foreman).
