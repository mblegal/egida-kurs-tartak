## Introduction

Friday, 7:00, the same hall as on Thursday. Oleksij continues work at the Storti DDM-200 edger, but today Marek additionally leads him to a second station: the **Weinig Powermat 700 4-sided planer** standing 15 metres away, against the wall of the finishing department. Two machines, one lesson, one shared core: **"side-stance position, never in the line of ejection"**. The rule is not new – it appeared in lesson 5 of week 1 as a general safety principle for saws, and it came back on Thursday at the riving knife of the circular saw. Today we consolidate it through the contrast between two different machines.

The edger and the planer look completely different: the edger has two vertical blades trimming the edges, the planer has four cutter heads shearing all four surfaces of the board simultaneously. But the risk geometry is shared: **material under a tool rotating at 3000–8000 revolutions per minute can be ejected in the direction of ejection** (rearwards at the edger, rearwards or sideways at the planer), at a speed of 20–40 metres per second. An operator standing in the ejection line takes a blow from the board or cutter with energy comparable to a projectile from an air gun. An operator standing to the side sees the event, hears the sound, but does not become the target.

This lesson teaches **body position relative to the machine** – not in the sense of setting your feet in one specific way, but in the sense of understanding where the machine ejects material and where the operator must be outside that line. The second lesson is **single-piece feeding in the direction of the grain** at the planer: for the first time in M2 you use the rule "the direction of the grain matters", which you knew theoretically from lesson 4 of week 2 (grain twist), but had not yet applied in practice.

## Objectives

After this lesson:

1. You apply the rule **"side-stance position, never in the line of ejection"** to every circular saw and planer. At the edger: you stand **to the side of the infeed table**, left foot forward, right foot back on the diagonal, the ejection line passing beside your hip, not in front of your hip. At the planer: you stand **to the side of the infeed table** feeding the material, and you step back 2 m after feeding the board into the pressure rollers (the machine then runs on its own).
2. You recognise the **3 danger zones** of the edger: (a) the ejection line behind the machine (2–3 m behind the table, no entry while running), (b) the blade area with guards (touch = amputation), (c) the feeding zone in front of the infeed table (material may "return" when it jams). Each zone marked on the floor with yellow-and-black tape.
3. You recognise the **3 danger zones** of the 4-sided planer: (a) the pressure rollers at the infeed (clamping force 1500–3000 N, direction top-down – a hand introduced between the roller and the material = crushing), (b) the cutter heads (3000–8000 rpm, 4 heads at once – touch is amputation at each of them), (c) ejection behind the machine (fine chips 20–30 m/s, larger fragments on kickback 30–40 m/s).
4. You feed material into the planer **one piece at a time, in the direction of the grain**: board after board, never two at once, the orientation matching the direction of the tree's growth (the cutter goes "from the butt to the crown", not "from the crown to the butt"). Planing "against the grain" = the cutter tears the fibres instead of shearing them = a rough surface + increased risk of chip kickback.
5. You understand that at the planer **you never press the material with your hand** – that is what the pressure rollers with knurled rolls are for. You use a wooden push stick only in the last 300 mm of the material, when the pressure roller no longer has anything to engage with. The operator's hand **never** closer than 500 mm to the first infeed roller.

## Content

::: info
**Two machines, one core.** The edger and the planer have different designs (blades vs. cutter heads, manual feeding vs. automatic, 1 processing stage vs. 4 simultaneous stages), but a shared safety logic: side-stance position, single-piece feeding, push stick in the last 300 mm. If you master both in this lesson, you have mastered 80% of the sawmill's circular and milling stations.
:::

### Part 1: The edger – position and feeding

Yesterday the Storti DDM-200 edger was trimming oak boards 150 × 25 × 3000 mm. Oleksij already knows the start-up procedure (6 steps from lesson 4), the riving knife (4 check parameters), the adjustable guard (gap ≤5 mm), the push stick (short <300 mm, narrow <150 mm). Today Marek adds **body position** – an element that was implicit yesterday, but today becomes a separate subject of learning.

**Foot placement at the edger**: left foot 20–30 cm in front of the right, both feet at a distance of **80–100 cm from the blade axis** (the blade line runs along the centre of the infeed table, the axis = the line from the blade to the end of the outfeed conveyor). The operator's hips are **to the side of the table**, not over the table. The right hand feeds the material, the left hand rests on the machine frame close to the STOP mushroom button. Body angle relative to the table axis: 15–30° (not perpendicular, not parallel, at an angle).

Why this position: if a kickback ejects the board rearwards at 30–40 m/s (108–144 km/h, data from lesson 5 of week 1), a board flying along the table axis **will pass beside the hip, it will not strike the hip**. An operator standing in the axis, even 30 cm behind the table, takes the full blow. An operator standing to the side loses 2–3 cm of jacket sleeve, not a hip.

**Three danger zones of the edger**:

1. **Ejection line** (2–3 m behind the outfeed table): yellow-and-black marking on the floor, **no entry while the machine is running**. The second operator receiving the boards stands to the side of the conveyor, 1 m from the ejection line, never in the axis.
2. **Blade area with fixed and adjustable guards**: access only when the blade is stopped (LOTO from lesson 8 of week 2, padlock with the operator's name). Touching a rotating blade at 55 m/s = amputation in 0.01 second.
3. **Feeding zone** in front of the infeed table (1–2 m): the second operator feeding boards stands to the side, never pushes material onto the table while the first board is still in the cut. A jam = the material may "return" to the feeding zone, and an operator with a hand over the table takes the blow.

Oleksij made 140 boards yesterday without incident. Today Marek asks him to make 20 boards, but with a helper: **Tadeusz Kowalski** (senior operator, who appeared at the belt change in lesson 8 of week 2) receives the boards from the outfeed conveyor. Oleksij feeds, Tadeusz receives, Marek supervises. Tadeusz immediately positions himself to the side of the conveyor (1 m from the ejection line), and Oleksij likewise to the side of the infeed table. Neither of them stands in the axis of the machine.

### Part 2: The 4-sided planer – feeding and grain direction

After 20 boards Marek leads Oleksij 15 m further, to the **Weinig Powermat 700 4-sided planer**: a machine the size of a passenger car, with a control panel on the side, a long infeed table (2 m) and an equally long outfeed table. On the infeed table you can see the **pressure rollers**: 6 steel knurled rolls with a diameter of 120 mm, spaced every 300 mm along the table, each pressing the material against the lower table with a force of 2000 N. The rollers are vertical, arranged in a row above the material.

Inside the machine, in the space between the infeed and outfeed tables, there are **four cutter heads**: lower (planes the bottom), upper (planes the top), left (planes the left side), right (planes the right side). Each head has 4–6 spiral tungsten-carbide knives, rotates at 6000 rpm, and shears 2–4 mm of material in a single pass. The board enters rough on one side and comes out **smooth on four sides**: a finished profile for windows, frames, or flooring, whatever the operator has programmed.

**Operator position at the planer**: you stand **to the side of the infeed table**, about 1 m from the first pressure roller. You feed one board after another onto the table, and the rollers grab and pull it in themselves. After feeding the board into the pressure rollers, **you step back 2 m** (the machine runs on its own, feed speed 6–30 m/min). You do not need to push anything: the pressure rollers pull with a greater force than any operator's hand.

**Grain direction – for the first time in practice**. Oleksij knows the theory from lesson 4 of week 2 (pine grain twist 3°/5°/8° from the dimension plate). Now he applies it: each pine board 45 × 95 × 2500 mm has visible on its sides **annual rings**: semicircular lines running from light early wood to dark late wood. The direction of the tree's growth runs from the **narrower side of the ring to the wider** (rings close to the butt are narrow, at the crown they are wider). The planer's cutter must go **with the direction of growth**, not against it.

**Planing "against the grain"** (*against the grain*) = the cutter lifts the fibres in the direction opposite to their natural arrangement, **tearing** them instead of **shearing** them. The surface is rough, small splinters fly towards the operator (20–30 m/s, smaller than the 40 m/s kickback of a full board, but enough to injure an eye without EN 166 eye protection). Marek shows Oleksij the difference: the first two boards deliberately placed against the grain – Oleksij sees fine chips shooting out of the machine, hears the "crunching" sound of the cutters. The next two boards correctly oriented: an even "brushing" sound, chips falling vertically downwards under the machine (picked up by conveyors beneath the housing).

**You never press with your hand**. The pressure rollers of the Weinig Powermat 700 clamp the material with a force of 2000 N each – 6 rollers = a combined force of 12 000 N, over a tonne. An operator's hand introduced between a roller and a board = **crushing of the fingers in 0.1 second**, amputation or a serious fracture. If you see that the board is lifting at the infeed, **you do not press down**: STOP, report to Marek. Reasons for the lifting: warped board (uneven moisture), material too thick for the machine setting, damaged roller. Each of these reasons = a matter for the foreman, not for the operator.

**Wooden push stick in the last 300 mm**. When the last 300 mm of the material enters the roller zone, the first infeed roller has already "released" (the board has passed), the last infeed roller still has the board, but the board is about to "drop out" from under it. At this moment the operator gives a gentle "nudge" with a 500 mm wooden push stick, so that the board does not stall between the infeed rollers and the first cutter head. **The hand is never closer than 500 mm to the first roller**, the push stick makes a "by feel" nudge (movement of 5–10 cm, not forceful).

### Shared core: side-stance, never in the line of ejection

The mantra of week 3: "I start up, I adjust, I cut, I sort, but always under the supervision of the foreman or a senior". Add today a fifth principle: **"I stand to the side, never in the line of ejection"**. This rule is not specific to the edger or the planer: it applies at **every machine** in the sawmill that ejects material or chips at a speed greater than 10 m/s (band saw, rip circular saw, cross-cut circular saw, edger, planer, jointer, thicknesser, multi-rip, milling machine).

::: warning
**"Ejection line" test** to be performed at every new station:

1. Stop the machine (no material under the tool).
2. Stand at the infeed table, look at the axis of the cutting tool (blade, head, cutter).
3. Imagine a straight line from the tool through the outfeed table **above the table**, and further **to the end of the hall**.
4. This line is the **ejection line**. In this line **no one stands** while the machine is running. To the side of it: permitted. On the axis: never.
5. Check that no static element (shelf, wall, colleague's workstation) is in the ejection line. If there is: report to the foreman, the hall must be reorganised.
:::

After lesson 5 Oleksij has in his head **three maps of danger zones**: the band saw (from lessons 1–3 of week 3), the edger (part 1 of this lesson), the planer (part 2 of this lesson). Four machines, twelve zones (3 per machine), one shared rule. In week 4 the kiln and the sorting yard will be added, each with their own zones.

## Key terms

- **pozycja z boku** (pl) / **side-stance position** (en) / **posición lateral** (es) / **бічна позиція** (uk): placement of the operator's body outside the ejection axis of the machine, with the hip outside the line running from the cutting tool through the outfeed table to the end of the hall
- **linia wyrzutu** (pl) / **ejection line** (en) / **línea de expulsión** (es) / **лінія викиду** (uk): the straight line from the cutting tool through the outfeed table, in which material may be ejected at 20–40 m/s on kickback, no entry while running
- **walki dociskowe** (pl) / **feed rollers** (en) / **rodillos de alimentación** (es) / **подавальні валки** (uk): steel knurled rolls on the planer's infeed table, pressing the material with a force of 1500–3000 N each, replacing the operator's hand
- **głowica frezowa** (pl) / **cutter head** (en) / **cabezal de fresado** (es) / **фрезерна головка** (uk): an assembly of 4–6 spiral tungsten-carbide knives rotating at 3000–8000 rpm, shearing 2–4 mm of material in a single pass
- **struganie w kierunku włókien** (pl) / **planing with the grain** (en) / **cepillado a favor de la fibra** (es) / **стругання за напрямом волокон** (uk): feeding the board in line with the direction of the tree's growth (from the butt to the crown); the cutter shears the fibres cleanly instead of tearing them
- **struganie pod włos** (pl) / **planing against the grain** (en) / **cepillado a contrapelo** (es) / **стругання проти волокон** (uk): feeding the board against the direction of growth; the cutter tears the fibres, the surface is rough, splinters fly at 20–30 m/s towards the operator
- **strefa niebezpieczna** (pl) / **danger zone** (en) / **zona peligrosa** (es) / **небезпечна зона** (uk): an area around the machine marked with yellow-and-black tape in which the risk of injury exceeds the acceptable level during operation (ejection line, tool area, feeding zone, roller zone)

## Trainer notes

**Execution time**: 120 minutes (30 min theory + 45 min walk-through of 2 stations + 45 min supervised practice: 20 edger boards, observation of 10 planer passes without own operation).

**Resources**: a sawmill edger (any design with 1–2 rip blades), a 4-sided planer or a jointer-thicknesser (in smaller sawmills as a minimum), yellow-and-black tape marking danger zones, 10 pine boards 45 × 95 × 2500 mm for grain-direction demonstration, a 500 mm wooden push stick, a printout of Ministry of Economy Regulation § 13.

**Most common trainee mistakes in this lesson**: (1) standing in the axis of the machine "because you can see better": the trainee looks ahead, sees the blade, does not notice that they are standing in the ejection line. Teach "feet to the side, eyes ahead": body at an angle, head turned towards the machine. (2) Pressing the material down with the hand at the planer: a natural reflex when the board "jumps" at the infeed. Teach the reaction: "jumping = STOP, not pressure". (3) Not perceiving grain direction: the trainee looks at the board one-directionally, does not distinguish the "narrower" and "wider" ring. Exercise: 5 boards at random, the trainee draws an arrow in chalk on each (arrow in the direction of growth), the trainer checks.
