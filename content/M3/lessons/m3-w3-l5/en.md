## Content

### 1. Five inspection steps, the order is not arbitrary

The order of the five batch-inspection steps has a **logical rationale**, it is not alphabetical:

**Step 1 (moisture) is first**, because if the average moisture is off target (>±2%) or the spread is outside tolerance (>2%), **the batch does not pass to release**, it goes straight back to drying. No point looking further.

**Step 2 (end-face inspection) is second**, because end checks are **the most common** and visible from the stack end (without unstacking board by board). If most boards have large end checks, the batch has a drying problem (too fast in the heating phase).

**Step 3 (surface inspection) is third**, requires pulling out individual boards from the top layer, takes more time.

**Step 4 (checker) is fourth**, requires the sampling saw and cutting. Only triggered if steps 1-3 give positive results (otherwise a waste of time).

**Step 5 (grading) is fifth**, because it is **the most expensive** (the grader works 8-10 hours for 145 boards). Only triggered on a **confirmed good batch**.

**The logic**: **each next step is more expensive than the previous one**, so filtering out bad batches at earlier steps saves time and money.

### 2. Why 5 measurement points, not 3 and not 10

Five measurement points come from the **EGIDA standard** based on **PN-EN 13183-2** (European standard for wood moisture measurement). The minimum is **3 points**, but for batches >2 m³ **5** is recommended. For batches >10 m³, **10 points** are recommended.

**The distribution of 5 points** (front-top, front-middle, middle length, rear-middle, rear-bottom) **covers 3 dimensions of non-uniformity**:
- **Along the chamber length** (front vs rear), fans push air from the front, the rear of the chamber gets weaker flow, wood dries 0.2-0.5% slower
- **Along the stack height** (top vs bottom), the bottom of the stack gets more heat from the chamber floor (heated), wood dries 0.3-0.5% faster
- **Depth into the stack** (edge vs centre), edges release water faster, the centre slower

**5 points are enough** to detect all three non-uniformities, **3 points are not enough** (by chance all could be from the centre, without an edge perspective), **10 points are excessive** (greater accuracy, but twice as much work for marginal information gain).

### 3. Moisture tolerances and wood classes

The **PN-EN 14081-1 strength class** requires moisture of **12% ±2%** for structural members. This means a batch with a 14% average for oak (target 14%) is **slightly above the structural standard**, but this is **typical for oak** (oak is hard to dry to 12% without brittleness risk, the 14% target is a compromise between strength quality and durability).

**The grader compares the moisture reading to the target for the species**:
- Pine (structural strength C24): target 12% ±2%, 10-14% batch fit
- Oak (structural strength C24 furniture): target 14% ±1%, 13-15% batch fit
- Beech (furniture strength C24 furniture): target 10% ±1%, 9-11% batch fit
- Birch (auxiliary strength C16): target 12% ±2%, 10-14% batch fit

**For today's oak batch**: average 14.12%, spread 0.9%. **Fit, very good.**

### 4. PN-EN 1611-1, visual grading

The standard **PN-EN 1611-1:2002 Sawn timber, Visual grading of coniferous sawn timber** (an analogous one exists for broadleaved) defines:
- **Quality classes**: G1 (best, no significant defects), G2 (good, limited defects), G3 (fit, larger defects), G4 (auxiliary, large defects).
- **Defect types**: knots (sound, resinous, rotten), cracks (end, pith, surface), wane, bark, resin streaks, mould patches, deformations (warp, twist).
- **Quantitative tolerances** for each defect in each class (maximum knot diameter, maximum crack length, etc.).

**At EGIDA, G1 + G2 = structural sale (C24)**, G3 = auxiliary sale (C16), G4 = shortening or reject.

Pani Ania is certified by **ITB Warsaw** (Building Research Institute) for visual grading of broadleaved sawn timber under PN-EN 1611-1 (certificate valid 5 years, renewed in 2024). A grader **without a certificate** may not stamp C24 boards, a legal requirement under ISO 9001 and a technical requirement from structural customers.

### 5. PN-EN 14081, strength grading

The standard **PN-EN 14081-1:2016 Timber structures, Strength graded structural timber** grades wood by **mechanical strength** (bending, tension, compression). Classes:
- **C16**: bending strength 16 N/mm² (auxiliary)
- **C24**: 24 N/mm² (standard, residential rafters, flooring, wall)
- **C30**: 30 N/mm² (higher, load-bearing structures, beams)
- **C35**: 35 N/mm² (heavily loaded members, exceptional)

**Visual strength grading** (per PN-EN 14081 Annex A) combines visual indicators (density, ring width, knots) with a **strength class**. Pani Ania does strength grading in parallel with visual, assessing both aspects on every board.

**Machine strength grading** (per PN-EN 14081 Annex B, MSR devices, Machine Stress Rating) gives higher confidence but requires equipment (EGIDA does not have it, sends samples to ITB on complaints). EGIDA uses Pani Ania's visual grading as the sole method.

### 8. Typical M3-operator mistakes in grading assistance

**Mistake 1. „I'll help the grader, I'll stamp the clean boards myself"**. The operator sees a board has no defects and stamps C24. **A legal error**: the grader is certified, the M3 operator is not. A board stamped by an M3 operator is **formally invalid**, yet from the customer's perspective it looks identical to a valid one. **Risk**: complaint, loss of the grader's ITB certificate (because she allowed an unauthorised person), loss of ISO 9001. **Correctly**: the M3 operator **never stamps**, even if sure of the class.

**Mistake 2. „I'll fill the card from memory in the evening"**. The operator assists the grader all day and plans to record all decisions at the end, thinking „I'll do it in the evening to save time". **Error**: after 8 hours and 145 boards the operator **does not remember the details** (which board had a 40 cm pith check, which had 30 cm, etc.), the record from memory is incomplete and potentially wrong. **Correctly**: the grader card is **filled in as you go, at the grader's dictation**, board by board.

**Mistake 3. „I won't record the moisture reading, it's within tolerance"**. The operator measures moisture at 5 points, all OK, thinks „no need to record, no problem". **Error**: the record is **required evidence for an ISO audit**. No record = no evidence = potential audit non-conformity. **Correctly**: **all measurements always recorded**, regardless of result.

**Mistake 4. „I'll show the customer the batch so they're pleased with the quality"**. A customer calls, says „I'm on the yard, show me what I'm buying". The operator thinks it is polite to show the batch **before the WZ card is issued**. **Error**: a batch without a WZ card should not leave the grading yard (it is not formally released), a customer seeing it without a card may pick „the best boards to take", which breaks release systematics. **Correctly**: **the customer sees the batch only after the WZ card**, at the release yard, in the foreman's presence.

**Mistake 5. „I'll send the checker sample to the lab myself"**. The operator assists in cutting samples and thinks „I'll do it all to save the master's time". **Error**: samples are archived in the **EGIDA lab**, not at the operator's personal desk. **Correctly**: **samples passed to the master**, who decides on archiving and labelling.

## Key terms

**Five-step post-cycle batch inspection** (*pięć kroków inspekcji*, EN *five-step post-cycle inspection*, ES *inspección pos-ciclo en cinco pasos*, UK *п'ятиетапна інспекція після циклу*): a sequential control procedure for wood dried in the chamber, from the cheapest step (moisture) to the most expensive (grading), filtering batches at earlier steps to save the grader's work.

**End check** (*pęknięcie czołowe*, EN *end check*, ES *fenda de testa*, UK *торцева тріщина*): a longitudinal crack on the end face of a board, formed by thermal shock in the heating phase or by a too-dry initial phase, tolerances C24 up to 10 cm × 3 mm, C16 up to 15 cm × 5 mm.

**Pith check** (*pęknięcie rdzeniowe*, EN *pith check*, ES *fenda de médula*, UK *серцевинна тріщина*): a crack along the pith line of a pith-containing board, a natural feature of pith boards, tolerated for C24 up to 40 cm × 3 mm deep.

**Checker (internal honeycomb check)** (*pęknięcie wewnętrzne*, EN *internal honeycomb check*, ES *fenda interna tipo panal*, UK *внутрішня тріщина типу стільники*): a crack inside a board invisible from the outside, formed by stresses not drained during conditioning, diagnosed with a sampling saw and a 10× magnifier.

**Longitudinal bow** (*paczenie wzdłużne*, EN *bow warp*, ES *alabeo longitudinal*, UK *повздовжнє жолоблення*): lengthwise bending of a board, C24 limit 6 mm/4 m, C16 8 mm/4 m.

**Transverse cup** (*paczenie poprzeczne*, EN *cup warp*, ES *alabeo transversal*, UK *поперечне жолоблення*): bending of a board across the width, C24 limit 2 mm/155 mm width, C16 3 mm.

**Twist** (*skręt*, EN *twist warp*, ES *alabeo en torsión*, UK *скручування*): twisting of a board along its length axis, C24 limit 3 mm/4 m, C16 5 mm.

**Handheld sampling saw** (*piła próbnikowa*, EN *sampling saw*, ES *sierra de muestreo*, UK *пилка для відбору зразків*): a small 100 mm cordless circular saw, used to cut 50 × 155 mm samples from the ends of representative boards for checker diagnosis.

**PN-EN 1611-1 grading** (*klasyfikacja wizualna*, EN *visual grading PN-EN 1611-1*, ES *clasificación visual PN-EN 1611-1*, UK *візуальне сортування PN-EN 1611-1*): a European standard for visual grading of sawn timber, defining classes G1-G4 based on visible defects, requiring grader certification by ITB.

**PN-EN 14081-1 grading** (*klasyfikacja wytrzymałościowa*, EN *strength grading PN-EN 14081-1*, ES *clasificación de resistencia PN-EN 14081-1*, UK *сортування за міцністю PN-EN 14081-1*): a European standard for strength grading of structural timber, classes C16/C24/C30/C35, visual grading (Annex A) or machine MSR (Annex B).

**Grader card** (*karta klasyfikatora*, EN *grader card*, ES *ficha del clasificador*, UK *картка класифікатора*): an EGIDA A3 form, one row per board, filled in by the M3 operator (documentation assistant) at Pani Ania's dictation during grading, signed by the grader at the end, retention 3 years paper + 5 years scan.

**WZ card (external goods issue)** (*karta WZ*, EN *goods issue card*, ES *albarán de salida*, UK *накладна видачі*): an EGIDA A4 form issued by the grader after grading and before release to the customer, containing all batch data (species, classes, moisture, certificates), forms the basis for a 12-month complaint, retention 5 years paper + 10 years scan.

**Pani Ania (Anna Kowalczyk), EGIDA grader** (*klasyfikatorka certyfikowana*, EN *certified grader*, ES *clasificadora certificada*, UK *сертифікований класифікатор*): an EGIDA employee with an ITB Warsaw certificate for visual grading under PN-EN 1611-1 and strength grading under PN-EN 14081-1, with EGIDA since 2012, decides on the class of every board, the M3 operator assists but does not grade.

**Wood scorching** (*zaparzenie*, EN *kiln burn / heat damage*, ES *quemado por calor*, UK *перепарювання / теплові ушкодження*): wood damage due to too high a temperature in the main cycle phase, visible as dark patches on the surface, sharp smoky smell, the batch does not qualify for any structural class.

**Internal wood mould** (*pleśń wewnętrzna*, EN *internal mold*, ES *moho interno*, UK *внутрішня цвіль*): fungal growth within the wood structure caused by too high RH in the pre-drying phase, recognisable by a **sweetish, faint smell**, invisible on the surface, causes a batch reject.
