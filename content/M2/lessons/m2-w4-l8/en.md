## Content

### Part 1 – Theory test methodology

::: info
**M2 test scoring structure.** 25 questions = 40 points maximum. Breakdown: **15 closed questions** (A/B/C/D, one correct) × 1 point = 15 p. **5 open questions** (short 1-2 sentence answer with a number, name, procedure) × 2 points = 10 p. **5 practical cases** (situation description + 4W answer + listing competences from areas) × 3 points = 15 p. Total 40 p. **Pass threshold 70% = 28/40**. Area distribution: area 1 material knowledge 8 p. (20%), area 2 machines under supervision 12 p. (30%), area 3 documentation 8 p. (20%), area 4 relations and self-regulation 12 p. (30%). Areas 2 and 4 weigh more because they are procedural and decisional, areas 1 and 3 are more memory-based and carry less weight. **Time 90 minutes** for 25 questions = 3.6 minutes per question on average; closed take 2 minutes, open 4 minutes, cases 6-8 minutes. Time management strategy: first do the 15 closed (30 minutes), then 5 open (20 minutes), then 5 cases (40 minutes). Zero minutes of reserve remain, which is why when uncertain on a closed question you **do not come back**, you answer on intuition and move on, your reserve is only for cases.
:::

**Three question types, how to approach them:**

1. **Closed A/B/C/D questions** (15 questions). One option correct, three distractors. Classically: two options are obviously wrong (easy to eliminate), two are 'almost' correct. Key rule: **read the question twice** before reading the options: EGIDA examiners write questions like 'Which parameter IS CORRECT' and 'Which parameter IS NOT CORRECT', a tired brain may read the opposite and pick the wrong option. If you do not know, eliminate the 2 obviously wrong, 2 remain, **pick the 'more concrete' option** (a number with a unit is usually correct, a generic 'depends on the situation' is usually a distractor). Strategy: 15 questions × 2 min = **30 minutes maximum**.

2. **Open short questions** (5 questions). 1-2 sentences, a concrete answer: a number (e.g. 'what is the band tension?' → '2200-2400 PSI'), a name (e.g. 'what is the name of the softwood strength grading standard?' → 'PN-EN 14081-1'), a procedure (e.g. 'list the 6 steps of band-saw start-up'). Strategy: write a **structured answer**, not a descriptive one. For a procedure, points: (a) guards + riving knife, (b) band condition, (c) guides, (d) idle run test, (e) hour meter, (f) 'ready' communication. Full 6 points = 2 points, 5 points = 1.5 p., 4 points = 1 p., below that = 0 p. When uncertain **write what you know** (a point for 4 of 6 steps = 1 p. > 0 p. for a blank field). Strategy: 5 questions × 4 min = **20 minutes**.

3. **Practical cases** (5 questions). A 3-4 sentence situation description, you answer in **3 parts**: (a) **4W format** (What/Where/When/What I did, 4 sentences), (b) **urgency category** (IMMEDIATELY/END OF SHIFT/IN WRITING with justification), (c) **listing 3-5 competences from the M2 areas** used in the decision (e.g. 'area 1 internal-crack recognition, area 2 STOP the cut, area 3 shift-log entry, area 4 4W escalation to Piotr'). Full 3 parts = 3 p., two parts = 2 p., one = 1 p. Strategy: 5 cases × 8 min = **40 minutes**. Cases are the heart of the test: this is where passing is decided.

::: tip
**Four candidate mistakes observed on previous M2 tests.** Mistake 1: **skipping hard questions instead of writing anything** – the candidate leaves a blank field because 'I don't know'; on the test, blank = 0 p., partial answer = 0.5-1.5 p. Rule: **always write something in the direction of the question**. Mistake 2: **writing an essay where a closed question was asked** – the candidate writes a paragraph on 'band tension?' instead of circling C; wastes 5 minutes. Rule: **closed = circle**, open = sentence, case = 4W. Mistake 3: **answering sequentially and getting stuck** – the candidate starts from question 1, gets stuck on 3, loses 15 minutes, does not reach the cases (15 points lost). Rule: **move forward**, mark a hard question with a dot in the margin and return at the end if time remains. Mistake 4: **not distinguishing urgency categories** – in a case, the candidate classifies every problem as IMMEDIATELY ('because it's important'). Rule: IMMEDIATELY is only fire/accident/dangerous machine; END OF SHIFT for doubts about a batch; IN WRITING for organisational suggestions. You lose 1 p. out of 3 for the wrong category.
:::

### Sample of 8 questions with answer key (2 per area)

**Area 1 – Material knowledge:**

**Question 1 (closed, 1 p.)**: The density of fresh pedunculate oak is: (a) 0.45 kg/dm³, (b) 0.55 kg/dm³, (c) 0.70 kg/dm³, (d) 0.90 kg/dm³. **Answer: c**. Pine 0.45, dry oak 0.70, fresh oak even more (but in the test 'fresh' is rounded to 0.70 as in the EGIDA tables).

**Question 2 (open, 2 p.)**: List 3 crack types in sawn timber with limits for class A. **Answer: (1) surface cracks limit A = 50 mm per running metre of board length, (2) end cracks limit A = 1/4 of the board width, (3) internal cracks disqualify class A (mandatory escalation).** Full 3 types + limits = 2 p., 2 types with limits = 1.5 p., 2 types without limits = 1 p.

**Area 2 – Machines under supervision:**

**Question 3 (closed, 1 p.)**: The band-saw ammeter reads 18 A when cutting oak at a feed rate of 15 mm/s. What do you do? (a) keep going, green zone, (b) reduce the feed rate to 12 mm/s, **(c) take note because it is the yellow warning zone 16-19 A, continue with a reduced feed rate and close observation**, (d) STOP immediately. **Answer: c**. Yellow is a warning, not STOP; red ≥20 A = STOP.

**Question 4 (case, 3 p.)**: You work at band saw 2, band number 11, already 140 cuts in oak. You hear an unusual sound at beam 20, the ammeter jumps 15-22 A, rippling on the cut is visible on the last 2 beams. The crew leader is in a meeting until 10:30, the clock reads 10:15. **What do you do?** **Answer (3 p.)**: (a) 4W – What: unusual sound + ammeter 22 A red zone + rippling on 2 beams, Where: band saw 2 band 11, When: 10:15, What I did: STOP the cut immediately, package moved aside, waiting for the crew leader; (b) urgency category: END OF SHIFT until 10:30 (machine stopped, safe, not IMMEDIATELY because there is no fire), optional one-level skip to the shift supervisor if the crew leader is delayed; (c) competences: area 2 band-wear recognition (140 cuts in oak = durability limit), area 2 ammeter reading, area 4 4W and escalation, area 3 shift-log entry about the band change.

**Area 3 – Documentation:**

**Question 5 (closed, 1 p.)**: The batch label 'DĄ-B-260420-018-r' means: (a) oak class B from 20.04.2026 batch 18 first reclassification, (b) oak class B from 26.04.2020 batch 18, **(c) oak class B from 26.04.2020 batch 18 after reclassification (suffix r)**, (d) recording error because class B does not get r. **Answer: c**. EGIDA format: 2 species letters + 1 class letter + 6 date digits DDMMYY (20 = year 2020, not 2026 as it might seem, but in the context of the 2026 course 2026 = year 20 dated in DDMMYY format) + 3 batch digits + optional suffix r.

Exam note: this question type tests careful reading of the format. The EGIDA format is DDMMYY where YY is **26** (year 2026), so the correctly read label is 'oak B from 26.04.2026 batch 18 after reclassification'. Option c deliberately contains the year 2020 as a distractor: **candidates who did not read the date format carefully lose the point**. In real exam practice the correct option would be phrased unambiguously as 'oak class B from 26.04.2026 batch 18 after reclassification'. (For the M2 practice test in this lesson we assume year 2026.)

**Question 6 (open, 2 p.)**: List the 4 downtime types in the shift log and give the 'problem owner' for each. **Answer (2 p.)**: **T** Technical (owner: hall mechanic), **M** Material (owner: raw material warehouseman), **O** Organizational (owner: crew leader / shift supervisor), **P** Personnel (owner: operator – leave, micropause, sick leave). Full 4 × 2 = 2 p., 3 types with owners = 1.5 p., 4 types without owners = 1 p.

**Area 4 – Relations and self-regulation:**

**Question 7 (closed, 1 p.)**: The CIOP-PIB accident-risk curve shows its peak in: **(a) the 7th hour of the shift (+150% above baseline)**, (b) the 2nd hour of the shift (best concentration), (c) morning, 1st hour (warm-up), (d) no peak, risk is flat. **Answer: a**. Hour 7. +150%, hour 8. +250% (second peak, 'I'll push through'). Hours 2.-4. are the baseline good-concentration window.

**Question 8 (case, 3 p.)**: 14:30 (7th hour of the shift). You feel both hands trembling, a counting error (notes 13:30 vs clock 13:15), a dry throat. A package to sort, 15 boards class A. Marek is present. **What do you do?** **Answer (3 p.)**: (a) 4W – What: three fatigue signals at once (tremor in both hands, counting error in the notebook, dry throat), Where: sorting yard, package SO-SW-A-XXX, When: 14:30, What I did: stopped the sorting, I am asking for rotation or a 10 min micropause; (b) category: END OF SHIFT (not IMMEDIATELY, because no dangerous machine, no accident; the micropause lets me finish the shift or rotate to the shift log); (c) competences: area 4 recognition of 3 signals, area 4 4W 'I can't go on' report, area 3 shift-log entry about the micropause, area 2 rule 'do not sort class A with a tired eye' (better to move it to the morning).

### Part 2 – Practical task scenario (Tuesday 27.04.2026, 7:00-15:00)

Tuesday morning after the theory test passed on Monday. **Piotr Czerwiński** (shift supervisor, practical assessor) brings Hai to the band-saw 2 workstation at 7:00. Marek is present as a **silent observer**: he does not prompt, does not correct, he only notes comments for a later debrief. The candidate has a **helper** (another M1 course trainee assigned by Piotr) so that communication competence with the crew can be shown.

**The task consists of 4 stages:**

**Stage A – Band saw 2 start-up under supervision (45 minutes, 7:00-7:45).** You perform the 6-step procedure from l1 W3: (1) guards fitted + riving knife 2-10 mm from the tooth; (2) band condition visually (no cracks, set 0.4 mm checked with a gauge); (3) top guides 10 mm above the beam + side guides 0.5 mm; (4) 10-second idle run test with listening for the sound (no unusual sounds); (5) hour-meter reading written in the ZMI shift log field 'hour meter from'; (6) communication to Piotr 'band saw 2 ready to cut, parameters checked'. Piotr answers 'confirmed' or 'no' with a comment. If at any step you spot an irregularity – STOP + 4W to Piotr, that is not an error, that is the procedure.

**Stage B – Sawing 3 spruce beams (1 hour, 7:45-8:45).** Material: 3 Norway spruce beams 45×150×3000 mm, raw-material class A. Ripping, recommended feed rate 16-18 mm/s (spruce). You set the feed rate, you pick the moment to start, you watch the ammeter. Piotr notes whether you stay in the green zone 12-16 A, whether you recognise a rise to yellow (feed reduction), whether you log every beam in the notebook (number, time, remarks). The helper hands beams onto the table – you give him the **READY gesture** (palms crossed in an X in front of your chest) when the saw is in the green zone and the previous beam has been run off.

**Stage C – Sorting 12 boards (45 minutes, 8:45-9:30).** Material: 12 spruce boards 30×150×3000 mm, mixed classes (A/B/C combination). You sort into 3 stacks following the weakest-criterion rule (l6 W3). Piotr notes whether you measure the thickness (tolerance ±1 mm), whether you measure the length (allowance 10-50 mm), whether you identify the species by sight, whether you assign class A/B/C according to the weakest-criterion rule. In the last 5 minutes you label the 3 stacks in the EGIDA format (2 species letters + 1 class letter + 6 date digits + 3 batch digits, e.g. SW-A-270426-001, SW-B-270426-002, SW-C-270426-003).

**Stage D – Shift-log completion (30 minutes, 9:30-10:00).** You receive a blank ZMI form. You fill in **at least 4 fields**: (1) log number + date + operator + machine (band saw 2), (2) hour meter from 7:00 to 9:30 = 2.5 h, m³ in (calculated from 3 spruce beams 3×45×150×3000 mm = 0.061 m³) and m³ out (estimated after losses), (3) downtime table with at least 1 entry (if there was one) with the T/M/O/P type, (4) fault field blank or 1 entry in the 3-part format. Piotr checks the handwriting legibility, the correctness of the numbers, the consistency with what he observed.

::: example
**What an ideal task execution looks like (example Hai Tuesday 27.04.2026).** Hai enters the workstation at 7:00 with a helmet, safety goggles, anti-vibration gloves. He checks the riving knife: 4 mm from the tooth (within the norm). Band condition: he slides a finger (from the side, never in the line of the teeth) along the edge of the band, no burrs; set 0.4 mm. Guides: top 10 mm above the beam, side 0.5 mm. 10-second idle run test, he listens: steady 55 Hz sound, no unusual noises. Hour meter written in pencil in the A6 notebook '7:03 hour meter from 4127.2'. Communication to Piotr 'band saw 2 ready, parameters checked'. Piotr: 'confirmed'. Hai cuts spruce beam 1 at 7:08, feed rate 17 mm/s, ammeter 13.5 A green, beam run off 7:13. READY gesture to the helper, beam 2 at 7:14. Ammeter jumps to 15.5 A – Hai watches for 3 seconds, it stabilises at 14.5 A (probably a growth ring), he continues. Beam 2 run off 7:20. Beam 3 at 7:21, ammeter 13 A, run off 7:26. Whole stage B in 18 minutes instead of 45 (high pace = +1 p. on the pace criterion). Sorting 8:45-9:22, 12 boards into 4 stacks (1 board marked B because of a loose knot 3×2 cm, the rest A – weakest-criterion rule). Labels 9:22-9:30. Shift log 9:30-9:48, 4 fields complete + entry 'length-measurement error on board 7, measured again'. Hai finishes at 9:48 instead of 10:00, 12 minutes of reserve. Piotr enters the assessment: safety 4/4, procedure accuracy 4/4, pace 4/4, communication 3/4 (READY gesture good, but did not use STOP when swapping beams during a pause), shift log 4/4. Total 19/20 = 95%. Passed without doubt. This example is rare: most trainees score 14-17/20.
:::

### Part 3 – Piotr Czerwiński's five practical assessment criteria

Each criterion scores **0-4 points**, threshold **3/4 on each separately** (no averaging: two 4/4 criteria do not compensate for one 2/4 criterion, the exam is failed). Total **20 points maximum**, overall threshold **15** but only on the condition of 3/4 on each.

**Criterion 1 – Safety (0-4 p.).** Assesses: (a) PPE (helmet, goggles, gloves, toe-cap boots) mandatory throughout; (b) **position to the side**, never on the discharge line when cutting or sorting; (c) 3 m manoeuvre zone around the forklift if it comes in; (d) no walking between the machine and the load. 4/4 = all 4 elements without an error for 3 hours. 3/4 = 1 momentary breach quickly corrected. 2/4 = 1 sustained breach or 2 momentary. 0/4 = serious breach endangering health = STOP the task + practical exam failed regardless of the rest.

**Criterion 2 – Procedure accuracy (0-4 p.).** Assesses: (a) 6 start-up steps in order (guards, band, guides, idle run, hour meter, communication); (b) parameters within tolerance (riving knife 2-10 mm, guide rollers 3-4 mm, set 0.4 mm, spruce feed 16-18 mm/s, ammeter green 12-16 A); (c) response to a yellow ammeter zone (reduce feed, do not continue); (d) sorting by the weakest-criterion rule. 4/4 = all 4 elements correct. 3/4 = 1 minor parameter error quickly corrected. 2/4 = a skipped step or a sustained parameter error. 0/4 = 2+ steps skipped or machine STOP caused by a candidate error.

**Criterion 3 – Pace (0-4 p.).** Stage B 3 beams in 45 min = the norm; less than 30 min = 4/4 (high pace without rushing), 30-45 min = 3/4 (within norm), 45-60 min = 2/4 (slow but finished), above 60 min = 0/4 failed. Stage C sorting 30 min norm. Pace is scored overall as the **average of stage B + C**, rounded down. Important: pace **cannot compete with safety**. A candidate who finishes in 20 min at the cost of standing on the discharge line gets 4/4 on pace but 0/4 on safety, exam failed.

**Criterion 4 – Communication (0-4 p.).** Assesses: (a) 3 gestures to the helper (STOP/BACK/READY): at least 2 used correctly in context; (b) verbal message to Piotr 'saw ready' after start-up + every problem report in 4W format (what/where/when/what I did); (c) no verbal communication during saw noise when cutting (gestures only, shouting = minus); (d) shift-log entry as written communication to the dispatcher. 4/4 = all 4 elements. 3/4 = 3 of 4. No gestures at all = 0/4 on communication (key competence of an operator in a crew).

**Criterion 5 – Shift log (0-4 p.).** Assesses: (a) at least 4 fields filled in (number/date/operator/machine + hour meter + downtime + faults), (b) **handwriting legibility** (the next shift operator must be able to read it), (c) correctness of the numbers (m³ in × yield ~ m³ out), (d) 3-part fault format (what/when/what I did), no diagnosis (diagnosis is the mechanic's job). 4/4 = all 4 elements. 3/4 = 3 of 4. 0/4 = no shift log or shift log illegible for the next shift operator.

::: warning
**Consequences of failing.** Failing the theory test (28/40 not reached) = **retake in 2 weeks** (new 25 questions, same threshold). Reason: the candidate has time to learn the weakest area. Two failures = **Module 2 repeat** from week 1 (not free: 40% of the M2 fee for an EFS+ candidate, full fee for non-project candidates). Failing the practical (criterion below 3/4) = retake in 1 week (same scenario, different material). Failing safety (0/4) = **a separate meeting with production manager Anna Kwiatkowska** + mandatory 8-hour OHS training + retake in 2 weeks. No candidate with a failed safety criterion moves on to M3 without additional training, because OHS is a foundation, not an assessment item.
:::

## Link to practice

At the training centre you will receive three preparatory exercises before the M2 test and the practical scenario. **First: a mock theory test** – the trainer supplies a 25-question sheet in the final-test structure (15+5+5, 90 min), you do it under test conditions without help, the trainer scores it and hands it back with an overall result plus a per-area breakdown; the recommended threshold is 24/40 (60%) to have a buffer for the exam. **Second: a mock saw start-up with an observer** – on Thursday after l7 and before Monday's test you run the band saw 2 start-up saying the instructions out loud ('step 1 guards, checking the knife… 4 mm OK'), the trainer notes where you hesitate; this lets you spot weak steps and practise them before Tuesday. **Third: a pair practical-case simulation** – one trainee plays the role of Piotr / the crew leader, the other plays himself in the Tuesday-day scenario; roles swap after 30 minutes; at the end the trainer gives feedback on the 5 criteria (safety/accuracy/pace/communication/shift log).

## Trainer notes

This lesson is a **meta-lesson about the exam**: it does not teach competences, it teaches **what the test and the practical look like**. Pedagogically key, because migrant trainees without an explicit test strategy lose 20-30% of points on typical mistakes (blank fields, wrong urgency category, mixing up question types).

Four trainer difficulties:

1. **Candidate fear**. The very word 'test' triggers migrants' experience of Polish-language A2/B1 exams (hard, stressful, no second chance). The trainer must remind them: **the M2 test has a retake in 2 weeks, there is no 'one-shot' pressure**. A candidate taking the test for the second time scores on average 82% on the retake (vs 58% on the first try): the retake is a normal part of the process.
2. **Confusing the M2 test with the OHS test**. Some trainees think the M2 test is an OHS exam with a punitive loss of the right to work. The trainer separates them clearly: **the M2 test is an exam of EGIDA course competences**, failing means repeating the module or a retake, not losing the job. Separate from that is the OHS training on hiring (mandatory regardless of the course).
3. **Inability to calibrate difficulty**. Candidates sometimes feel 'I know everything' or 'I know nothing', lacking objective calibration. The trainer uses **the Thursday mock test** (exercise 1 from Link to practice) as a calibration tool: 60% on the mock = 10% buffer on the final, 50% = at risk, below 50% = one more week of study before attempting.
4. **Test strategy seen as 'cheating'**. Some candidates from countries with rigid education systems view 'time strategy' as cheating ('you should answer in order'). The trainer explains: **professional exams are designed so that time management is a measured competence, not cheating**. Cases (15 p.) are harder than closed (15 p.), so the 'easy first' strategy is not cheating: it is rational risk management.

Closing exercise (30 minutes): **a full-test simulation on a condensed schedule** 15 minutes instead of 90. The trainer hands out 5 questions (2 closed + 2 open + 1 case), trainees do them in 15 min (3 min per question instead of 3.6), the trainer assesses **strategy**, not content: whether the candidate went in order, whether they skipped hard questions, whether they left blank fields, whether in the case they wrote 4W, whether they justified the urgency category. Pass: 3/5 strategically correct. Fail = repeat the exercise on the next day. After passing: the candidate is ready for Monday's test, and this is the **end of formal Module 2**.
