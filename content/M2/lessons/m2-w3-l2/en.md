## Introduction

Tuesday, 7:10 a.m., Wood-Mizer LT40 bandsaw number two. Yesterday Oleksij started it up on his own for the first time – six steps, 2 minutes 45 seconds, and Marek wrote in the observation notebook: "first start-up, OK, slow pace, sequence without errors." Today the second thing he performs by himself under supervision: **adjustment of the band-guide rollers**. A week ago Sofía was handing the dial indicator to Tadeusz during a band change (lesson 8 of week 2): today Oleksij holds that same indicator in his own hand. The parameters that Sofía memorised by observation, Oleksij must now **execute with a 0.5 mm tolerance** on the actual machine. Marek stands two metres away, in his left hand the same observation notebook, in his right a back-up dial indicator in case Oleksij's fails. It is a quiet moment, but an important one: the trainee is about to move from watching numbers to producing them.

This lesson is the **first mechanical adjustment** performed by the trainee in module two. Up to now you were observing parameters (2200–2400 PSI, 3–4 mm, 30 s – lesson 8 of week 2), assisting by passing tools, and knowing the trade numbers by heart. Now you are **physically changing the machine setting**: you turn a screw by 0.5 mm, you measure, you turn again, until the dial indicator reads a value inside the 3.0–4.0 mm band. After your adjustment the machine will cut better (or worse): that is your responsibility, your signature in the workstation adjustment log, your hand on the screw.

Guide-roller adjustment and band-tension check form a **five-step procedure**. You perform it after every band change (lesson 8 of week 2), after every suspicion of a wavy kerf (lesson 7 of week 2 – wear indicators), and once a week as a control routine (preventive maintenance). Frequency: 3–4 times a week when cutting oak, 1–2 times when cutting pine.

## Objectives

After this lesson:

1. You know the **five steps of the band-guide adjustment procedure**: LOTO and lock-out → measuring four rollers with the dial indicator → adjusting the fixing screw (4 mm hex key, moves of 0.5 mm) → re-measurement → checking evenness in the retracted and extended head positions. Closing the guards, 30 seconds of idle run, and a test kerf – just as in lesson 1 of this week.
2. You understand **why 3–4 mm is not an engineer's whim** but the safe working window: below 3 mm the roller rubs against the back of the band, heats up (to 80–120 °C within an hour), causes premature roller wear and micro-cracks in the band's back; above 4 mm the band has 0.5–1.5 mm of lateral play, the kerf is wavy (waviness above 0.3 mm/m means loss of Class A lumber – lesson 6 of week 2).
3. You can **read the dial indicator** with an accuracy of 0.01 mm: you attach the magnetic base to the saw body, slide the probe between the roller and the band, read the large hand (tenths of a mm) and the small hand (whole mm). You learn to tell 3.2 mm from 3.8 mm without hesitation: these are two different answers, not the same result.
4. You know the **difference between the retracted and extended head position**: the LT40 has a movable head that travels on rails along the log. In the retracted position (at the start of the rails, without cutting) the rollers are looser; in the extended position (mid-rail, in the cutting position) the band is loaded by the timber's pressure and the rollers must hold it in axis. **You check in both positions**, because a difference larger than 0.3 mm between them means a worn roller bearing or a deformed rail.
5. You apply the rule **"adjust under supervision, always with the indicator, always on both sides"**: you never adjust "by eye", you never adjust without a second person nearby (even as an experienced operator), and you never limit yourself to a single roller (symmetrical adjustment: 4 rollers, 4 measurements, 4 entries in the notebook).

## Content

::: info
At the Fundacja EGIDA sawmill, guide adjustment takes **8–15 minutes** for an experienced operator (15–25 minutes for a junior during the first weeks). It is performed after every band change (mandatory), after a suspected wavy kerf (diagnostic), and once a week (preventive). Annually: 150–250 adjustments. Each one is recorded in the **workstation adjustment log** with an entry containing: date, time, saw number, four roller readings (upper front, upper rear, lower front, lower rear), who adjusted, who supervised.
:::

### Five procedure steps – what you do, what you measure, what you record

**Step 1: LOTO and opening the guards.** The rule from lesson 8 of week 2 is unchanged. You press STOP (mushroom button), wait 15 seconds for the band to coast down, turn the main switch to OFF, fit **your own named LOTO padlock** ("Oleksij B.") and a tag showing the start time of the adjustment. You open the upper and lower guards with a 6 mm hex key (4 M8 screws each). Difference compared with lesson 8 of week 2: you do not dismount the band, you **adjust the rollers with the band mounted but stationary**. The rollers are accessible from the console side: you do not need to crawl under the machine.

**Step 2: Measuring the four rollers with the dial indicator.** The saw has four guide rollers: two **upper** ones (near the upper tensioning wheel, holding the band on the tooth side) and two **lower** ones (near the work table, just before and just after the cutting point, holding the band on the back side). The dial indicator is a device with a dial 40–50 mm in diameter, a 0–10 mm scale and a magnetic base that sticks to the saw body. The probe has a 2 mm ball tip.

Procedure for measuring one roller (repeat 4 times):
1. Stick the magnetic base of the indicator to a stable surface of the saw (the body, not the guard).
2. Slide the probe into the gap between the roller and the back of the band (the probe touches the roller on one side, the back of the band on the other).
3. Read: the large hand shows tenths of a mm (1 full revolution = 1 mm), the small hand shows whole mm. A reading of "3.3" means 3 mm + 0.3 mm.
4. Note in the notebook: "upper front: 3.3 mm", "upper rear: 3.1 mm", "lower front: 3.5 mm", "lower rear: 3.2 mm".

::: tip
Trainer: "The dial indicator is not a tool for perfectionists. It is a tool for operators who know that 3.0 mm and 3.8 mm are two different answers. You do not have to measure to 0.01 mm accuracy: you have to be sure that it is 3.3 and not 4.1. Certainty comes from repetition: the first five measurements take 2 minutes each, after the twentieth they take 20 seconds. After a month Oleksij will measure a roller in 15 seconds, without losing accuracy."
:::

**Step 3: Adjusting the fixing screw – moves of 0.5 mm.** Each roller has a fixing screw (4 mm hex, accessible from the inside of the guard) that lets you move the roller across the band. Turning it **clockwise** brings the roller closer to the band (the indicator value drops); **anticlockwise** moves it away (the value rises). One full turn of the screw equals 0.8 mm of roller travel (M4 × 0.7 thread plus mechanism tolerance).

Rule: **moves of half a turn = 0.4 mm**. You do not crank it to the stop, you do not spin whole turns "fast". After every half-turn you check with the indicator. You adjust until the value sits in the 3.5 ± 0.3 mm band (the centre of the 3–4 mm range, following the "centre, not minimum, not maximum" rule from lesson 8 of week 2). Repeat for all four rollers. Do not adjust "to an average": each roller individually, each to the centre of the band.

::: warning
**Do not move the roller with your fingers, bypassing the screw.** A roller looks like a small part (40 mm in diameter), but it is mounted on a precision bearing: pressure from your fingers shifts the bearing off its axis, the roller starts running crooked, and within an hour the band begins to flap. Adjust only with the screw, only with the 4 mm hex key, only in half-turn moves. If the screw does not respond (seized nut, stripped thread): STOP, report to Marek, do not force it.
:::

**Step 4: Re-measurement after adjustment.** After finishing the adjustment of all four rollers, **measure each one again**. The goal is to confirm that no screw has "let go" during the adjustment of a neighbour (sometimes adjusting the lower front roller slightly changes the setting of the lower rear: a mechanical coupling in the structure). Four measurements, four entries in the notebook – a **separate "after adjustment" column** next to the "before adjustment" column. The difference between the two columns shows how much you adjusted (usually 0.2–0.8 mm per roller).

**Step 5: Checking evenness in the extended head position.** So far you have been adjusting with the head in the retracted position (at the start of the rails, without material). Now you manually move the head **to the middle of the rails**: to the position in which it would normally cut the material. The LT40 has a mechanical safety interlock for manual movement (a lever below the console); the head slides along the rails under the force of your hand (30–50 N). In the extended position you measure all four rollers again.

**Allowable difference between retracted and extended position: up to 0.3 mm per roller.** If the difference is larger, report to Marek; do not fix it yourself. Reasons: a worn roller bearing (requires service replacement), a deformed head rail (requires geodetic adjustment), or a loose roller mounting in the body (can be tightened, but by Marek after notifying the UDT).

After checking in both positions: return the head to the retracted position, close the guards (4 M8 screws each, 6 mm hex), remove the LOTO padlock and tag, turn the main switch to ON, announce the start-up loudly, run 30 seconds of idle and a test kerf on a piece of scrap wood (per lesson 1 of this week, steps 4–6). Record in the workstation adjustment log: 8 readings (4 rollers × 2 positions), saw number, time, signature, Marek's supervision.

### Why symmetrical adjustment – four rollers, not one

Trainees sometimes ask: "Why adjust all four? If one is OK, that's enough." The answer: **the band of a bandsaw is guided in three planes simultaneously** (vertical axis of motion, horizontal axis of timber pressure, diagonal axis of cutting forces). One unadjusted roller means the band compensates through the other three, but that compensation introduces **asymmetrical stresses**: within 6–10 hours of cutting, tooth wear rises by 20–40 %, and in extreme cases it causes a crack in the gullet (back to lesson 7 of week 2, module 2, the 5 wear indicators).

Symmetrical adjustment = 4 rollers each in the 3.2–3.8 mm band, with a difference between them of no more than 0.5 mm. One roller at 3.0 mm and another at 4.0 mm is formally "in band" but practically wrong: 1 mm of asymmetry means 15–20 % worse band guidance, visibly wavy kerf on the first boards of the day, and accelerated wear on the corner where the asymmetry loads the band most. That is why the rule is "four rollers, four measurements, four entries" – never "three out of four is close enough".

## Link to practice

**Exercise 1 (first adjustments under supervision, 3 times in the first week):** Every morning during the first week after lesson 2 you perform a guide adjustment on saw number two under Marek's eye. Marek records in the observation notebook the pace (target: 15 minutes after 10 repetitions) and the accuracy of the four final measurements (target: all in the 3.2–3.8 mm band, the difference between rollers no more than 0.5 mm). You do not grade yourself – Marek grades you.

**Exercise 2 (dial-indicator reading in 15 seconds, daily):** For two weeks Marek gives you one task each day: "Measure the upper front roller and give me the result in 15 seconds." You practise until the reading becomes automatic: hand in position X = result 3.4 mm, without counting lines, without hesitation.

**Exercise 3 (adjustment log, after every completed adjustment):** After every adjustment done under supervision you write in your personal notebook the full record of eight readings (4 rollers × 2 positions) plus notes on screw behaviour (easy to turn, stiff, seized). After 5 entries Marek checks the notebook and comments. You learn to write in the company log before you start signing off adjustments on your own.

## Trainer notes

**Pedagogical goal**: the first mechanical adjustment performed by the trainee in M2. Transition from "I know the parameters" (lesson 8 of week 2 as an assistant) to "I set the parameters myself" (lesson 2 of week 3 as the operator under supervision). Key difference: in lesson 8 of week 2 Sofía knew 3–4 mm as a number to memorise; in lesson 2 of week 3 Oleksij has to **produce that number with his own hand** to 0.3 mm accuracy.

**The protagonist Oleksij continues**: the full week 3 (lessons 1–8) with one character gives narrative coherence and shows the competence arc "from first start-up, to first solo adjustment, to first solo cut". Oleksij's craft background (ex-carpenter from Kharkiv) helps: he knows the idea of a "0.3 mm tolerance" from the furniture workshop. But an industrial bandsaw is a new environment – the tolerance is the same, but the forces, speeds and risks are different.

**Continuation from lesson 8 of week 2**: the same saw (LT40 #2), the same rollers, the same dial indicator. Oleksij holds in his hand the tool that Sofía was handing to Tadeusz a week ago. Pedagogically: show that numerical parameters (3–4 mm) are not abstract knowledge – they are a physical thing you produce with a screw and an indicator.

**Facilitation pitfalls**:
1. **Do not allow "by eye" adjustment.** A trainee who "feels it looks right" sets the roller out of band in 30 % of cases. The dial indicator is not optional – it is a control tool, just like the pressure gauge in the tension step (lesson 8 of week 2).
2. **Do not shorten the procedure by skipping step 4 (re-measurement) or step 5 (extended position).** These two steps catch 80 % of adjustment errors: step 4 detects screws that "let go" during a neighbour's adjustment, step 5 detects worn bearings and deformed rails. A trainee who shortens to 3 steps is a trainee who, three months from now, will adjust asymmetrically.

**Integration with earlier lessons**: lesson 8 of week 2 (trade parameters 3–4 mm, dial indicator as a tool) → lesson 2 of week 3 performs the adjustment. Lesson 7 of week 2 (5 wear indicators, including kerf waviness) → adjustment prevents waviness. Lesson 4 of week 1 of M2 (5-point checklist) → adjustment is part of point 5 (environment and parameter check). Lesson 1 of week 3 (6 start-up steps) → after the adjustment you run steps 4–6 (idle run, observation, test kerf).

**Preview of lesson 3 of this week**: cutting parameters and feed rate – the first lesson in which the trainee **matches the machine to the material**, not only to themselves. Feed rate depends on species (pine faster, oak slower), moisture (fresh slower), and board thickness (thin faster). Block 1 of week 3 closes with the triad "start-up – adjustment – cutting parameters" = full competence to operate a bandsaw at junior-under-supervision level.
