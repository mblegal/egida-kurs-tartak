## Content

### Why internal defects are invisible on the *dłużyca*

In Polish sawmilling practice a distinction is drawn between external defects (visible on the outside of the log, assessed at the raw-material intake before breakdown) and internal ones (revealed only during cutting, i.e. after the interior zone is exposed). External defects include knots, *oblina* (wane), crook, bark defects, end cracks up to 10 mm (transport-drying cracks). The operator and the grader see them at intake in the yard, reject bad logs, accept good ones. External defects are **mappable** before breakdown.

Internal defects are a different world. Wood as a living material carries within it the history of events from the growth period (several decades to several hundred years) plus the storage history after felling (a few days to several months, depending on the logistics operation of the forest and the sawmill). Some of those events leave traces **in the pith zone** or in the inner part of the cross-section, to which there is no visual access without cutting. The bark, the outer sapwood, even the end face of the log may be sound, and the pith may be cracked, *zaparzony* (heat-damaged), rotten, mouldy.

The operator at P3 at EGIDA learns during M3 T2 that **every log before cutting is a hypothesis, not a certainty**. The hypothesis is supported by the external assessment (the end face, the bark, the smell, the sap columns running out of the end face on a fresh log), but you only obtain full verification after breakdown. From this principle follows the discipline: the self-reliant operator always checks the first, third, and fifth board from every important log before he starts cutting in series. If an internal defect is revealed, he interrupts and escalates (operationally to the grader, formally to dispatch).

::: info
The Polish sawmilling tradition knows five kinds of wood for which internal defects are particularly common: **English oak** (pith shakes in old trees over 150 years), **European beech** (*zaparzenie*, heat damage, during longer storage in a warm summer), **Norway spruce** (frost cracks in trees growing on northern slopes above 800 m a.s.l.), **European aspen** (pith rot in trees with bark damage in youth), **common ash** (frost cracks plus *zaparzenie*, especially in ash from plantings after 2010 in Greater Poland). For each of these species the self-reliant operator learns a specific defect signature, does not treat them as "just wood".
:::

### Pith shake (*pęknięcie rdzeniowe*)

A pith shake is a separation of the wood tissue running **radially from the pith** (the centre of the trunk) towards the perimeter. You see it on the log end face as a line leaving the pith, but in hardwoods of the oak type this line often **does not reach the bark** and ends inside the cross-section. If it ends before the perimeter, it is not visible on the end face of the *dłużyca*, only inside the log.

**Formation mechanism:**

- **Ageing of the standing tree**: trees over 150 years old (typical for English oak in the forests of north-east Poland: Białowieża, Knyszyn, Augustów) accumulate internal stresses. With age the pith dries more slowly than the sapwood, the difference in elastic tension grows, and at some point the pith cracks along the grain. The tree keeps standing, keeps growing, but the pith remains cracked.
- **Drying stresses**: after felling, the log loses water in the outer zones faster than in the centre. The sapwood tries to shrink, the still-wet pith resists, and in the middle zone a radial tension develops. If the wood is old and was already under stress earlier, the crack forms, or widens, now.

**Appearance after cutting:**

A radial crack, 1 to 8 mm wide (typically 2 to 4 mm in oak, 1 to 2 mm in spruce), 50 to 800 mm long (typically 150 to 250 mm in medium-sized logs), running along the grain (not on a slant, not spiralling). On the cross-section (the end face of the board) you see a clear dark line from the pith. On the longitudinal section (the face of the board) the line runs parallel to the axis of the board, open or closed (it may be hidden between growth rings).

**Operational implications:**

A board with a pith shake more than 2 mm wide and more than 100 mm long **does not meet class A** (furniture, parquet). It meets class C (hidden structural elements: floor joists, roof structure inside partitions). A board with a crack up to 1 mm and up to 50 mm long may be accepted to class B (hidden joinery: concealed skirting, elements under cladding) provided the grader accepts it.

If the crack is only in one board (the middle board containing the pith), the remaining class A boards are **unchanged**. The middle board goes to class C, the rest stay in class A. This is the "reject one board, carry on" variant.

If the crack is in several boards (the pith has cracked along the whole length of the log), all the boards within 50 mm of the pith (so 2 to 3 boards out of 14) are affected. This does not disqualify the rest, but it **lowers the class A yield**. The batch then gives, say, 11 class A boards instead of 14 from a normal log.

::: warning
A pith shake that runs from the pith all the way to the perimeter and **reaches the bark** may cause the log to break apart during cutting. If you notice, on the end face of the *dłużyca* or after the first kerf, that the crack reaches the sapwood, **stop cutting and escalate** to the grader. Continuing a cut with a full-depth crack may lead to the log splitting violently open under its own weight, damage to the band (which enters the gap), and a board being thrown off the carriage onto the operator. Safety before yield.
:::

### Frost crack (*pęknięcie mrozowe*)

A frost crack looks similar to a pith shake (a radial line), but differs in **origin** and **course**: it arises in the standing tree in winter under severe frost, when the outer part of the trunk shrinks faster than the inner part (ice in the outer wood cells causes local shrinkage). As a result the **outer layer splits**, often with a clear crack audible in the forest on frosty nights. The tree goes on living, the crack remains, partly heals (the tree produces reaction wood trying to seal the fissure).

**Mechanism:**

At temperatures below -20°C, especially with a sudden temperature drop (a 15°C difference within 6 to 8 hours), the outer sapwood of a hardwood or softwood shrinks mechanically (ice in the cell walls takes up 9% more volume than water, but the water in the free cells shrinks on freezing; on balance the sapwood decreases). The pith, insulated by the outer layers, cools more slowly, retains more water, and does not shrink so strongly. The difference between external and internal shrinkage produces a **radial tensile stress** in the outer zone. The crack forms along the grain.

**Visual characteristics:**

Unlike a pith shake, a frost crack may start **not from the pith, but from halfway along the radius** and run towards the bark. Sometimes it is visible on the bark as a vertical fissure wrapping around the trunk at a height of 1 to 3 m. After breakdown, the frost crack shows up as a line that does not reach the pith: it runs from the sapwood zone inward and stops halfway. Typical width 2 to 5 mm, length 200 to 600 mm, colour darker than sound wood (the inside of the crack has been exposed for years to fungi and bacteria, the wood is slightly mouldy at the edge of the fissure).

**Risk species:** Norway spruce (the main risk, especially from mountain regions at 600 to 1200 m a.s.l.), silver fir (the Bieszczady, the Beskidy), common ash (Greater Poland, Kuyavia), less often oak (oak is resistant, but in extreme winters such as 1996 or 1963 it is also affected).

**Implications:**

A board with a frost crack is **usually disqualified from class A** (an open crack plus slight blackening at the edges equals a visible aesthetic and static risk). Class C (hidden elements) in most cases. Class B is possible if the crack is short (up to 100 mm) and positioned at the edge of the board (which can be edged so that the crack ends up in waste).

The **"recut differently" variant** is often effective here: changing the cutting pattern so that the crack runs along the edge of the board (not through its middle) allows the crack to be edged off into waste during the trimming phase. The grader helps choose the new pattern, the operator shifts the carriage settings by 15 to 25 mm.

### *Zaparzenie* (heat damage)

*Zaparzenie* is **thermal damage to wood** during storage of the log in conditions of high humidity and elevated temperature (typically above 25°C) for a period of over 3 to 4 weeks in the summer. It most often affects logs that, after felling, lay in a stack without adequate ventilation (too tight a stack, no sticks, on the sunny side of the yard), or were transported for a long time in full summer.

**Mechanism:**

In freshly felled wood a normal process of fermentation of sugars and starches takes place in the parenchymatic cells (the cells of the soft storage tissue). At low temperature (up to 15°C) the process is slow, the wood "breathes" healthily. At high temperature (above 25°C) the microbiology becomes aggressive, anaerobic bacteria multiply, they begin to break down cellular proteins. The products are volatile compounds (organic acids, thiols), which destroy the lignin structure and stain the wood dark brown, locally grey.

**Appearance:**

Heat-damaged wood has a **uniformly darkened** colour, in the pith zone (the worst ventilation) typically dark brown or blackish. The smell is **clearly unpleasant**: vinegar, mustiness, sometimes a smell close to rotten apples. This is the key signature for the operator: if, after the first kerf, the log gives off a sharp acid smell, you have a **strong suspicion of zaparzenie**. Rustam teaches Wahan: "first board, you hold your nose over the end face, you smell. Fresh wood smell equals OK. Vinegar equals escalation."

After drying, the heat-damaged wood is still visually dark (the colour does not return to normal), but structurally it may be entirely in order (if *zaparzenie* was brief, 1 to 2 weeks) or weakened (3 to 4 weeks, bending strength reduced by 15 to 30%).

**Risk species:** European beech (the main risk, beech in summer in a tight stack reaches *zaparzenie* in 3 weeks), silver birch, sycamore maple, black alder. Oak and spruce are more resistant (lower starch content, slower fermentation).

**Operational implications:**

Heat-damaged wood **has no furniture application** (the dark colour is impossible to hide under natural varnish, the customer sees stains). Class C as a hidden element: admissible, if the strength meets the standard after testing (the grader has the right to order a strength test in the EGIDA laboratory if strong *zaparzenie* is suspected). Class D (fuel, pallets, sawdust): always admissible.

The **"reject"** decision variant: if *zaparzenie* is severe (black stains, a repellent smell), the log goes as a whole to the fuel assortment. The **"recut differently"** variant: rarely effective, because *zaparzenie* affects the entire inner zone, it cannot be cut out into waste.

### Internal mould (*pleśń wewnętrzna*)

Internal mould is **fungal infection of wood tissue** in the pith or inner zone, when the log after felling has been exposed to prolonged damp conditions (a stack in the rain without cover, a low-lying yard with puddles, transport under a wet tarpaulin).

**Mechanism:**

Spores of mould fungi (most often of the genera *Aspergillus*, *Penicillium*, *Cladosporium*) are present in the air and on the bark of every tree. In sound, quickly dried wood they find no conditions for development. If the log after felling remains in conditions of humidity above 65% RH and a temperature of 10 to 25°C for more than 4 to 5 weeks, the spores germinate, the mycelium penetrates the sapwood zone through the pith rays, reaches the pith within 6 to 8 weeks. The wood tissue undergoes surface decay, discolorations appear (yellow, greenish, blue-grey, black).

**Appearance:**

Internal mould looks like **stains** of various colours depending on the species of fungus. Typically:

- **Blue-grey streaks** (*blue stain*) on pine and spruce, along the grain: this is the most common mould in Polish sawmills, does not endanger strength, affects only aesthetics.
- **Black dot-like stains**: *Aspergillus niger*, appears with longer storage of oak and beech in damp conditions.
- **Yellowish or greenish zones**: *Penicillium*, mainly on birch, alder.

Smell: slightly musty, mushroomy (not acid as in *zaparzenie*, not sweetish as in rot).

Wood structure: internal mould **does not destroy the fibre structure**, it only stains it. Mechanical strength is preserved (confirmed by ITB tests for blue stain in pine: the loss in bending strength is below 5%). The problem is **aesthetic and health-related** (the spores can be an allergen).

**Implications:**

Class A (furniture, visible): **excluded** if there are any stains at all. The customer will not accept blue streaks on parquet or yellowish marks on a desk. Class B (hidden joinery, skirting under cladding): admissible, provided the wood is dried below 12% (the mycelium dies in dry wood, does not develop further, but the colours remain). Class C (hidden structure): admissible without restriction.

The **"recut differently"** variant is often effective here: mould stains are localised (e.g. a 200 mm stretch of one board) and can be edged off into waste. The **"downgrade"** variant: lowering class A to B for the boards with stains, leaving class A for the boards without stains.

### Pith rot / heart rot (*zgnilizna rdzeniowa*)

Pith rot is **deep infection of wood tissue** caused by fungi that break down cellulose and lignin in the pith zone. Unlike mould (surface staining), rot **destroys the fibre structure**, the wood loses strength, becomes soft, brittle, or fibrous.

**Mechanism:**

Pith rot usually begins **in the standing tree**, not after felling. Spores of wood-decaying fungi (typically *Fomes fomentarius* for beech, *Phellinus robustus* for oak, *Heterobasidion annosum* for spruce and pine) penetrate the tree through bark damage (branch cuts by wildlife, abrasion by forestry machines, frost cracks) or through the roots. The mycelium develops in the pith zone (where there is most sugar and least tree defence), the tree tries to defend itself by producing a defensive zone, but the fungus wins. After a few years the pith zone is **completely broken down**, the tree stands hollow inside.

**Appearance after cutting:**

The rot zone is visible as **soft, discoloured** tissue, usually in the centre of the log (hence the name "pith rot"). Colour: dark brown, blackish, or bright yellowish (depending on the species of fungus). Structure: the wood **can be crumbled between the fingers** or even **scratched with a fingernail** (you cannot do this with sound wood). In extreme cases the pith is **hollowed out** (an empty space in the centre of the log, sometimes filled with mycelium).

Smell: sweetish, mushroomy, sometimes clearly "forest-like" (similar to rotting leaves). Different from the acid smell of *zaparzenie* and different from the musty smell of mould.

**Extent:**

Pith rot typically affects **the central 20 to 50%** of the cross-section (not the whole log). The outer zone (sapwood) is sound, because the tree while alive defended itself against the fungus in the zones of active metabolism. A log with pith rot thus offers **two different worlds**: a sound outer sapwood, a rotten inner pith.

**Implications:**

The rotten zone: **rejected without exception** (not even suitable for fuel, because decayed wood burns poorly, the smoke is unpleasant; it goes to compost or to a disposal site for wood waste). The sound sapwood zone: can be used for class B or C after edging.

The **"recut differently"** variant is **most effective** here as a strategy: the operator changes the cutting pattern so that the first 2 to 3 boards through the pith are rejected to fuel, and the next boards (from the clean sapwood zone) are cut normally. Class B is generally achievable from the outer boards, class A rarely (because the rings close to the pith are often stained by the fungus's influence, even if structurally sound).

::: tip
Graders with long experience (Ms. Ania with her twelve years) can recognise pith rot **before breakdown**, from external signals: the colour of the bark, the presence of bracket fungi (fruiting bodies of the fungus) on the trunk, the sound of the log when struck with a hammer (a sound log rings "clear", a rotten one "dull"). The operator learns this skill gradually, not yet fully in the first year of self-reliant work. That is why the grader's assistance on high-class logs is a **protective** practice for the operator, not an insult to his competence. Joint assessment builds the experience that in 5 to 7 years will let the operator diagnose defects on his own before breakdown.
:::

### Three operational decisions after recognising a defect

After recognising an internal defect the operator faces three decision variants. The choice depends on: (a) the type of defect, (b) its extent, (c) the target class of the order, (d) the availability of an alternative destination.

**Variant 1: recut differently (change the cutting pattern).**

The operator alters the carriage and band settings so that the defect ends up in waste or in a lower-class board. Examples:
- A pith shake confined to a single middle board: that board goes to class C, the remaining class A boards continue without changes.
- Blue mould in the outer layer: the edging is changed so that the marks end up in the edge waste.
- Pith rot: the first 2 to 3 boards through the pith are rejected, the outer boards are cut to class B.

The decision is taken by the operator and communicated to the grader at batch acceptance. The grader verifies and signs the KLA-002.

**Variant 2: downgrade the batch (lower the class of all the boards).**

The operator continues cutting without changing the pattern, but **takes the whole batch into a lower class** (e.g. C instead of A). Typical for widespread *zaparzenie* or a frost crack running through the whole length of the log: the defect is so pervasive that recutting differently is not worth it, and the class A customer will not be satisfied anyway.

The decision requires **the grader's signature** (here Ms. Ania's) plus **dispatch approval** (shift master Piotr or foreman Marek), because it changes the value of the batch. If the batch was intended for a premium customer from Poznań at 4,200 PLN/m³, and goes to a construction customer at 1,800 PLN/m³, the account needs to be corrected in the system.

**Variant 3: reject (transfer to the fuel or technical assortment).**

The log is excluded as a whole from the current order. It goes to: fuel (sawdust, slabs, chips), the technical assortment (pallets, crates), compost (if rotten). A decision for extreme situations: strong *zaparzenie* with a repellent smell, rot taking more than 50% of the cross-section, a frost crack running through the whole length of the log.

Requires **the grader's acceptance** plus **notification of the forestry supplier** (at EGIDA Marek makes a note to the supplier with the log number, a photograph of the defect, the delivery date; if the delivery came from the State Forests, the Knyszyn Forest District, the note goes by e-mail, the invoice can be corrected).

### Record of the decision: the KLA-002 card

The EGIDA form KLA-002 (class assessment card) is the standard document filled in for every batch in which an internal defect has been revealed after cutting. It contains:

- **Batch data:** batch number, cutting date, tree species, log dimensions (diameter, length), operator in charge, target order (number, customer, class).
- **Description of the defect:** type (one of the five: pith shake, frost crack, *zaparzenie*, internal mould, pith rot), location (which board, which zone of the cross-section), dimensions (length, width, depth in mm), photographic documentation (3 photos: board end face, board face, detail of the defect).
- **Operational decision:** variant (1: recut differently, 2: downgrade, 3: reject), justification (1 to 3 sentences), effect on yield (how many boards of class A, B, C, and waste).
- **Signatures:** operator (in charge of cutting), grader (verifying the decision), dispatch (accepting the deviation from the order, only for variants 2 and 3).
- **Retention:** the card goes into the batch folder plus into the claims archive (5 years under EGIDA's internal regulations). If the customer lodges a claim within 12 months of purchase, the KLA-002 card is the first document that Marek pulls out.

::: warning
Without a KLA-002 card an internal defect that shows up at the customer's after further drying and processing becomes the basis of a claim with no defence. The customer shows the crack, EGIDA looks in the files and finds no record. The claim is then well-founded, money is refunded or the batch is replaced. A KLA-002 card with your signature and the grader's signature means: "we saw the defect, we consciously chose variant X, the customer bought a batch consistent with our description." This is your legal and economic defence. Filling in such a card costs you 8 minutes of work. Not filling it in costs EGIDA, depending on the size of the batch, from 2,000 to 40,000 PLN of claims per year.
:::

### Second scene: blue stain in pine, 11:15

Ms. Ania has gone back to the office. Rustam is finishing the second batch of the day: Scots pine from a delivery from the Drawno Forest District, 300 mm diameter, 4.8 m long, destined for a class B decking board. He is breaking down the fourth log of the shipment. After taking off three boards, he notices in the fourth **blue-grey streaks** in the sapwood zone, stretched along the grain, 400 mm long, 80 mm wide (so almost the full width of the board).

This time Rustam **does not phone** Ms. Ania at once. First he diagnoses on his own. He asks himself in his head:

- **What is it?** Blue-grey streaks along the grain in pine equal *blue stain*, pine mould.
- **Extent:** across the full width of one board, over 400 mm. In the following boards (he will check in a moment), probably similar.
- **Target class:** B (decking board, light discolouration admissible).
- **Strength:** preserved (blue stain in pine, loss below 5% per ITB).
- **Operational decision:** probably variant 1 (recut differently: edge so that the largest streaks fall into waste). Alternatively variant 2 (downgrade the whole shipment from B to C, if the streaks are in every board on long stretches).

Rustam cuts boards 5, 6, 7 for verification. He confirms: blue streaks in most boards, but on shorter stretches (100 to 200 mm). Edging will allow the largest zones to be cut off into waste. Variant 1.

He phones Ms. Ania:

### Summary of lesson one

Five types of internal wood defects that are revealed only after cutting: pith shake (ageing plus drying stresses), frost crack (severe winter frosts in a standing tree), *zaparzenie* (thermal damage during storage), internal mould (mould fungi in damp conditions), pith rot (wood-decaying fungi in the standing tree).

Each defect has a characteristic appearance (line, stain, change of structure), smell (fresh, acid, musty, sweetish) and extent (local vs. pervasive, pith vs. sapwood, a single board vs. the whole log).

Three operational decisions: recut differently (change the pattern), downgrade the batch (lower the class), reject (exclude from the order). The choice depends on the type of defect, its extent, the target class.

Role of the grader (Ms. Ania at EGIDA): signs the decision, verifies the choice of variant, secures the batch against claims. Role of the operator: recognises, describes, proposes. The two roles are complementary, not competing.

The KLA-002 card as the record of the decision: batch data, description of the defect, decision variant, signatures, photographs. Retention 5 years. No card means no defence against a claim.

Two scenarios today: oak with a pith shake (variant 1, 2 boards to class C), pine with blue stain (variant 1, edging, class B). Rustam went through both: the first with the grader's assistance before the decision, the second with his own diagnosis and the grader's signature after the decision.

In the next lesson (m3-w2-l2) we move from internal defects to **visual classification to PN-EN 1611-1**: how Ms. Ania looks at knots, *oblina* (wane), crook, and why you assist her but do not classify on your own without a certificate.

## Key terms

- **Dłużyca** (cały, jeszcze nieprzetarty pień drzewa, zazwyczaj z korą, długości od 3 do 12 m, stanowi podstawową jednostkę surowca w tartaku)
  - en: **whole trunk / long log** (*dłużyca* in Polish sawmill terminology: the entire, not-yet-sawn tree trunk, usually with bark, from 3 to 12 m long, the basic raw-material unit in a sawmill)
  - pl: dłużyca (cały, jeszcze nieprzetarty pień drzewa, zazwyczaj z korą, długości od 3 do 12 m, stanowi podstawową jednostkę surowca w tartaku)
  - es: rollizo entero (*dłużyca* en la terminología polaca del aserradero: el tronco completo aún sin aserrar, normalmente con corteza, de 3 a 12 m de largo, la unidad básica de materia prima en el aserradero)
  - uk: цілий стовбур (*dłużyca* у польській лісопильній термінології: цілий, ще не розпиляний стовбур дерева, зазвичай з корою, довжиною від 3 до 12 м, основна одиниця сировини на лісопилці)
- **Pęknięcie rdzeniowe** (rozdzielenie tkanki drzewnej biegnące promieniście od rdzenia, powstałe ze starzenia drzewa lub naprężeń wysychania)
  - en: **pith shake** (a splitting of wood tissue running radially from the pith, arising from tree ageing or drying stresses)
  - pl: pęknięcie rdzeniowe (rozdzielenie tkanki drzewnej biegnące promieniście od rdzenia, powstałe ze starzenia drzewa lub naprężeń wysychania)
  - es: fenda de médula (separación del tejido leñoso que corre radialmente desde la médula, originada por envejecimiento del árbol o tensiones de secado)
  - uk: серцевинна тріщина (розділення деревної тканини, що йде радіально від серцевини, виникає від старіння дерева або напружень висушування)
- **Pęknięcie mrozowe** (pęknięcie powstałe zimą w drzewie stojącym z powodu różnicy skurczu między bielem a rdzeniem przy silnych mrozach)
  - en: **frost crack** (a split that arises in winter in a standing tree due to the shrinkage difference between sapwood and heartwood during severe frost)
  - pl: pęknięcie mrozowe (pęknięcie powstałe zimą w drzewie stojącym z powodu różnicy skurczu między bielem a rdzeniem przy silnych mrozach)
  - es: grieta por helada (fisura que surge en invierno en un árbol en pie por la diferencia de contracción entre la albura y el duramen durante heladas fuertes)
  - uk: морозобоїна (тріщина, що виникає взимку в дереві на пні через різницю скорочення між заболонню і серцевиною при сильних морозах)
- **Zaparzenie** (termiczne uszkodzenie drewna podczas przechowywania kłody w wilgotności i cieple, objawiające się ciemną barwą i kwaśnym zapachem)
  - en: **heat damage** (*zaparzenie* in Polish sawmill terminology: thermal damage to wood during storage of the log in humidity and warmth, showing as dark colour and acid smell)
  - pl: zaparzenie (termiczne uszkodzenie drewna podczas przechowywania kłody w wilgotności i cieple, objawiające się ciemną barwą i kwaśnym zapachem)
  - es: recalentamiento (*zaparzenie* en la terminología polaca del aserradero: daño térmico de la madera durante el almacenamiento del rollizo en humedad y calor, manifestado por color oscuro y olor ácido)
  - uk: запарювання (*zaparzenie* у польській лісопильній термінології: термічне пошкодження деревини під час зберігання колоди у вологості й теплі, виявляється темним кольором і кислим запахом)
- **Pleśń wewnętrzna** (grzybowe barwienie tkanki drzewnej w strefie bielu lub rdzenia, niszczy estetykę, zachowuje wytrzymałość w 95%)
  - en: **internal mould** (fungal staining of wood tissue in the sapwood or heartwood, damages aesthetics but preserves about 95% of strength)
  - pl: pleśń wewnętrzna (grzybowe barwienie tkanki drzewnej w strefie bielu lub rdzenia, niszczy estetykę, zachowuje wytrzymałość w 95%)
  - es: moho interno (tinción fúngica del tejido leñoso en la albura o el duramen, daña la estética pero conserva cerca del 95% de la resistencia)
  - uk: внутрішня цвіль (грибкове забарвлення деревної тканини в зоні заболоні або серцевини, псує естетику, зберігає близько 95% міцності)
- **Zgnilizna rdzeniowa** (głębokie porażenie tkanki drzewnej przez grzyby rozkładające celulozę i ligninę, niszczy strukturę włókien, drewno staje się miękkie i kruche)
  - en: **pith rot / heart rot** (deep wood-tissue attack by fungi that break down cellulose and lignin, destroys fibre structure, wood becomes soft and brittle)
  - pl: zgnilizna rdzeniowa (głębokie porażenie tkanki drzewnej przez grzyby rozkładające celulozę i ligninę, niszczy strukturę włókien, drewno staje się miękkie i kruche)
  - es: pudrición de médula (ataque profundo del tejido leñoso por hongos que descomponen la celulosa y la lignina, destruye la estructura de las fibras, la madera se vuelve blanda y frágil)
  - uk: серцевинна гниль (глибоке ураження деревної тканини грибами, що розкладають целюлозу та лігнін, руйнує структуру волокон, деревина стає м'якою і крихкою)
- **Blue stain** (niebieskoszare smugi pleśni sosnowej w strefie bielu, najczęstsza pleśń w tartaku polskim, strata wytrzymałości poniżej 5%)
  - en: **blue stain** (bluish-grey streaks of pine mould in the sapwood, the most common mould in Polish sawmills, strength loss below 5%)
  - pl: blue stain (niebieskoszare smugi pleśni sosnowej w strefie bielu, najczęstsza pleśń w tartaku polskim, strata wytrzymałości poniżej 5%)
  - es: azulado de la madera (estrías azul grisáceas de moho del pino en la albura, el moho más común en aserraderos polacos, pérdida de resistencia inferior al 5%)
  - uk: синє забарвлення (синювато-сірі смуги соснової цвілі в зоні заболоні, найпоширеніша цвіль у польських лісопилках, втрата міцності менше 5%)
- **Karta KLA-002** (karta oceny klasy EGIDA, dokument zapisujący wadę wewnętrzną, decyzję operacyjną, podpisy operatora i klasyfikatora, retencja 5 lat)
  - en: **KLA-002 card** (EGIDA class assessment card, document recording the internal defect, operational decision, signatures of the operator and grader, 5-year retention)
  - pl: karta KLA-002 (karta oceny klasy EGIDA, dokument zapisujący wadę wewnętrzną, decyzję operacyjną, podpisy operatora i klasyfikatora, retencja 5 lat)
  - es: ficha KLA-002 (ficha de evaluación de clase EGIDA, documento que registra el defecto interno, la decisión operativa, las firmas del operador y del clasificador, retención de 5 años)
  - uk: картка KLA-002 (картка оцінки класу EGIDA, документ, що фіксує внутрішню ваду, операційне рішення, підписи оператора і класифікатора, зберігання 5 років)
- **Trzy decyzje operacyjne** (dociąć inaczej, zdeklasować partię, odrzucić, trzy warianty decyzji operatora po rozpoznaniu wady wewnętrznej)

  - pl: trzy decyzje operacyjne (dociąć inaczej, zdeklasować partię, odrzucić, trzy warianty decyzji operatora po rozpoznaniu wady wewnętrznej)
  - es: tres decisiones operativas (recortar de otro modo, bajar de clase el lote, descartar, los tres variantes de decisión del operador tras reconocer un defecto interno)
  - uk: три операційні рішення (розрізати інакше, знизити клас партії, відбракувати, три варіанти рішення оператора після виявлення внутрішньої вади)
