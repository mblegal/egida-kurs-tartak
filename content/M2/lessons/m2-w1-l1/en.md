## Introduction

Four weeks ago you walked into the *tartak* (sawmill) as a **pomocnik** (helper). Today, on the door of the changing room, someone has added two words under the note "EGIDA kurs tartakowy": **Moduł 2** (Module 2). Those two words change more than it looks.

In M1 you watched, you listened, you passed material, you stepped aside. Your word "pomocnik" had a legal meaning: **you did not operate the machine**. Today you step into a role in which **you start the machine yourself**. Between the green button and your hand there is nobody anymore. That is the difference which, in Polish law, weighs more than in any course book.

This lesson explains what has changed, why every action you take now needs **authorization from the brygadzista** (foreman), and why for the next month you will be repeating one short ritual: *sprawdź – uruchom – zgłoś* (check – start – report). At the end you will see a map of your career path. Today you stand on the second rung. Above you there are still two more.

## Objectives

After this lesson:

1. You understand what changes in your duties when you move from the role of *pomocnik* (helper) to the role of *operator junior* (junior operator).
2. You know why every machine start-up requires authorization from the *brygadzista* (foreman) and which article of the *Kodeks pracy* (Polish Labour Code) it is based on.
3. You can say and perform the *sprawdź – uruchom – zgłoś* ritual in the order required by a Polish workplace.
4. You recognize five typical traps of the first month of M2 and you know which one threatens you personally the most.
5. You can describe the four career rungs in a sawmill and mark which one you are on today.

## Content

### What has changed – from helper to operator

A helper and an operator sometimes do the same things. The difference is not in the actions, but in the **decision**. As a *pomocnik* you were responsible for your own body. As an *operator junior* you are additionally responsible for **the safety of the material, the machine, and the colleague next to you**. One mistake of yours at start-up can cost a hand that is not yours. That is exactly why M2 does not begin with machine operation, but with the principle of **authorization**.

::: info
In M1 you heard the sentence "I help but I do not operate". In M2 it changes wording: "I operate, but not without consent". Two different positions, one shared thought: there is always somebody who knows more and sees more than you alone.
:::

### Foreman's authorization – the legal line

**Authorization** is a word that sounds official, but in a *tartak* it means a simple thing: **the brygadzista says "you may"** before you start the machine. This is not a courtesy. This is a **legal requirement**, renewed every day, based on two documents:

- **Kodeks pracy, art. 211 pkt 1** (Polish Labour Code, art. 211 point 1): the worker performs work in accordance with OHS regulations and rules, and follows instructions from superiors. The *brygadzista* is your direct superior at the workstation.
- **Rozporządzenie Ministra Gospodarki z 14.04.2000 r.** (Regulation of the Polish Ministry of Economy of 14 April 2000) on OHS at woodworking machines (§3, §5, §7): a woodworking machine is started by an authorized person, after checking the condition of the machine, in conditions that are safe for the surroundings.

::: warning
The most frequent mistake of migrant operators in the first week of M2: **starting the machine after a nod of the head from a distance**. The foreman at the other end of the hall does not see your checklist. A nod is not authorization. Authorization is **a spoken word from a distance of less than 3 metres, with eye contact**.
:::

### The *Sprawdź – Uruchom – Zgłoś* ritual

This ritual will come back to you a thousand times in M2. Three steps, three short words: *sprawdź* (check), *uruchom* (start), *zgłoś* (report).

**Sprawdź (check).** Five points of the checklist (details in l4 of this week): guards, the band or blade, dust extraction, idle run, clear surroundings. You confirm each point with a head gesture or with your voice. If one point does not pass, **you do not start**.

**Uruchom (start).** Only after the word *"ruszasz"* (you go / you run it) from the foreman. The first 10 seconds after the start you look at the machine, not at the material. You listen to the sound (is it steady), you look at the band or the disc (is it wobbling). If anything is wrong, **STOP before touching the material**.

**Zgłoś (report).** After 15 to 30 minutes of work you report the state to the foreman: "quality good", "band warm", "smoke at the cut", whatever it is. It is not a report in the paperwork sense. It is **a signal that you are alive, the machine is alive, the work is going**. Silence in the plant during the first month of M2 worries the foreman more than a shout.

The dialogue you will hear tomorrow (learn it by heart):

> – **Brygadzista:** "Sprawdź, Andrij." [Check, Andrij.]
>
> – **You:** [you run the checklist] "Sprawdzone, Marek." [Checked, Marek.]
>
> – **Brygadzista:** "Ruszasz." [You go / start it.]
>
> – **You:** [you press start, you watch 10 seconds, then the material]
>
> – **You:** [after 20 minutes] "Jakość dobra, taśma ciepła." [Quality good, band warm.]
>
> – **Brygadzista:** "Dobrze. Dalej." [Good. Keep going.]

::: example
Notice three things. First, the *brygadzista* calls you by **name**, not "hey". Second, every word is **short** (noise is 95 dB, you will not hear long sentences). Third, **the report is not a question**. You state a fact, the foreman acknowledges. That saves time and does not force the foreman to guess.
:::

### How it looks in practice – Andrij on his first day

Andrij is from Lviv in Ukraine. You met him in M1, when he was learning to pronounce the word *kłoda* (log). For the first two weeks he said "kwoda" and the *brygadzista* kept correcting him. After four weeks the "ł" came out of his muscle memory and Andrij no longer thinks about pronunciation.

Today Andrij is standing alone at the band saw for the first time. Next to him Marek, the *brygadzista*, is holding the workstation card with today's date and an empty box for a signature. Andrij looks at the five points of the checklist. Everything looks fine: guard closed, band tensioned, dust extraction humming, idle run smooth, no one around the machine.

For a fraction of a second Andrij thinks: "everything is visible, the foreman sees it, it is enough to press the button." His hand moves toward the green button. But it stops halfway. Andrij remembers what the instructor said: **a nod is not authorization**. He turns his head toward Marek.

"Sprawdzone, Marek." [Checked, Marek.]

Marek looks at Andrij, not at the machine. "Ruszasz, Andrij." [You go, Andrij.]

Andrij presses the green button. The band starts. For the first 10 seconds he watches only the band. The sound is steady. The band does not wobble. He takes the first board. After 20 minutes he reports: "Jakość dobra, taśma ciepła." Marek acknowledges: "Dobrze. Dalej."

What Andrij feels then is something he did not expect before noon. Tears. Not out of fear, but **out of relief**. For the four weeks of M1 he watched others start machines. Today he started his. Correctly. With the words of the foreman. Without a shortcut.

::: tip
The emotion Andrij felt after his first correct cycle has a name in work psychology: **role closure**. You stop being "the one who is learning" and you start being "the one who does". That moment will come to you in the first week of M2. Do not fear it. It is a sign that the body understood the role faster than the head.
:::

### Five typical traps of the first month of M2

From ten years of work by EGIDA instructors, five mistakes have emerged that most new junior operators make. Remember them. Each of them has cost somebody their health or their job.

1. **False confidence after M1.** "I have seen this a hundred times, I know how." Observation is not a skill. The hand learns differently than the eye.
2. **Skipping authorization.** "The foreman sees me, a nod is enough." It is not enough. A word, eye contact, less than 3 metres.
3. **A shortcut because I already know this machine.** "I know this saw, I do not need to check the guard." The saw does not know that you know it. Today someone may have removed the guard for cleaning.
4. **Failure to report a fault.** "I will fix it myself, nobody will notice." You breach *Kodeks pracy*, art. 211 pkt 6 (the duty to report to the superior). You risk losing your job without notice.
5. **Confusing "a helper may" with "an operator must".** As a *pomocnik* you could refuse to pass a *kłoda* (log) under *art. 210 § 1 KP* (Polish Labour Code, the right to refuse dangerous work). As an operator **you have the duty to stop the machine at every hazard**, not only the right. A duty is stronger than a right.

::: warning
Trap number 5 is the most serious change of legal status between M1 and M2. In M1 "you had the right" not to expose yourself. In M2 "you have the duty" to protect others. This is exactly the difference that is paid for at a higher rate.
:::

### The map of your career – where you are heading

Today you stand on the second of four rungs in a Polish sawmill:

**Rung 1. Pomocnik (helper)** (completed, 1 month of M1). 12 OHS rules, the 2-second rule, the rhythm of the day. Work under constant supervision.

**Rung 2. Operator junior (junior operator)** (you start today, 4 weeks of M2 + 6 to 12 months of practice). You start the band saw and the circular saw under supervision, you measure moisture, you sort *tarcica* (sawn timber), you assist with band changes. You work with authorization, but within it you already make your own decisions.

**Rung 3. Senior operator with UDT licence** (goal for M3, 12 to 24 months from today). You change the band on your own, you hold the forklift licence issued by UDT (the Polish forklift certification authority), you run the drying kiln.

**Rung 4. Mistrz zmiany (shift master)** (3 to 5 years of tenure). You lead a crew of 4 to 8 people, you receive raw material from the supplier, you talk to the customer about quality.

::: info
Promotion between rungs does not depend on "the boss's whim". It depends on **paperwork** (course certificate, UDT certificate, tenure) and on **confirmed skills** (entries in the workstation card, foremen's opinions). This means that every migrant with a full portfolio has the same path as a Polish worker. The difference is only in language, and within 6 to 12 months language stops being an obstacle. Actual wages differ between regions and employers; check current job postings before negotiating a contract.
:::

## Link to practice

Tomorrow morning, before you touch any machine, ask the *brygadzista* for **fifteen minutes of observation**. Stand 2 metres from the band saw, notebook in hand. Observe the full cycle: *sprawdź, uruchom, zgłoś*. Write down:

- Which words did the foreman say exactly (do not paraphrase)?
- At what time intervals did the operator's reports come?
- What would change in this sequence if you were the one standing at the button?

Bring the notebook to the afternoon lesson l2 ("Operator's PPE"). Group discussion will start from these observations.

## Trainer notes

- **120-min rhythm (suggested):** 15 min Introduction + Objectives; 20 min "What has changed" + "Authorization"; 20 min "The *sprawdź–uruchom–zgłoś* ritual" (with a paired role-play of the dialogue); 20 min Andrij narrative (reading with pauses, open questions); 15 min five traps (brainstorm on which one threatens whom); 10 min Key terms (shared pronunciation out loud); 20 min Career map (on the board, each trainee marks where they want to be).
- **Pedagogical trap no. 1:** the section "Andrij on his first day" contains an emotional moment (tears of relief). Do not skip it and do not comment ironically. For part of the group this is the first time they hear that **a worker's emotion is allowed in a Polish workplace**. Leave silence after that passage.
- **Pedagogical trap no. 2:** the self-assessment of the 12 rules may tempt the trainee to "fill it in by rote". Do it out loud, in a round, each trainee for one rule. If someone has "I need to revise" at more than three rules, plan an individual revision hour from M1 l7.
- **Career map:** draw four rungs on the board from bottom to top. Ask each trainee to **mark with a cross** the rung they want to reach in a year. Do not comment on the choices. The map is meant to show that different paths are available, not to impose one.
- **Time of return to this lesson:** refer back to the *sprawdź – uruchom – zgłoś* ritual in every lesson of this week (l2 PPE, l3 STOP > START, l4 checklist, l5 Rozp. MG, l6 dust, l7 jams, l8 first aid). This ritual is the axis of the whole M2, not only the introduction.
