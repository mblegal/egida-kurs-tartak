## Introduction

In l3 you learned **gestures** (the non-verbal language of the hall). In l4 you learned **words** (the verbal language of quiet windows). Now you learn the third kind of communication: **paper documents**, the part that is **written down** at your workstation and that you **sign** every day.

Paper documentation sounds like a boring topic. „What do I need it for? I help at the saw, I do not fill out forms". But documents in a sawmill have **concrete functions**:

1. **Legal protection** – when something happens, your signatures prove that you received training, that you knew how to act, that the foreman handed you the authorization. Without documents you are defenseless before the insurer, the Labour Inspectorate and the court.
2. **Shift record** – the attendance log shows who worked when. If someone gets cut at 9:30, the log shows who was a witness. If there is a dispute („Jacek told me it was fine"), the log shows whether Jacek was even on shift that day.
3. **Quality management** – the workstation card says what you may and may not do there. If every helper does it his own way, the quality of the wood leaving the plant becomes random. The card standardizes.
4. **Communication with the foreman** – the minor-incident log is your way of saying „something happened, I want to report it". Even if the foreman does not see it at that moment, the written entry stays and reaches the decision-maker.

In this lesson you will learn **4 documents** that are at your workstation **every day**: the **workstation card**, the **attendance log**, the **minor-incident log**, the **tool-use authorization**. You will learn **what they contain**, **who signs them**, **how often**, and **what to do if you are unsure**.

One thing up front: **never sign a document you do not understand**. Even if the foreman says „sign quickly, no time, it is standard". You have the right to read it, to ask for a translation, to ask questions. A signature is **your legal responsibility**, not just a formality.

## Objectives

After this lesson:

- You understand that paper documentation fulfils **4 functions**: legal protection, shift record, quality standardization, communication with the supervisor.
- You know **4 documents** at your workstation: the workstation card, the attendance log, the minor-incident log, the tool-use authorization. You know **what each contains**.
- You know **which you sign yourself** (the attendance log every day, the minor-incident log if you want to report something) and **which the foreman fills in** (the workstation card, the tool-use authorization).
- You understand that a signature on a document **is your legal responsibility**. You have the right to read and ask for an explanation before signing.
- You know the legal basis: **art. 94 and art. 211 of the Polish Labour Code** + **the Regulation of the Minister of Labour from 2003 on general OHS rules** + **the Regulation of the Minister of Economy from 2000 on OHS in wood processing**.
- You know how to **write an entry in the minor-incident log**: what, when, where, who was a witness, what was done.
- You understand that the **PIP inspection** (State Labour Inspectorate) may check these documents at any time. A plant without complete documentation gets a fine and often a work stoppage until it is fixed.

## Content

### Why a helper must be able to read the documents

A helper often says: „I only help, the foreman can fill in the papers". That is **wrong thinking**.

The documents at your workstation describe **your authorizations and your limits**. The workstation card says **what you are allowed to do alone** and **what you must never do**. The attendance log is **your proof** that you were on shift. The minor-incident log is **your communication tool** when you want to report something. The tool-use authorization is **your legal permission** to use that tool.

Not reading them = not knowing what you are allowed to do. Not signing = not having legal worker status. Signing without reading = taking on responsibility for something you did not read.

That is why on your first day of work you **must understand** the 4 documents. The foreman will show them to you, explain, answer questions. If you do not understand enough Polish, **ask for a translation** – the EGIDA foundation has reference translations of the document templates into EN, ES, UK in the office. Or ask a co-helper for an oral translation. Or use a phone with an online translator.

**Do not sign blind**. Ever.

::: warning
**Art. 94 pt 1 of the Labour Code**: „The employer is in particular obliged to (...) acquaint employees taking up work with the scope of their duties, the manner of performing work at the assigned positions and their basic rights". This means: **the workstation card must be shown to you** on day one and **explained in a language you understand**. If the foreman fails to do so, you have the right to demand it. If he refuses, report it to HR or to the State Labour Inspectorate (PIP, phone 801 002 006, anonymous).
:::

### Document 1: workstation card

The **workstation card** is a document describing **your concrete place** in the sawmill. Not „sawmill helper in general", but „helper at band saw number 2 in hall A". Every workstation has its own card.

**What the workstation card contains**:

1. **Position name and location** („helper of the operator at band saw PT-02, hall A, zone 3").
2. **Employee first and last name** (yours).
3. **Foreman first and last name** (the one responsible for you).
4. **Permitted activities** – what you may do alone:
   - feeding logs to the operator (from the safe side, behind the zone line)
   - receiving rejected timber
   - placing stickers on the stack
   - cleaning sawdust with the extractor switched on (when the machine is off)
   - carrying waste to the container
5. **Forbidden activities** – what you must never do:
   - enter the machine working zone during operation
   - adjust machines (saws, sharpeners, planers)
   - change band or circular blades
   - operate a forklift (if you have no UDT license)
   - work the shift alone without the operator present
6. **Required PPE** (from T1 l5):
   - safety helmet
   - steel-toe boots, class S3
   - safety goggles
   - anti-slip gloves (NOT cut-resistant in rotating-machine zones)
   - hearing protection (foam plugs + ear muffs above 95 dB)
   - dust mask FFP2 (pine dust) / FFP3 (oak, beech)
   - work jacket and trousers (non-restrictive)
7. **Workstation hazards** (from T1):
   - entanglement by the saw band (fatal)
   - kickback of the cut material (body impact)
   - wood dust (IARC 1 for oak / beech)
   - noise above 95 dB (long-term hearing loss)
   - tripping over spilled sawdust
   - being struck by falling material from the stack
8. **Completed training** – date and trainer signature:
   - general OHS induction
   - workstation-specific induction
   - periodic training (every 3 years or more often)
   - PPE briefing
9. **Signatures**:
   - your signature (helper): „I read it, I understand, I accept"
   - foreman signature: „I transferred the information, I am responsible for the induction"
   - OHS specialist signature (if the company has one on staff): „compliant with the rules"
10. **Validity date** – usually **3 years** or when the position changes. After 3 years the card is updated (OHS review, hazard update, new PPE if any).

The card hangs in a visible place near the station (e.g. on the tool cabinet or a pillar 2 meters from the machine). **You should have it in your line of sight** 100% of the work time.

::: info
**Why the workstation card is so detailed**. The Regulation of the Minister of Economy of 14 April 2000 on OHS in wood processing (Journal of Laws 2000 no. 36 item 409) requires the employer to give **each employee an assigned card with the scope of activities**. In sawmills there have historically been accidents where „the helper wanted to help" and stepped into the machine working zone. The foreman then said „I did not order it, he did it on his own". The workstation card is the proof: **if it is not written in the card that you may, then you may not**. It protects you (from responsibility shifting) and the employer (from allegations of missing training).
:::

### Document 2: attendance log

The **attendance log** is the simplest document. A list of worker names on the shift, with columns „in" and „out". You write the **time by hand** and **sign**.

Or (in plants with a newer system) instead of a sheet there is a **time-registration reader** (RCP in Polish): you tap a card / chip, the system writes the time automatically.

**When you enter**:
- **Arrival**: directly after changing into work clothes, before entering the hall. Not earlier, not later. If you arrive at 6:40 and write 6:30, that is **document forgery**, Penal Code art. 271 (criminal liability).
- **Departure**: directly after finishing the shift, before changing out. If you leave earlier (e.g. for a doctor's appointment), write the real time plus a reason in the „remarks" column.

**What the log must contain**:
- first and last name
- employee number / ID
- date
- arrival time (HH:MM)
- departure time (HH:MM)
- handwritten signature (always, even with RCP often also required)
- remarks (late arrival, early departure, absence)

**Why this matters**. The log is the basis for:
- wage payment (hours worked × rate)
- overtime calculation (Labour Code art. 151)
- leave entitlement
- accident insurance (if you are not in the log that day and you have an accident, the insurer will say „he was not at work")
- PIP inspections (missing logs = a fine for the employer)

::: warning
**Never ask a colleague to „sign you in" on the log**. And do not sign in for someone else. That is **document forgery** and **fraudulent wage claim**. In sawmills every year there are disciplinary dismissals for such practices. One case from Greater Poland in 2023: a foreman logged „present" colleagues who had not come in. The court gave him 10 months suspended + a PLN 8,000 fine. For you as a migrant the consequence would be worse: loss of work permit, deportation, 5-year entry ban.
:::

### Document 3: minor-incident log

The **minor-incident log** (sometimes called the **dangerous-event log** or the **OHS event diary**) is a form in which you **write down everything that happened** but was not a „full work accident" as defined by the Regulation of the Council of Ministers of 1 July 2009.

**What counts as a minor incident**:
- a cut that needs a plaster (not stitches)
- a small machine breakdown fixed on the spot
- spilled sawdust / chips / oil
- a damaged timber stack (in danger of falling, not yet fallen)
- a missing machine guard noticed before start-up
- unreliable or damaged PPE (cracked mask, helmet, gloves)
- a colleague's unusual behaviour (drunk? sick? tired?)
- „something smelled odd" (a leak, burnt smell)
- „a near-miss" (a situation where only chance kept anything from happening)

**Why we log minor incidents**. Because **minor incidents are the warning signs of serious accidents**. The Heinrich statistic (classic in OHS): for every serious accident there are about **30 minor accidents**, about **300 near-misses**, and about **3000 unsafe behaviours**. If you ignore near-misses, you will see a serious accident within a month. If you log them and analyse them, you can prevent it.

**Who writes**:
- **You (helper)** – **if the incident concerns you** (you got cut, you noticed a breakdown, you saw a near-miss). The entry must be **legible, concise, factual**.
- **Foreman** – when the incident concerns everyone or needs a technical evaluation.
- **OHS specialist** – when the incident is serious and requires an investigation.

**How to word an entry (the „who, what, when, where, why, what was done" formula)**:

```
Date: 14.04.2026
Time: 10:45
Reporter: Jan Kowalski (helper at PT-02)
What happened: While feeding an oak log to the saw, the log slipped from the grip and struck my left leg. A fragment flew to the side and narrowly missed the operator (Marek Nowak).
Where: Workstation PT-02, hall A, zone 3.
Why (my hypothesis): The log was wet, slippery. The operator's grip did not hold well.
Action taken: The operator stopped work, we changed the grip to a stronger one. The foreman was informed (Paweł Kamiński, on shift).
Witnesses: Marek Nowak (operator), Paweł Kamiński (foreman).
Signature: Jan Kowalski [signature]
```

**Key wording rules**:
- **Facts, not opinions**. Not „the saw is bad", but „the log slipped from the grip".
- **People by name**. Not „a colleague", but „Marek Nowak, operator of PT-02".
- **Precise time**. „Around 10:45" is OK, „in the morning" is not.
- **Precise location**. Not „in the hall", but „workstation PT-02, zone 3".
- **What was done**. The foreman needs to know the incident is closed. If nothing was done, write that too („we stopped work, waiting for the mechanic").

::: example
**From life**: a helper from Moldova, sawmill in the Lublin region, first week on the job. On day two he noticed that the guard over the drive belt of the planer was cracked (visible lengthwise crack). The foreman was not on site (he was running another hall). The helper could have: **(a)** pretended not to see, **(b)** told the operator verbally: the operator would have said „OK, I will pass it on", and forgotten, **(c)** written it in the minor-incident log. He chose (c). Entry: „Date 02.03, 8:15, guard over the belt on S-04 cracked lengthwise, planer must not be used until the guard is replaced. Noticed by: Ion Popa, helper. Witness: Adam Kowal, operator". The foreman saw the entry at 9:00, ordered a replacement guard, stopped the planer for 2 days. A week later it turned out the crack was a major one and within a week the guard could have snapped and exposed the belt. The foreman praised Ion in the end-of-shift report. **Lesson**: an entry in the minor-incident log is not „snitching", it is a **professional procedure** that protects everyone.
:::

### Document 4: tool-use authorization

The **tool-use authorization** is a form issued by the foreman when he gives you **the right to use a specific tool after training**.

**Key distinction**:
- **Hand tools** (manual cart, wheelbarrow, hammer, axe, hand saw, file, cordless drill) = **requires the foreman's authorization after training**. Does not require a UDT license or formal exams.
- **Machines** (saw, planer, moulder, sharpener) = **requires a UDT or sector license**, not the foreman's signature. A helper does NOT operate machines (from T3 l1 „I help but I do not operate").
- **Forklift** = **requires a UDT class II WJO license** (Operator Forklift Trucks). A helper without a license does NOT drive a forklift.

**What the authorization form contains**:
- employee first and last name
- tool (name, type, inventory number)
- training date (usually 1 day: a few hours of theory + practical exercises)
- trainer (foreman / long-tenured operator / OHS specialist)
- scope of use („horizontal transport of timber on the yard only", „only loads up to 200 kg")
- validity date (usually **1 year**; retraining after a year)
- employee signature: „trained, confirmed"
- trainer signature: „I trained him, I am responsible"

**What the training for the authorization must contain**:
- safe tool use
- maintenance (e.g. greasing the bearings of the manual cart, sharpening the file, replacing the drill battery)
- typical mistakes and their consequences
- what to do if the tool breaks during work (STOP, set it down, report)

**Why this matters**. If you use a tool **without authorization** and get injured, the insurer will say: „the employee used a tool without a license, the accident is not covered". An accident = your treatment, your lost income, your rehabilitation costs. That is why **the authorization is your legal shield**.

::: tip
**If the day ever comes when the foreman tells you to take a tool you have no authorization for**, **refuse politely**. „Foreman, I do not have authorization for the drill, I would rather wait for training so there is no insurance problem later". The foreman will understand. If he insists („take it, just for a moment"), you write in the minor-incident log: „Date X, time Y, foreman Z ordered me to use a tool without authorization. I refused. I am reporting it". This is **art. 210 § 1 of the Labour Code** – the right to refuse dangerous work. You cannot be punished for it (art. 210 § 5).
:::

### Legal basis: summary

| Document | Legal basis |
|---|---|
| Workstation card | Art. 94 pt 1, art. 237³ Labour Code + MLSP Regulation of 27.07.2004 on OHS training |
| Attendance log | Art. 149 Labour Code (working-time records) + Minister of Labour Regulation of 10.12.2018 on employee documentation |
| Minor-incident log | Council of Ministers Regulation of 01.07.2009 on determining the circumstances of work accidents (§ 2: the employer records „dangerous events") |
| Tool-use authorization | Art. 211 pt 3 Labour Code + Minister of Economy Regulation of 14.04.2000 on OHS in wood processing |

These 4 documents together form the **workstation documentation package** required by Polish labour law. Missing any of them = **non-compliance** = a fine for the employer (PLN 1,000 to 30,000 per missing item) and a **work stoppage** by the PIP inspector until the paperwork is complete.

### What to do when you do not understand a document

Polish OHS documents are written in **formal legal language**, difficult even for Poles. Migrants have an extra problem: the language barrier.

**The „3 questions" strategy**:
1. **What does this mean?** – ask the foreman to explain in simple words. Even in Polish, but slowly and with examples.
2. **What am I signing?** – ask him to point to your signature field and tell you what that signature confirms.
3. **What if I do not sign?** – you have the right to ask. A good employer answers: „take your time, sign tomorrow". A bad one: „sign or you are fired" (that is a breach of law, you report it to PIP at once).

**In addition**: the EGIDA foundation has in the office **reference translations of the 4 documents** into EN, ES, UK. You can take copies, read them in your own language, and then go back to the Polish original with better understanding.

::: info
**PIP inspection**. The State Labour Inspectorate (PIP) can enter the sawmill **at any time** without warning and check the documentation. If an inspector finds missing workstation cards, unkept logs, expired authorizations, the employer gets a fine (PLN 1,000 to 30,000) or the case goes to court (where fines reach PLN 100,000). As a helper you have the right to request a conversation with the inspector without the foreman present: the inspector must keep your anonymity. PIP phone: **801 002 006** (free, anonymous).
:::

### Common helper mistakes

**Mistake 1 – signing without reading**. „The foreman says it is standard, so I will sign". **Consequence**: you take responsibility for something you did not read. Example: you signed a forklift authorization not knowing the forklift requires a UDT license. In case of an accident, you are liable for driving without a license. **Correction**: **always read**, even for 2 minutes, before signing.

**Mistake 2 – „I will log it in a minute" for attendance**. Came at 6:45, forgot to log, started work. At 10:00 he remembered and wrote „6:40". **Consequence**: if anything happens in between, the missing signature between 6:40 and 10:00 is grounds for a dispute with the insurer. **Correction**: **log immediately after arrival**, not later.

**Mistake 3 – not reporting a minor incident**. You cut your little finger, stuck on a plaster, told no one. **Consequence**: if the wound gets infected and you go on sick leave, the insurer asks „when and where did it happen?". No entry = no work accident = treatment cost on you. **Correction**: **log minor incidents even if they feel trivial**. 2 minutes of paperwork protect you from thousands of zlotys in costs.

**Mistake 4 – forging a colleague's signature**. „A colleague asked me to sign him in because he was late". **Consequence**: document forgery = Penal Code art. 270 = a fine or up to 3 years' prison. For a migrant: loss of work permit. **Correction**: **never sign for another person**. If a colleague was late, he writes the real time himself with a remark: „10 min late, reason: traffic".

## Summary

Today you learned that paper documentation fulfils **4 functions**: legal protection, shift record, quality standardization, communication with the supervisor. It is not „pointless bureaucracy", it is a **tool that protects your rights and your safety**.

**4 documents** at your workstation:

1. **Workstation card** – filled in by the **foreman**, you **sign after the induction**. Contains: permitted and forbidden activities, PPE, hazards, training, signatures. Usually valid 3 years.
2. **Attendance log** – **you enter** the arrival and departure time **every day** and sign. Or electronic RCP. Basis for wages and insurance.
3. **Minor-incident log** – written by **whoever noticed** (you or the foreman). Cuts, breakdowns, near-misses, non-compliance. Formula: who, what, when, where, why, what was done.
4. **Tool-use authorization** – issued by the **foreman** after training. Hand tools YES (after authorization), machines NO (require UDT). Usually valid 1 year.

**Legal basis**: Labour Code art. 94, 149, 211, 237³ + Ministry of Labour Regulation 2003 + Ministry of Economy Regulation 2000 + Council of Ministers Regulation 2009.

**The „never sign blind" rule**. You have the right to read, ask for explanation, ask for translation. If the foreman insists: call PIP at 801 002 006.

**4 common mistakes**: signing without reading, late attendance entry, not reporting a minor incident, forging a colleague's signature. Each one costs you either money or your right to work in Poland.

In the next lesson (l6) we move to the **extended incident reporting**: **how to report** events, to whom, in what order, what deadlines apply, what happens after the report. We will close the triangle: **records** (l5) → **reporting** (l6). Then a recap (l7) and a test (l8).

- 📋 **karta stanowiska** (pl) – workstation card / job card (en) – ficha del puesto (es) – картка робочого місця (uk). Usually valid 3 years.

- 🕰️ **rejestr obecności** (pl) – attendance log / time register (en) – registro de asistencia (es) – журнал присутності (uk). Document with arrival and departure times. You fill it in yourself every day. Basis for wages and insurance. Labour Code art. 149.

- 📓 **rejestr drobnych zdarzeń** (pl) – minor-incident log (en) – registro de incidentes menores (es) – журнал дрібних подій (uk). Document recording cuts, breakdowns, near-misses, non-compliance. Formula: who, what, when, where, why, what was done. Council of Ministers Regulation of 01.07.2009.

- 📝 **zgoda na użycie narzędzia** (pl) – tool-use authorization (en) – autorización de uso de herramienta (es) – дозвіл на використання інструменту (uk). Form issued by the foreman after training. Hand tools YES, machines NO (require UDT). Usually valid 1 year.

- ⚖️ **art. 94 KP** (pl) – Labour Code art. 94 (en) – Código de Trabajo art. 94 (es) – Трудовий кодекс ст. 94 (uk). Employer's duties, including acquainting employees with the scope of their duties and the workstation. Legal basis for the workstation card.

- 🚫 **art. 210 KP** (pl) – Labour Code art. 210 (en) – Código de Trabajo art. 210 (es) – Трудовий кодекс ст. 210 (uk). Right to refuse work that endangers health / life. Cannot be grounds for sanctions (art. 210 § 5).

- 🏛️ **PIP** (pl) – State Labour Inspectorate (en) – Inspección Estatal de Trabajo (es) – Державна інспекція праці (uk). Państwowa Inspekcja Pracy. Inspections at any time, without warning. Anonymous phone: 801 002 006.

- ⚠️ **near-miss** (pl) – near-miss / prawie wypadek (en) – casi accidente (es) – ледве уникнута подія (uk). A situation where only chance kept anything from happening. Written in the minor-incident log. Heinrich statistic: 1 serious accident ~ 300 near-misses.

- 🎓 **UDT** (pl) – Technical Inspection Office (en) – Oficina de Inspección Técnica (es) – Управління технічного нагляду (uk). Urząd Dozoru Technicznego. Issues licenses to operate machines (forklifts, cranes, overhead cranes). A helper usually does NOT have a UDT license.

- ✍️ **fałszowanie dokumentów** (pl) – document forgery (en) – falsificación de documentos (es) – підробка документів (uk). Penal Code art. 270: a fine or up to 3 years' prison. For a migrant: loss of work permit, deportation, entry ban. Includes signing in for a colleague on the attendance log.

## Notatki dla trenera

**Pedagogical goal**: build in the trainee the **habit of reading and signing consciously** the 4 workstation documents. Break the attitude „paperwork is boring" and show that documents are a **tool that protects rights**. Teach how to write an entry in the minor-incident log using the „who, what, when, where, why, what was done" formula.

**120-minute rhythm**:

- **0–10 min**: opening. Question: „what would you do if the foreman said 'sign quickly, standard, no time'?". Collect answers. Introduce: we always read, even for 2 minutes.
- **10–40 min**: **workstation card**. Trainer shows a sample card (real from a plant or the EGIDA template). Walks through the 10 elements. Pair exercise: one „foreman", one „helper". Foreman explains the card, helper asks 3 questions and signs. Rotate. 20 minutes exercise.
- **40–55 min**: **attendance log**. Trainer shows a sample sheet and an electronic RCP (if available). Exercise: trainees fill in a simulated log on a blank form (fictional times). Discussion of mistakes: late entry, forgetting.
- **55–85 min**: **minor-incident log**. Trainer shows a sample entry (from the lesson: the Moldovan and the cracked guard). Exercise: trainees get 3 scenarios (cut, breakdown, near-miss) and write entries on blank forms using the „who, what, when, where, why, what was done" formula. Trainer reads 3 entries out loud; the group evaluates: are they factual? do they have witnesses? do they state what was done? Corrections.
- **85–100 min**: **tool-use authorization**. Trainer shows the form, walks through the distinction hand tools vs machines (referring back to T3 l1 „I help but I do not operate"). Exercise: trainees get a list of 10 tools (hammer, manual cart, saw, forklift, cordless drill, sharpener, wheelbarrow, planer, file, moulder) and classify them: „needs foreman authorization", „needs UDT license", „forbidden for a helper".
- **100–115 min**: **common mistakes and consequences**. Discuss the 4 mistakes from the lesson: signing without reading, late attendance entry, not reporting a minor incident, forging a signature. For each one the trainer shows a real case (media, PIP reports) and the consequences. Goal: emotional anchoring.
- **115–120 min**: key terms. Announce l6 (extended reporting).

**Common mistake (1)**: the trainee **does not understand the difference between „I fill it in myself" and „the foreman fills it in"**. He assumes all documents belong to „the foreman" and he just signs. **Correction**: trainer draws a board „who fills in, who signs" with the 4 documents in rows and 2 columns. Trainees fill it in together. Trainer comments afterwards.

**Common mistake (2)**: the trainee **is afraid to report minor incidents** („nobody did it before me, I won't either"). Result: the near-miss repeats and becomes a serious accident. **Correction**: trainer shows the Heinrich statistic (1 serious = 30 minor = 300 near-misses = 3000 unsafe behaviours). Shows that reporting a near-miss is **professional, not cowardly**. The Moldovan example shows how one entry saved the planer from a disaster.

**Practical recommendation**: hand out to trainees a **set of 4 reference document templates** (blank, ready to fill) in Polish + translation in the trainee's language (EN / ES / UK). A4 format, stapled. The trainee keeps it as a **handbook** for the first month of work. When the foreman shows him the original, he compares to the template and knows where to sign.

**Link to the next lesson**: l6 „Reporting Incidents, Faults, and Hazards". Today you learned the **documents** (logs). In l6 you will learn the **reporting procedure**: to whom, in what order, within what deadline, what happens after the report. This closes the written communication triad: **the form** (l5) → **the procedure** (l6) → **case closure**.
