# M3 Theoretical exercises: 8 integrative mini-cases for group discussion

## Purpose

A set of **8 extended mini-cases** for use **between lesson l7 (M3 recap) and lesson l8 (final test)** in week 4. Each exercise **activates a minimum of 3 out of the 8 operator principles for M3** (from l7 T4) and lets the trainee practice integrating knowledge in open group discussion before encountering the same principles in the closed-form questions of the test.

Two modes of use:

1. **Trainer-led group discussion mode** (preferred). The trainer reads the context aloud to the group, gives 3 minutes for individual reflection, then opens discussion (12-15 min per exercise), then provides the analytical hint (3-5 min). 8 x 20 min is roughly 2.5 hours. Usually delivered in 2 sessions of 4 exercises each.
2. **Self-study preparation mode**. The trainee writes answers in a notebook for 10-15 min per exercise, then reviews jointly with the trainer. Slower, but well suited to A2-B1 Polish speakers who prefer their own pace.

**The exercises are NOT graded**. They do not affect M3 completion. They are a **diagnostic** tool (trainee: "what do I understand?"; trainer: "who has gaps in which principles?").

## Distribution of M3 principles across the exercises

| Ex. | Topic | Activated M3 principles (from l7) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | M2 supervision plus a fault at the same time | Z6 supervision + Z7 diagnosis + Z1 boundary |
| 2 | Customer complaint on the day an order is being finished | Z5 complaints + Z4 economics + Z3 documentation |
| 3 | FSC audit plus team conflict | Z3 documentation + Z6 supervision + Z1 boundary |
| 4 | Solo shift with 3 matters at once | Z2 KDP + Z6 supervision + Z7 diagnosis + Z4 economics |
| 5 | Promotion offer: kiln master vs M3 | Z8 resources + Z4 economics + Z1 boundary |
| 6 | External vs internal boundary escalation | Z1 boundary + Z7 diagnosis + Z3 documentation |
| 7 | Adrian's KAO-001 score on the authorization edge (18/25) | Z6 supervision + Z3 documentation + Z5 KC (interpretation of section 8) |
| 8 | Career plan: UDT+SEP vs BTM vs UPP | Z8 resources + Z4 economics |

## Exercise 1: "Adrian has waves and you have a wheel runout"

### Context

Tuesday 18 June 2026, 9:45. You are at P3 LT70 (oak C30, 5 m3 for Stolarz Meblowy Szczytno, ZLE-2026-06-021). Your trainee that day: **Adrian Kowal** on the neighboring P2 LT30 (pine C24, 2.5 m3 for Dom-Bud Mragowo, ZLE-2026-06-022); this is Adrian's twelfth shift under your supervision, KAO-001 score from last week 22/25 (full 30-day authorization).

At 9:45, **two things happen at once**:
1. On **your** LT70 head, after 40 seconds of cutting you feel an 8-10 Hz vibration in the handle and hear a rhythmic knock every 0.5 s. The kerf surface is slightly wavy. This is lower-wheel runout (EGIDA fault table item 4).
2. **Adrian** shouts from 8 m away: "Rustam, I have waves on the third board, the ammeter is climbing." You can see his sawmill: the kerf surface is clearly wavy.

Marek (foreman) is in a meeting with Anna Kwiatkowska until 10:30, phone on silent.

### Discussion questions

1. Which fault has priority and why?
2. Whose competence is each of the two cases (operator / maintenance / external service)?
3. What sequence of actions do you take in the next 10 minutes?
4. Which forms do you fill out?

### Analytical hint (for the trainer)

**3 M3 principles** activate: **Z1 boundary** (your LT70 = hard external boundary, Wood-Mizer service; Adrian's LT30 = most likely operator-level, blade change), **Z6 supervision** (Adrian under authorization, you assist), **Z7 diagnosis** (both cases, sensory assessment, you feel it in the handle, Adrian sees and hears it).

**Priority: Adrian**, not your LT70. Your LT70 already requires a STOP (wheel runout greater than 0.5 mm does not allow continued cutting without risk to the machine and its warranty), so a STOP is natural. Adrian is waiting for a decision while cutting, an active situation.

**10-minute sequence**: (1) STOP LT70 first: stop the head and hit the emergency switch, log into KKS-001 "9:45, wheel runout ~10 Hz, STOP, awaiting Marek + service"; (2) walk over to Adrian, 8 m away, visually assess the waves; if they are minor and appeared on the third board (not earlier), most likely a dull blade or a drop in tension; (3) instruct Adrian under your observation: STOP, check the ammeter (yellow/red?), check tension, swap the blade if needed (operator-level, Adrian has independent M2 competence for blade changes); (4) log in his KS-001 "9:50, waves on board 3, swapped blade 10 instead of blade 7, Rustam supervisory approval"; (5) return to the LT70 and call Marek using the 4W format plus an SMS ("Marek, Rustam, P3, 9:45, wheel runout, STOP, Wood-Mizer service notified, awaiting authorization").

**Forms**: your KKS-001 (fault + time + three-sense description) + Adrian's KS-001 (supervisory note with your initials) + optionally your own KS-001 (downtime due to fault).

**Common junior M3 mistake**: handles his own fault first, ignoring Adrian ("I have a more important problem"). Consequence: Adrian keeps cutting with waves, ruins more boards, supervisory liability falls on M3 (you), margin drops. Principle: **in supervision, the trainee's active situation comes first**; your own fault has by definition turned into downtime (STOP = stable situation).

## Exercise 2: "Dom-Bud calls on the day a new order is finishing"

### Context

Friday 19 June 2026, 14:15. You are finishing the last cuts on P3 LT70 for order ZLE-2026-06-023 (oak FSC C30, 8 m3 for Natalia at Stolarz Meblowy Szczytno, deadline Saturday 10:00). The shift report EGIDA-RZ-001 looks great: margin +2180 zł, yield 58%, 0 complaints from classifier Ms. Ania.

**Jacek Wesolowski from Dom-Bud Mragowo calls** (the customer of the previous order ZLE-2026-06-015, pine C24, batch released on 12 June 2026, a week ago): "Rustam, we have a problem. The boards from your batch have a tolerance of 174 mm instead of 175. I have to rescind the contract and get back 3200 zł." The call lasts 4 minutes. The customer is emotional but not aggressive. Rustam, the foreman Marek is on leave until Monday (he already left at 14:00 Friday).

The batch is 5 m3 x 640 zł/m3 of pine C24 = 3200 zł in value. On the shift report for ZLE-2026-06-015: margin minus 529 zł (a known problem batch from small logs).

### Questions

1. What do you say to Wesolowski in the first 60 seconds of the call?
2. Which standard plus which Civil Code article are key, and why?
3. Which documents do you check within 30 minutes after the call?
4. What email do you send, when, and to whom (with whom in CC)?
5. How does this case relate to the minus 529 zł margin (if at all)?

### Analytical hint

**3 M3 principles** activate: **Z5 complaints** (Polish Civil Code (KC) art. 556-563 + Polish Supreme Court ruling + email template), **Z4 economics** (the negative margin is independent of the complaint), **Z3 documentation** (KEP-001 of the batch + dispatch order + customer paperwork).

**60 seconds of the call**: a calm tone + the 4W scheme + a written-follow-up offer. "Mr. Jacek, thank you for calling. I understand the matter: batch ZLE-015, pine, tolerance 174 mm vs 175, demand to rescind for 3200 zł. I will check the batch documentation and PN-EN 336 and call you back by 16:00 with a detailed written response. Before I call back, one specific question: do you still have the boards physically, or have they already been used? (This affects KC art. 563, inspection within a customary timeframe.)" You do NOT agree to a refund on the spot. You do NOT deny outright. You buy yourself time for documents and consultation.

**Standards and KC**: **PN-EN 336** "Structural timber - sizes and permitted deviations" = standard tolerance plus or minus 2 mm; plus or minus 1 mm only for special requirements (the "tolerance" field in the dispatch order would have to read plus or minus 1 mm explicitly). **KC art. 556(1) section 4**: a material defect = lack of fitness for ordinary use. **KC art. 560 section 4**: "the buyer may not rescind the contract if the defect is immaterial." The **Polish Supreme Court ruling I NSNc 152/20 of 17 Feb 2021** confirms this.

**Documents in 30 min**: (1) KEP-001 for batch ZLE-015 (the tolerance field, default plus or minus 2 mm if no special value entered); (2) dispatch order DP-2026-06-015 (whether the customer required plus or minus 1 mm in writing); (3) shift report EGIDA-RZ-001 (margin -529, explanation about small logs); (4) emails or SMS messages with the customer before shipping (whether the customer accepted the PN-EN 336 standard).

**Email + CC**: to Wesolowski + CC to Marek (foreman, despite his leave you leave a paper trail) + CC to Anna Kwiatkowska (production manager). Use EGIDA template variant 1 "immaterial defect" (from the trainer's template library). By 16:00 today, do not wait until Monday (preserves credibility plus KC art. 561 section 3 "reasonable time"). Offer a 3-5% price reduction = 96-160 zł in compensation = a goodwill gesture without acknowledging a material defect.

**Relationship to the negative margin**: these are **two independent facts** (Z4 economics vs Z5 complaints). Margin minus 529 zł = the economics of small logs, you are not at fault. The customer complaint = civil law, unrelated to the batch's economics. But **an M3 operator sees both** on the report and can tell them apart. If the customer demanded a refund and you felt guilty about the negative margin, you might wrongly accept. Z4 protects against this emotional/decisional error.

**Common junior M3 mistake**: caves to the customer's tone, agrees verbally to a refund, then has to explain it to Marek. Principle: **we accept nothing verbally; everything in writing with a KC citation**.

## Exercise 3: "FSC audit by Mr. Krzysztof Kowalski + Adrian speaks ill of Rustam"

### Context

Thursday 18 June 2026, 7:30. **NEPCon auditor Krzysztof Kowalski** arrives (annual FSC-C112233 audit, standard procedure). He is sitting in the EGIDA conference room with Marek, Anna Kwiatkowska, and **Adrian Kowal** (your M2 trainee). You are on shop floor P3 LT70, starting order ZLE-2026-06-024 (beech, 4 m3 FSC for MebelPro Warszawa). Marek texts: "Rustam, in 40 minutes the auditor wants to talk with you about chain of custody and about Adrian's training." At 8:10, Adrian arrives with the auditor.

**Before they enter the floor**, Adrian had said to Kowalski in the room (unintentionally, thinking it was private): "Mr. Rustam is a good operator, but sometimes he corrects me too harshly. Once he told me that guides at 4.2 mm instead of 3 was a mistake, but I was just tired." Auditor Kowalski heard it. Now they enter the floor.

The auditor asks you at the machine: "Mr. Rustam, please show me the chain of custody for this order. And while we are at it: Mr. Adrian mentioned something about your supervisory methods, I would like to understand the structure of your authority."

### Questions

1. In what order do you answer the auditor's two questions?
2. How do you show the chain of custody for the FSC beech?
3. How do you talk about Adrian without undermining him publicly and without backing down from your supervisory role?
4. Which paragraph of the EGIDA internal regulations is key, and why?

### Analytical hint

**3 M3 principles** activate: **Z3 documentation** (8-element chain of custody), **Z6 supervision** (pedagogy + regulations section 8 subsection 3), **Z1 boundary** (the auditor is an external party, not a subordinate).

**Order**: (1) chain of custody first, a 10-minute document walk-through. The Adrian question (2) goes second, a conversation after the walk-through, in the conference room, not in public.

**Chain of custody for the FSC beech ZLE-024**:
1. Receipt papers for the bundle with FSC label and supplier certificate (bundle SO-BUK-FSC-20260615)
2. KDP-001 (parametric decisions, 5 items)
3. KEP-001 (batch with classifier Ms. Ania)
4. KS-001 (shift card, yours)
5. Quarterly FSC Connect register (formal filing)
6. Final batch label with code EGIDA-FSC-C112233-20260618
7. MebelPro customer sales invoice (not yet issued, planned for Monday)
8. Drying documentation (Drew-Sus premium cycle, if planned)

Availability within 15 min (ISO 9001). All in the ZLE-024 folder + in TartakERP + a scanned copy in the EGIDA cloud.

**Conversation about Adrian** is 3-part:
- (1) Fact: "Mr. Krzysztof, the guides set by Adrian at 4.2 mm for oak C30 was a substantive error (the KDP-001 standard for that batch was 3 mm). Correction in line with EGIDA regulations section 8 subsection 3: 'I correct what is wrong, not what is merely different from mine.'"
- (2) Pedagogy: "The correction was verbal + recorded in his KS-001 + in KAO-001. Adrian received full authorization, 21/25. One substantive correction does not strip authorization, but it educates."
- (3) Relationship: "I understand that Adrian may have felt the correction as 'too harsh' at a moment when he was tired. That does not conflict with the supervisory role: an M3 operator must correct substantive errors regardless of the trainee's emotional state, because OSH and batch quality are at stake. I am happy to discuss this with Adrian after the audit, in Marek's presence, to explain why the correction was in line with the regulations and to ask whether I can adapt my pedagogical tone."

**Regulations section 8 subsection 3**: "I correct what is wrong, not what is merely different from mine." Key because it protects both the trainee (against arbitrary control) and the supervisor (against liability for unreported errors).

**Why the auditor is asking**. FSC-C112233 includes a requirement to train operators (FSC STD-40-004 clause 7.3). The auditor is checking whether the EGIDA training system works. The Rustam-Adrian-Krzysztof exchange is a **test** of procedures, not an attack on Rustam. A calm, factual answer satisfies him.

**Common junior M3 mistake**: reacts emotionally ("Adrian is lying, I was not too harsh, it is all written down"). Consequence: the auditor sees nerves, suspects other problems, deepens the audit, finds other gaps. Principle: **in an audit you are calm and factual; emotions only afterwards**.

## Exercise 4: "Monday 6:45, three matters at once"

### Context

Monday 22 June 2026, 6:45. You enter the shop floor, 15 minutes before shift start at 7:00. Three matters are waiting:

1. **Dispatch order DP-2026-06-026** from Marek on your desk. Oak FSC, 6 m3 for Bouwhout van Doorn (export customer, Netherlands, your first time solo), priority A, deadline Thursday 14:00, requires plus or minus 1 mm tolerance (not the standard plus or minus 2). You must complete KDP-001 before starting.
2. **Ms. Ania the classifier** is waiting in the meeting room with Saturday's report: "Rustam, 4 boards from Friday's batch ZLE-023 (beech) are at the C24/C16 boundary. I need your decision before closing KEP-001." She places the card on your desk marked "URGENT before start."
3. **Adrian** texts at 6:40: "Rustam, I am running late until 7:15, bus problem. What about my LT30 (ZLE-025 pine)? Start without you or wait?"

Marek is in the Monday management meeting from 6:30 to 7:30.

### Questions

1. In what order do you handle the 3 matters within 15 minutes?
2. Do you start your LT70 at 7:00 or later?
3. What are the risks and costs of each of the 3 prioritization options?
4. How do you inform Marek and when?

### Analytical hint

**4 M3 principles** activate: **Z2 KDP-001 before start** (the Bouwhout dispatch requires a full card, not on the fly), **Z6 supervision** (Adrian is late, supervisory decision), **Z7 diagnosis** (Ms. Ania at the class boundary, your co-classification decision), **Z4 economics** (export customer, priority A, premium price).

**15-minute sequence**:
- **0-2 min**: quick triage. Adrian's SMS, you reply in 15 s ("Adrian, wait for me until 7:15, do not start LT30 alone, safety > deadline"). Immediate decision because Adrian is waiting on an answer.
- **2-10 min**: Ms. Ania. You go to the meeting room, look at the 4 boards, joint C24/C16 decision (most likely 2 to C24, 2 to C16 at the boundary), KEP-001 closed, Ms. Ania signs. 8 minutes for an expert decision with a specialist = good pace.
- **10-15 min**: **Bouwhout dispatch order**. You read it through, notice plus or minus 1 mm (non-standard, requires schema B quarter sawing for oak + slower feed of 18 ft/min + high tension 2400 PSI). You complete KDP-001 quickly but completely. This is **not a startup on the fly**: if you do not finish in 5 min, YOU START LATER.

**Start LT70 at 7:00 or later?** If KDP-001 is ready and Ms. Ania is sorted by 7:00 = start on time. If KDP-001 is incomplete or Ms. Ania needs longer consultation = **start at 7:10-7:20, log in KS-001 "7:10 start, delay due to a complete KDP-001 for an export customer"**. Bouwhout = export customer, plus or minus 1 mm = the customer does not care whether you start at 7:00 or 7:15, but does care whether the KDP parameters are correct. Z2 principle: **the parametric decision always before cutting, not during it**.

**Option risks**:
- Option A (ignore Adrian, start LT70 at 7:00): Adrian may start LT30 alone without supervision, risk of accident + breach of independent M2 authorization (Adrian holds full 30-day authorization, but without supervision on days marked as "under Rustam").
- Option B (ignore Ms. Ania): KEP-001 closed without boundary classification, risk of a complaint from the customer Natalia at Stolarz Meblowy (class C24 vs C16 = price difference of 200-400 zł per m3).
- Option C (ignore the Bouwhout dispatch order, start LT70 with rough-set parameters): risk of plus or minus 2 mm tolerance instead of plus or minus 1 mm = export-customer complaint of a material defect + loss of 3 years of cooperation with Bouwhout (annual turnover 800,000 zł).

**Update to Marek**: SMS at 7:05 in the 4W format: "Marek, Rustam, P3, 7:05, starting at 7:10-7:15 after consultation with Ms. Ania (KEP-023 closed: 2 C24 + 2 C16) + completing KDP-001 for Bouwhout ZLE-026 (plus or minus 1 mm, schema B). Adrian late until 7:15, told him to wait. Everything under control, but a 10-15 min delay for Bouwhout is documented in KS-001."

**Common junior M3 mistake**: starts LT70 at 7:00 under the pressure of "deadline," skips Ms. Ania's classification, skips Adrian, fills out KDP-001 "on the fly." Cumulative consequences: (a) Bouwhout complaint about the 1 mm tolerance, (b) Stolarz Meblowy complaint about classification, (c) Adrian in an accident without supervision. Z2 mantra: **before, not during**.

## Exercise 5: "Mr. Henryk proposes a kiln-master internship at BTM"

### Context

Monday 22 June 2026, 15:45, end of shift. Mr. Henryk Domanski (62 y.o., BTM kiln master with 38 years of experience, EGIDA mentor) stops you at the exit: "Rustam, I spoke with Gdansk at BTM. You know that next year a kiln-master internship is planned: 6 months, 14000 zł course, commute twice a week, target salary of 8500 zł gross at BTM as a chamber-kiln master. You are a candidate. I can recommend you. What do you say?"

Currently: M3 hourly rate 55 zł gross x 168 h/month = **9240 zł gross** (with a 15% supervisory bonus when you supervise M2, on average about +500 to +800 zł/month = **9800-10000 zł gross total**). After UDT+SEP planned for September: +500 zł/month = **10500 zł**.

BTM offer: **8500 zł gross** as kiln-master salary. Course cost 14000 zł (who pays? you ask Mr. Henryk, who replies "you negotiate with BTM, usually 50/50 with BTM if you sign on for 3+ years").

### Questions

1. Calculate the financials: is it worth changing roles from M3 at EGIDA to kiln master at BTM?
2. What **non-financial** criteria must you weigh?
3. What would Marek advise? What about Ms. Ania? What about Damian (M3 with 4 years of experience, your former M2 mentor)?
4. What would your choice be and why?

### Analytical hint

**3 M3 principles** activate: **Z8 professional resources** (career planning by the numbers), **Z4 economics** (variant comparison), **Z1 boundary** (a competence change: from operator to master).

**Financial calculation**:
- M3 EGIDA current: 9800-10000 zł gross/month + after UDT+SEP in September: 10500 zł.
- BTM kiln master: 8500 zł gross. **Minus 2000 zł/month relative to M3 EGIDA + UDT+SEP**.
- But: **trajectory**. BTM after 2 years as master = possible promotion to drying-section manager (11000-13000 zł gross). M3 EGIDA without further courses = stable 10-11k for years; with a UPP degree = chance at 15-18k in 3 years.
- Course cost 14000 zł / 50% covered by BTM = 7000 zł out of pocket + 6 months of Ketrzyn-Gdansk commuting (around 350 km, twice a week, around 1500 zł/month for fuel + time x 6 months = 9000 zł). **Total investment 16000 zł**.
- BTM ROI: salary minus 2000 zł x 12 months = minus 24000 zł in year one. After promotion in year 3: +3000-5000 zł/month x 12 = +36000-60000 zł/year. **ROI 2-3 years if promoted, 5+ years without promotion**.

**Non-financial criteria**:
- **Job satisfaction**. Drying is a specialization, less operator-like, more process-oriented. Some people love it, some do not. Rustam in M3 enjoys direct contact with the machine and the trainee; drying means sitting at a panel + monitoring.
- **Location**. Gdansk vs Ketrzyn. Rustam settled in Ketrzyn (residence permit, apartment rented for 2 years). Gdansk = relocation or commuting. Relocation = a fresh start in a city 3x larger, anonymity.
- **Family and culture**. Rustam is single in Poland, no family here. Gdansk = a new circle. Ketrzyn = friends from the course + EGIDA.
- **Long-term career path**. BTM specialization = risk of being "locked in" to drying (harder to return to M3 operator work after 3 years). M3 EGIDA + courses = broader options.
- **Relationships**. Mr. Henryk recommends out of affection (he sees master potential in you), Marek = at risk of losing a strong M3, Ms. Ania = neutral, Damian = likely to say "do not rush, M3 is good."

**What they would advise**:
- **Marek**: "Rustam, you are an excellent M3, do not rush. Maybe in 2-3 years." A foreman's perspective, wants to keep you.
- **Ms. Ania**: "Do you like sitting at a computer for 8 hours, or do you prefer a tool?" A question about preferences.
- **Damian**: "I have been M3 for 4 years, never rushed anywhere. BTM is for people who really love drying. You like the sawmill. Leave drying to Mr. Henryk, he loves it."

**Choice**: there is no objectively "right" answer. The case teaches the **decision process** (numbers + non-financial + conversations + time to think), not the **outcome**. Rustam's likely choice: **not now**, but keep in touch with Mr. Henryk (the offer comes back in 2 years), in the meantime do UDT+SEP in September + think about UPP in 12 months.

**Common junior M3 mistake**: looks only at the first number (BTM 8500 < M3 10000 = "not worth it"). No trajectory analysis. Or the opposite: looks only at "master" (ego) and ignores the year-one pay drop. Z8 principle: **careers are planned in tables, not in feelings**.

## Exercise 6: "Chamber 3 panel shows alarm 82 deg C in the oak cycle, Mr. Henryk on leave"

### Context

Tuesday 23 June 2026, 10:30. Drying chamber no. 3 (BTM Polska, model KS-30) is in an oak cycle for ZLE-2026-06-027 (oak 12 m3 for Bouwhout van Doorn, 22-day cycle, day 8, current moisture 22%). The digital panel shows: **temperature 82 deg C, red alarm, oak limit 60 deg C, rising for the past 18 minutes**. Mr. Henryk Domanski (kiln master) is on a 2-week leave until 06 July 2026.

You are on the shop floor, P3 LT70, mid-cut. Marek is in the hall, 15 m from the chambers. Next to you: Adrian on LT30 (Dom-Bud pine order). In the office: Anna Kwiatkowska (production manager).

Possible alarm sources:
- Heating element failure (set point 60 deg C exceeded due to controller error)
- Temperature sensor failure (false reading)
- Chamber fan failure (no circulation, leading to overheating)

Consequences if 82 deg C lasts 30+ minutes: heart-rot cracks in the oak (60-80% batch loss risk, loss of 12 m3 x 2800 zł/m3 = **33600 zł**), loss of customer Bouwhout (annual turnover 800,000 zł), impact on the FSC audit (drying procedure non-conformity).

### Questions

1. Whose competence is this: P3 operator, EGIDA maintenance, or external BTM service?
2. Who decides to shut the chamber down (you, Marek, Anna Kwiatkowska)?
3. What is the action sequence over 10 minutes?
4. Which documents are generated and which escalations triggered?

### Analytical hint

**3 M3 principles** activate: **Z1 external vs internal boundary** (the BTM chamber = external service for repairs, but monitoring + emergency shutdown = operator-level), **Z7 diagnosis** (visual reading of the panel + auditory check of the fans + tactile check of the chamber door), **Z3 documentation** (chamber KKS-001 + a report for Mr. Henryk on his return).

**Competence split**:
- **Emergency chamber shutdown** (red STOP button on the panel) = **operator-level** (every M2 and M3 has both the right and the duty in the event of immediate batch risk). Does NOT require Mr. Henryk or maintenance.
- **Diagnosis of the alarm source** = **maintenance-level** (soft internal boundary): heating element? sensor? fan? An EGIDA mechanic checks.
- **Hardware repair of the chamber** = **external BTM service** (hard external boundary): replacing a heating element, sensor, or fan requires certified BTM parts + warranty cover.

**Shutdown decision**: **you first** (operator present at the machine) + **immediate escalation** to Marek and Anna. You do not wait for Anna Kwiatkowska. Temperature 82 deg C for 18 min and rising = **immediate batch risk of 33600 zł**, every minute increases the probability of heart-rot cracks. Z2 principle: **decision before, not during** = you shut down now, paperwork settles afterwards.

**10-minute sequence**:
- **0-1 min**: STOP chamber 3 (emergency button on the panel). Watch for the temperature drop (if it falls = the shutdown works; if it does not fall = heating-element failure, the cycle is stopped but temperature is rising another way, leading to opening the chamber door with maintenance).
- **1-3 min**: triple escalation: (a) phone call to Marek in 4W format "Marek, Rustam, chamber 3, 10:30, alarm 82 deg C in the oak cycle, emergency STOP, please come to the chambers"; (b) SMS to Anna Kwiatkowska: "Ms. Anna, chamber 3 alarm 82 deg C, STOP, please advise"; (c) phone to EGIDA maintenance on duty: "chamber 3, alarm 82 deg C in oak, emergency STOP, please diagnose the heating element."
- **3-5 min**: log in chamber 3 KKS-001: "23 June 2026 10:30, alarm 82 deg C in oak cycle ZLE-027 day 8, operator emergency STOP, escalation Marek + Kwiatkowska + maintenance." Full 3 senses: sight (panel), hearing (fans), touch (whether the chamber door is very hot).
- **5-10 min**: Marek arrives (within 5 min), joint decision: whether to open the chamber door (if temperature is not falling), or wait for maintenance (if it falls below 70 deg C). Decision on contacting BTM Polska (external service, Mr. Grzegorz Mazur) if maintenance does not solve it within 30 minutes.

**Documents + escalations**:
- Chamber 3 KKS-001 (detailed, 3 senses, chronology, escalations)
- EGIDA-PZ-001 for Mr. Henryk on his return (7 July 2026): "Mr. Henryk, a week without you, chamber 3 alarm 23 June, here is the full documentation"
- Report for Bouwhout van Doorn (customer of batch ZLE-027) in the event of damage (after final diagnosis, not immediately; there may be no problem if maintenance fixes it quickly)
- Report for the FSC audit (drying procedure non-conformity vs the planned one + corrective action plan)

**Common junior M3 mistake**: waits for Marek's authorization before shutting down. Temperature climbs for 10 min = oak damage threshold exceeded. Z1 principle: **the hard external boundary applies to SERVICE, but an emergency shutdown is NOT crossing the boundary**, it is the operator's duty.

## Exercise 7: "Adrian scores 18/25 in KAO-001, conditional or refusal?"

### Context

Friday 26 June 2026, 14:30. You are finishing your 5th supervisory shift over Adrian Kowal (M2) this week. You are filling out KAO-001 cumulatively (5 shifts x KS-001 + observations = a summary card).

**Scores on 5 criteria**:
1. **OSH** (0-5): 4 - reliable in PPE, but once forgot hearing protection for 3 minutes (Tuesday 14:00)
2. **KDP parameters** (0-5): 3 - wrong three times (feed rate 85% instead of 62% for oak, guides 4.2 mm instead of 3, tension 1900 PSI instead of 2200 for fresh pine)
3. **Cutting technique** (0-5): 4 - straight, no waving, average yield 55% (good for an M2)
4. **Sorting/classification** (0-5): 4 - knowledge of A/B/C/D OK, sometimes confuses the C24/C16 boundary
5. **Documentation** (0-5): 3 - KS-001 complete, but once written in pencil (replaced by pen under your correction)

**Sum: 18/25**. Per M3 regulations: full 30 days for >= 20/25, conditional 15 days for 17-19/25, refusal for < 17/25 or any single criterion < 3.

Adrian has two criteria = 3 (KDP parameters and documentation), both at the threshold (3, not <3). Sum 18 = conditional range (17-19).

Adrian asks: "Rustam, please give me the full one. I promise to improve over the next 30 days. A refusal will break my career." Marek (foreman) walks by and says: "Rustam, your call. Regulations are regulations, but you see the kid, I see the last month. What do you think?"

### Questions

1. What do EGIDA regulations section 8 + the KAO-001 scale say?
2. What are the arguments for full authorization and for conditional?
3. Should Adrian's pleading affect the decision?
4. Should Marek's opinion affect it?
5. What decision do you make and with what written justification on the card?

### Analytical hint

**3 M3 principles** activate: **Z6 supervision** (pedagogy + regulations), **Z3 documentation** (KAO-001 with evidentiary force), **Z5 KC** (the skill of reading a norm strictly, here the EGIDA regulations section 8).

**Regulations section 8 + KAO-001 scale**:
- 20-25/25 = full authorization, 30 days (without M3 supervision)
- 17-19/25 = conditional authorization, 15 days (with M3 supervision every other shift)
- < 17 or any single criterion < 3 = refusal + improvement plan + reassessment in 7 days

Adrian: 18/25, all criteria >= 3 = **conditional 15 days**. Unambiguous reading of the regulations.

**Arguments for full** (against the regulations): (a) positive trend (last shift 4/4/5/5/4 = 22); (b) the three parameter errors were in the first shift of the week and were not repeated; (c) Adrian has a good character, eager to learn; (d) foreman Marek informally supports.

**Arguments for conditional** (in line with the regulations): (a) the regulations are not a suggestion but a norm; (b) full authorization at 18/25 sets a precedent for other M2s (anyone can ask for an "exception," the system loses integrity); (c) 15 days of conditional is not a punishment but a verification window: if Adrian is genuinely better, he will finish at 22/25 in 2 weeks and earn full; (d) documentation with evidentiary force - KAO-001 goes into Adrian's personnel file for 10 years, and in the event of an incident an auditor (PIP labor inspectorate, customer, insurer) reads it; (e) Polish Labour Code (KP) art. 120 section 1 - full authorization at 18 = partial Rustam liability for any Adrian errors over the next 30 days.

**Adrian's pleading**: does NOT affect the decision. Trainee emotions are not an evaluation criterion. Pedagogy: "Adrian, I understand your feelings, but the regulations are as much a safeguard for you as they are for me. 18/25 conditional is not a punishment, it is a rhythm of verification. In 2 weeks I will reassess, and if the trend matches the last shift, you get full."

**Marek's opinion**: contextually relevant, but does NOT change the decision. As foreman, Marek observes Adrian holistically, but KAO-001 is the formal competence of M3, not the foreman. Marek himself said, "your call." Z6 principle: **M3 supervision is pedagogy with responsibility, not consensus with the foreman**.

**Decision**: **conditional 15 days** with the following justification in KAO-001:

> "Total 18/25. Criteria 2 (KDP parameters) and 5 (documentation) = 3 (lower threshold). Per EGIDA regulations section 8 subsection 3 and the authorization scale: conditional 15 days. Positive trend over the last 2 shifts (22/25 and 20/25), trainee eager to improve. Verification plan: KAO-001 reassessment at 7 and 14 days. Expected transition to full if >= 20/25 is maintained over the next 2 supervisory shifts. If 17-19 is maintained or there is a drop: continue conditional + improvement plan with Marek and the trainee."

**Common junior M3 mistake**: gives in to Adrian's pleading + Marek's influence + own sympathy + grants full. Effects: (a) if Adrian errs in 30 days, Rustam is co-liable; (b) Bekzod (another M2) asks "why did Adrian get full at 18 when I got conditional at 19?" = system inconsistency; (c) regulations section 8 broken, internal audit detects it, Anna Kwiatkowska has a conversation with Rustam. ## Exercise 8: "UDT+SEP this fall vs BTM internship in spring vs UPP studies"

### Context

Friday 26 June 2026, after lesson l6 T4 on career planning. At the "Mazurska" cafe in Ketrzyn, with Marek, Mr. Henryk, and Damian, you mentally walk through 3 options for the next 12 months:

**Option A: UDT IIWJO W-M + SEP G1-E** (ZDZ Olsztyn, 15-19 Sept 2026, 1 week of training leave):
- Cost: 3000 zł (2500 UDT + 500 SEP)
- Pay impact: +500 zł/month (350 UDT + 150 SEP)
- Simple ROI: 5.7 months
- After: you are an M3 operator with forklift + electrician credentials, can sub for the forklift driver (Antek Bondarenko on leave)
- Flexibility: high, further courses remain open

**Option B: BTM kiln-master internship** (Gdansk, start spring 2027, 6 months):

- ROI: 2-3 years if promoted, 5+ years without promotion

**Option C: UPP Poznan studies** (part-time, 3 years, start October 2026 or 2027):

- Pay impact during studies: neutral (full M3 + special "development" allowance of 200 zł/month under EGIDA regulations)
- Pay impact after studies: wood technology engineer, possible managerial roles 15-18k zł

- After: open high path, possible production manager or sawmill owner

### Questions

1. Calculate the ROI of each option over 5 years (2026-2031).
2. What are the **non-financial** differences between A, B, C?
3. Are the options **mutually exclusive** or can they be combined?
4. What do Mr. Henryk, Marek, and Damian advise (different perspectives)?
5. What is your recommendation for yourself?

### Analytical hint

**2 M3 principles** activate: **Z8 professional resources** (planning in tables) + **Z4 economics** (numerical ROI).

**5-year ROI**:
- **Option A UDT+SEP**: investment 3000 zł; return 500 zł x 60 months = 30000 zł. Net gain 27000 zł.
- **Option B BTM**: investment 16000 zł + 2 years of pay lower by 2000 zł = 16000 + 48000 = 64000 zł in cost. Return if promoted after 2 years: +3000-5000 x 36 months = 108000-180000 zł. Net gain 44000-116000 zł (a wide band depending on promotion). If no promotion: 24000 zł loss vs M3.
- **Option C UPP**: investment 33000 zł; return after 3 years of studies + 2 years of promotion = year 5 net 0 zł, year 6 +5000-8000 x 12 = 60000-96000 zł/year. In 5 years gain is limited (studies not finished); in 10 years it dominates.

**5-year ranking**: A > B (if promoted) > B (without promotion) vs A. C dominates long-term.

**Non-financial differences**:
- **A** minimally invasive (1 week of leave, Ketrzyn) - a big plus for someone settled
- **B** very invasive (6 months 2x a week Ketrzyn-Gdansk, then possible relocation)
- **C** chronically invasive (3 years of weekends in Poznan, if on-site sessions + exams) + demands self-discipline

**Mutual exclusion vs combination**:
- **A + B** possible: UDT+SEP fall 2026, BTM spring 2027 (A helps qualify for BTM, electrician credentials for the chambers).
- **A + C** possible: UDT+SEP fall 2026, UPP start October 2027 (a year to settle after the course).
- **B + C** difficult: BTM 6 months + studies 3 years = overload.
- **A as a base** + later choice of B or C = the most strategic.

**Opinions**:

**Recommendation for yourself**:
- **Most likely strategy 'A first, B or C later'**. UDT+SEP in fall 2026, a 12-18 month stabilization period, then a B or C decision based on actual preferences (whether drying pulls you = B; or academic managerial paths = C).

## For the trainer: how to lead

**Preparation**. Print each exercise on a double-sided A4 sheet (8 sheets). For exercises 2 (complaint), 5 (BTM), 6 (chamber), and 8 (career plan), prepare **support tables** with figures (PN-EN 336, KC art. 560, sawmill cost table, 2026 price lists, ROI scale). Print the analytical hints for yourself and show them to trainees only after the discussion.

**In the room**. Layout: 2 sessions of 4 exercises each = 2 x 1.5 h. Session 1 (ex. 1-4, operational): Tuesday of week 4 after l6. Session 2 (ex. 5-8, strategic): Wednesday of week 4 after l6. Thursday l7 recap, Friday l8 test.

**Discussion**. Read the context aloud (3-4 min), allow 3 min of silence for reflection, open the discussion: "who starts?" For Polish B1- learners, give linguistic support (you may allow short statements in their native language, then have the group translate). Exercise 7 (Adrian's KAO 18/25) is **emotionally difficult** and may provoke strong opinions. The trainer moderates and does not collapse it to "one correct answer" too quickly.

**Feedback**. After each exercise, read the analytical hint and the **map of activated M3 principles**. Trainees identify themselves which principle they missed or applied well. The goal is a **personal gap map** (trainee: "I am weak on Z5 complaints, strong on Z6 supervision"), not ranking.

**Diagnostics**. Note in a private spreadsheet who has trouble with which principle. Before the l8 test, run 20 min of 1-on-1 consultations with the 3-5 trainees with the largest gaps (priority: Z5 KC and Z1 boundary, the legally hardest).

**Strategic exercises 5, 8**. These have **less unambiguous answers** than 1-4. The trainer does not impose an outcome but cares about **the quality of the decision process** (tables + non-financial + opinions + own choice). Different trainees may reach different conclusions, and that is OK.

**Cultures**.
- Ukrainians and Belarusians readily engage in strategic discussion (ex. 5, 8); they are realists.
- Uzbeks and Tajiks are often surprisingly mature in ex. 3 (FSC audit), as they come from cultures that respect formal procedures.
- Vietnamese and Filipinos may be too "agreeable" in ex. 7 (Adrian); the trainer pushes for a clear decision rather than a compromise.
- Latin Americans (Peru, Colombia) react very emotionally to ex. 2 (customer complaint); the trainer separates the emotion from the KC.

**Mixing pairs in discussion**. Rotate pairings each exercise ("today ex. 3 in a Ukrainian + Uzbek pair, ex. 4 a Vietnamese + Peruvian"), so cultural perspectives interleave. Do not leave "monocultural" tables for the entire week 4.
