## Introduction

Thursday, the ninth day of week 1. Monday: Oleh and the law. Tuesday: Andrei and dust. Wednesday: Siarhei and the five steps of a fault. Today we close the week with a lesson that is the hardest and the simplest at the same time. The hardest, because it describes a situation no one wants to think about. The simplest, because in this situation there is no time to think, there is only ritual.

**The hero of today's lesson is Hai**, the Vietnamese worker from week 1 lesson 1, the same one who on Monday stood alone at the band saw for the first time. At 10:23 Hai is carrying a bag of sawdust from the dust extraction hall to the container in the yard. He passes hall 1. Band saw no. 4. Operator: **Paweł Nowak, 52 years old, fifteen years in the sawmill**. Paweł is a veteran. Paweł knows the machine by heart. Paweł was in a hurry that morning because a supplier was waiting in the office for his signature. Paweł had a jam at 10:22. Paweł skipped step three (ANCHOR) from lesson 7, because "it already stopped, I will pull it out quickly and get back to work." Paweł pulled the board with his left hand. The residual torque of the blade (40 Nm, remember?) jerked the disc. **At 10:23:08 the index finger of Paweł's left hand lies on the table next to the board. Bright red blood spurts from the stump in a pulsating stream.**

Hai enters at 10:23:15. **He has three minutes to keep Paweł from bleeding out.** In l7 there were five steps, because the machine can wait. Here there are **four steps, because blood does not wait**: **SECURE – STAUNCH – CALL – SAVE**. You will learn them today, you will repeat them tomorrow and the day after. Do not count on never needing them. Count on the fact that when your moment comes, they will be in your fingers, not in your head.

## Objectives

After this lesson:

1. You know the four-step protocol for a witness of an accident with hemorrhage: **SECURE (yourself and the site) – STAUNCH (pressure, then tourniquet) – CALL (112) – SAVE (the amputated fragment)**. You can perform it in three minutes.
2. You know when **direct pressure** is enough (dark blood, flowing out) and when a **tourniquet** is required (bright red pulsating blood; or pressure did not stop the bleeding within 2–3 min).
3. You know how to apply a **CAT tourniquet** (Combat Application Tourniquet): 5–7 cm above the wound, not on a joint, tighten until the blood stops spurting, write down the time of application with a marker on the tourniquet.
4. You know the **112 call script** for a workplace accident and you know **who meets the ambulance** (the foreman, not you: you stay with the injured person).
5. You can secure an **amputated fragment** (dry gauze; plastic bag tightly sealed; container with ice and water, not directly on ice). You know the replantation time limit: **finger 12 hours**, hand 6 hours.
6. You understand your legal obligations: **art. 162 § 1 of the Polish Penal Code** (duty to help, up to 3 years for failure to render aid) and **art. 234 of the Polish Labor Code** (employer reports the accident to PIP within 24 hours).

## Content

### Why three minutes, not five, not ten

In an injury the radial artery pumps out 250–400 ml of blood per minute. **In three minutes a person loses 750–1200 ml**, that is 15–25% of blood volume. Hypovolemic shock begins at 30%. An ambulance reaches you in 8–12 minutes in the city, 15–25 in the countryside. **The witness alone is the bridge between the injury and the ambulance.**

Direct pressure in the first minute stops venous hemorrhage in 90% of cases (data from the Polish Resuscitation Council, PRR). **A tourniquet applied in the second minute** reduces blood loss by 70–90%. A tourniquet in the fifth minute saves only the life, no longer the hand. Three minutes is biology, not a slogan.

### Step 1: SECURE (seconds 0–20)

Hai runs in. He sees Paweł at the saw, blood spurting, Paweł pale. The first reflex of an inexperienced witness: run to the injured person. **Bad reflex.** If the machine is running, you may become the second victim.

**What Hai does**:

1. **Checks the machine**. Saw no. 4 is stopped (Paweł managed to hit STOP). If the blade were running, Hai would **first** press the mushroom button.
2. **Checks the surroundings**. No pool of oil, no loose board. It is safe to approach.
3. **Calls out loudly**. "FOREMAN, HELP, HALL 1, SAW 4!" Short, addressed. The operator from hall 2 runs to notify. **Hai does not go for the foreman himself**: he stays with Paweł.
4. **Approaches with a firm step**, not running (running + blood on the floor = a fall).

::: warning
**Art. 162 § 1 of the Polish Penal Code**: anyone who fails to render aid to a person in danger of death or grievous bodily harm, where he can do so without exposing himself to danger, is subject to a penalty of up to 3 years. However, the law does not require putting your own life at risk. **First secure yourself, then help the injured person.** This is not egoism, this is the order of operations.
:::

### Step 2: STAUNCH (seconds 20–120)

Paweł sits on the floor. His right hand grips his left, blood drips between the fingers. Hai kneels beside him.

**2a. Direct pressure (seconds 20–60)**

Hai grabs the **roll of paper towels from the saw** (every machine keeps them for resin). Not the first aid kit, because it is down the corridor, and the blood is here now. He folds a thick pad, presses it **firmly and steadily** directly onto the stump. Not "gently." His other hand lifts Paweł's forearm above heart level.

::: info
**Why raise the arm**: gravity works against the bleeding, reducing the outflow by 15–25%. Combined with pressure, this is usually enough for a venous hemorrhage.
:::

**Hold the pressure for 60 seconds without releasing** (every release resets the clot). At the 60-second mark you glance: if the towel stops soaking, the venous bleeding is under control, hold on for another 4–5 minutes just to be sure. **If blood is still spurting in pulses**, it is arterial hemorrhage. Time for a tourniquet.

**2b. Tourniquet (seconds 60–120)**

A type K first aid kit (standard DIN 13169, mandatory under the Polish Ministry of Health Regulation 2019) contains a **CAT tourniquet**: Combat Application Tourniquet, NATO-approved, applicable with one hand. If there is none, **you improvise**: a tie, a scarf, a strap from the first aid kit (not a trouser belt, too narrow, it damages tissue).

**Where you apply it**: **5–7 cm above the wound**, halfway up the forearm, **not on a joint** (neither wrist nor elbow: a tourniquet on a joint will not compress the artery, it will only damage the bone).

**How you apply it**:

1. You put it on loosely, like a bracelet.
2. You pull the Velcro strap tight.
3. **You turn the windlass until the blood stops spurting**. Usually 3–5 turns. The patient screams in pain: this is normal, this means it is working.
4. **You write with a marker on the tourniquet**: **T = 10:25**. If you have no marker, use a pen on the skin above the tourniquet. If neither, **memorize the time** and say it to every responder who comes next.

::: warning
**A tourniquet for 2 hours is safe, 6 hours is the absolute maximum.** Above that, tissues die from oxygen deprivation and the amputation extends higher than the original wound. **Once applied, you as a witness do not remove the tourniquet.** It is removed only by medical staff at the hospital with a transfusion ready and an operating room available.
:::

### Step 3: CALL (seconds 120–180)

Bleeding under control. Hai takes out his phone.

**112. Call script**:

> **Dispatcher**: "Emergency number 112, I am listening."
>
> **Hai**: "Workplace accident. Fundacja EGIDA sawmill, ul. Dębowa 12, Dębowa Góra. Hall no. 1, band saw. Amputation of the index finger of the left hand. Injured: Paweł Nowak, 52 years old, conscious. I applied a tourniquet at 10:25. We need an ambulance."
>
> **Dispatcher**: "I am not hanging up, help is on the way. Is the amputated fragment visible?"
>
> **Hai**: "Yes, it is on the saw table."
>
> **Dispatcher**: "I will tell you how to secure it. Someone should wait at the gate with the gate open, ambulance in 11 minutes."

**Seven rules of the 112 call**:

1. **Exact address** (street, number, city, name of the plant). The address must be **visible on the first aid kit**.
2. **What happened** (one sentence).
3. **How many injured**.
4. **Condition of the injured** (conscious/unconscious, bleeding under control).
5. **What you have already done** (tourniquet applied at what time).
6. **Who is calling** (name, role).
7. **Do not hang up** until the dispatcher says "you may end the call."

::: tip
**Who meets the ambulance**: not you. You stay with the injured person. The **foreman** goes to the plant gate with **the gate open**: an ambulance does not stop in front of a closed gate. At night he waves a flashlight, during the day his arms, and leads the ambulance straight to the hall.
:::

### Step 4: SAVE (seconds 180+)

Foreman Marek Kowalski (the same one from l7) arrives at 10:26. He sees a stable situation and opens the type K first aid kit. Contents needed: **sterile gauze 10×10 cm, a zip plastic bag, a plastic container with a lid, gel ice**.

**Procedure for securing the amputated fragment**:

1. **Pick it up with gauze**, not with a bare hand (infection lowers replantation chances).
2. **Wrap it dry, not wet.** Water destroys the tissue through osmosis, ice directly through contact necrosis. **Dry and cool**, nothing more. Do not clean, do not wash.
3. **Into the plastic bag**, seal it tightly.
4. **The bag goes into the container with ice and water** in a 50/50 ratio. Optimal temperature **+4°C**. Do not freeze.
5. **Label the container**: "Paweł Nowak, index finger of the left hand, amputation 10:23, secured 10:28."
6. **The container rides with the injured person** in the same ambulance. Never separately.

::: example
**Replantation time limit (cold chain)**:

- **Finger**: up to 12 hours from amputation.
- **Hand**: up to 6 hours.
- **Forearm/arm**: up to 6 hours (greater muscle mass, faster necrosis).

Without cooling, the time limit drops to 2 hours for a finger. **That is why ice and water, that is why the bag, that is why the container rides in the ambulance.**
:::

### Legal obligations after an accident

The ambulance takes Paweł and his finger at 10:34. Amputation of a finger is **grievous bodily harm** (art. 156 of the Penal Code in the medical glossary). The employer has obligations:

- **Secure the accident site** (saw no. 4 waits for the commission with a sign "ACCIDENT SITE, DO NOT TOUCH").
- **Report to PIP and the prosecutor** without delay (art. 234 § 2 of the Polish Labor Code, by phone within 2–4 hours, in writing within 24 hours).
- **Accident report card** prepared by the **accident investigation team** (the OSH officer + the social labor inspector), deadline **14 days** from the accident (Polish Cabinet Regulation of 1 July 2009).

**Hai's role as a witness**: he will be questioned by the investigation team. He will describe exactly what he saw and what steps he took. He has the right to **psychological support** (seeing an amputation is a trauma, a stress reaction lasting days or weeks is normal).

### Case Hai: three minutes that stay with you

**Chronology 10:23–10:34**:

- **10:23:00** : Paweł pulls out the board with his hand, the disc jerks, amputation.
- **10:23:15** : Hai enters, checks the machine (stopped) and the surroundings.
- **10:23:20** : Hai calls for help. Operator from hall 2 runs.
- **10:23:35** : Hai kneels, grabs the roll of paper towels, applies pressure.
- **10:24:30** : Blood is still spurting. Hai opens the type K first aid kit, takes out the CAT.
- **10:25:00** : Tourniquet applied, 4 windlass turns, marker: **T = 10:25**.
- **10:25:30** : Hai calls 112.
- **10:26:00** : Foreman Marek in the hall.
- **10:27:00** : Marek packs the finger: gauze, bag, container with ice, label 10:28.
- **10:34:00** : Ambulance. Paweł and the finger travel together to the emergency room of the county hospital. Replantation the same day, finger saved.

**Eleven minutes from accident to ambulance, three minutes from accident to tourniquet.** That is the difference between "Paweł survived with an amputation" and "Paweł died in hypovolemic shock."

::: tip
**Lesson from the case**: Hai went through first aid training at the plant (8 hours, Polish Red Cross, PCK) half a year before that day. He said back then: "Waste of time, nothing will happen to me." The three minutes of ritual that saved Paweł's hand were 8 hours of training half a year earlier. **Art. 237³ § 2 of the Polish Labor Code** requires first aid training once every 5 years. It is not paperwork, it is Paweł with a finger or Paweł without a finger.
:::

## Check yourself

**Question 1**: You enter the hall, you see a colleague on the floor with a cut hand, blood is spurting. A machine next to him is running. What do you do **first**?

*Good answer*: you stop the machine (the STOP mushroom from l3). Only then do you approach the injured person. If you run into blood on the floor, into a running machine, you become the second victim. Art. 162 of the Penal Code does not require you to risk your own life.

**Question 2**: The blood is bright red, spurting in a pulsating stream. Direct pressure for 90 seconds did not stop the bleeding. What do you do?

*Good answer*: this is arterial hemorrhage. You apply a tourniquet (a CAT from the type K first aid kit or an improvised one from a tie) **5–7 cm above the wound, not on a joint**, tighten until the blood stops spurting, **write the time of application with a marker** (T = HH:MM). You do not remove the tourniquet, medical staff at the hospital will remove it.

**Question 3**: You are calling 112. Which six pieces of information must you pass on in the first 20 seconds?

*Good answer*: (1) type of event, (2) exact address (street, number, city, name of the plant), (3) number of injured, (4) condition of the injured, (5) what you have already done (tourniquet applied at what time), (6) who is calling. Then **do not hang up**.

**Question 4**: The amputated finger lies on the table next to you. How do you secure it?

*Good answer*: pick it up with clean gauze (not with a bare hand). Wrap it **dry**. Put it into a **tightly sealed plastic bag**. Put the bag into a **container with ice and water** (50/50, +4°C). **Not directly on ice** (contact necrosis). Label the container. It rides with the injured person in the ambulance. Replantation time limit for a finger in the cold chain: **12 hours**.

**Question 5**: The tourniquet has been on since 10:25. Ambulance at 10:34, hospital at 10:55. Can you remove the tourniquet in the ambulance to shorten its action time?

*Good answer*: no. The tourniquet stays until the hospital with a transfusion ready and an operating room available. Removing it earlier risks secondary hemorrhage and shock. Safe tourniquet time **2 hours**, absolute limit **6 hours**. 30 minutes to the hospital fits many times over within the safety limit.

## Link to practice

This week:

1. Check where the **type K first aid kit** is in your plant. Open it once. Check the contents: CAT tourniquet, sterile gauze, plastic bag, plastic container, gel ice. Something missing, report it to the foreman.
2. Check whether the **plant address** is visible on the first aid kit. If not, write it on a note and stick it to the lid. In panic a person forgets his own address.
3. Once a month **imagine the scenario** (30 seconds, on the way to work): you walk into the hall, you see a colleague on the floor, blood on the floor. What do you do in the first second? In the first minute? Repeat the 4S: **SECURE – STAUNCH – CALL – SAVE**.
4. Sign up for **first aid training** (PCK or plant-run, 8 hours). A legal obligation once every 5 years (art. 237³ § 2 of the Polish Labor Code), the real value is one wound you will not kill through ignorance.

**Dry simulation (20 minutes)**:

1. Learner A sits on the floor, pretends an amputation (red marker on the hand).
2. Learner B enters the room with 3 minutes to execute the 4-step ritual.
3. The trainer times it: 0–20 s SECURE, 20–120 s STAUNCH, 120–180 s CALL, 180+ s SAVE.
4. After the drill, 5 minutes of discussion: what was easy, what was hard, what he forgot. Repeat with another pair.

**Most common learner mistakes in the simulation**:

- They run to the injured person without stopping the imaginary machine. Tell them: **machine first, person second**.
- They press gently because it hurts the injured person. Tell them: **it has to hurt, that means you are pressing correctly**.
- They forget to write down the time of tourniquet application. Tell them: **without a time the tourniquet is a hazard, with a time it is medicine**.
- They do not give the address on the phone ("accident at the sawmill"). Tell them: **the address is in the first sentence**.
