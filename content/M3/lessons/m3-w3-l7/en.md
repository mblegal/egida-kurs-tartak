**Level 1, motor and dynamics**. The LT30 has a 20 kW electric motor, a single belt speed (around 2200 RPM constant). **Level 2, feed dynamics**. The LT30 has a linear hydraulic feed (one moving speed, gas pedal, proportional). The LT70 has **stepped regulation** with two scales: **coarse scale** 0-100% of the main feed, **fine scale** ±20% of dynamic correction during cutting. This gives greater precision but requires a different way of thinking: on the LT30 you instinctively slow down with the pedal, on the LT70 you have to decide the percentages and enter them on the scale.

Adrian asks *"Can you change the fine scale during cutting?"*. Rustam answers: *"Yes, and you should. When you hear the motor slow down (scaling, entering denser wood), you reduce the fine by 5-10%. When the motor 'flies' without resistance, you increase. This is done during cutting with the thumb, without looking."*

**Level 3, guides**. The LT30 has **one pair of guides** (two UHMW blocks on each side of the blade, gap adjustment with manual screws). The LT70 has **two pairs of guides** (front of machine + rear of machine, four blocks in total), **separate** adjustment for each pair (front for the blade's first entry into the wood, rear for stabilisation after the pass). Standard gap **3-4 mm** for pine, **2-3 mm** for oak (stiffer wood, less damping needed). This is **the most important technical difference** which Adrian could get confused about (unconsciously set both pairs to the same value, which gives worse cutting precision at the other end).

Rustam demonstrates on the stopped machine: opens the cover of the upper blade wheel, shows four blocks, two front and two rear. *"Today you'll set 3 mm front, 3.5 mm rear. ## Theory of supervision: the M3 → M2 → M1 pyramid

The EGIDA sawmill works in a **three-tier operator hierarchy**, recorded in the internal regulations (reg.wew.02/2024, current, signed by Marek) and resulting from Polish OHS requirements (MGiP regulation 28.06.2005 for woodworking equipment, specification of the workstation instruction):

- **M1 helper**: does not operate any sawmill independently, performs assistant tasks (feeding, collection, sorting on rollers, marking). In EGIDA practice: **M1 for the first 3-4 months of a new employee's employment**, rotation between all workstations (sorting room, yard, drying chamber, sawmill as helper). Wahan Petrosjan is a stationary M1 at P3 (he chose permanently to assist Rustam, doesn't want to go for the M2 exam yet, fear of independence). In the regulations: **always works under the supervision of an M2 or M3 operator**, not independently.

- **M2 junior operator independent under supervision**: passed the M2 exam (60-question test, threshold 75%, OHS + material + processes modules), **operates the sawmill independently but in the presence of an M3 operator** (the same or another). In practice: **M2 works on P2 LT30 or P1 Serra SM40 (smaller sawmills)**, admitted to P3 LT70 only after **20 motor-hours of work under supervision** (i.e. about 5-7 full shifts on P3). Adrian has been M2 since 05.05.2026, today is his first shift on P3 under supervision, in about 4-5 more shifts he'll get full P3 authorization.

- **M3 fully independent operator**: passed the M3 exam (80-question test, threshold 80%, full EGIDA sawmill course modules), operates each sawmill independently, **can supervise M2 and M1**, is responsible for KDP-001 documentation and shift cards. Rustam Nazarov and Damian have been M3 fully independent since April 2026 (respectively Rustam 04.04.2026, Damian 01.11.2024, a year older).

**The pyramid means**: an M3 operator during a shift with M2 and M1 is **responsible for three levels at the same time**. His own tasks (e.g. does not carry out cutting personally, but watches decisions, verifies documentation, approves preliminary class), supervision over M2 (corrects errors, assesses rhythm, fills in KAO-001), supervision over M1 (posture correction, no-go zone, assistance in sorting). This is **a cognitive load greater than independent cutting**, which is why EGIDA grants a **supervisory allowance of 15%** on the M3 rate for shifts with the full pyramid.

### Principle: I correct when wrong, not when different from mine

This is the **most important pedagogical principle** of supervision at EGIDA, expressly recorded in the regulations (reg.wew.02/2024 § 8 para. 3):

> The supervising M3 operator corrects the decisions of the M2 operator only in the case of a **substantive error** (parameter outside the norm, non-compliance with KDP-001, OHS hazard, risk to batch quality). The supervising operator **does not correct** decisions that are technically correct, even if he himself would have chosen a different variant. A decision within the acceptance space is **the property of the M2 operator**, who learns responsibility for his own choices. The M3 operator may, after the shift ends, **comment** on other variants (pedagogical reflection), but during the operation he keeps silent if there is no error.

Practical: if Adrian chooses the cut order 'left-right' and Rustam would have chosen 'middle-outwards', both give the same 6 rafters from the same log, both in the same time, neither creates more waste. **Rustam stays silent during the work**. After the shift (evening reflection in the canteen or at the shift card) he may say: *"Adrian, **you could also try** 'middle-outwards', it gives you the wide rafters at the start, easier early sorting to exclude B and C."* This is a **suggestion**, not a correction.

Conversely: if Adrian sets the feed speed to 85% coarse scale (above the recommended 75% upper limit for fresh pine at 28% moisture), **this is an error** (parameter outside the norm), Rustam corrects immediately (*"Adrian, 85 is too much, max 75 for this moisture, lower it"*). Adrian changes to 70%, Rustam closes the matter. **He does not ask why Adrian tried 85%**, that's Adrian's learning path (maybe a second of bravado, maybe a mistake, no matter, the correction was effective).

### Boundaries of correction: a remark during cutting, reflection after the shift

**During cutting** (the M2 operator has his head in the task, any correction distracts):

- Correction **only by gesture or a short tap on the shoulder + 1-2 words**. Not a long lecture.
- Order of priority: (a) OHS / safety of people, (b) risk of damage to the machine / blade, (c) risk of loss of batch / class error, (d) something that can wait for reflection after the shift.
- When the correction is of type (d), **stay silent in the moment**, note it mentally, say it after.

**After the shift** (the M2 operator is already relaxed, head open to reflection):

- You sit together for 5-10 minutes, KAO-001 in hand.
- You discuss chronologically: what went well, what was weaker, what was different.
- **Don't start with "weaker"** (it demotivates), start with "well", then "weaker", then "reflections and suggestions".
- Ask the M2 operator about **his feelings** ("What did you find difficult? What was easy?"), because he sees different things than the M3 from the side.
- KAO-001 card signed jointly.

Today Rustam did exactly this: during the cutting he stayed silent about the cut order (a difference, not an error), in the evening at the KAO-001 he mentioned it as a reflection. Adrian received it as **space**, not as **delayed criticism**. That's a difference in style.

## Authorization: when full, conditional, refusal

The M3's authorization decision recorded in KAO-001 has **three variants** with definitions:

### Variant A: Full authorization (30 days)

The M2 operator **scored 20/25 points or more**, **all criteria ≥ 3/5**, **zero OHS incidents**, **zero blade breakage through his fault**. Authorization valid **30 days from the date of the shift** on the same machine. After 30 days the M2 operator must do **another shift under the supervision of any M3** for renewal (automatic after 20 full motor-hours of independent work without incident).

Today Adrian received variant A: 21/25, all criteria ≥3, zero incident. Full P3 authorization to 16.07.2026.

### Variant B: Conditional authorization (15 days with retake)

The M2 operator scored **17-19 points** or **one criterion = 3/5 and needs bringing up**, but **no criterion dropped below 3**. Authorization valid **15 days** with the obligation of **another shift under supervision within 2 weeks** (retake targeted at the weaker criterion). If the retake brings the result up to 20/25, he moves on to full authorization. If the retake again shows gaps, the decision escalates to the foreman (variant C).

At EGIDA the typical situation: M2 second or third day of independence under supervision, criterion 3 (technique) or 5 (documentation) imprecise, conditional authorization with 2 weeks under supervision.

### Variant C: Refusal of authorization

The M2 operator scored **below 17 points** or **one criterion < 3/5** (fundamental lack), or an **OHS incident through his fault** (e.g. the helper entered the no-go zone because the operator didn't watch, or PPE removed during cutting). Decision: **return to M1 helper** (if a fundamental lack) or **additional theoretical training of 16 hours** before re-admission to the machine.

At EGIDA history: 1 case of variant C since 2024. The M2 operator completed the course in September 2024, first independent shift under foreman supervision, he pushed the coarse scale to 95% for frozen oak (grossly wrong, for frozen oak max 50%), the blade broke in 15 minutes, the helper next to him suffered a minor hand injury from chip kickback. Refusal of authorization, return to M1 + mandatory training. The operator left EGIDA in October 2024. This is an extreme case, rare but real.

### Notes on supervision rotation

The EGIDA regulations require that **the M2 operator is supervised by different M3s** (not always the same one). Rotation Rustam / Damian / Marek (he can do it himself too, though more rarely). This has two reasons: **avoiding style matching** (the M2 may learn only the style of one supervisor) and **verification from different perspectives** (two M3s may see the same problems differently). In May-June 2026 a new practice: M2 first 5 shifts under supervision with the same M3 (rhythm), subsequent shifts with different M3s (verification). ## Nine typical M3 supervisor errors

From EGIDA observations 2024-2026 (Marek keeps a consultation diary for all M3s, enters cases anonymously), **nine typical errors** that a new M3 supervisor commits and which are worth knowing:

1. **Butting in on every decision** (Damian-a-year-ago style). Causes the M2 to learn to **copy** instead of **think**. Correction: stay silent when the decision is different-but-correct.
2. **Correction without justification** ("Do it differently"). Without explaining **why**, the M2 doesn't know what to learn. Correction: always "Do X, **because** Y".
3. **Doing the operator's job for him**. The supervisor takes the blade in hand, changes it himself, because "it's faster". The M2 doesn't learn the changeover. Correction: **hands in pockets**, only mouth and eyes work.
4. **Ignoring documentation**. The supervisor focuses on the cutting, doesn't watch KDP-001 and the shift card. The M2 learns that documentation is secondary, in the evening there are backlogs. Correction: **documentation in real time**, like checking whether things are being kept current.
5. **Favouring speed over quality**. The supervisor pushes the M2 "faster, faster". The M2 loses precision, the risk of error grows. Correction: **first shift slower, second equal, third at speed**.
6. **No positive feedback**. The supervisor only corrects, never praises. The M2 doesn't know **what he's doing well**, and loses motivation. Correction: **for every 3 corrections one positive** ("You did this well, specifically this decision").
7. **Public feedback**. The supervisor scolds the M2 in front of the M1 helper ("Look Wahan, Adrian made a mistake"). The M2 loses face in front of the team. Correction: **private feedback**, KAO-001 card in private.
8. **Reluctance to fill in KAO-001 honestly**. The supervisor gives 24/25 because "he's a nice guy", ignores real weaknesses. The M2 doesn't develop, because he thinks he's perfect. Correction: **honest numbers**, a conditional with retake is better than a false full.
9. **No escalation on serious errors**. The supervisor sees a fundamental M2 error (OHS), but doesn't report it to the foreman. The operator puts himself at risk on subsequent shifts alone. Correction: **always escalate** to Marek when a criterion is <3 or on an OHS incident.
