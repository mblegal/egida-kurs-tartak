# M2 Supplementary Final Quiz

## Purpose

This quiz is a **supplement to the l8 Module 2 test** (25 questions x 40 pts, passing threshold 70% = 28 pts). It contains **55 items** in three types (30 closed + 15 open + 10 cases) mapped to four competency areas of the operator under supervision: material knowledge (T2), machines under supervision (T3), operational documentation (T4 Block 1), crew relations and self-regulation (T4 Block 2).

Used in two modes:

1. **Self-assessment mode.** The trainee solves it after the l8 test (if they passed), to check deeper knowledge and diagnose gaps before the practical exam on Tuesday. No impact on credit.
2. **Retake mode.** A trainee who did not pass l8 (below 28 pts out of 40) solves both tests (l8 + supplementary quiz) during the retake window two weeks later. Combined threshold **70% of 40 + 90 = 130 pts = 91 pts**.

## Instructions

- **55 items, total 90 pts, passing threshold 70% = 63 pts**.
- Distribution of questions across areas: **20% / 30% / 20% / 30%** (identical to the l8 test).
- Part A: **30 closed questions x 1 pt** (A/B/C/D, one correct).
- Part B: **15 open questions x 2 pts** (answer 1-3 sentences).
- Part C: **10 cases x 3 pts** (situation description + short analysis 4-6 sentences).
- Time: **120 minutes** (30+40+50 min).
- No consultations, solo, on paper.
- If you don't know, mark "don't know" (0 pts, information for the trainer, not a penalty).

## Part B: open questions (15 x 2 pts = 30 pts)

**Answer in 1-3 sentences, the point is sense, not word count. At least one key numerical fact or definition.**

### Area 1: Material knowledge (31-33)

**31. Explain why English oak requires a longer drying cycle than pine (at the same thickness of 50 mm).**

*(Expected answer: denser structure + medullary rays cracking above 60°C + slower moisture diffusion; oak cycle 22-25 days vs pine 10 days for 50 mm, max temperature 60°C for oak vs 70°C for pine.)*

**32. Describe the difference between PN-EN 1611-1 visual grading (S0-S4) and PN-EN 14081-1 strength grading (C14-C50).**

*(1611-1 visual assessment of defects: knots, cracks, twist, general softwood construction use; 14081-1 assessment of the bending strength parameter in N/mm², C classes for softwoods D for hardwoods, structural use; one board can carry both classifications in parallel.)*

**33. What does the "r" suffix in the code SO-B-260420-012-r mean and when is it used?**

*(Reclassification after the drying cycle: the original class A after assessment of 4 MC + crack positions dropped to class B, e.g. 2 boards with end cracks 1/3 of the width; new label with the r suffix + entry in the kiln register + TartakERP update, invoice difference in favor of documentation.)*

### Area 2: Machines under supervision (34-37)

**34. Explain the division of responsibility between the batch loader and the kiln supervisor (Zbigniew Majchrzak).**

*(Loader = stack geometry + spacer sticks 20-25 mm every 600-800 mm + closing the door + report; supervisor = cycle program on the computer + parameter monitoring + cycle completion + responsibility for the result; mantra "I load, I close, I report, I do NOT program cycles".)*

**35. What are the three characteristic signs of band saw dulling?**

*(Increase in cutting resistance visible on the ammeter in the yellow zone; change in cutting sound from an even whistle to a rough growl; appearance of a wavy kerf surface with fine fibers; junior operator's decision: STOP + report to the foreman, no independent replacement.)*

**36. Describe the band saw startup procedure under supervision (6 steps from stage A of the exam).**

*(General visual inspection : check oil level and band tension pressure 2200-2400 PSI : check guards and emergency switch : start the motor from the operator's position, not from the side : observe the ammeter for 30 s at idle : confirm readiness to the foreman or senior.)*

**37. Explain why a bundle without an FSC label on an FSC yard is treated as a warehouseman's mistake and escalated.**

*(Mixing certified wood with non-certified wood on the storage yard = loss of the chain of evidence = loss of the company's FSC certificate = fine of 150-300 thousand PLN + loss of customers such as IKEA/Kingfisher; the operator always STOP + escalation to the foreman, does not accept a bundle without a label.)*

### Area 3: Documentation (38-40)

**38. Why is the operator's logbook green and what does "the logbook has two audiences" mean?**

*(Green color for visual identification in the sawmill archive; "two audiences" = the white copy of the sawing order goes to the foreman : dispatcher : accounting with batch settlement; the green logbook goes to the replacement operator with operational knowledge about the machine's condition, band, faults; 25 extra characters in the entry saves the replacement 20 min of lost time.)*

**39. What four types of questions (the "I see X, I understand Y, is that correct?" pattern) does the operator use when signing off on a sawing order?**

*(Dimension and tolerance, exact species, priority during a verbal change, consistency with the raw material state; the goal is to transfer the weight of the decision from the junior to the foreman without breaking the process; paradigm "operator in the crew asks if I have understood correctly that I am to do X", junior asks "what am I supposed to do".)*

**40. Explain the role of the A6 notebook in the operator's daily work and the related "about 8" error.**

*(A6 notebook always in the jacket pocket, entries made on the spot at the workstation; transcribing into the logbook from the notebook at the end of the shift, not from memory; eliminates 90% of "about 8" type errors where the operator writes inaccurately from memory; concrete: "8 min 14 s" instead of "about 8 min" = the difference for settling a T2 downtime.)*

### Area 4: Relations and self-regulation (41-45)

**41. Explain the transparency rule for skipping a level in the EGIDA escalation chain.**

*(If the operator escalates directly from operator to shift supervisor bypassing an unavailable foreman, an immediate SMS to the foreman about the skip; goal: the foreman finds out formally, not third-hand; a skip is allowed only when level 1 is unavailable and the matter cannot wait.)*

**42. Describe the difference between a symptomatic and a diagnostic description when reporting a problem.**

*(The operator reports the SYMPTOM: "strange smell, you can hear a growl, surface crack 8 mm with a rusty coating"; the foreman or supervisor diagnoses the CAUSE: "damaged bearing / internal crack / band dulling"; diagnosis requires experience and tools that a junior operator does not have.)*

**43. What five micro-ergonomic exercises does the operator perform every 90 min (2-3 min each)?**

*(Neck rotation 8+8, wrist stretching 10+10 for both hands, shoulder circles 10+10, lateral bend 15+15, the 20-20-20 rule for the eyes; goal: prevention of carpal tunnel syndrome + dispersing muscle tension + reducing eye fatigue.)*

**44. Explain why "I can't handle it" is reported in the 4W format, not in general terms.**

*(The 4W format translates the subjective "I can't handle it" into observable facts: Marek, Andrij, saw 2, 13:30, three signs of fatigue, please rotate me or give me a 10 min micro-break; the foreman gets a basis for an operational decision without negotiation; protection of the operator against being interpreted as "lazy".)*

**45. Describe five differences between an operator in the crew and a junior under supervision (synthesis of M1 vs M2).**

*(1. The junior asks "what am I to do", the operator asks "have I understood X correctly"; 2. The junior observes, the operator proposes an understanding; 3. The junior is under constant supervision, the operator is accountable for the result; 4. The junior does not sign documents, the operator signs off on the sawing order and the logbook; 5. The junior observes their own body, the operator also monitors the fatigue of colleagues in the crew.)*

## Part C: cases (10 x 3 pts = 30 pts)

**Read the situation description and answer in 4-6 sentences: (1) what you do immediately, (2) which regulation or M2 rule is activated, (3) whom and how you inform.**

### Area 1: Material knowledge (46-47)

**46. Case: oak bundle without a label on the regular yard**

Monday 6:45. You walk onto the yard and see a bundle of 60 oak boards marked only with tape "DĄB 50x200", without an SO-DĄB-number code or FSC marking, on the regular yard (not FSC). The earlier sawing order stated that the customer requires FSC. Foreman Marek is in a briefing until 7:15. Your reaction?

*(Expected answer: STOP, I will not start work with this bundle; the rule "certification without proof = no certification" + FSC substitution not allowed for customers such as IKEA; call to Marek despite the briefing + 4W SMS "foreman, saw 2, 6:45, oak bundle without FSC label on the regular yard, STOP, please verify with the warehouseman"; entry in the logbook as an event; the bundle goes into quarantine until clarified with the warehouseman and the certifier.)*

**47. Case: grading class discrepancy**

Wednesday 11:00. You are grading 24 pine boards after saw 2. The moisture measurement shows an average of 11.2%, spread 0.6%. But 4 boards have surface cracks of 80 mm/lm (class A threshold = 50 mm/lm) and 2 boards with cracked knots. You have a sawing order for 24 pcs. of class A. What do you do?

*(Reclassify 6 boards to class B, label them SO-B-datestamp-numbers, the remaining 18 boards stay class A; entry in the logbook with the count of B and A; call to Marek in 4W format "SO-pine grading, 11:00, 18A + 6B instead of 24A, difference purely visual not moisture-based, please approve"; defense documentation for the customer if they receive less A than ordered; TartakERP update after the end of the shift.)*

### Area 2: Machines under supervision (48-50)

**48. Case: ammeter in the yellow zone**

Tuesday 9:20. You are cutting the third spruce beam 100x200x4000. The ammeter on saw 2 starts regularly entering the yellow zone on every cut (earlier it was in the green). Band number 11, has made about 80 cuts today. Foreman Marek is in the office. Your reaction?

*(STOP at the end of the current cut, not mid-cut; observe whether the ammeter returns to green after 30 s at idle; report 4W to Marek by phone "saw 2, 9:20, ammeter regularly in the yellow on every cut, band 11 after 80 cuts, probable dulling, please decide replacement or continuation"; entry in the logbook band table "band 11 ~80 cuts dulling"; I do not replace the band on my own because that is not my competence as a junior operator.)*

**49. Case: internal crack in a beam**

Friday 9:15. You are sawing the fourth oak beam 50x200x3000 from the bundle SO-DĄB-FSC-210420-004. In beam 4 you see an internal crack 8x3 mm with a rusty coating (oxidation, the crack existed previously). Marek is unavailable until 10:00. What do you do?

*(STOP immediately, I set the beam aside in quarantine, I do not continue through the next beams to avoid propagation of losses; urgency classification IMMEDIATELY because losses grow with every beam; call to Piotr Czerwiński the shift supervisor with a level skip + SMS to Marek about the skip; symptomatic description not diagnostic: "internal crack 8x3 mm with rusty coating", not "hidden supplier defect"; bundle reclassified to quarantine, I take SO-DĄB-FSC-002 from the yard.)*

**50. Case: first day of loading into the kiln**

Tuesday 7:30, drying chamber, Nardi chamber 2. Zbigniew Majchrzak is showing you the geometry of a pine stack. You are loading the bundle SO-SW-A-260420-015, but on boards number 18-20 you see that the vertical columns of spacer sticks have shifted by 40 mm relative to the columns below. What do you do?

*(I do not close the chamber door, I stop the stacking, I dismantle the last 3 layers and rebuild them with the correct columns; the rule "vertical columns of spacer sticks cannot be shifted, shift = board deformation during the cycle"; I inform Zbigniew: "Zbyszek, I see a 40 mm shift from layer 18, I am dismantling and rebuilding, it will take 15 min"; entry in the loading geometry report; mantra "I load, I close, I report, I do NOT program cycles".)*

### Area 3: Documentation (51-52)

**51. Case: verbal priority change**

Monday 9:30. Sawing order DP-2026-048 for 45 pine beams for the customer Dębowy Dom. At 9:30 Marek calls and says verbally: "change priority B to A, the export customer called, we need to ship 30 today". The sawing order on the desk has priority B. What do you do?

*(I do not change the priority field on my own; I ask Marek to come to the workstation and update with a pencil next to field 9 with his initials "M.N. 9:30 change B->A, 30 today"; I cite the PIP statistic 4% of accidents = verbal instruction inconsistent with the raw material state; entry in the logbook "9:30 verbal priority change B->A by Marek, order updated in pencil at 9:35"; the "I see, I understand, is that correct" pattern = "I understand that I am to cut 30 today priority A, is that correct".)*

**52. Case: missing item in the logbook**

Friday 13:45, end of shift. You are filling in the green logbook ZMI-2026-087-01. You notice that you did not record the 11-minute downtime from 10:35 (band 7 replacement, dulling). You remember the time approximately. What do you do?

*(I check the A6 notebook in my jacket pocket to see whether I noted it; if yes, the exact time "10:35-10:46, band 7 replacement after dulling, downtime type T technical, problem owner mechanic"; if I did not note it, I write the approximate time with the addition "about 10:30-10:45 +/-5 min, entry reconstructed" without faking accuracy; I report to Marek when handing over the logbook "foreman, I recorded the T2 downtime approximately, in the future I will enter it right away"; the A6 notebook habit from tomorrow.)*

### Area 4: Relations and self-regulation (53-55)

**53. Case: three signs of fatigue**

Friday 14:15, 6.5 hours into the shift. You notice in yourself: finger tremor at the pusher (paper test confirms), squinting eyes at the ammeter (you cannot see fine values from 2 m), slowed reaction when writing in the notebook. Marek is on the far end of the hall. What do you do?

*(Rule of three signals activated: three = mandatory micro-break or 4W report; STOP at the end of the current cut; report to Marek by gesture within sight or 4W by phone: "Marek, Andrij, saw 2, 14:15, three signs of fatigue tremor-squinting-slowdown, please rotate me to the logbook or give me a 10 min micro-break"; foreman's decision: rotation or a micro-break in the break room; entry in the logbook as evidence of good self-regulation practice.)*

**54. Case: forklift operator without eye contact**

Thursday 11:45. You are receiving the bundle SO-FSC-260420-020 from Krzysztof Zagrodny on the FSC yard. Krzysztof is maneuvering the Toyota forklift, but is not looking at you, the bundle is already 2 m from your position. You have not made the READY gesture because you are not ready. What do you do?

*(Step back out of the 3 m maneuvering zone; loud shout "Krzysiek STOP, STOP!" + STOP gesture palm above the head; eye contact with the forklift operator absolutely mandatory before lowering the bundle; I cite the PIP statistic 2019-2023: 48% of forklift + operator accidents = hitting a standing operator; report to Marek in 4W format after the incident: "Marek, Andrij, FSC yard, 11:45, Krzysztof lowered the bundle without my READY gesture and without eye contact, please hold a calibration conversation"; entry in the logbook as a near-miss.)*

**55. Case: escalation straight to Anna Kwiatkowska**

Monday 7:40, 5 min before the start. You see that in drying chamber number 3 the control panel shows an alarm "temperature 82°C in the chamber with oak", Zbigniew Majchrzak has not yet arrived, Marek is in a briefing until 7:55, Piotr Czerwiński is on a business trip. Production manager Anna Kwiatkowska is in her office, 30 m away. What do you do?

*(Urgency classification IMMEDIATELY: oak temperature 82°C >> limit 60°C = risk of medullary ray cracks + loss of the entire batch of 15-25 m³ of oak in the cycle; skipping 3 levels (foreman + shift supervisor + kiln supervisor) up to the production manager is allowed because all are unavailable and the matter cannot wait; I run 30 m to Anna's office with the 4W format: "manager, Andrij, kiln chamber 3, 7:40, alarm 82°C in oak cycle, limit 60°C, Zbigniew and Marek unavailable, please intervene immediately"; Anna decides to shut down the chamber; immediate SMS to Marek and Zbigniew about the 3-level skip; entry in the logbook with a full timeline.)*
