## Introduction

Sixth lesson of Week 3. So far you've learned to **move around machines**: how to feed the operator, how to receive, how to stack. Today **the first lesson where you use tools yourself**: but ones that **have no motor**.

Hand tools are **your world** during the first 3–6 months of work. Without UDT authorisation and without workplace training, **you don't touch machines** (you remember from l1: "I help, but I don't operate"). But **a hand saw, a plane, a measuring rule, a wedge, a mallet**: **you can and must** know how to use. They **have no plug**, **have no motor**, are **within your competence from day one**.

Why are hand tools **still important** in 2026, when there are machines?

1. **Precision for small corrections.** A board that **almost fits** needs 2–3 cm trimming off. Instead of starting the saw for 2 cm, you take a hand saw. 30 seconds, without machine PPE, without dust, without risk.

2. **Access in difficult places.** A board stuck in a stack at 2 m height. You won't dismantle the whole stack for it. **Hand saw + plane** and you work it **on the spot**.

3. **Places without power.** A yard behind the sawmill, in winter, no cable. Hand tools **always work**.

4. **Quality control without a machine.** The operator cuts fast. You **check with the rule**, **mark with a pencil**, **fit by hand**: you catch errors before they reach the customer.

5. **A lifetime skill.** Machines change, fashion for a saw type passes. **The hand saw, plane and rule** have been unchanged for **200 years**. Learn once, you know for life.

In this lesson you'll meet the **four basic tools** (hand saw, hand plane, rule/try-square, wedge + mallet), the **technique of each**, the **five most common injuries** (splinter, cut, hammer-on-finger, burns, back strain) and the **"hand on the other side" rule**: the most important OHS rule of hand tools.

## Objectives

After this lesson:

- You know the **four basic tools** of the helper: **hand saw (cross-cut and rip)**, **hand plane**, **rule / try-square**, **wooden wedge + mallet**.
- You understand the **"hand on the other side" rule**: the hand holding the wood always **on the other side** of the cut line from the hand with the tool.
- You know how to **hold the wood** for work: **bench clamps**, **vice**, **knee + bench**, **hand + a second helper**.
- You can **tell a cross-cut saw (for cutting across the grain) from a rip saw** (for cutting along) and you know why the wrong saw = **burnt blade** + fatigue.
- You know the **five typical injuries** with hand tools and **how to prevent them**.

## Content

### Four basic tools

**1. Hand saw (cross-cut and rip).**

A hand saw for wood has a **specialised tooth**. There are **two main types**:

- **Cross-cut saw**: **angled teeth**, cutting **fibres across**. Used when you cut **short along the length** (e.g. cutting off 20 cm from the end of a board). Teeth on **both sides** (cuts in both directions of the stroke). **Typically 7–10 teeth per inch**.
- **Rip saw**: **straight teeth**, cutting **fibres lengthwise**. Used when you **split** a board **along** (e.g. a thin strip from a thick board). **4–6 teeth per inch** (fewer teeth = deeper cut per stroke).

**Key rule**: **the wrong saw for the cut direction = fast fatigue + burnt blade**. If you cut along the grain with a cross-cut saw, **60% more effort**, and the saw **binds**. Always **look at the grain direction** before choosing the saw.

**2. Hand plane.**

A hand plane is a **wooden box** with a steel blade sticking out of the bottom. It serves to **smooth** a board's surface (remove unevenness, splinters after sawing, square the corner).

**Two main types**:
- **Fore plane**: long (40–50 cm), thick blade, **fast removal** of material.
- **Smoothing plane**: short (20–25 cm), thin blade, **finishing** the surface.

**Technique**: you push the plane **forward, along the grain**, pressure **even**. Do NOT drag backwards (the blade dulls). Do NOT pull across (you'll tear the grain). You stand **perpendicular** to the board, the other hand **holds the board or the plane end**.

**3. Rule / try-square.**

Measuring tools. **The most important**:
- **Folding rule** (wooden "cal" rule), classic, folded from 10 segments of 20 cm. Resistant to moisture, doesn't snap like a metal one. **Standard in the sawmill**.
- **Steel tape measure**: faster for long distances (>3 m), but less durable.
- **Try square**: steel right angle 90°. For checking **whether the board's corners are square**. Indispensable for marking.
- **Carpenter's pencil**: flat, thick, durable. Draws a clear line. Never use an ordinary office pencil (snaps).

**Technique**: **always measure twice**, cut once. "Measure twice, cut once", it's the **oldest carpentry rule**. A 2 mm measuring error sometimes costs **a whole board**, and in furniture making, **the whole project**.

**4. Wooden wedge + mallet.**

A wedge is a **simple wooden triangle**, 15–40 cm long. The mallet, most often a **wooden mallet** (**kijanka** in Polish, a carpenter's mallet), because **a metal one breaks the wood**.

You use the wedge + mallet:
- For **splitting** wood along the grain (e.g. splitting a log into two parts).
- For **prying apart a stuck board** in a stack.
- For **wedging** a log on the yard (from l3).
- For **clamping** a board in a vice (a wedge pushes to the jaw).

**Technique**: you place the wedge **edge along the grain**, strike with the mallet **evenly**, **gradually**. Don't strike **with full force**: the wedge **shoots out** or **splits the wood in an unplanned place**.

### The "hand on the other side" rule

The **most important OHS rule** of hand tools. **Simple as a stone**:

> **The hand holding the wood, always on the other side of the cut line from the hand with the tool.**

If you saw a board **with your right hand**: the board is clamped (vice, clamp) or **held with the left hand on the left side** of the cut line. If the saw **slips**: it hits **empty space** or **the held piece of wood**, NOT **fingers**.

**Why?** Because the **most common injury** with a hand saw is a **slip**: the saw runs in the **kerf**, hits a knot, **jumps sideways**. If your fingers are **on the cut line** or **on the same side as the saw**: **cut**. If they are **on the other side**: the saw hits **wood**, **safe**.

::: warning
**Don't hold the board "close to the cut line"**: minimum **10 cm** from the saw teeth. For the plane, **15 cm from the blade**. For the wedge, **never a hand on the wedge line**, always to the side.
:::

### Clamping the wood

Wood **isn't still by itself**. If it isn't clamped, it moves, the **tool slips**, **fingers cut**. Clamping methods, from most safe to least:

**1. Bench vice** (workbench vice). **The safest**. The board is pressed into the jaws, **doesn't move**. Both hands free. Use it **always** when you work pieces **up to 1 m**.

**2. Bench clamps** ("spring" or "F-clamps"). They fix the board to the **edge of the bench**. **The other hand** is free, holds the tool. Great for longer boards (1–3 m).

**3. Knee + bench** (working "on the knee"). You sit on a stool, the board on your knee, the helping hand holds it. **Limited to thin boards** (1–2 cm thick) and **short cuts** (up to 30 cm).

**4. Hand + second helper.** One person holds the board, the other works with the tool. **Teamwork**: but requires **synchronisation** (as with feeding the saw from l5). The person holding **stands upright**, hands **to the sides of the cut line**, never on the line.

**5. "Rough" (only as a last resort).** You're on the yard, without a vice, without a bench, without help. You clamp with your foot, **wedges on both sides**: you try to make a makeshift vice out of the ground. **Worse safety**, **you do it only when you MUST**.

### Five typical injuries

**1. Splinter.** Under a nail, in a finger, in a palm. The most common injury (50% of all cases). Source: **no gloves**, **knots in the wood**, **a hand sliding on a rough board**. Prevention: **always anti-slip gloves** (even with wood!), **avoid rough edges**, **inspect wood before work**. Treatment: **tweezers + alcohol**, if the splinter is deep, **to a doctor**.

**2. Cut from a hand saw.** Source: **slip** of the saw from the cut line (via a knot, bad angle) or **hand too close** ("other-side" rule broken). Prevention: **a sharp saw** (a dull one slips more often), **proper clamping**, **hand ALWAYS on the other side**. Treatment: pressure dressing, **112 if arterial bleeding** (pulsing).

**3. Hammer on a finger.** Source: **wrong grip** (too close to the wedge), **too hard a blow**, **the worker gets spooked**. Prevention: **first blows light**, **full strike only after the wedge is set**, **eyes on the wedge** (not on the wood!), **fingers of the other hand away from the strike point**.

**4. Burn from plane friction.** A hand plane in longer work **heats up** (friction). You cut in a glove, fine. Without a glove + 30 minutes of planing = **second-degree burn**. Prevention: **gloves ALWAYS**, breaks every 15 minutes (plane cools), **don't press** too hard.

**5. Back strain from long work in a bad posture.** Source: planing hunched over a bench for **an hour**, sawing a board **on the ground**. Prevention: **bench at waist height** (80–100 cm), **breaks every 20 minutes**, **stretching the back**. This is a **continuation of the theme from l2** (manual handling).

::: example
**PIP 2022 statistics**: 40% of hand-tool injuries in sawmills = **splinters + hand cuts** (main cause: no gloves). 20% = **spine injuries** (bad posture). 15% = **hammer on a finger**. 15% = **burns from plane/friction**. 10% = **other** (splinter in the eye, blade snap, splinter in the face).
:::

### Order of work with a hand tool: checklist

Before you start work with a hand saw, a plane or another tool, a **5-point checklist**:

1. **Wood clamped?** (vice / clamp / knee / helper). **Never "in the air"**.
2. **Hand on the other side** of the cut line? **Check with the eyes**.
3. **Tool sharp?** A blunt tool = **more force, more slips**. If the saw is dull, tell the foreman, don't work with it. If the plane has nicks, don't work with it.
4. **Anti-slip gloves?** Always.
5. **Eyes on the tool**, not elsewhere. No chatting with a mate, no looking at the phone. **Eyes on the cut line** throughout the work.

These 5 steps take **5 seconds**. They prevent **80% of injuries** with hand tools. Helpers who skip them, are **PIP statistics**.

### Tool maintenance: briefly

- **Hand saw**: after use, **wipe the blade with a cloth** (wood sap), **anti-corrosion oil** (a thin layer). **Don't leave out in the rain**.
- **Hand plane**: **replace the blade** when it starts leaving **rough surfaces**. A plane **is not a miracle**: blades dull every 5–10 hours of work.
- **Try square / rule**: **check calibration** once a month (90° angle = **yes?** compare with another angle). A rule dropped on a concrete floor = **2–5 mm accuracy lost**. Ask the foreman how to check.
- **Wedge**: throw away when it has **cracked** or is **crushed**. A beech wedge on heavy work lives **20–50 uses**.
- **Hammer/mallet**: check **the head seating** (head on the handle). A loose head = **dangerous to swing**. Change the handle (easy in 10 minutes).

**A good tool** costs 50–500 zł. **A bad tool** costs **a cut finger + 2 weeks of sick leave**. The economics are clear.

## Summary

Today you learned **to use your tool world**:

- **Four basic tools**: hand saw (cross-cut/rip), hand plane (fore/smoothing), rule + try-square + pencil, wedge + mallet.
- **"Hand on the other side" rule** of the cut line, the most important OHS rule, 80% of cuts come from breaking it.
- **Clamping the wood** (best to worst): vice > clamps > knee + bench > second helper > rough. **Never "in the air".**
- **Five injuries**: splinter (50%), cut (the bloodiest), hammer on finger, plane burn, back strain from posture.
- **5-point checklist** before every job: wood clamped, hand on the other side, tool sharp, gloves, eyes on the tool. **5 seconds = 80% safety**.
- **Maintenance**: after use clean, anti-corrosion oil, calibration check monthly, change blade when dull.

A hand tool is **an extension of your hand**. A good saw in **good technique** is **surgical precision**. A bad saw or **drunken technique** is **fingers in plaster for 5 weeks**. The difference, **only in your training**. Today you got the base. **Another 1000 hours** of work with tools = a life's skill.

## Key terms

- 🪚 **Piła ręczna poprzeczna / wzdłużna** (pl), cross-cut / rip hand saw (en), sierra de mano transversal / longitudinal (es), ручна пилка поперечна / повздовжня (uk). Two types. Cross-cut (7–10 teeth/inch) cuts across grain, rip (4–6 teeth/inch), along. Wrong saw for the direction = fatigue + burnt blade.

- ✋ **Zasada „ręka za drugą stroną"** (pl), "hand on the other side" rule (en), regla "mano al otro lado" (es), правило «рука з іншого боку» (uk). The hand holding the wood always on the other side of the cut line from the hand with the tool. 80% of cuts from breaking this rule.

- 🪓 **Strug ręczny (zgrubny / gładzik)** (pl), hand plane (fore / smoothing) (en), cepillo de mano (de desbaste / de acabado) (es), ручний рубанок (грубий / чистовий) (uk). A wooden box with a protruding blade. Smooths a board's surface. Technique: forward motion along the grain, even pressure.

- 📏 **Przymiar składany (calówka)** (pl), folding rule (en), regla plegable (es), складаний вимірювач (uk). A wooden rule of 10 segments of 20 cm. Moisture-resistant. Sawmill standard. "Measure twice, cut once."

- 📐 **Kątomierz (try square)** (pl), try square (en), escuadra (es), косинець (uk). Steel right angle 90°. Checks squareness of the board's corners. Indispensable for marking before cutting.

- 🪵 **Klin drewniany + kijanka** (pl), wedge + wooden mallet (en), cuña + mazo de madera (es), клин + дерев'яна киянка (uk). For splitting, wedging, clamping. Mallet (not a metal hammer!), doesn't break the wood.

- ✅ **5-punktowy checklist** (pl), 5-point checklist (en), checklist de 5 puntos (es), 5-пунктовий чек-лист (uk). Before every job: wood clamped, hand on the other side, tool sharp, gloves, eyes on the tool. 5 sec = 80% safety.

- 🔧 **Imadło stolarskie** (pl), bench vice (en), tornillo de banco (es), слюсарні лещата (uk). Safest wood clamp. The jaws press the board, both hands free. A workbench standard.

- 💉 **Drzazga** (pl), splinter (en), astilla (es), скалка (uk). 50% of hand-tool injuries. Prevention: gloves always. Treatment: tweezers + alcohol, deep = doctor.

- 🎯 **„Measure twice, cut once"** (pl), "measure twice, cut once" (en), "mide dos veces, corta una" (es), «виміряй двічі, ріж один раз» (uk). The oldest carpentry rule. A 2 mm measuring error = sometimes a whole board scrapped.

## Trainer's notes

**Pedagogical goal**: deliver **physical competence** with the 4 basic tools. After the lesson the learner should **be able** to cut straight with a hand saw, smooth a surface with a plane, measure precisely with a rule, drive a wedge with a mallet, **without injuries**.

**120-minute rhythm**:
- **0–10 min**: opening, "what does a hand saw remind you of?" Collect answers. Usually learners say "an old tool, we have machines now". React: **no, used daily**. Show the 4-tool set on a table.
- **10–35 min**: **demonstration + try each tool**: the trainer shows proper grip + stroke, then each learner tries **10–15 seconds** on a scrap piece. 5–10 minutes per tool.
- **35–50 min**: **"hand on the other side" rule**: **bad demo → good demo**. The trainer first **holds the wood badly** (hand close to the saw), asks "what do you see that is dangerous?". Then **well**. Learners practise in pairs: one holds, the other shows the "wooden gesture" of the saw (without real one). Check hand positions.
- **50–75 min**: **clamping the wood**: if a vice and a workbench are available, **physical exercise**: each learner clamps a board in the vice, saws a 5 cm piece off. Trainer corrects. If no workshop, a mock-up + photos/video.
- **75–95 min**: **five injuries and prevention**: each injury with an example (photo or story), the group discusses: "what was done wrong?". PIP statistic as background.
- **95–110 min**: **5-point checklist**: act it out on a scrap of wood. The learner approaches the bench, through the whole checklist **says out loud** "wood clamped? yes. Hand on the other side? yes. Tool sharp? yes. Gloves? yes. Eyes? yes. I work." Repeat until automatic.
- **110–115 min**: tool maintenance, briefly, demonstration of oiling a saw, replacing a plane blade.
- **115–120 min**: key terms, oral quiz ("which tool for cutting along the grain?"), announcement of l7 (workstation tidiness, sawdust, chips, scrap).

**Frequent learner mistake**: they hold the saw **at the tip of the handle** (weak grip) or **over the middle** (hard to control). **Properly**: the handle **in the whole hand**, thumb **on the side** (not on top), wrist **straight**.

**Practical recommendation**: introduce the **"scrap wood" rule**. Every learner in the first week of work gets 10 scrap pieces to **"practise cuts"**: without time pressure, without product quality. 20 minutes a day. After a week, **50% better** saw control.

**Link to the next lesson**: l7 is **workstation tidiness**: sawdust, chips, scrap. You use **the same tools as today** (saw, plane), but we focus on what **is left behind** and how to clear it so that **there are no slips and no fires**.
