## Content

### The scope of the PN-EN 1611-1 standard

The Polish Standard PN-EN 1611-1 (2002 edition, with 2013 amendments, Polish text approved by the Polish Committee for Standardization, PKN/TK-184) is a translation of the European standard EN 1611-1 titled "Sawn timber, appearance grading of softwoods, part 1, European spruces, firs, pines, larches and Douglas firs". The key word is **softwoods**. The standard **does not cover** hardwoods. Oak, beech, ash, alder, birch, poplar, lime and maple have their own criteria (PN-EN 975-1 for hardwoods, or the sawmill's internal criteria aligned with a given client specification).

The species covered by PN-EN 1611-1 are: Norway spruce (*Picea abies*), Sitka spruce (*Picea sitchensis*, imported), Scots pine (*Pinus sylvestris*, the main Polish species), black pine (*Pinus nigra*), silver fir (*Abies alba*), grand fir (*Abies grandis*), European larch (*Larix decidua*), Douglas fir (*Pseudotsuga menziesii*, imported).

In practice in a Polish sawmill **95% of the standard's use** concerns spruce and pine (together 85% of Polish softwood production). Fir and larch are rarer (5 to 7% each). Douglas fir is very rare (experimental plantations in the forest districts of Wielkopolska).

::: info
Why separate standards for softwoods and hardwoods? Because they have **entirely different anatomical structures** and **different types of characteristic defects**. Softwoods have clearly defined annual rings (light spring growth, dark summer growth), regular knots, typical splits along the grain. Hardwoods have a more complex structure (water vessels, fibres, rays), different knot types, different defects (pith cracks running across, tylose stains, mineral streaks). The PN-EN 1611-1 standard uses the language of defects typical of softwoods; that language fails for oak or beech. Hence the separate standards.
:::

### The four G2 grades under PN-EN 1611-1

The "G2" mark in the standard stands for **General grade 2**, that is, grading based on **two visible faces of the board** (left and right face). The third side (edge) and the fourth (lower face) are assessed more leniently. There are also the G4 marks (four sides evaluated equally, i.e., cladding boards or structural boards with a full view) and S (Structural, strength grading, but that is the domain of PN-EN 14081, covered in lesson 3).

The four G2 grades differ in the tolerance of defects:

**G2-0 (highest grade, furniture, visible window joinery):**
- Single sound knot: maximum diameter of 10 mm (about 1/15 of a 150 mm board width).
- Dead knots: not permitted.
- End splits: up to 3 mm wide, up to 50 mm long.
- Face splits: not permitted in the visible zone.
- Wane: not permitted (the edge of the board must be full).
- Longitudinal curvature *sweep*: maximum 4 mm over 2 m length (0.2%).
- Edge curvature *spring*: 4 mm over 2 m.
- *Twist*: 1 mm per 25 mm of board width.
- Blue stain and mould: not permitted.
- Resin streaks: small, up to 10 mm long, few (max 1 per 1 m).

Grade G2-0 is about **3 to 5% of production in a Polish sawmill**. It is used for window frames, entrance doors with glazing, visible elements of garden furniture. The price is 50 to 80% higher than G2-2.

**G2-1 (high grade, flooring, visible construction joinery):**
- Sound knots: up to 20 mm, single or clustered (max 2 per 1 m).
- Dead knots: single pieces up to 10 mm permitted (max 1 per 1 m).
- End splits: up to 5 mm wide, up to 100 mm long.
- Visible face splits: up to 2 mm wide, up to 500 mm long, max 1 per board.
- Wane: permitted up to 10 mm wide by 100 mm long on the edge.
- Longitudinal curvature *sweep*: 6 mm over 2 m.
- Blue stain: not permitted.
- Mould: a single small spot up to 100 mm² is permitted.
- Resin streaks: up to 20 mm, max 3 per 1 m.

Grade G2-1 is **15 to 20% of production**. Pine flooring boards, economy windows, painted finishing strips (the paint hides small defects).

**G2-2 (medium grade, skirting, visible construction):**
- Sound knots: up to 40 mm.
- Dead knots: up to 20 mm, max 2 per 1 m.
- End splits: up to 10 mm wide, up to 200 mm long.
- Face splits: up to 3 mm wide, up to 800 mm long, max 2 per board.
- Wane: up to 20 mm by 300 mm.
- Longitudinal curvature: 10 mm over 2 m.
- Blue stain: permitted on the surface, not deep (up to 2 mm in depth).
- Mould: up to 5% of the surface.
- Resin streaks: no quantitative limit, a single streak up to 40 mm.

Grade G2-2 is the **largest segment, about 45 to 50% of production**. Skirting, terrace decking, visible construction elements (roof trusses with exposed beams).

**G2-3 (lowest grade, hidden construction, sheathing):**
- Sound knots: up to 60 mm or 1/3 of the board's width.
- Dead knots: up to 40 mm.
- Splits: practically without limit (as long as the board holds in one piece).
- Wane: up to 30 mm by 500 mm.
- Curvature: up to 15 mm over 2 m.
- Blue stain, mould: permitted widely.
- Resin streaks: no limit.

Grade G2-3 is **20 to 25% of production**. Roof sheathing (under tiles), battens, partition walls inside dividing structures, elements of non-export pallets.

**Below G2-3**: the timber is not graded under PN-EN 1611-1, it goes to the **firewood assortment, pallets, sawdust**. In EGIDA documentation the entry is "below G2-3" or "reject".

::: tip
Commercial grading in Poland often uses **trade names** instead of G2-X codes: "class I" = G2-0, "class II" = G2-1, "class III" = G2-2, "class IV" = G2-3, "reject" = below G2-3. The construction client says "I want class III", the grader and operator understand G2-2. Remember: the trade name is binding on the invoice, the code of the standard is binding in a claim. In the internal document (KLA-002, the order specification) you write down **both** ("class III / G2-2").
:::

### The five main visual criteria

**Criterion 1: knots.**

A knot is the end of a branch in the trunk which, after cutting, shows on the board as a round or oval zone with a different grain structure. The grader assesses:

- **Knot diameter:** measured with a calliper in two directions, with the arithmetic mean taken. A round knot gives one clear result. An oval knot gives two different measurements; we take the larger one.
- **Knot condition:** sound (integrated with the surrounding tissue, holding in the wood, not letting air through), dead (separated, pulls out, leaves a hole), decayed (fungal breakdown, soft, dark brown).
- **The number of knots** per 1 m of board length (criterion for G2-1 and below).
- **A group of knots** (knots less than 150 mm apart treated as a single group with a combined diameter).

The assistant operator measures knot after knot with the calliper and dictates the measurements to the grader ("knot 1: 22 mm, sound"; "knot 2: 8 mm, sound"; "knot 3: 32 mm, dead"). The grader notes them on the form.

**Criterion 2: splits.**

A distinction is drawn between:
- **End splits** (*checks*): transverse, on the end of the board, typically from drying.
- **Visible face splits** (*splits*): parallel to the grain, open.
- **Ring shakes** (*shake*): rare, along the spiral of the grain, a sign of wood ageing.

Measurement: length (steel tape), width (calliper, at the widest point).

**Criterion 3: wane** (*oblina* in Polish, *wane* in English).

Wane is a remnant of the natural bark surface on the edge of a board, arising when the board comes from the outer part of the log and was not edged to a full rectangular cross-section. It looks like a rounded edge with a visible sapwood structure or bark.

Measurement: length along the edge of the board, width at the widest point (from the edge of the board to the line where the full cross-section begins).

Wane is assessed separately for each edge. Its permissibility depends on the grade and function of the board (a flooring board means wane is almost never permitted; a hidden structural board means wane is permitted).

**Criterion 4: curvature.**

Three types:
- **Sweep** (longitudinal bow, *longitudinal bow*): the board is bowed along its length, with the maximum deflection usually in the middle. Measurement: the board is laid flat on the table, we measure the maximum gap between the lower face and the table surface in the middle.
- **Spring** (edge bow, *edge bow*, "sabre"): the board is bent sideways (the edge is not symmetrical). Measurement: the board is laid on its side, we measure the gap at the edge.
- **Twist** (twist, *twist*, "propeller"): the board is twisted about its own axis (one end up, the other down). Measurement: the board is laid on the table, one end pressed down, we measure the lift of the other end.

All three dimensions are measured **after drying** to the target moisture content (otherwise the curvature will change after drying; grading of freshly cut timber is preliminary, the final grade is set after the kiln).

**Criterion 5: biological defects.**

- **Blue stain** (*blue stain*): from l1, blueish-grey fungal streaks in the sapwood. Measurement: % of the board surface affected by staining.
- **Mould**: spot or extensive stains of colour (black, yellow, greenish). Measurement: % of surface plus depth (surface vs within the wood).
- **Decay**: soft, decomposing zones. Usually disqualifies a board from grades G2-0, G2-1, G2-2.
- **Resin** (*resin*): a transparent or amber substance filling streaks or knots. The grader evaluates the size of individual streaks plus the count per 1 m.

### The role of the operator assistant, what is allowed and what is not

**Allowed:**
- Measuring all defects (knots, splits, wane, curvature, stains) with the tape and the calliper.
- Noting the measurements down in a work notebook.
- Writing them on the grader's form (under dictation by the grader, with her signature at the end).
- Placing boards on the stack of the grade indicated by the grader.
- Asking for justification of every decision (the grader has the duty to explain, because assistance is also an educational process).
- Proposing a grade as you see it ("I think this is G2-2 because of the dead knot"), as an exercise, but the grader **takes the final decision**.

**Not allowed:**
- Signing off a batch's classification (a grader's signature requires a certificate; an operator without a certificate who signs = forgery of documentation plus exposing the firm to an undefended claim).
- Issuing a WZ dispatch document with a declared grade without the grader's signature under the assigned grade.
- Changing the grade of a board already signed off by the grader (if you see an error, you ask; the grader decides on the correction).
- Grading a batch on your own when the grader is not available (e.g., on holiday, sick). In that case the batch **waits**, or it is graded on a substitute basis by a grader from a neighbouring sawmill (EGIDA has agreements with three sawmills in the region on grading substitution).

::: warning
In 2022 at a sawmill near Bydgoszcz a self-reliant operator signed off the grading of 80 m³ of spruce boards while the grader was off sick. The client in Germany took the delivery and after two weeks lodged a claim (30% of the boards in a lower grade than declared). The sawmill lost the court case, because under Polish law and the PN-EN 1611-1 standard grading without a certificate is **legally invalid** (a document with an invalid signature = no document). Return of 80 m³ (about 90,000 PLN) plus transport costs (20,000 PLN) plus a contractual penalty (30,000 PLN) plus loss of the client. The operator was dismissed for disciplinary reasons. That is the real consequence of crossing the line "I assist, I do not grade".
:::

### The path to the certificate: when an operator can become a grader

The Polish certification path for graders of softwood sawn timber is run by the **Wood Technology Institute in Poznań** (ITD), an entity accredited by the Polish Centre for Accreditation (PCA) in line with PN-EN ISO/IEC 17024. The course runs for 5 days (40 hours of theory and practice) and covers:

- Knowledge of the standards PN-EN 1611-1 and PN-EN 14081 (practical, with exercises).
- Recognition of softwood defects (species theory: spruce, pine, fir, larch).
- Measurement and interpretation of defects (practice with the calliper, the tape, measuring instruments).
- Documentation of grading (forms, records, signatures).
- Final exam: 100 reference boards to grade in 3 hours, minimum result 85% agreement with the expert's assessment.

Entry conditions: at least 3 years of sawmill work in direct contact with sawn timber, 6 months of grader assistance documented in the employer's records (a signed certificate), an entrance exam on the basics of wood anatomy (30-question test, pass mark 70%). Cost: 3,500 PLN net plus accommodation (EGIDA funds it under staff development, provided the operator declares the intention at least a year before the planned course).

The certificate is valid for **5 years**, after which a refresher course is required (2 days). EGIDA's graders refresh every 4 years, with a safety margin.

Mykoła is currently in the first year of self-reliant work. If he decides on the grader's path, he will be able to take the course at the earliest in 2029 (after three years of experience plus six months of assistance). He would obtain the certificate around 2029-2030.

::: tip
At EGIDA six workers hold the visual grading certificate: Ms. Ania (lead, 12 years), Ms. Grażyna (9 years), Mr. Mirosław (retired, working as a reserve), three operators from M3 after training in the years 2021 to 2024. Weekly rotation: Ania four days, Grażyna two days, Mirosław as a substitute. Grading covers 5 shifts per week. If Mykoła obtains the certificate in 2030 he will become the seventh grader, which will allow EGIDA to add a further grading shift (current capacity 60 m³ per day, with a seventh person 75 m³). The decision to invest in an employee's certification is directly convertible to an increase in throughput.
:::

### The pace and rhythm of assistance: 25 boards per hour

Ms. Ania with Mykoła grades the spruce from Drawno at the following pace:

- **First board:** 5 minutes (Mykoła is learning, Ms. Ania explains each criterion, together they measure slowly).
- **Boards 2 to 10:** 4 minutes per board (Mykoła is still learning, but faster).
- **Boards 11 to 30:** 3 minutes per board (Mykoła measures fluently now, Ms. Ania decides quickly).
- **Boards 31 to 60:** 2.5 minutes per board (rhythm set, few questions).

On average 2.5 to 3 minutes per board = 20 to 24 boards per hour. Ms. Ania without assistance would do 15 boards per hour (she has to measure, note and stack on her own). A difference of 60% in favour of the pair.

Over a 60-board batch (about 3 hours of work):
- Grade G2-0: 2 boards (3%)
- Grade G2-1: 12 boards (20%)
- Grade G2-2: 30 boards (50%)
- Grade G2-3: 14 boards (23%)
- Reject (below G2-3): 2 boards (3%)

A typical distribution for a delivery from the Drawno Forest District (good growth conditions for spruce, lower mould pressure than in Pomorze).

### What Mykoła notes down and what remains on the card

In his notebook Mykoła records each board: number, grade, 2 to 3 justifications covering the most important defects. After 60 boards (13:30) Ms. Ania copies the data from Mykoła's notebook onto the **EGIDA KLW-001** form (the visual grading card), one form for the whole batch. The form contains:

- **Batch data**: number, delivery, species, number of boards, standard dimensions.
- **Grade distribution**: number of boards in each grade plus percentage.
- **Notes**: the most significant issues (e.g., "increased blue staining in the sapwood due to long log storage before cutting, 3 weeks in April").
- **Grader data**: given name, family name, certificate number, validity date.
- **Assistant operator data**: given name, family name, role ("measurement and documentation assistance").
- **Signatures**: grader (mandatory), assistant (mandatory as confirmation of participation), foreman (mandatory as acceptance of the batch for release).

The KLW-001 card goes into the batch file (plus a scan into the ERP system), 5-year retention, available in the event of a client claim.

### Summary of lesson two

The PN-EN 1611-1 standard grades softwoods visually (spruce, pine, fir, larch, Douglas fir). Hardwoods have separate standards (PN-EN 975-1). Four grades: G2-0 (highest, furniture), G2-1 (high-class joinery), G2-2 (most common in Poland, 45 to 50%), G2-3 (hidden construction, sheathing). Below G2-3 = reject, firewood assortment.

Five visual criteria: knots (size, condition, count), splits (length, width, type), wane (length by width), curvature (*sweep*, *spring*, *twist*), biological defects (blue stain, mould, resin, decay).

The role of the assistant operator: measures, notes, asks, learns. **Does not sign**, does not grade without a certificate, does not release a batch without the grader. The pace with assistance is 25 boards per hour, without assistance 15, a difference of 60%.

The path to the PN-EN 1611-1 certificate: a 5-day course at ITD Poznań, an exam, entry condition of 3 years' experience plus 6 months of assistance. Valid for 5 years. Cost 3,500 PLN net, funded by EGIDA for interested employees.

The KLW-001 card signed by Ms. Ania, Mykoła, and Marek. The first independent grade proposal (board 58, G2-3) matched the grader's.
