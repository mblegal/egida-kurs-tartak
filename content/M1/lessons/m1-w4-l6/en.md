## Introduction

In l5 you learned about the **4 documents** at your workstation. Workstation card, attendance log, minor-incident log, tool-use authorization. These are **forms**, the **place** where you record information.

But the form alone is not enough. **You also need to know how and to whom to report**. Because reporting is a **procedure**, not just an entry. A procedure that has:
- **an order** (who first, who next, when the State Labour Inspectorate)
- **deadlines** (immediately, within 24 hours, within 14 days)
- **a form** (oral, written, by phone, by e-mail)
- **consequences** (what happens after your report, who makes the decision, what you get back as an answer)

In this lesson you will learn the **full reporting procedure** in a sawmill. From an oral „attention, something happened" thrown to the foreman, up to an entry in the log that may become evidence in court or in a dispute with the insurer.

Reports fall into **4 categories**:

1. **A cut or other injury to yourself or a colleague** (bleeding, bruising, splinter in the eye, burn, fainting).
2. **A machine or tool failure** (the saw stopped, the blade came off, the belt snapped, the emergency switch does not work).
3. **A hazard that has not yet materialized** (a damaged stack, a missing guard, spilled oil, a sparking cable).
4. **A near-miss** (an accident narrowly avoided: a log flew past, the blade broke a moment after being switched off, the forklift turned at the last second).

For each category the procedure is a little different. But the **common foundation**: **do not hide, do not gloss over, do not pretend it was nothing**. Reporting is a **legal duty** (Labour Code art. 211 and 234) and a **professional reflex**.

In this lesson we close the triangle of written communication: **the form** (l5) → **the reporting procedure** (l6) → **case closure**. And within the whole week we close the organizational arc of Week 4: **macro** (l1 daily rhythm) → **micro** (l2 workstation) → **non-verbal** (l3 gestures) → **verbal** (l4 words) → **written** (l5 documents) → **procedural** (l6 reporting). Still to come: **recap** (l7) and **test** (l8). But those are the next lessons.

## Objectives

After this lesson:

- You understand that **reporting is a procedure**, not just an entry. It has an order, deadlines, a form and consequences.
- You know the **4 categories of reports**: injury, failure, hazard, near-miss. You know **what procedure to apply for each**.
- You know the **escalation pyramid**: foreman → shift manager → OHS specialist → employer → State Labour Inspectorate / prosecutor. You know **when each level kicks in**.
- You know the **deadlines**: 0 min (oral, immediately), 15 min (medical help), 60 min (informing the shift manager), 24 h (entry in the log), 14 days (accident investigation), 5 years (archive).
- You understand the **legal basis of the reporting duty**: **Labour Code art. 211 pt 6** (employee) + **art. 234** (employer).
- You know the **3 myths that block reporting** („I do not want to get my colleague in trouble", „it is trivial, not worth it", „I will be punished for reporting"), and you know how to debunk them.
- You can formulate an **oral report** using Polish words from l4 („uwaga, something happened") and a **written report** following the template from l5.
- You understand that **hiding a minor incident is the cause of a major accident** (Heinrich statistic: 1 serious = 30 minor = 300 near-misses = 3 000 unsafe behaviours).

## Content

### The legal duty to report

Reporting is not „optional". It is a **legal duty**.

**Labour Code art. 211 pt 6**: „An employee is obliged (...) to notify the supervisor without delay of an observed accident at the workplace or of a danger to human life or health, and to warn co-workers and other persons in the hazard area of the danger threatening them".

Translating from legalese into normal speech:
- **„without delay"** = immediately, not after the break, not at the end of the shift, not „when I have time".
- **„the supervisor"** = the foreman. If he is not present: the shift manager. If he is absent too: the OHS specialist. If none of these three are available: you dial 112 yourself.
- **„an accident or a danger"** = both: the fact (something has happened) AND the risk (something may happen).
- **„warn co-workers"** = a shout „UWAGA!" (from l4), a STOP gesture (from l3), if the danger is immediate.

**Labour Code art. 234 § 1**: „In case of a work accident, the employer is obliged to take the necessary action to eliminate or limit the danger, to ensure first aid is given to the injured, to establish the circumstances and causes of the accident in the prescribed manner, and to apply appropriate measures preventing similar accidents".

You as a helper **are not responsible for these actions** (the employer is, through the foreman). But **your report triggers this obligation**. Without your report the employer does not even know something happened and has nothing to trigger.

::: warning
**Hiding an accident is punishable**. Penal Code art. 221: „Whoever, contrary to duty, fails to notify the competent authority in due time about a work accident or an occupational disease (...) is liable to a fine of up to 180 daily units or to restriction of liberty". This mainly concerns the employer. But if an employee deliberately hides an accident (e.g. to cover his fault), he may also get a disciplinary penalty, up to termination of the contract. For a migrant: loss of work permit.
:::

### The escalation pyramid: 5 levels

A report travels up the hierarchy, **always starting at the lowest level** (foreman) and **escalating only when needed**.

**Level 1: foreman** (present on the shift)
- Always to him **first**.
- Form: oral, face to face. Short, factual, without emotion.
- Timing: **immediately** (0 minutes) after noticing.
- Handles: 80% of all reports (minor cuts, failures, hazards).

**Level 2: shift manager** (when the foreman is absent or the matter is beyond him)
- When: the foreman is not present, the foreman deemed the matter too serious, the foreman refused to report (and you see it should be reported).
- Form: oral + by phone (internal extension or mobile).
- Timing: **within 15–60 minutes**.
- Handles: more serious failures, accidents with casualties.

**Level 3: OHS specialist** (in-house or external)
- When: an accident with injuries above minor (blood, stitches, bone, fainting), a repeating failure of the same part, a systemic hazard (multiple stations).
- Form: a written report (form) + a conversation.
- Timing: **within 24 hours**.
- Handles: accident investigations, workstation card updates, risk analyses.

**Level 4: employer (management / owner)** (serious, fatal, collective accidents)
- When: a serious accident (loss of limb, permanent health damage), a collective accident (2+ people), a fatal accident.
- Form: written notification + investigation documentation.
- Timing: **within 14 days** (serious) or **immediately** (fatal).
- Handles: reporting to ZUS, the insurer, production suspension decisions.

**Level 5: State Labour Inspectorate (PIP) and prosecutor** (mandatory for serious / fatal accidents)
- The **employer** reports, not you. But **any employee** has the right to report anonymously.
- Form: by phone (801 002 006 anonymous) or in writing.
- Timing: fatal accident = **without delay, maximum 24 h**. Serious = within 14 days.
- Handles: public-law investigations, penalties for the employer, criminal cases.

**Your role as a helper**: you always report at **level 1** (foreman). The foreman himself escalates upward according to the weight of the case. If the foreman does not escalate and you see he should, **you go to level 2 yourself** (shift manager). That is your right and your duty.

### Category 1: cut or injury

**Step-by-step procedure**:

**Step 1 (0 min): save life**
- If you are the injured: you sit down, stop work, press the wound if it bleeds, shout „UWAGA!" or „POTRZEBUJĘ POMOCY!" (gesture from l3).
- If the injured is a colleague: you approach (carefully, not into the machine zone), check consciousness, stop the bleeding with a sterile dressing from the workstation first-aid kit, do not move the person if you suspect a spine injury.
- In both cases: **one of you dials 112** (ambulance + fire), if the injury is serious (heavy bleeding, loss of consciousness, amputation, head impact, fainting, electric shock, burn >10% of the body).

**Step 2 (1–5 min): foreman**
- Your colleague or you (if you can speak) tell the foreman: „Attention, a cut, Jan has a cut palm, right hand, bleeding under pressure, medical help needed". Facts, no speculation.
- The foreman takes over the coordination: calls the shift manager, checks the injured person, decides on the ambulance if not yet called.

**Step 3 (5–15 min): medical help**
- The ambulance arrives (time depends on the sawmill location; urban up to 15 min, rural 20–30 min).
- The foreman or an appointed **workplace first-aider** (Red Cross course, first-aider card) gives first aid until the ambulance arrives.
- Witnesses (you, the colleague) **do not leave the place** until the foreman / manager gives the all-clear. Because there will be an investigation.

**Step 4 (15–60 min): informing the shift manager**
- The foreman phones the manager: „At workstation X we had a cut, Jan Kowalski, injury Y, ambulance took / did not take him, I need backup".
- The manager decides: whether to suspend production in the zone, whether to inform the OHS specialist, whether to immediately inspect the machine.

**Step 5 (within 24 h): entry in the minor-incident log (or accident report form)**
- Minor cut (plaster, no stitches): minor-incident log (the form from l5).
- More serious (stitches, bone, hospital): **a work-accident report form** (Council of Ministers Regulation of 01.07.2009, annex no. 1). Filled in by the accident team (foreman + OHS specialist + employee representative), NOT by you alone. But **you must give a statement** as a witness or as the injured person.

**Step 6 (2–14 days): accident investigation**
- The accident team gathers evidence: photographs, a diagram of the accident, witness statements, OHS specialist opinion, technical report on the machine.
- Purpose: to establish **why** the accident happened and **what to do so it does not happen again**.
- You have a duty to testify. You have the right to an **assistant** (co-worker, lawyer, trade-union representative).

**Step 7 (archive, 5 years)**
- The accident protocol stays in the plant archive for **5 years** (Minister of Labour Regulation of 10.12.2018 on employee documentation). If years later you have a health problem linked to this accident, you can go back to this document.

::: info
**112 – when to call**. Regardless of the internal procedure, **you call 112 always** when: (1) loss of consciousness, (2) arterial bleeding (pulsing, bright red, not stopped by pressure within 5 minutes), (3) amputation of a limb or finger, (4) head impact with loss of consciousness, (5) electric shock, (6) burn >10% of the body, (7) smoke or vapor poisoning, (8) fainting lasting >30 seconds. The dispatcher decides whether to send an ambulance. You tell them: „Address: [sawmill, street, city]. Injury: [what happened]. Injured: [name or „a colleague"]. I am [name]". The dispatcher may keep you on the line until the ambulance arrives.
:::

### Category 2: machine or tool failure

**Step-by-step procedure**:

**Step 1 (0 min): switch off and secure**
- Neither you nor the operator **try to repair it yourselves**. A helper has no authorization. Even the operator, in a serious electrical or hydraulic failure, does not touch: that is work for the in-house mechanic or the external service.
- The operator switches the machine off with the emergency stop (red mushroom). The helper secures the **zone**, placing a sign „DO NOT SWITCH ON – FAULT" or yellow tape if available.

**Step 2 (1–5 min): foreman**
- The operator (or you, if the operator is absent): „Failure on PT-02, the saw band snapped / drive belt broke / hydraulics do not hold / emergency switch does not work". Facts, no guesswork.
- The foreman decides whether to call the mechanic at once, postpone it to the next shift, or call an external service.

**Step 3 (5–60 min): diagnosis and repair**
- The in-house mechanic / maintenance specialist comes, diagnoses, repairs or classifies the part as needing replacement.
- If the repair requires a part not in stock, **the machine is out of service until delivery**. That may take 2 hours or 2 weeks.
- Meanwhile you: cleaning, other tasks from your workstation card, learning with a colleague at another station.

**Step 4 (within 24 h): entry in the minor-incident log or machine-failure log**
- Minor failure (repaired in <30 min, no material damage): minor-incident log.
- More serious failure (needs parts, downtime >1 h): **the machine-failure log** (kept by maintenance).
- The entry contains: machine, fault description, downtime, cause (if identified), corrective action, operator and mechanic signatures.

**Step 5 (maintenance report)**
- For repeating failures (the same saw 3 times a month), maintenance does a **root-cause analysis**: machine age, material quality, operating practice, need for a full overhaul. Decisions on replacement / modernization go to management.

::: example
**From life**: a helper from Ukraine, sawmill near Białystok, second week on the job. On the band saw a **ringing sound** appeared during work, „as if something was knocking". The operator did not pay attention („it is always like that, old kit"). The helper noticed that **the chassis vibrations were stronger than usual**. He reported to the foreman: „I hear an uneven sound, vibrations too strong, I think there is something with the bearings". The foreman ordered the machine switched off and called the mechanic. Diagnosis: **a cracked bearing on the band-guide wheel**. Had the bearing failed completely, the band could have broken and hit the helper (he stood 1.5 m from the machine, in the throw range). The employer ordered a new bearing for PLN 800, 3-day downtime, no accident. **Lesson**: do not dismiss unusual sounds or vibrations. A helper often **hears better** than the operator (because he stands to the side, while the operator focuses on cutting). Your report can save the operator colleague's life.
:::

### Category 3: unrealized hazard

These are situations where **nothing has happened yet**, but the conditions are such that something may happen.

**Typical hazards in a sawmill**:
- A damaged timber stack (cracked stickers, uneven arrangement, stack tilted >5°) – risk of the stack falling.
- Missing machine guard (removed for service and not refitted).
- Hydraulic oil / grease spilled on the floor – slip risk.
- A sparking electrical cable, scorched insulation, loose plugs – electric shock / fire risk.
- A leaking dust extractor (dust collects instead of being pulled out) – explosive-dust risk and long-term cancer risk.
- Blocked evacuation routes (pallets placed in the corridor, an open cabinet blocking a passage).
- A broken emergency switch (the mushroom button does not stop the machine when pressed).
- A broken fire alarm (the smoke detector blinks, the alarm does not sound).

**Procedure**:

**Step 1 (0 min): secure the zone**
- If the risk is active (e.g. a tilted stack that may fall), **do not get close**. You mark a boundary (2-3 meters from the hazard) and warn others („UWAGA! Stack on number three cracking, keep away!").
- If the risk is static (e.g. missing guard on a switched-off machine), a sign „DO NOT SWITCH ON" at the switch is enough.

**Step 2 (1–5 min): foreman**
- Orally, factually: „Foreman, stack on number three is tilting, I estimate 10 degrees, no one has been hurt, but we need to secure it". Facts, not „I think" or „maybe".

**Step 3 (5–30 min): corrective action**
- The foreman decides: strengthen the stack, restack, call the manager, close the zone.
- You help with the action if the foreman so decides and if it is an **activity on your workstation card**. If it is not, you step aside, watch, do not enter.

**Step 4 (within 24 h): entry in the log**
- A hazard that was eliminated without an accident is still logged. So that the foreman / maintenance / OHS see that this kind of problem appears, and can think through the systemic cause („why does the stack tilt? maybe the stickers are too thin, maybe the stacking procedure is not precise enough").

::: tip
**„Principle of permanent vigilance"**: look around your station at least **once per hour**. Look for things that were „different yesterday". A new crack in the guard, a spilled patch, a shifted stack, a new gap in a cable. These are **early signals**. Whoever notices them saves himself and others. Whoever does not notice them finds out only after the accident.
:::

### Category 4: near-miss

A near-miss is an **accident narrowly avoided**. Something could have happened but did not. OHS definition: „an event which, under other circumstances, would have caused injury or material damage".

**Examples of near-misses in a sawmill**:
- A log slipped from the grip and flew **15 cm past** your head.
- The saw blade broke **1 second** after being switched off (had it broken 2 seconds earlier it would have been in motion).
- A forklift turned in the corridor and **stopped 30 cm** before you.
- A board falling off the stack **bounced off a helmet** (without the helmet, it would have hit the head).
- The saw band snapped but **flew in the opposite direction** from your workstation.
- You slipped on spilled oil but **caught a handrail at the last moment**.
- The belt guard was missing but **the operator had not yet switched the machine on** and you noticed.

**Why near-misses matter more than they seem**. The Heinrich statistic (1931), repeatedly confirmed:

```
1 serious / fatal accident
↕
~30 minor accidents
↕
~300 near-misses
↕
~3 000 unsafe behaviours
```

Meaning: if your sawmill logs **300 near-misses a year**, statistically you have **1 serious accident a year**. If you reduce near-misses to 100 (by identifying and removing causes), statistically a serious accident every 3 years.

**That is why reporting a near-miss is as important as reporting an accident with blood**. It is **prevention, not reaction**.

**Near-miss procedure**:
- **Step 1 (0 min)**: after the shock passes, sit down for 30 seconds, breathe. The adrenaline falls.
- **Step 2 (1–5 min)**: foreman. „Attention, near-miss. A log slipped from the grip, flew 15 cm past my head. No one hurt. I think the cause was a wet log + a weak grip".
- **Step 3 (15–60 min)**: the foreman talks to the operator, they inspect the grip, decide to change the procedure (e.g. a new sign „for wet logs, two-arm grip").
- **Step 4 (within 24 h)**: entry in the minor-incident log (or a dedicated „near-miss log" if the plant keeps one).

### 3 myths that block reporting

**Myth 1: „I do not want to get my colleague in trouble"**

Situation: you saw the operator work without hearing protection. You think „I will not report, I would get him in trouble with the foreman".

**Reality**: no hearing protection is a **risk for himself** (hearing loss). Your report **protects the colleague**, it does not harm him. The foreman will not punish him immediately: he will remind, explain, check that PPE is available. Only at a **repeat violation** do sanctions kick in (warning, bonus deduction).

**Cost of not reporting**: in 5–10 years your colleague loses his hearing, does not detect it early because he has got used to silence after work. Your silence = his deafness.

**Myth 2: „It is trivial, not worth it"**

Situation: you noticed a small crack in the drive-belt guard. You think „it is 2 mm, not worth reporting, it will not fall off anyway".

**Reality**: the Heinrich statistic shows that **per 1 accident there are 3000 unsafe behaviours**. One of those 3000 is your „minor crack". If you do not report, the crack grows (vibration, resonance, material age) until the guard breaks during work. And then the belt hits the operator in the face.

**Cost of not reporting**: 2 minutes of paperwork turn into several thousand zlotys of colleague's treatment plus months of his sick leave.

**Myth 3: „I will be punished for reporting"**

Situation: you noticed that the blade-changing procedure is not followed. You think „if I report, the foreman gets angry, I will get hard tasks next, or they will fire me".

**Reality**: **Labour Code art. 210 § 5**: „An employee shall not bear any adverse consequences for (...) notifying the supervisor of observed irregularities". If the foreman punishes you for a report, that is a **breach of law**, call PIP at 801 002 006 anonymously.

**In practice**: a good foreman treats reports as a **source of information**, not a provocation. A bad foreman is a systemic problem, not your problem. You have the right to report a bad foreman to HR or PIP.

**Cost of the myth**: silence = quiet consent to procedure breaches. The first accident will hit you (because you are closest). And then it will be „too late".

### Reporting culture: why good plants reward

In good sawmills (Stora Enso, IKEA Industry, Paged, Barlinek) a **reporting culture** operates. Employees are **actively rewarded** for reports:

- Reporting a near-miss = a point in the incentive scheme. 10 points = PLN 500 bonus.
- A report that prevented an accident (the employer evaluates after the fact) = an extra bonus PLN 1 000–3 000.
- The reporter's name in the plant newsletter („thanks to Ion Popa we prevented a planer failure").

Why does the employer do this? Because **reports are cheaper than accidents**. One serious accident = PLN 50 000–200 000 (treatment, pensions, court proceedings, PIP fine, production downtime, reputation). Ten near-miss reports × PLN 500 = PLN 5 000. Simple maths.

As a helper in a smaller sawmill your chances of a cash bonus are smaller. But **the reporter's reputation** works everywhere. A foreman who sees in you „the eye that catches problems before colleagues" treats you more seriously. You get promoted faster. You get tool authorizations faster. You get recommended faster to another plant when you are done here.

::: warning
**If your foreman „punishes" for reports** (e.g. gives harder tasks, shows anger, hints at dismissal) – that is a **red flag for the plant**. Options:
1. Talk to HR (anonymous or direct).
2. Report to the OHS specialist (if the company has an in-house one).
3. Report to PIP (anonymously, 801 002 006).
4. Last resort: change plants (your health is more important than loyalty to a toxic employer).

A good employer **never** punishes a report. The EGIDA foundation keeps a list of sawmills with a „reporting culture" and a list „to avoid". Ask at the office before you sign the contract.
:::

### Common helper mistakes in reporting

**Mistake 1 – reporting too late**. You saw a crack at 8:00, you reported at 14:00 „at the break". In the meantime the failure developed and the operator got injured. **Correction**: **„without delay" = immediately**, not „when I have a moment".

**Mistake 2 – reporting without facts**. „Something feels wrong on number three". The foreman does not know what to do. **Correction**: **5W1H** = **what**, **when**, **where**, **who**, **why** (hypothesis), **how**. E.g. „On number three (where) at 10:15 (when) the stack is tilting ~10° (what) because the stickers are uneven (why hypothesis), I saw it myself (who), tilt toward the corridor (how)".

**Mistake 3 – reporting „over the head" of the foreman**. You called the shift manager directly, telling the foreman nothing. The foreman is offended, the relationship is ruined. **Correction**: **foreman always first**. Escalation to the shift manager only if the foreman is not present or does not respond.

**Mistake 4 – not giving a hypothesis for the cause**. „The stack is tilting, I do not know why". The foreman has to come and diagnose alone. **Correction**: **state a hypothesis** even if you are not sure. „I think the stickers are too thin" or „maybe the stacker did not level the base". The foreman verifies. Your hypothesis is not „guessing", it is **careful observation** worth taking into account.

**Mistake 5 – reporting without a signature / date**. You wrote it in the log but did not sign. In case of dispute: „who wrote this?". There is no evidence. **Correction**: **always date + time + full name + signature**.

## Summary

Today you learned that reporting is a **procedure**, not just an entry. It has an order, deadlines, a form, consequences.

**Legal duty**: **Labour Code art. 211 pt 6** (employee reports without delay) + **art. 234** (employer takes action). Hiding an accident = legal and disciplinary responsibility.

**4 categories of reports**:
1. **Cut / injury** – 7-step procedure (save life → foreman → medical help → manager → log → investigation → archive). 112 always for a serious case.
2. **Machine failure** – switch off → foreman → mechanic → failure log. You do not repair yourself.
3. **Unrealized hazard** – secure the zone → foreman → corrective action → entry. A quiet alerting job.
4. **Near-miss** – adrenaline → foreman → cause analysis → entry. **Just as important** as an accident report (Heinrich statistic).

**Escalation pyramid**: foreman (always first) → shift manager → OHS specialist → employer → PIP / prosecutor. Your role: always level 1. You escalate to level 2 only if the foreman is absent or does not respond.

**Deadlines**: 0 min oral, 15 min medical help, 60 min informing the manager, 24 h entry in the log, 14 days investigation, 5 years archive.

**3 myths to debunk**: „I do not want to get my colleague in trouble" (your report PROTECTS him), „it is trivial, not worth it" (the Heinrich statistic says otherwise), „I will be punished for reporting" (Labour Code art. 210 § 5 forbids it: punishment = a breach of law).

**The report formula** (5W1H): what, when, where, who, why, how. Orally, short, to the foreman. In writing, detailed, to the log. Always date + signature.

**Reporting culture**: good sawmills reward. Bad ones punish. If your sawmill punishes, call PIP 801 002 006 or change jobs.

## Notatki dla trenera

**Pedagogical goal**: build in the trainee the **reflex of immediate reporting** for all 4 categories of events. Debunk the 3 myths that block reporting. Teach the 5W1H formula for a quick, factual message. Show the escalation pyramid and the helper's role in it.

**120-minute rhythm**:

- **0–10 min**: opening. Question to the group: „when was the last time you saw something that worried you at your workstation and you did nothing about it?". Collect anonymous answers (trainees write on cards, trainer reads without names). Introduction: today we break the reasons why we stay silent and we learn to report.
- **10–25 min**: **legal duty and escalation pyramid**. Board with the 5 levels. The trainer explains when each level kicks in. Short test: „Scenario 1: cut finger, plaster is enough. To whom?". Trainees answer „foreman". „Scenario 2: loss of consciousness, blood. To whom?". „112 + foreman". Rotate 5 scenarios.
- **25–55 min**: **4 report categories**. Detailed walk-through of each category (step-by-step procedure). For each, one pair simulation (5 min × 4 categories = 20 min). Category 1 (cut): one „injured", one „first-aider + reporter". Category 2 (failure): one „operator seeing the failure", one „foreman hearing the report". Category 3 (hazard): one „detector", one „foreman". Category 4 (near-miss): one „experiencer", one „listener analyzing causes". After each simulation 1 minute of group feedback.
- **55–75 min**: **the 3 myths and their debunking**. The trainer reads each myth, the group discusses. Labour Code art. 210 § 5 for myth 3. The Heinrich statistic for myth 2. Cost-of-silence reasoning (co-worker responsibility) for myth 1. **Goal**: emotional, not just intellectual, debunking.
- **75–100 min**: **5W1H in practice**. The trainer gives 5 scenarios on cards. In pairs, trainees practice formulating an oral report (maximum 30 seconds per report, no card). The trainer records one report (with consent), plays it to the group, the group evaluates: was there what, when, where, who, why, how? Corrections.
- **100–115 min**: **the Ukrainian case** (saw bearing near-miss). Discussion: how he noticed, what he said, what happened. Discussion: „how many of you hear the difference between a normal and an uneven machine sound?". Those who do not: they train in breaks in the sawmill. Hearing is a professional skill.
- **115–120 min**: key terms. Announce l7 (M1 recap) and l8 (test).

**Common mistake (1)**: the trainee **confuses category weights**. E.g. treats a near-miss as „trivial" and a plaster cut as „must call the director". **Correction**: a weight table on the board. „Near-miss and minor cut = foreman, log. Serious cut = 112 + foreman + log. Machine failure = foreman + mechanic + failure log. Unrealized hazard = foreman + securing + log". Trainees draw the table in their own notes.

**Common mistake (2)**: the trainee **is afraid to voice a hypothesis** because „I am not an expert". **Correction**: show that the foreman is not an expert on every machine either. Your observation + his knowledge = better diagnosis than his knowledge alone. Exercise: trainees voice 3 hypotheses for the same situation („what could cause the stack to tilt?"). The group picks the most likely one. The trainer shows that even „weird" hypotheses are worth voicing.

**Practical recommendation**: hand out to the trainees an **A6 card** with the 5W1H formula + the escalation pyramid + numbers (foreman's name, shift manager's name, OHS specialist's phone, PIP 801 002 006, 112). Laminated, in the PPE pocket. **Use for 3 months**.

**Link to the next lesson**: l7 „M1 Recap – What You've Learned". A synthetic review of the whole M1 (27 lessons): 4 weeks × 8 lessons + the current l7. Its purpose is **consolidation**, not adding new knowledge. Checking that the trainee remembers the essentials: helper's role („I help but I do not operate"), 6 hazards, PPE, manual transport, log yard, 5 gestures, 30 words, 4 documents, reporting procedure. After l7 comes l8, a practical test. And then we close M1.
