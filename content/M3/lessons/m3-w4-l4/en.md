## Operator as communication hub, theory

### What is a "communication hub" in a sawmill

An M3 operator on a full coordination day (foreman's deputy) serves as the **central communication hub of the hall**. The foreman is off-site (directors' meeting, business trip, holiday). The team is working, but instructions need to be made dynamically – who does what, in what order, with what resources. Without the foreman, instructions can come either from the day plan (Marek's schedule from yesterday) or from the M3 operator's decisions in real time. **This M3 operator becomes the dispatcher**.

Being a communication hub means that:
1. **Information comes to you from many directions**: from Mr Henryk in the drying area, from Anton in the yard, from Damian on P1, from Adrian on P2, from Mrs Ania at grading, from clients by phone (you pick up, or maybe not – that's your call).
2. **Information goes out in many directions**: instructions to Anton about what to bring, to Mr Henryk about unloading times, to Adrian about blade swap, to Wasyl for help on P3.
3. **You decide the priorities** if two or more people want the same resource at the same time (the forklift, you yourself, the pallets, spots on the storage yard).

Without a communication hub the shift scatters: everyone works at their own pace, resource conflicts flare up later, emergency exits get blocked, the client doesn't get goods on time. **A coordinator is always needed, even in a small 6-person team**.

### Four communication channels at the EGIDA sawmill

At EGIDA there are **4 channels of operational communication** between the shift and the foreman:

**Channel 1: Kenwood TK-3401 radio (UHF 446 MHz)**

Handheld radiotelephone, two-channel, in-hall range 200 m (enough for the whole hall + yard), NiMH battery 8 hours of work. Channel 2 (446.00625 MHz) dedicated to the EGIDA day shift. Every shift worker has one device. Training on use – 15 minutes on the first day of work. Procedure: "[recipient name], this is [sender name], [message]. Over." End of transmission – "Over" (awaiting reply), "Out" (closing the call), "Break" (resume later). **Advantages**: immediacy, short messages, voice recognized quickly. **Disadvantages**: no recording, two people cannot speak simultaneously (half-duplex), interference when machines are running at full power.

**Channel 2: WhatsApp group "EGIDA day shift"**

WhatsApp group, 8 participants (shift + Marek offline on business trip). Used for **messages that need a permanent record**: shift report photo, damage photos, delivery confirmations, queries that can wait 5-10 minutes. **Advantages**: record for Marek, photos, offline notifications, visible to all members. **Disadvantages**: delays (not everyone watches the phone every 30 seconds), distracts during work (phone out of pocket = risk of dropping, hitting, losing). EGIDA rule: **while working at a machine, do NOT check WhatsApp**, only during a break or after a radio call.

**Channel 3: Magnetic board at P3**

Whiteboard 1.2 × 0.8 m with markers and magnets, hanging in a central spot of the saw hall (visible from every machine). Contains: **active ZLE numbers** (currently ZLE-022, 024, 025), **machine status** (P1 OK, P2 blade swap 11:00, P3 OK), **priorities of the day** (oak DOM-STOL deadline Friday 15:00), **absentees** (today Marek in Olsztyn, Mrs Ania from 14:00). **Updated every morning** by the shift lead (today Mykoła 7:10 before briefing). **Advantages**: static, everyone in the hall sees it, no need to ask. **Disadvantages**: not updated in real time (status change during the day requires physically walking up to the board).

**Channel 4: EGIDA-RZ-001 shift report**

A4 form, printed from the EGIDA CRM system once a day (in the afternoon 14:30 by the shift lead), 7 sections (machine status, completed orders, raw material, lumber, faults, safety briefing, remarks). Original signed on the magnetic board until evening (for the foreman), photo copy sent immediately via WhatsApp to Marek. **Retention 5 years** (together with other operational documents). **Advantages**: permanent record, identical template each day (easy to compare), mandatory content (no optionality – the faults section MUST be filled in, even with "none"). **Disadvantages**: once a day (no finer resolution), takes time to fill in (20-30 min).

**Synergy principle of the 4 channels**: radio for immediacy, WhatsApp for confirmations with a record, board for the permanent status of the day, report for the historical log of the shift. A good coordinator uses all four in parallel, not just one. **Error: using only WhatsApp** (delays = resource conflicts). **Error: using only radio** (no record = Marek in the evening doesn't know what happened).

### Prioritizing operational conflicts

An operational conflict is a situation in which **two people want the same resource at the same time**: the forklift, yard space, the attention of the M3 operator, pallets, space in the drying kiln. On a day without the foreman, conflicts are settled by the M3 deputy operator.

**EGIDA prioritization rules** (internal regulation reg.wew.07/2024 § 3):

1. **Safety always before production**. If a resource conflict blocks the emergency exit, fire extinguisher, evacuation route, first-aid point – **immediate resolution, even at the cost of production losses**.
2. **Client deadlines before internal convenience**. If a truck is booked for 13:30 and a worker would like a short pause – the worker waits, the truck moves.
3. **Continuous operations (machines running) before starting stopped machines**. If Damian is finishing rafters on P1 and Adrian is swapping the blade on P2 – Damian first finishes transport, then Adrian starts up.
4. **Single-person worker (Henryk in drying) before doubled tasks**. If Mr Henryk cannot move the pallets alone and Anton has a second forklift in the workshop – Mr Henryk has priority.
5. **When the decision is hard, the M3 operator may step away from the machine for 10-15 min to help manually**. This is an **unconventional** solution, but acceptable if it secures safety and doesn't extend the shift.

The 11:30 conflict (Henryk with pallets vs Damian with rafter transport) Mykoła resolved through **rule five** – he stepped off P3 for 10 minutes, manually helped Henryk. The alternative would have been: stop Damian (breach of DHL deadline = contractual penalty), or call Anton (breach of internal transport schedule). Mykoła's decision was pragmatic: **people are working, the shift finishes, safety is maintained**.

### Assertiveness without aggression

A shift coordinator often refuses requests. "I can't now", "No, that's not a priority", "Wait 20 minutes, then I'll do it". Assertiveness is **the ability to refuse with respect for the other person**, without aggression or avoidance.

**Bad reaction** (aggressive): *"Henryk, sort out the pallets yourself, I'm not your helper!"*
**Bad reaction** (avoidant): *"Henryk, I don't know what to do, ask Marek when he's back."*
**Good reaction** (assertive): *"Henryk, I understand the problem. I can't pull Anton away from Damian, but I can step off P3 for 10 minutes and help manually. Agreed?"*

Assertiveness has **three components**:
1. **Acknowledgement of the other person's problem** (I understand, I see, I hear),
2. **Stating the constraint** (I can't do X, because Y),
3. **Proposing an alternative** (but I can do Z, if that works for you).

Without these three components, a refusal sounds either aggressive (rejecting the person) or avoidant (no decision). Good EGIDA shift coordinators practise assertiveness year after year – it isn't an innate trait, it's trained.

### Delegating to a lower-level operator

Delegation is **handing a specific task to a person with a lower authorization level in the team**, while the coordinator retains responsibility for the result. At EGIDA the foreman's deputy can delegate to:

- **M2 (authorized operator, Adrian)**: independent blade swap on P2 under M3 radio oversight. Responsibility: Adrian physically swaps, Mykoła M3 is responsible for checking the weld before start-up.
- **M1 (helper, Wasyl)**: cleaning, manual transport of offcuts, and standard auxiliary tasks. Without delegating operator work – **an M1 helper does NOT start the machine**, not even for a moment.
- **Yard foreman (Anton)**: transport of roundwood, pallets, lumber with the forklift. Anton is an independent worker, but on a day without Marek, the M3 deputy has the right to give him priority instructions.
- **Drying master (Mr Henryk)**: **you do NOT delegate** to the drying master – he is an independent worker with his own area, equivalent to M3. You can cooperate, coordinate, but not delegate.

**Delegation boundaries**:
1. **Do not delegate legal decisions** (client complaints, forestry contracts, order cancellations – that's the foreman).
2. **Do not delegate safety** to a person without authorization (M1 does not lead the safety briefing, even if he claims he can).
3. **Always retain ultimate responsibility**: if M2 Adrian swaps the blade badly and a board snaps, Marek will ask M3 operator Mykoła, not Adrian – because Mykoła delegated it.

### Shift report, the coordinator's insurance policy

The EGIDA-RZ-001 report is not just a historical document. It is **the coordinator's insurance policy against accusations**. If a week later the client DOM-STOL files a complaint ("3 countertops have excess moisture"), Marek opens the report from the production day and reads: "Countertop moisture 39-41%, Tanel ET-6 calibration 03.2026, 5 measurement points." If the client claims the "report was falsified", Marek shows the WhatsApp photo sent at 14:52 that day to Marek – **hard to dispute**.

An exemplary report has:
- **All 7 sections filled in**, even "no faults" (don't leave it blank),
- **Concrete numbers**: "40 C24 rafters 175 × 38 × 4050", not "plenty of rafters",
- **Times of decisions**: "11:30 forklift conflict, 11:40 resolved", not "something around noon",
- **Honest remarks section**: if something went wrong, record it. Hiding a problem = worse than openness.

Mykoła today wrote "Communication conflict 11:30...production loss 11 min on P3. Safety maintained." – **an honest self-assessment**, praised by Marek. In a week it can be analyzed: would another solution have been better? Is a second forklift needed? Should the drying-unloading procedure be changed?

## Key terms

- **Pełnomocnik brygadzisty** (PL) / **Foreman's deputy** (EN) / **Delegado del capataz** (ES) / **Заступник бригадира** (UK) – operator M3 prowadzący zmianę organizacyjnie w dniu nieobecności brygadzisty.
- **Węzeł komunikacji** (PL) / **Communication hub** (EN) / **Nodo de comunicación** (ES) / **Вузол комунікації** (UK) – osoba centralna, przez którą przechodzą dyspozycje i informacje operacyjne w zmianie.
- **Radio Kenwood TK-3401** (PL) / **Kenwood TK-3401 handheld radio** (EN) / **Radio Kenwood TK-3401** (ES) / **Радіостанція Kenwood TK-3401** (UK) – radiotelefon UHF 446 MHz używany w EGIDA, kanał 2 dla zmiany dziennej.
- **WhatsApp grupa zmianowa** (PL) / **Shift WhatsApp group** (EN) / **Grupo de WhatsApp del turno** (ES) / **Група WhatsApp зміни** (UK) – grupa komunikacyjna z zapisem dla brygadzisty, zdjęć raportów, potwierdzeń.
- **Tablica magnetyczna** (PL) / **Magnetic board** (EN) / **Pizarra magnética** (ES) / **Магнітна дошка** (UK) – tablica w hali pilarek ze statusem maszyn, aktywnych ZLE i nieobecności.
- **Raport EGIDA-RZ-001** (PL) / **EGIDA-RZ-001 shift report** (EN) / **Informe de turno EGIDA-RZ-001** (ES) / **Звіт зміни EGIDA-RZ-001** (UK) – formularz A4 z 7 sekcji wprowadzony 04.2026 przez EGIDA.
- **Priorytetyzacja konfliktów** (PL) / **Conflict prioritization** (EN) / **Priorización de conflictos** (ES) / **Пріоритизація конфліктів** (UK) – zasady rozstrzygania sporów o zasoby w dniu pracy zmiany (reg.wew.07/2024 § 3).
- **BHP przed produkcją** (PL) / **Safety before production** (EN) / **Seguridad antes que producción** (ES) / **Безпека перед виробництвом** (UK) – pierwsza zasada priorytetyzacji: ochrona wyjść awaryjnych i dróg ewakuacyjnych.
- **Asertywność** (PL) / **Assertiveness** (EN) / **Asertividad** (ES) / **Асертивність** (UK) – umiejętność odmowy z szacunkiem i propozycją alternatywy, bez agresji ani uniku.
- **Delegowanie** (PL) / **Delegation** (EN) / **Delegación** (ES) / **Делегування** (UK) – przekazanie konkretnego zadania osobie o niższym poziomie uprawnień z zachowaniem odpowiedzialności koordynatora.
- **Odpowiedzialność końcowa** (PL) / **Ultimate responsibility** (EN) / **Responsabilidad final** (ES) / **Остаточна відповідальність** (UK) – zasada, że koordynator delegujący zadanie nadal odpowiada za wynik przed brygadzistą.
- **Half-duplex** (PL) / **Half-duplex** (EN) / **Half-duplex** (ES) / **Half-duplex** (UK) – tryb pracy radia, w którym tylko jedna osoba mówi jednocześnie na danym kanale.
- **Kanał 2 UHF 446 MHz** (PL) / **Channel 2 UHF 446 MHz** (EN) / **Canal 2 UHF 446 MHz** (ES) / **Канал 2 UHF 446 МГц** (UK) – dedykowana częstotliwość zmiany dziennej EGIDA w paśmie PMR446.
- **Procedura wywołania radiowego** (PL) / **Radio call procedure** (EN) / **Procedimiento de llamada por radio** (ES) / **Процедура радіовиклику** (UK) – schemat: imię odbiorcy + „tu" + imię nadawcy + komunikat + „Odbiór / Bez odbioru".
- **Retrospekcja zmiany** (PL) / **Shift retrospective** (EN) / **Retrospectiva del turno** (ES) / **Ретроспектива зміни** (UK) – krótkie podsumowanie brygadzisty z prowadzącym zmianę po powrocie, analiza decyzji i alternatyw.
- **Regulamin wewnętrzny reg.wew.07/2024** (PL) / **Internal regulation 07/2024** (EN) / **Reglamento interno 07/2024** (ES) / **Внутрішній регламент 07/2024** (UK) – dokument EGIDA z 07.2024 opisujący zasady priorytetyzacji konfliktów operacyjnych.
