## Content

### Three types of cutting bands in a Polish sawmill

A cutting band is a **consumable tool** on a band saw. It consists of a steel strip (3500-4500 mm long for 38 mm saws, 4000-5500 mm for 50 mm), on which the teeth are either integral or made of a harder material. Band type = tooth material + strip material + hardening method.

**Type 1: alloy steel (*alloy steel*, universal standard):**

Steel strip with chromium, vanadium, and molybdenum additions (alloy designated 15N5M in Polish nomenclature, or 51CrV4 in European). Teeth **cut from the strip** (integral teeth, single material for strip and tooth). Hardening by induction hardening of the teeth (teeth hard at 58-62 HRC, strip plastic at 35-40 HRC).

- **Cost**: 180 zł/m (a 4.2 m strip for the Wood-Mizer LT70 costs 756 zł).
- **Service life**:
  - Fresh pine: 3-4 m³ of cutting (then the teeth dull, kerf quality drops).
  - Fresh spruce: 4-5 m³.
  - Dry larch: 2-3 m³.
  - Dry oak: 2-3 m³ (not recommended, rapid wear).
  - Dry beech: 1-2 m³ (dulls very quickly).
- **Application:** 60-70% of daily work in a Polish sawmill (fresh pine and spruce).
- **Reconditioning:** possible only 1-2 times. After sharpening the teeth lose their set and the kerf is worse.

**Type 2: stellite (*stellite overlay*, premium for hard species):**

Steel strip similar to the alloy one, but with teeth **tipped with stellite alloy** (a cobalt, chromium, and tungsten alloy, hardness 48-52 HRC but with very high abrasion resistance, indestructible in contact with sand and hard species). Stellite is applied to the teeth by thermal overlay (a specialist factory process, not something a sawmill can do). Visible as a white tip on top of the tooth.

- **Cost**: 320 zł/m (a 4.2 m strip = 1344 zł, twice as expensive as alloy).
- **Service life**:
  - Dry oak: 8-10 m³.
  - Dry beech: 6-8 m³.
  - Ash: 5-7 m³.
  - Fresh pine: 8-12 m³ (excessive durability for a soft species, cost not optimised).
- **Application:** 15-20% of daily work (hardwood campaigns).
- **Reconditioning:** possible 3-4 times (sharpening without removing the stellite layer on top of the tooth). Reconditioning at 60-80 zł per cycle.

**Type 3: bi-metal (*bi-metal*, reinforced for extreme conditions):**

Steel strip + teeth **of a second material** (typically HSS high-speed steel or stellite with a tungsten-carbide addition), electron-welded to the strip. Tooth hardness 60-65 HRC. Resistance to frost, vibration, impacts.

- **Cost**: 450 zł/m (a 4.2 m strip = 1890 zł).
- **Service life**: depends on conditions, but 3-4 times longer than alloy under the same conditions.
- **Application:** 3-5% of daily work (frozen wood in winter, exceptionally hard species).
- **Reconditioning:** difficult, usually a single-life band.

### Band width and matching to the machine

A band saw has a **band-wheel groove** designed for a specific band width. The Wood-Mizer LT70 (EGIDA P3) has 500 mm diameter wheels with a 42 mm groove (38 mm band + 4 mm spare for set). The Serra SM40 (EGIDA P1 and P2, larger stationary saws) has 600 mm wheels with a 55 mm groove (50 mm band + 5 mm spare). Changing to a band of a different width than the one designed **is not possible** without replacing the wheels (a general overhaul of the saw, 15-25 thousand zł, beyond the operator's competence).

**The role of width in kerf quality:**

A wider band = **more stable** (less lateral vibration, straighter kerf), but **thicker** (more steel, more energy drawn from the motor, slower feed). A narrower band = less stable (more vibration), but faster and more material-efficient.

- **38 mm band** (P3 Wood-Mizer): optimal for boards 20-50 mm thick and cross-sections up to 300 x 300 mm. Our P3 has so far cut pine 50 x 150 (an easy kerf); today it will cut oak 32 x 200 (medium thickness, but a harder species, more demanding).
- **50 mm band** (P1, P2 Serra): for boards 30-80 mm thick, cross-sections up to 500 x 500 mm. The Serra SM40 cut 400 mm diameter logs for roof trusses through May.
- **For a 32 x 200 board on P3**: a 38 mm band width gives a kerf depth up to 30 mm with stability (we fit). If we wanted to cut a 50 x 300 board, we would have to use P1 or P2 (50 mm band).

### The three tooth pitches

Tooth pitch is the **distance between the tips of adjacent teeth** on the band, measured in mm. The pitch defines the **gullet volume between teeth** (the space for chips) that the chip has to fill and be ejected from the kerf by the rotating band.

**Pitch 22 mm (*coarse*):**

- Few teeth per unit length of band (for 4200 mm: 4200/22 = 191 teeth).
- Large gullet: takes large chips.
- **Optimal for:** wet/fresh wood (large, moist, sticky chips, requiring a lot of ejection space).
- **Target species:** fresh pine at 50-65% moisture, fresh spruce, wet winter oak (after felling in snow).
- **Typical feed:** 3-4 m/min for a 50 x 150 mm board.
- **Advantages:** fast cutting, little vibration, low power draw (the 7.5 kW P3 motor works comfortably).
- **Disadvantages:** rough kerf quality (tooth marks visible), requiring additional finishing for visual boards.

**Pitch 19 mm (*medium*, standard):**

- Tooth count: 4200/19 = 221 teeth.
- Medium gullet.
- **Optimal for:** moderately moist wood (18-25%), semi-hard species.
- **Target species:** pine after initial drying, dried spruce, wet beech, semi-hard ash.
- **Typical feed:** 2.5-3 m/min.
- **Advantages:** universality, compromise between speed and quality.
- **Disadvantages:** not optimal for extreme cases (too dense for wet, too sparse for very dry).

**Pitch 15 mm (*fine*):**

- Tooth count: 4200/15 = 280 teeth.
- Small gullet.
- **Optimal for:** dry wood (8-20% moisture), hard species, dense wood.
- **Target species:** dry oak 15-25%, dry beech, dry ash, tropical species (teak, iroko).
- **Typical feed:** 1.5-2 m/min (slower, because more teeth to process).
- **Advantages:** smooth kerf (almost like sandpaper), little vibration when cutting hard material, small chips (dry material does not form large chips, the dense teeth take them away).
- **Disadvantages:** slower cutting, higher power draw (the motor works under load, more wear), greater risk of band overheating if the feed is too fast.

::: tip
**Why the pitch has to match the wood moisture**. A kerf creates chips of a size proportional to **feed times band thickness**. The chips have to fit in the **tooth gullets** and be ejected from the kerf by the rotation of the band (18-24 m/s linear tooth speed). For wet wood the chips are **large and sticky** (water glues the shavings together). They require a large gullet, hence the 22 mm pitch. For dry wood the chips are **small and loose** (fine dust rather than shavings). Dense 15 mm teeth can take them away. Getting the pitch wrong causes a **kerf jam**: wet chips do not fit in 15 mm gullets, they build up, overheat the band, and can cause a break. Dry chips are lost in 22 mm gullets (they do not fill them), the kerf becomes rough because the teeth cut with an unfilled gullet.
:::

### The three selection scenarios in daily work

**Scenario 1: fresh frozen pine (winter, December-February):**

- **Wood condition:** fresh (50-65% moisture), frozen core (below -5°C), outer rings loose but the centre is ice.
- **Band:** alloy 38 mm.
- **Pitch:** 22 mm (large gullet, wet and sticky chips).
- **Feed pressure:** reduced by 10% of standard (from 2400 PSI to 2200 PSI), because frost increases cutting resistance and wet frozen wood is harder than summer wet wood.
- **Feed:** 2.5-3 m/min (slower than summer pine at 3-4 m/min).
- **Band service life:** 2-3 m³ (shorter than summer 3-4, because frost wears the teeth more).
- **Change frequency:** every 2 days in the winter campaign (vs. every 3-4 days in summer).

**Scenario 2: dry oak (25-35% moisture, after initial seasoning):**

- **Wood condition:** dry on the outside (outer rings 15-20%), semi-moist core (30-40%). A hard species (density 680 kg/m³), difficult to cut.
- **Band:** **stellite** 38 mm (the key decision, alloy wears out too fast).
- **Pitch:** 15 mm (dense teeth for dry material).
- **Feed pressure:** standard 2400 PSI.
- **Feed:** 1.5-2 m/min (slower than pine because of oak density).
- **Band service life:** 8-10 m³ with stellite.
- **Change frequency:** every 5-7 days.

**Scenario 3: standard spruce (main summer campaign):**

- **Wood condition:** fresh (40-55% moisture), soft species (density 430 kg/m³).
- **Band:** alloy 38 mm.
- **Pitch:** 19 mm (standard, because spruce has an intermediate consistency).
- **Pressure:** standard.
- **Feed:** 3-3.5 m/min (spruce cuts more easily than pine).
- **Service life:** 4-5 m³.
- **Change frequency:** every 3-4 days.

Beyond these three scenarios there are rarer combinations (dry beech, ash, larch, tropical wood), but for a beginner operator in M3 (like Rustam) the three main scenarios cover **85% of the work**.

::: warning
**The tooth pitch on an existing band cannot be changed**. The pitch is fixed, cut/cast at the factory. Changing the pitch = changing the band. A beginner operator's mistake: trying to cut fresh pine with a 15 mm band (because it was left over from oak). The result: a kerf jam, overheating, a band break after 20-30 minutes. **Always check the band type before starting the machine**: the label on the band (burned in or on a sticker) shows the type, width, pitch, date of last reconditioning. The check takes 20 seconds; the wrong band means a break, 30 min of downtime, potential damage to the wheels or guides.
:::

### Band service life and reconditioning

A band is not thrown out after its first campaign. **Reconditioning** (an external service at a specialist firm; in Poland typically "Ostrzex" Skawina or "Zmechanizowane Drewno" Poznań) consists of:

1. **Collection of the used band** (packaged, sent by DPD courier or picked up once a week by the supplier). Transit time 2-3 days.
2. **Tooth sharpening** (CNC grinder with a profile matching the original tooth geometry; removes 0.1-0.3 mm of material from each tooth).
3. **Set check** (*set*): lateral measurement of left/right teeth, adjustment to the 0.5-0.7 mm standard each way.
4. **Strip geometry test** (check of straightness and absence of micro-cracks, via magnetic inspection and bend tests).
5. **Return** to the sawmill (total 5-7 days procedure).

Reconditioning cost:
- **Stellite 38 mm**: 60-80 zł per cycle (vs. 320 zł new = saving 240-260 zł).
- **Alloy 38 mm**: 40-50 zł per cycle (vs. 180 zł = saving 130-140 zł).
- **Bi-metal**: 100-150 zł (vs. 450 zł).

**How many reconditionings per band:**

- Stellite: 3-4 reconditionings before total wear. In total one stellite band makes 8-10 reconditionings x 9 m³ = 80 m³ of oak through its full life cycle. Full cost: 320 + 4x70 = 600 zł over 80 m³ = 7.50 zł/m³ of band cost.
- Alloy: 1-2 reconditionings. One band handles 3 x 3 m³ = 9 m³ of pine. Cost: 180 + 2x45 = 270 zł over 9 m³ = 30 zł/m³.
- Bi-metal: 0-1 reconditioning. 2 x 10 m³ = 20 m³. Cost: 450 + 125 = 575 zł over 20 m³ = 28.75 zł/m³.

Table of **band cost per m³** of wood:

| Type | New price | Reconditionings | m³/life | zł/m³ |
|-----|-----------|-------------|----------|-------|
| Alloy (pine) | 180 zł | 2 x 45 | 9 m³ | 30.0 |
| **Stellite (oak)** | 320 zł | 4 x 70 | 80 m³ | **7.5** |
| Bi-metal | 450 zł | 1 x 125 | 20 m³ | 28.75 |

**Conclusion for the operator:** stellite on oak looks more expensive at purchase (320 vs 180 zł), but the cost per m³ of wood is **four times lower** than alloy. Stellite on pine (not recommended) would also be cheap per m³, but pine does not need such a hard band, so alloy is the economical choice.

### What a self-reliant operator checks before every change

Before every start of a new campaign (species change, assortment change):

1. **Band type currently mounted**: label, whether it matches the planned campaign.
2. **Band wear**: visual check of teeth (even or chipped, whether there are micro-cracks at the tooth base), the m³ counter since the last reconditioning.
3. **Tension**: gauge reads 2400 PSI in working position.
4. **Guides**: 3-4 mm clearance, no lateral wobble in the rotation test.
5. **Cleanliness of the band wheels**: no resin, sawdust, stray grease.

If any point is not met, the operator **replaces the band before starting the campaign**, not during it. Starting a campaign with an incorrect band = a break within the first hour, downtime, loss of material in the log, potential damage to the wheels.
