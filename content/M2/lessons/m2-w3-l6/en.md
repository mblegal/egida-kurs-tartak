## Introduction

Monday, 7:00 a.m., the sorting yard of the EGIDA Foundation sawmill: an open covered area of 400 m², 15 m from the saw hall, with 12 marked stack stations. Each station has a board with a code: "A-25-150-3000" (class A, thickness 25 mm, width 150 mm, length 3000 mm), "B-30-180-2500", "C-45-95-2500". Oleksij is here as the executor for the first time: over the previous three weeks he watched the sorting from the side, performed by Tadeusz Kowalski and Sofía. Today Marek says: "You have 30 boards from Friday's planer today: pine, mixed thicknesses from 25 to 45 mm, lengths 2500 and 3000 mm, classes A, B, C. Sort them onto the correct stacks. Tadeusz helps with transport, but you decide where each one goes."

Sorting is the **last stage of the process** before shipment to the customer. The band saw divides the log into a cant and unedged lumber (week 2 lesson 5), the circular saw edges the sides (this week, lesson 4), the planer finishes the surface (this week, lesson 5). Between these stages the board has the identity "board in production", meaning it is still an anonymous piece moving along the workflow. After sorting, the board has the identity **"board in a batch"**: with a code, date, class, species and dimensions, ready for shipment or storage. This change of identity is not merely administrative, it is the single moment at which the board becomes a commercial product with a clear price and a clear customer expectation.

The week 3 mantra from lesson 1 reads: **"I start up, I adjust, I cut, I sort, but always under the supervision of the foreman or a senior"**. Today you learn the fourth activity: **I sort**. This is not work at a machine, it is not cutting, it is not cutter processing: it is a **classification decision** ("where does this specific board go"). A decision based on criteria: thickness, length, species, quality class. A classification error = the board ends up in a batch with the wrong intended use = the customer returns the batch = the sawmill loses its margin. Good classification = a homogeneous batch = the customer pays a premium for certainty. In Oleksij's case today, a batch of 12 class A boards at 30 × 150 × 3000 mm may be worth 150 PLN; a single misclassified board in that batch can cancel the entire premium, which is why sorting, despite looking static and slow, carries the same economic weight as any of the machine operations he has already learned.

## Objectives

After this lesson:

1. You sort lumber according to **four criteria** simultaneously: (a) **thickness** (25, 30, 45, 50, 70 mm, the 2025 standard classes), (b) **length** (2000, 2500, 3000, 4000, 5000 mm, the commercial lengths of the Polish market), (c) **species** (pine, spruce, oak, beech, the four columns in the price-list table), (d) **quality class** (A, B, C, D, introduced in week 2 lesson 6). Each board has **four labels** that must match the target stack.
2. You understand that a **homogeneous batch** is a batch in which **all four criteria** are identical for every board. A customer ordering "pine A 30 × 150 × 3000" expects every board in the batch to be pine, class A, thickness 30 mm, width 150 mm, length 3000 mm. Mixing even one criterion (for example, 2 class B boards in a class A batch) = complaint = return of the entire batch + loss of margin (50–300 PLN/m³ × 2 m³ per batch = 100–600 PLN loss).
3. You read and understand the **batch label**: batch code (format: 2 letters of species + 1 letter of class + 6 digits of date + 3 digits of number, e.g. "SO-A-260420-012" = pine class A from 20 April 2026, batch number 12), date, dimensions column (thickness × width × length), batch mass in m³ (for accounting), executing operator (your initials). The label is stuck to **every third board from the top of the stack**, with a duplicate on the station board.
4. You apply the **3 safety rules of the sorting yard**: (a) a stack no higher than 2 m (higher = risk of boards falling during loader transport, Ministry of Labour and Social Policy Regulation of 2003 § 64), (b) a 1 m gap between stacks (passage for the transport trolley and a second operator), (c) **you never place a board on the stack by throwing** it: you hand it over in two steps (tilt → careful placement), to avoid destabilising the existing stack.
5. You apply the **manual transport rules from module 1 week 1 lesson 3** (M 30 kg / M 50 kg / F 12 kg / F 20 kg, Ministry of Labour and Social Policy Regulation of 2000 on manual transport): a pine board 45 × 150 × 3000 mm = about 14 kg (dry pine density 500 kg/m³ × 0.02 m³), within the F 20 kg lifting limit for women and M 30 kg for men. An oak board 50 × 200 × 4000 mm = about 28 kg, at the F 20 limit: a man may carry it alone, a woman **in a pair**. An oak board 70 × 250 × 5000 mm = about 60 kg: **always in a pair or on a trolley**, regardless of gender.

### Four sorting criteria – simultaneous check

**Thickness** you check with a caliper **or a calibrated template** (the template is faster: fits / does not fit in 1 second; the caliper gives an exact result in 10 seconds). In practice, an experienced sorter uses the template for the first screening of a stack and reaches for the caliper only for boards that sit on the edge of a class. 2025 standard thicknesses on the Polish market: 19, 22, 25, 30, 32, 38, 45, 50, 60, 70, 80 mm. Tolerance for edged, unplaned lumber: **±1 mm** (PN-EN 1313-1). Four-sided planed lumber: **±0.3 mm** (our boards from the Weinig planer in lesson 5 fit within this tolerance). If the actual thickness is 44 mm instead of the declared 45 mm, it is **class C** (tolerance exceeded), regardless of knots and defects. The reason is straightforward: the customer who ordered 45 mm plans his product (floor, beam, panel) for that dimension, and a 1 mm deviation compounds over the length of a wall or a deck.

**Length** you check with a measuring tape or two people with a 5 m tape. Commercial lengths PL: 2000, 2500, 3000, 4000, 5000 mm + tolerance 10–50 mm (usually a cut at the sawmill gives boards 30 mm longer than the nominal length, so that the customer can "trim" the ends). A board of 3030 mm = commercial length 3000. A board of 2970 mm = **a board for the lower category** (nominal 2500, not 3000). If the customer orders "3000 mm" and receives 2970 mm boards: complaint.

**Species** you check by sight and smell. Pine: distinct light–dark rings (earlywood / latewood), colour light yellow to light brown, resinous scent at a fresh cut. Spruce: less distinct rings, colour white-cream, sweetish scent. Oak: coarse rings 2–4 mm, dark medullary rays visible as silvery bands, colour light brown. Beech: diffuse-porous wood (rings barely visible), colour pinkish-cream, very dense (650–720 kg/m³). A **species mistake** (pine labelled as spruce) = a price-list mistake: pine 950 PLN/m³ vs. spruce 650 PLN/m³ = a difference of 300 PLN/m³; the sawmill loses or the customer receives a better product at a lower price. Beyond the price, a species mistake also breaks the customer's technological plan: oak ordered for a staircase tread cannot be replaced with beech without recalculating the fastening and the finish.

**Quality class** (A/B/C/D from week 2 lesson 6) you check by looking at the board from four sides: left, right, top, bottom. Criteria: sound knots (A up to 30 mm / 3 lm, B up to 50 mm / 5 lm, C up to 70 mm / 7 lm), loose knots (A: none, B up to 30 mm / 2 lm, C up to 50 mm), end cracks (A up to 1/4 of the width, B up to 1/3, C up to 1/2). The **weakest-criterion rule** (key rule from week 2 lesson 6): the class = the lowest of all assessed criteria, not the average. A board with class A sound knots but an end crack of 1/3 of the width = **class B**, not "almost A with a small crack".

### Batch label – the board's identity document

The batch label is a piece of cardboard 100 × 60 mm, stuck with paper tape to **every third board from the top of the stack** (visible from the outside, without having to break the stack open for inspection). The content of the label is strict:

```
EGIDA FOUNDATION SAWMILL
Batch code:  SO-A-260420-012
Date:        20.04.2026
Species:     pine (Pinus sylvestris)
Class:       A (PN-EN 1611-1)
Dimensions:  30 × 150 × 3000 mm
Mass:        0.95 m³ (70 boards)
Operator:    O.B. (Oleksij Bohdaniuk)
Foreman:     M.K.
```

**Batch code format** (EGIDA Foundation standard): 2 letters of species (SO = sosna/pine, SW = świerk/spruce, DĄ = dąb/oak, BU = buk/beech) + 1 letter of class (A/B/C/D) + 6 digits of date (DDMMYY) + 3 digits of the sequential daily batch number. "SO-A-260420-012" = **pine A from 20 April 2026, the twelfth batch of that day**. The code is unique throughout the sawmill's history: it allows the customer to file a complaint 2 months after delivery ("batch SO-A-260420-012 had 3 boards with end cracks above 1/4 of the width").

A duplicate of the label hangs on the **station board** (a plastic board 200 × 150 mm on the column of the station, format identical to the label on the board). The board allows the stack state to be checked from a distance: the shift supervisor enters the yard, looks at the board, knows what is in the stack, without breaking the boards open. After the stack is shipped, the board goes to the plant archive (5 years of storage under the Council of Ministers Regulation of 2009 on archiving OHS documents).

### Sorting yard – 3 safety rules

**Rule 1: A stack no higher than 2 m.** The Ministry of Labour and Social Policy Regulation of 26 September 1997 § 64 requires that storage stacks be "stable" and not create a falling hazard. Wood industry practice: the maximum height of a board stack = 2 m (about 50 boards of 25 mm thickness or 30 boards of 45 mm thickness). Above this height the stack becomes unstable during loader operations, with the risk of the boards "sliding apart" during transport. If a batch has more boards than fit in 2 m, you split it into **two stacks side by side**, each with its own label (the batch code is identical, the stack number "A" and "B" is added).

**Rule 2: A 1 m gap between stacks.** The minimum passage for a manual transport trolley and for safe movement of a second operator. If two stacks are closer than 1 m, the loader hits the second stack with its forks while lifting the first, and the entire sorting yard destabilises. In large sawmills (more than 20 employees) the gaps are 1.5–2 m due to forklift traffic with 1.2 m width. At the EGIDA Foundation (8 employees, manual trolley) a 1 m gap is enough.

**Rule 3: You hand over in two steps, never by throwing.** A board placed on the stack is **never thrown from a height**: always in **two steps**: (1) tilt the board over the stack with the hands low at the edge, (2) carefully lower it into contact. A throw from a height = impact on the top layer with a force of 200–500 N = destabilisation of the stack, a possible board fall on the other side, a risk of hitting a colleague on the other side of the stack. Oleksij places the first 5 boards too quickly (the natural reflex of "haste"), Marek stops him: "Slower. Two steps. Tilt, lower. Always."

### Oleksij's case – 30 boards onto 4 stacks

30 pine boards from Friday's planer are waiting on a pallet. A mixed set: thicknesses 25, 30, 45 mm, lengths 2500 and 3000 mm, classes A, B, C (Oleksij assesses each board separately, looking at knots, end cracks, curvatures). Tadeusz transports 5 boards at a time on a manual trolley to the assessment zone.

Oleksij sorts for 25 minutes. From the 30 boards come **4 stacks**:

- **SO-A-260420-012**: pine class A, 30 × 150 × 3000 mm, 12 boards, 0.16 m³
- **SO-A-260420-013**: pine class A, 25 × 150 × 2500 mm, 8 boards, 0.075 m³
- **SO-B-260420-004**: pine class B, 45 × 150 × 3000 mm, 6 boards, 0.12 m³
- **SO-C-260420-002**: pine class C, 30 × 150 × 2500 mm, 4 boards, 0.045 m³

**One mistake**: board 29 has a loose knot of 35 mm, Oleksij places it on stack A (he overlooked it, because the knot is on the side, not on the face). During inspection Marek takes the board out, shows the knot: "Loose, 35 mm. Class B, the 30 mm threshold exceeded. We move it." Oleksij moves the board to stack B, updates the label of stack A (12 → 11 boards) and of stack B (6 → 7). The label has the update date added in pencil.

**Lesson of the day**: sorting requires **viewing the board from all sides**, not only from above. Loose knots are often on the side (visible only when the board lies on its side). The EGIDA Foundation 80/20 rule (from week 2 lesson 6): after 3 months the operator sorts 80% correctly on their own, 20% is checked by the supervisor. After 6 months 90/10. After a year 95/5.

## Key terms

- **partia jednorodna** (pl) / **homogeneous batch** (en) / **lote homogéneo** (es) / **однорідна партія** (uk): a set of boards in which all four criteria (species, class, thickness, length) are identical for every board; the commercial basis of wholesale deliveries
- **kod partii** (pl) / **batch code** (en) / **código de lote** (es) / **код партії** (uk): the unique batch identifier in the EGIDA format "2 letters of species + 1 letter of class + date DDMMYY + 3 digits", e.g. SO-A-260420-012
- **etykieta partii** (pl) / **batch label** (en) / **etiqueta de lote** (es) / **етикетка партії** (uk): a 100 × 60 mm piece of cardboard with the code, date, dimensions, mass and operator, stuck to every third board from the top of the stack
- **reguła najsłabszego kryterium** (pl) / **weakest criterion rule** (en) / **regla del criterio más débil** (es) / **правило найслабшого критерію** (uk): quality class = the lowest of all assessed criteria, not the average (class A knots + class B crack = class B)
- **tolerancja wymiarowa** (pl) / **dimensional tolerance** (en) / **tolerancia dimensional** (es) / **розмірний допуск** (uk): the permissible difference between the declared and actual dimension: ±1 mm for edged lumber, ±0.3 mm for four-sided planed lumber (PN-EN 1313-1)
- **podawanie dwuetapowe** (pl) / **two-step stacking** (en) / **apilado en dos pasos** (es) / **двоетапне укладання** (uk): placing a board on the stack by tilting and carefully lowering it, never by throwing; prevents stack destabilisation and layer fall
- **długość handlowa** (pl) / **commercial length** (en) / **longitud comercial** (es) / **комерційна довжина** (uk): the standard nominal length of lumber on the Polish market (2000, 2500, 3000, 4000, 5000 mm) + tolerance 10–50 mm, allowing the customer to trim the ends

## Check yourself

1. A board has class A sound knots (25 mm per 3 lm) and an end crack of 1/3 of the board's width. What class?
   - a) A (the knots are class A)
   - b) B (crack 1/3 = class B, weakest-criterion rule)
   - c) Average A/B

2. The customer orders pine A 30 × 150 × 3000 mm. The batch contains 19 boards of pine A 30 × 150 × 3000 mm + 1 board of pine B 30 × 150 × 3000 mm. Is the batch homogeneous?
   - a) Yes, the dimensions and species are identical
   - b) No, a class B board in a class A batch = mixing, the batch is not homogeneous
   - c) Yes, if the difference is only 1 board

3. What is the maximum height of a board stack in the sorting yard?
   - a) 1 m (always safer)
   - b) 2 m (Ministry of Labour and Social Policy Regulation § 64 and industry practice)
   - c) 3 m (limited only by the height of the loader forks)

4. An oak board 50 × 200 × 4000 mm weighs about 28 kg. It can be carried alone by:
   - a) Any operator, without restrictions
   - b) A man alone (within the M 30 kg limit), a woman in a pair (above F 20 kg)
   - c) Only on a transport trolley

5. What does the batch code "DĄ-B-150420-007" mean?
   - a) Oak class B from 15 April 2026, the seventh batch of that day
   - b) Oak class B, day 150, year 2020, batch 7
   - c) An oak batch of 150 kg weight, class B, number 7

**Answers**: 1b, 2b, 3b, 4b, 5a.

## Link to practice

After this lesson, in the sorting yard:

- Go with Marek to 4 existing stacks (any classes and species). Read the labels: batch code, species, class, dimensions, mass. Check whether the label on the board matches the station board.
- Measure the **height** of one stack (from the floor to the top layer). If above 2 m: report to the supervisor (splitting into two stacks required).
- Measure the **gap** between two neighbouring stacks. If below 1 m: report to the supervisor (shifting required).
- Take a pine board 30 × 150 × 3000 mm from the stock (about 7 kg, within every operator's limit). Place it on the stack in two steps (tilt, lower). Compare the impression with a "throw" attempt (with no boards underneath!): you will feel the difference in the force of impact against the surface.
- **Do not classify boards on your own without Marek for the first 2 weeks**. Week 2 lesson 6 taught the criteria, today's lesson showed the application, but the 80/20 discipline requires supervisor control at every class decision for the first 3 months.

## Trainer notes

**Delivery time**: 120 minutes (20 min theory of criteria in the classroom + 30 min walk around the sorting yard reading existing labels + 45 min practice sorting 15–20 boards under supervision + 25 min summary and filling in labels for 4 stacks).

**Resources**: a sorting yard with 6–10 active stations, 20–30 pine boards in various classes (A/B/C) and dimensions (25/30/45 mm × 150/180 mm × 2500/3000 mm) prepared in advance, label cardboards 100 × 60 mm, paper tape, a 5 m measuring tape, a caliper, a calibrated thickness template (optional), the batch-code template written on the training board.

**Most frequent trainee mistakes in this lesson**: (1) assessing the board **only from above**, not from 4 sides; loose knots and end cracks are often on the side. Teach "board rotation": each board turned 4 times, one glance at each side. (2) **Averaging classes** instead of the weakest-criterion rule. The trainee sees a board "80% class A, 20% class B" and wants to give "A minus" or "B plus". Teach: the class is one, it is the lowest of the criteria. (3) **Throwing the board onto the stack**, the natural reflex of speeding up work. Requires a firm reminder at the first 10 boards of a new trainee.

**Bridge to week 3 block 3**: lessons 7–8 (chamber drying) will introduce the last element of the processing chain before shipment: the drying chamber. Oleksij continues through the whole of week 3 (8 lessons, the Sofía narrative pattern in blocks 1+3 of W2). Lesson 7: the role of the chamber loader (species parameters 35–70 °C pine, 30–60 °C oak/beech, cooling ≤6 °C/h). Lesson 8: the protocol for taking the batch out after drying and crack inspection. The sorter's role from today's lesson links to the chamber loader's role: the same batch codes, the same board identity; only the material state changes (moisture 30% → 15%).
