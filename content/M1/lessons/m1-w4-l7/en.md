## Introduction

You have reached the **end of module 1**. Behind you are 29 lessons across 4 weeks: OHS (T1), wood material (T2), work processes (T3), organization (T4). You have written, heard, repeated hundreds of facts, more than 100 industry terms, dozens of procedures.

Now it is time for **consolidation**. This lesson **does not introduce new knowledge**. It does something else: **it distils 29 lessons into 12 great principles** that you are to carry in your head through your first months of work in the sawmill. Each of these principles has its **source** (which lesson it comes from), its **rationale** (why it works), its **application** (when and how you invoke it) and its **typical failure mode** (what happens when you forget it).

Why 12 principles and not 100? Because the human brain under pressure (a shouting operator, a falling board, a sparking cable) **cannot recall 100 rules**. It can recall 5 to 10. That is why OHS trainings around the world (OSHA in the US, HSE in the UK, Polish PIP) always condense knowledge into a **small number of big principles**. The rest are details that come back when you have time to think.

If you remember these 12 principles and understand why each one exists, **you will survive the first month without an accident**. If you forget any of them, you risk yourself, your colleague, and your future (dismissal, loss of permit, deportation).

In the next lesson (l8) you will check whether you remember. Today you revise.

## Objectives

After this lesson:

- **You remember the 12 great principles of M1** and you can list them.
- For each principle you know its **source** (which M1 lesson it comes from).
- You understand **why each principle exists** (what accidents / losses happen without it).
- You can **invoke a principle in a situation** – you have a prepared response to typical sawmill scenarios.
- You know **when each principle activates** and do not confuse principles from different areas.
- You are **ready for the test in l8**. You have structure in your head, not loose facts.

## Content

### Principle 1: „I help but I do not operate"

**Source**: T3 l1 (The helper's role in the process).

**Content**: as a helper you **support the operator** (hand over material, clean, receive, stack, mark). But **you do not touch the machine**: you do not switch it on, you do not adjust it, you do not change the blade, you do not enter the working zone during operation. The boundary is **sharp**: on the operator's side are activities with authorizations (UDT, training, experience); on your side are supporting activities that do not require authorizations.

**Why**: sawmill machines (band saw 95-110 dB, circular saw, planer, moulder, forklift) have **no-forgiveness zones**. A helper who enters such a zone „to help" loses a finger or hand in 2-3 seconds. In extreme cases, life. PIP statistic: 19 out of 23 serious helper accidents in 2017-2022 were the result of crossing the „I help vs I operate" boundary.

**Application**: before you do anything at a machine, ask yourself „is this on my workstation card?". If you do not know, **stop** and ask the foreman. Better to lose 2 minutes than a hand.

**Typical failure**: the operator says „hand me the key, I will loosen the blade". The helper hands it. 5 seconds later the blade drops and the operator reaches for it with the motor running. The helper stood 1 m next to it. A serious accident. **Correct response**: „operator, I have no authorization for blade adjustment. Let us call the mechanic".

### Principle 2: „A sawmill is different" – 6 hazards

**Source**: T1 l1 (Why a sawmill is different) + T1 l2-l7 (each hazard separately).

**Content**: a sawmill **differs from an ordinary workplace** by six hazards that occur at the same time: **mechanical hazards** (band, blade, kickback), **wood dust** (IARC 1 for oak and beech), **noise >95 dB** (hearing loss), **fire** (explosive dust, sparks), **moving weight** (logs, stacks, carts), **unforgiving machines** (1-2 seconds to an accident).

**Why**: in a regular office the hazards are 0-1 (trip, electric shock). In a regular factory 2-3. In a sawmill **6 hazards work in parallel** and **interact** (dust + spark = fire; noise + gestures = no warning; weight + slipperiness = amputation). Statistics: the Polish wood industry has a **3× higher fatal accident rate** than the industry average.

**Application**: when you enter the hall, **consciously assess the 6 hazards**: what do I hear today (noise)? do I have my mask (dust)? what is moving (weight)? where does it spark (fire)? which machine is running (mechanical)? where not to enter (unforgiving)?

**Typical failure**: the helper focuses on one hazard (e.g. only the saw) and ignores others (e.g. the forklift behind him). The forklift hits him. **Correct response**: a constant 360° scan every 1-2 minutes – do not trust that „one bad thing happened, there will not be more".

### Principle 3: PPE – 6 items, gloves with an exception

**Source**: T1 l5 (Protective clothing and PPE).

**Content**: **6 mandatory PPE items** in a sawmill: safety helmet, S3 steel-toe boots, safety goggles, gloves, hearing protection, dust mask (FFP2 pine dust, FFP3 oak / beech). **EXCEPTION**: gloves are **NOT to be worn** at **rotating machines** (band saw, circular saw, planer) – because a glove may get caught and pull the hand in.

**Why**: each PPE item eliminates one of the 6 hazards (principle 2). Without a helmet – a board to the head. Without S3 boots – a falling piece pierces the foot. Without goggles – a splinter in the eye. Without a mask – oak dust = cancer. Without ear protection – hearing loss in 5-10 years. With gloves at the saw – the hand gets pulled in.

**Application**: before entering the hall **every day**, 30 seconds of check: helmet? boots? goggles? gloves (if not at a machine)? ear protection? mask? If any is missing, **you do not enter the hall**. You go back to the locker / warehouse.

**Typical failure**: „it is hot, I will not put the mask on", „I see well, I do not need the goggles", „just for a moment without gloves, I will throw a board on the pallet". Each of these exceptions has cost someone's health. **Correct response**: PPE **is not optional**. It is like a seatbelt in a car: you wear it 100% of the time, not „when it suits you".

### Principle 4: Labour Code art. 210 – right to refuse, „red workstation"

**Source**: T1 l8 (Evacuation and STOP) + T4 l2 (Workstation preparation) + T4 l5 (Paperwork) + T4 l6 (Reporting).

**Content**: **Labour Code art. 210 § 1**: „An employee has the right to refrain from performing work if the working conditions do not comply with OHS rules and pose a direct threat to the employee's life or health". **Labour Code art. 210 § 5**: for exercising this right he **cannot be punished**. It is your legal **shield**.

**Why**: without this provision the employer could force work on a broken machine, without PPE, in a toxic environment. With this provision **you decide** whether the conditions are safe. If you consider them unsafe, **you refuse** and report to the foreman.

**Application**: „red workstation" = the situation in which you refuse. Saw without a guard = red. Dust at full-visibility level = red. A drunk colleague on shift = red. The foreman forces you to use a forklift without a UDT license = red. In each of these situations you say „foreman, I am refraining from work due to a hazard, I am logging it, Labour Code art. 210 § 1".

**Typical failure**: the migrant is afraid to refuse, because „they will fire me" or „the foreman will get mad". He works under hazardous conditions. An accident follows. **Correct response**: Labour Code art. 210 § 5 protects you legally. If the foreman punishes you for refusing, call PIP 801 002 006, the case is won; in practice employers do not do this because the court will force reinstatement.

### Principle 5: 2 seconds to decide

**Source**: T3 l5 (Feeding the operator) + T4 l3 (Gestures) + scattered T1.

**Content**: when working with sawmill machines you have **1-2 seconds to react** to an alarm signal. An operator's STOP gesture = 1 second, no more. A shout „UWAGA!" = 1 second to step aside. A log kickback = 0.5 seconds to duck or flee. **The decision must be automatic**, not thought-through.

**Why**: muscles react in 0.2-0.3 seconds to a known stimulus. But only when the reaction has been **drilled many times**. A „considered" decision (cortex) takes 2-3 seconds. In a sawmill 2-3 seconds can be the difference between health and amputation. PIP statistic: in 19 of 23 serious helper accidents the helper reacted **after 3 seconds** or not at all.

**Application**: **drill alarm situations** with a colleague in breaks. STOP gesture → you step aside in 1 sec. Shout „UWAGA!" → step sideways + eye contact. Kickback → duck or jump back. After 4-5 repetitions of each situation the reaction becomes automatic.

**Typical failure**: the helper „thinks" at an alarm signal. „Why is the operator giving STOP? Probably to explain something". Meanwhile STOP means „**stop everything now**", without explanation. **Correct response**: you react first, you ask later.

### Principle 6: The 15+30 ritual / 6-point list

**Source**: T4 l1 (Daily rhythm) + T4 l2 (Workstation preparation).

**Content**: a day in a sawmill starts with the **15+30 ritual**: the first **15 minutes** (6:45-7:00) are the morning briefing + 5-minute safety talk. The next **30 minutes** (7:00-7:30) are the **6-point micro-ritual** of preparing the workstation: (1) PPE checked, (2) workstation cleanliness, (3) walkways clear, (4) lighting 300-500 lux, (5) tools within reach, (6) emergency stop within reach and working. **Only then** do you work.

**Why**: 80% of „first-hour accidents" in a sawmill are caused by skipping one of the 6 points. Example: lighting not checked (cloudy day, 200 lux instead of 500) → operator does not see a log defect → kickback → helper hit. A pilot analogy: even the most experienced pilot goes through a checklist **before every flight**. Not because he is incompetent – because routine is the most dangerous thing.

**Application**: every day the 6-point list **out loud** (even under your breath): „PPE – present. Clean – yes. Walkways – yes. Light – yes. Tools – here. Switch – working". Saying words instead of silent „checking" activates working memory and reduces the chance of skipping.

**Typical failure**: „I am in a hurry, foreman is nervous, I start without the checklist". Within 2 hours an accident involving the item you skipped. **Correct response**: checklist **always**, even if it takes 7 minutes. Better 7 minutes lost than a week in hospital.

### Principle 7: Manual handling – limits M 30/50, W 12/20

**Source**: T3 l2 (Manual handling + Regulation 2017).

**Content**: a **man** may lift at most **30 kg at one time** + 50 kg with two people. A **woman**: 12 kg at one time + 20 kg with two people. Above these values – **hand cart, forklift or crane** (operated with UDT authorizations). There are no exceptions. **Regulation of the Minister of Labour of 18 May 2017** on OHS in manual handling.

**Why**: the lumbar spine under habitual load >30 kg undergoes **accelerated degeneration**. After 10 years of working above the limit – disc disease, surgery, disability pension. CIOP-PIB statistics: 47% of wood-industry employees after 15 years have permanent spine damage, mostly linked to exceeding manual-handling limits.

**Application**: before lifting, **estimate the weight**. A pine board 3 m × 5 cm × 10 cm = ~7 kg, OK. An oak beam 2 m × 10 cm × 10 cm = ~25 kg, OK. A fresh pine log 2 m × diameter 40 cm = ~200 kg – **cart or crane**. Rule of thumb: if you need to „tense up", you may be over the limit. **Ask** the foreman or use a scale (often in the warehouse).

**Typical failure**: „I will lift it, I am strong". Young men 20-25 years old most often exceed limits. At age 35-45 the first spine surgery. **Correct response**: the limit is a **legal norm**, not a suggestion. Like a speed limit on the road.

### Principle 8: The log yard – #1 fatality statistic

**Source**: T3 l3 (Log yard) + T1 l6 (Work zones).

**Content**: the **log yard** (outdoor, in front of the hall) is the place with the **highest fatal-accident statistic** in the Polish wood industry. Causes: logs roll, stacks collapse, forklifts manoeuvre, slippery surface (mud, ice, offcuts), icy logs have variable grip. Rules: **5 situations of departure** from the zone (a forklift is coming, a stack is shifting, a log is rolling, a crack sounds, the operator shouts „uwaga!") – each requires **an immediate step back 3-5 meters**.

**Why**: PIP + ZUS statistics for the Polish wood industry 2017-2022: **37% of fatal accidents** in the industry happen on the log yard. A log weighs 100-500 kg; a rolling log has kinetic energy enough to crush a chest. A person falling among logs is often not found immediately (body hidden, visibility 1-2 m).

**Application**: on the log yard you **always** stand so that you **see the machine and colleagues** (peripheral field of view). You **never** stand behind a log, behind a stack, in a corner. Wearing a **high-visibility vest** is mandatory (so the forklift sees you). After every activity at a log – **2 steps back** and assess the situation before the next move.

**Typical failure**: the helper steps behind a log to „shelter from the wind". The log rolls, pins him to the stack behind. Found 30 minutes later. **Correct response**: on the log yard you **never hide behind anything**. Visibility beats comfort.

### Principle 9: 5 gestures + 5 commands + eye contact

**Source**: T3 l5 (Feeding + ISO 7010) + T4 l3 (Gestures) + T4 l4 (Words).

**Content**: communication in a sawmill works in **three layers**:
1. **Non-verbal** (hall running, >95 dB): **5 basic gestures** – STOP (hand above the head, palm open), PODAJ (hand pulling toward you), CZEKAJ (hand forward, palm open to you), ODSUŃ SIĘ (hand to the side + pushing motion), COFNIJ (fingers to self + back). **Precondition**: eye contact.
2. **Verbal** (hall stopped, quiet): **5 alarm commands** – UWAGA, STOP, ODSUŃ SIĘ, ZABLOKUJ, PUŚĆ/TRZYMAJ.
3. **Contact layer**: without eye contact a **gesture is useless**. 4 ways to establish it: step into field of view, peripheral wave, touch from behind (never from the front at a machine), whistle.

**Why**: an operator working at a machine **does not hear words** (ear protection + noise) and **does not look at you** (he looks at the blade). If you try to warn him with words, he will not react in time. A peripheral gesture + STOP → reaction in 1 second.

**Application**: before any move toward a machine – **eye contact**. Then gesture. You wait for confirmation (nod, DOBRZE/OK). Only then do you act.

**Typical failure**: the helper enters the machine zone without eye contact, because „the operator knows I am coming". The operator does not know, turns with the blade, hits the helper. **Correct response**: eye contact **every time**, even when it seems unnecessary.

### Principle 10: 4 workstation documents

**Source**: T4 l5 (Basic paperwork).

**Content**: **4 documents** are at your workstation **every day**:
1. **Workstation card** (filled in by the foreman, you sign after the induction, valid 3 years) – permitted and forbidden activities, PPE, hazards, training.
2. **Attendance log** (you fill in daily) – arrival and departure time, signature. Basis for wages and insurance.
3. **Minor-incident log** (written by whoever noticed) – cuts, failures, near-misses, non-compliance. Formula 5W1H.
4. **Tool-use authorization** (issued by the foreman after training, valid 1 year) – hand tools YES after authorization, machines NO (require UDT).

**Why**: without documents you have no **legal status of a protected employee**. In an accident the insurer asks: „did he have a workstation card?", „did he have authorization for that tool?". If missing – accident „not related to work" – treatment at your own cost.

**Application**: on day one **you read all 4 documents**. You do not sign blind. You ask the foreman about every unclear point. Ask for a translation – the EGIDA foundation has EN/ES/UK templates.

**Typical failure**: the migrant signs the workstation card without reading („the foreman says it is standard"). A month later he uses a tool that was not in the card. Cut. Insurer: „use of tool without authorization, treatment at your own cost". **Correct response**: read everything. If you do not understand – translation.

### Principle 11: Escalation pyramid + 5W1H

**Source**: T4 l6 (Reporting incidents).

**Content**: a report of an accident / failure / hazard / near-miss has a **5-level pyramid**:
- Level 1: **foreman** (always first, immediately, orally).
- Level 2: **shift manager** (if foreman absent or matter more serious, within 15-60 min).
- Level 3: **OHS specialist** (within 24 h, in writing).
- Level 4: **employer** (serious / fatal accidents, 14 days / immediately).
- Level 5: **PIP, prosecutor, 112** (public, anonymous 801 002 006).

**Formula 5W1H**: **what**, **when**, **where**, **who** (witness), **why** (cause hypothesis), **how** (what it looked like). Used orally to the foreman (30 sec) and in writing in the log (detailed).

**Why**: without the escalation pyramid reports **get lost in the organization** – the foreman does not pass them on, the manager does not know, the OHS specialist has no time. With the pyramid each level **activates by weight**, nothing is lost. 5W1H is the minimum set of information for another person to **make a decision**.

**Application**: every report = 5W1H, orally to the foreman. If the foreman does not respond, you go to level 2 yourself. If a serious accident – 112 immediately, you do not wait for the foreman.

**Typical failure**: „I will tell the foreman tomorrow", „I will log it when I have time". The matter slips, repeats, ends in an accident. **Correct response**: **without delay** = immediately, per Labour Code art. 211 pt 6.

### Principle 12: Reporting culture – Heinrich 1:30:300:3000

**Source**: T4 l6 (Reporting) + T1 l1 (recurring motif).

**Content**: the **Heinrich statistic** (classic in OHS, 1931, repeatedly confirmed): for **1 serious accident** there are **30 minor accidents**, **300 near-misses** and **3 000 unsafe behaviours**. Conclusion: **every minor report prevents a serious accident**. „Trivial, not worth it" is the **most dangerous myth in a sawmill**.

**Why**: minor events are **warning signs**. A small crack in the guard → break → belt hit. A wet log → slip → kickback → impact. Dust on the floor → spark → fire. Every chain starts from a small thing. If you break the chain **at the first link**, the accident does not happen.

**Application**: **you report everything**. A crooked board, a weak grip, an unusual sound, a tired colleague, a strange-smelling log, a poorly visible sign. In good sawmills (Stora Enso, IKEA Industry, Paged, Barlinek) reports are **rewarded** (PLN 500-3 000 for a report that prevented an accident). In regular ones – at least not punished.

**Typical failure**: the 3 blocking myths (T4 l6): „I do not want to get my colleague in trouble", „it is trivial, not worth it", „I will be punished for reporting". Each is debunked by Labour Code art. 210 § 5 + the Heinrich statistic + the cost of silence. **Correct response**: reporting is a **professional reflex**, not „snitching".

### The M1 arc: from „why" to „how"

Look at the M1 structure as a **narrative arc**:

- **T1 OHS (why)**: 8 lessons on hazards. Why a sawmill is different, why PPE, why zones, why first aid, why evacuation.
- **T2 Material (what you work with)**: 8 lessons on wood. Log, species, defects, hand tools, machines, transport, dry / wet wood.
- **T3 Processes (what you do)**: 8 lessons on activities. The helper's role, manual handling, log yard, timber stack, feeding, hand tools, housekeeping, marking.
- **T4 Organization (how you work in the system)**: 8 lessons on coordination. Daily rhythm, workstation, gestures, words, documents, reporting + recap + test.

**„Why" (T1) → „what with" (T2) → „what you do" (T3) → „how you work in the system" (T4)**. The logic is: you cannot work safely if you do not understand the hazards (T1). You cannot work efficiently if you do not know the material (T2). You cannot work competently if you do not know the processes (T3). You cannot work professionally if you do not understand the organization (T4).

M1 = **the foundation**. M2 covers specific workstations (saw operator, warehouse keeper, stacker). M3 is professional practice and certification. Without M1, M2 and M3 make no sense – that is why the helper module is mandatory **for everyone**, even for those who will not ultimately be helpers.

## Notatki dla trenera

**Pedagogical goal**: consolidate 29 M1 lessons into **12 principles remembered long term**. Practice active recall instead of passive recognition. Prepare trainees for the l8 test by structuring knowledge in their heads.

**120-minute rhythm**:

- **0–10 min**: opening. „Why 12 principles, not 100?". Discussion. Working-memory limits (5-9 items, Miller 1956). Condensation as a pedagogical tool.
- **10–80 min**: **12 principles in 12 five-minute capsules**. For each principle: (a) trainer names it, (b) trainees try to recall the source and content (30 sec, alone), (c) trainer adds the rationale and typical failure, (d) a quick check question („when does principle 3 have an exception?", „when do we do the 6-point list?"). Rotation through 12 principles = 60 minutes.
- **80–100 min**: **association exercise**. The trainer reads random M1 situations („the operator calls you to change the blade", „the foreman says a forklift is OK without a license", „a nervous colleague is shouting, gestures not visible"). The trainees say: **which principle activates?** and **what do you do?**. 6-8 situations, group-wide.
- **100–115 min**: **communication triad + M1 arc**. Summary: non-verbal → verbal → written. T1 why → T4 how. Board, drawing. Trainees copy to notes.
- **115–120 min**: key terms. Announce l8: closed questions + 3 scenarios + practical tasks. Threshold 70%.

**Common mistake (1)**: the trainee **mixes up the source** of a principle. E.g. assigns principle 6 (15+30) to T3 because „processes". Correction: every principle has a **specific lesson** (or two). Knowing the source helps: if you forget a principle, you go back to the lesson.

**Common mistake (2)**: the trainee **can repeat the principle but cannot apply it**. Question: „what do you do when the operator gives STOP?" – answer „I apply principle 5 of two seconds". Correction: that is not enough. He has to finish: „I step aside 2-3 m toward a safe side, without asking why". **Passive knowledge ≠ skill**.

**Practical recommendation**: hand the trainees an **A4 sheet** with a table of 12 principles: column 1 number + name, column 2 source, column 3 rationale in one sentence, column 4 typical failure in one sentence. Laminated, in the PPE pocket. Use for 3 months. After 3 months throw it away (in the head already).

**Link to the next lesson**: l8 „Practical Test". Verification of mastery of the 12 principles + selected deeper areas. Format: ~30 multiple-choice questions (4 answers, 1 correct) + 3 decision scenarios (descriptive) + 5-7 practical tasks (recognizing a gesture, pronouncing a command, formulating a 5W1H report). Pass threshold 70%. The key is in a separate Phase 4 artefact.
