## Introduction

Thursday, 7:00 a.m., the circular-saw hall of the EGIDA Foundation sawmill. Oleksii has spent the previous three days on the Wood-Mizer LT40 number two band saw: Monday start-up, Tuesday roller adjustment, Wednesday first production cutting of oak. Today he changes workstation. Marek walks him to the other side of the hall, to the **Storti DDM-200 edger**, a machine that processes the unedged lumber coming straight off the band saw. The oak boards 150 × 25 × 3000 mm that Oleksii cut on Wednesday are stacked on a cart: they have wane on two sides (bark edges), and they need to be edged to a rectangular cross section. This is where the band saw ends and the circular saw begins.

At first glance the edger looks nothing like a band saw: instead of one long vertical blade running on two wheels, you see **two horizontal circular blades, 350 mm in diameter**, set parallel about 130 mm apart, protruding 40 mm above the feed table. Material enters on a roller conveyor between the blades and leaves edged on both sides in a single pass. Marek hands Oleksii a 500 mm wooden push stick and says: "Rip saw. Same rules as the band saw: check, start, feed, but with three differences. **Riving knife, adjustable guard, push stick.** Without those three, you don't go near the blade."

This lesson introduces the **circular saw** as the second wood-processing workstation you are allowed to approach under supervision, after the band saw. The edger is a **rip circular saw**: it cuts along the grain, along the length of the board. A **cross-cut circular saw** cuts across the grain and is used to trim logs or lumber to a defined length. Two designs, one cutting principle (a rotating toothed blade), but different risk geometries.

The lesson ends with an **introduction to the multi-rip saw**, a machine with 2 to 12 blades working simultaneously that processes a cant into several boards in a single pass. The multi-rip is an **extension of the rip circular saw**, not a new category. But its risks are multiplied. You will not operate it this month. You do, however, need to know how it differs and when you will return to it in your professional path.

## Objectives

After this lesson:

1. You understand that the **riving knife** (Ministry of Economy Regulation of 14 April 2000, § 10 item 3) is a mandatory component of a rip circular saw, positioned 2–10 mm behind the blade, with a thickness 0.1–0.2 mm smaller than the kerf width. Its job: to separate the material after cutting so that boards do not jam on the blade and get hurled back toward the operator at 30–40 m/s (the **kickback** phenomenon). Lack of a riving knife accounts for **85% of all kickbacks** according to studies by the German BGHW (Berufsgenossenschaft Holz und Metall).
2. You know the **6 start-up steps for a circular saw** under supervision: (1) check of the fixed guard (over the inactive part of the blade) and the adjustable guard (which lowers onto the material, Ministry of Economy Regulation § 11), (2) check of the riving knife (position 2–10 mm behind the blade, M10 bolts tightened, longitudinal movement locked), (3) STOP test (PN-EN ISO 13850 mushroom button, left hand before right hand on START), (4) START and 30 s idle run (observation: no rubbing of the blade against the guard, even motor sound at 2000–4000 rpm), (5) test cut on a scrap board, (6) start of production cutting with a push stick.
3. You know how to use a **push stick** in two situations: the **short push stick** when the last 300 mm of material are passing through the blade (no hand closer than 300 mm to the blade); the **narrow push stick** when you are cutting a board less than 150 mm wide (your hand would naturally be in line with the blade). The push stick is **wooden** (never metal: kickback with a metal push stick means an additional projectile), 400–600 mm long, with a cut-out notch on the bottom for stable pressure on the material.
4. You distinguish a **rip circular saw** (cuts along the grain, kickback along the axis of the board, riving knife mandatory) from a **cross-cut circular saw** (cuts across the grain, kickback directed sideways and upward, riving knife does not apply because the material does not jam longitudinally). Edgers are rip saws; lumber trimmers are cross-cut saws.
5. You know that a **multi-rip saw** has 2–12 blades working simultaneously on a shared shaft, operates with an **automatic feed of 10–80 m/min** (three times faster than manual feeding on a single rip saw), and requires **master authorization** after a minimum of 6 months on a single rip saw plus additional UDT training. You do not operate one in this course, but a junior is allowed to **feed material onto the infeed conveyor** under the direct supervision of a senior operator.

### The riving knife: the first condition for access

A **riving knife** (German *Spaltkeil*, English *riving knife*) is a flat, curved steel plate positioned **behind the blade** (in the direction of material feed), with a width 0.1–0.2 mm **smaller** than the blade kerf. Here is why: after cutting, the material splits into two parts that tend to **jam on the back of the blade** (especially when the wood is internally stressed: fresh pine, oak with false heartwood). If the material jams, the blade spinning at 3000 rpm (350 mm diameter, peripheral speed 55 m/s) **will throw the board** forward toward the operator at 30–40 m/s (108–144 km/h). That is the kickback phenomenon. The riving knife mechanically separates the boards before jamming can occur.

**Four parameters of the riving knife** you check before every start-up:

1. **Distance from the blade**: 2–10 mm (less means risk of tooth-to-knife contact when the blade flexes, more means jamming can begin before the knife engages).
2. **Height above the table**: the knife stands 2–5 mm below the highest tooth of the blade (no higher, or it would block the cutting of thin boards).
3. **Thickness**: 0.1–0.2 mm less than the blade kerf. Example: a 350 × 3.2 mm blade needs a 3.0–3.1 mm knife. The nameplates must match.
4. **Position locked**: two M10 bolts with lock nuts (Nord-Lock or a nut with a counter-nut). Play means the knife shifts during operation, meaning the protection disappears.

**Law**: Ministry of Economy Regulation of 14 April 2000 on the minimum requirements for woodworking machines, § 10 item 3: "Rip circular saws must be equipped with a riving knife." No knife means the machine is unfit, meaning **it must not be started**. You have the right to refuse work under Art. 210 § 1 of the Labour Code (see Lesson 5 of Week 1), just as with missing guards.

### The adjustable guard: the second condition for access

A rip circular saw has **two guards**: a **fixed** guard (covers the rear, inactive part of the blade) and an **adjustable** guard (lowers onto the material, covers the blade in the active area). The adjustable guard shields the operator's hand from chips and splinters, and in a minor kickback it acts as a screen.

Setting rule: **a gap no greater than 5 mm above the material**. Higher means a window through which a hand or chip can reach. Resting on the material means friction blocks movement, so the operator lifts it and "forgets" to lower it. On the Storti edger Oleksii checks it this way: he places a 25 mm board under the blade, lowers the guard until contact, then raises it by the thickness of a sheet of paper (≤1 mm).

**Law**: § 11 of the Regulation of 14 April 2000 requires a gap no larger than necessary for the work. Polish practice: 5 mm maximum.

### Circular-saw start-up procedure: 6 steps

The procedure is **analogous to the band saw** (Lesson 1 of this week), but with three differences: step 1 covers **two guards** (fixed and adjustable, not one), step 2 covers the **riving knife rather than the condition of the blade strap**, and steps 5–6 cover **feeding material with a push stick**, not a freehand cut.

**Step 1: Guard check (30 seconds).** Fixed guard over the rear of the blade: tightened with 4 M8 bolts, 6 mm Allen key, no play. Adjustable guard: lowers freely, returns on its spring, no binding. Gap ≤5 mm above the target material.

**Step 2: Riving-knife check (20 seconds).** Distance 2–10 mm from the blade (millimetre-scale ruler, Marek keeps one on the console), height 2–5 mm below the highest tooth (visual check), knife nameplate thickness matching the blade nameplate, two M10 lock bolts tightened.

**Step 3: STOP button test (10 seconds).** Mushroom button on the edger console: protrudes 30 mm in the ready position, latched 10 mm in after being pressed. Oleksii runs the test without starting the blade: press, release, press, release. **Left hand on STOP before the right on START**: a mechanical habit from Lesson 3 of Week 1, which returns on every machine until the end of your career.

**Step 4: START and 30-second idle run (30 seconds).** Loud announcement: "Starting edger number one, thirty-second idle run!" START. Observation with 3 senses: even motor sound with no "puffing" (the blade is not rubbing against the guard), no vibration transmitted through the table to the hand (operator's hand resting lightly on the machine frame), no smell of burning motor. **Specific to the circular saw**: observation of the rpm gauge (2000–4000 rpm in the green zone, a 350 mm blade typically at 3000 rpm = peripheral speed of 55 m/s).

**Step 5: Test cut (30 seconds).** Scrap pine board 25 × 150 × 500 mm. Single feed, in line with the blade axis, never in the line of ejection (the **"never stand in line with the blade"** rule: sideways body stance, right foot forward, left foot back diagonally). The test cut evaluates: straightness of the edge, absence of smoke, absence of waviness, even cutting sound.

**Step 6: Production cutting with a push stick (lasts for hours).** Board after board, one at a time, with the push stick engaged in the last 300 mm or when the board is less than 150 mm wide.

### The push stick: the third condition for access

**Short push stick (<300 mm from the blade).** As the material passes through the blade, a 300 mm section remains before the blade in the final phase. If the operator pushed it with a hand, the hand would be **closer than 300 mm from the rotating 3000 rpm blade**. That is the kill zone: on a kickback or a slip of the hand, amputation of four fingers takes 0.1 seconds. The solution: a wooden push stick 400–600 mm long with a cut-out notch on the bottom, replacing the hand in the last 300 mm.

**Narrow push stick (board width <150 mm).** When you are cutting a 100 mm wide board, the hand holding the board from the side is **in line with the blade** (the natural hand position is the middle of the board). The solution: a push stick on the side adjacent to the fence, with the outside hand kept out of the blade's reach (at least 200 mm sideways from the blade).

**Push-stick material: wood**, never metal. On a kickback, a metal push stick becomes an additional projectile flying at 30 m/s. A wooden push stick breaks, but does not injure.

Oleksii opens the cabinet next to the edger: inside are 6 push sticks of different sizes, each marked with the date of manufacture and the initials of the operator (Tadeusz Kowalski carved three of them 2 months ago). Marek shows him which one fits the 25 × 150 mm oak: a 500 mm push stick with a 25 mm notch.

### Rip or cross-cut: two risk geometries

A **rip circular saw** cuts along the grain. The material enters the blade along its long side, the kerf runs parallel to the fibres, and the kickback **travels along the axis of the board** toward the operator. That is why the sideways body stance and the riving knife are critical. The edger, the table saw, and the multi-rip saw are all rip circular saws.

A **cross-cut circular saw** cuts across the grain (short side under the blade); the kickback is directed sideways and upward (perpendicular fibres break easily rather than jam), so the riving knife does not apply, but **the lower guard must surround the blade on three sides**. A junior identifies these two categories by the direction of material feed: long side means rip, short side means cross-cut.

### The multi-rip saw: a preview, not operation

In the second part of the hall stands the **Paul Y-40M**, a multi-rip saw with 4 blades of 300 mm on a single shaft and an automatic feed of 25 m/min. It processes a cant (the product of the band saw, Lesson 5 of Week 2) into 4 boards in a single pass (7 seconds per 3 m × 200 mm cant). A junior **does not operate** this machine, but learns to recognize it as an extension of the rip saw, not a new category.

**Three reasons why the multi-rip requires master authorization and UDT certification**:

- **4× more blades = 4× more directions of kickback at the same time.** The riving knife works separately at each blade, but a single jammed board from the cant strikes the neighbouring board: a domino effect.
- **Automatic feed without operator control**: the machine runs on its own from the moment the cant is fed in. Stopping requires the STOP mushroom on the console or the safety line above the conveyor.
- **Formal requirements**: a minimum of 6 months on a single rip saw, a UDT course, 20–30 cuts under direct supervision before working independently.

A junior is allowed to: feed cants onto the infeed conveyor (a zone 2 m in front of the machine), receive boards from the outfeed conveyor, report irregularities. **Not allowed**: operating the console, adjusting blades, approaching closer than 2 m while the machine is running.

::: warning
**The "one piece through the saw" rule** (applies to every circular saw, especially a rip saw):

- One board in the blade at a time. Never two. Never three.
- While one board is still in the cut, the next one waits on the infeed table, at least 500 mm behind the first.
- Exception: the multi-rip (a cant yields 4 boards in a single cut, but then the material is the cant, not 4 boards).

Reason: two boards of different thickness under the blade cause asymmetric loading, the blade flexes, the riving knife stops working, and the kickback reaches 30–40 m/s.
:::

### Oleksii's case: the first 40 boards and the first sign of wear

Thursday, 7:30, Oleksii finishes the 6-step procedure (knife with 4 parameters, guards checked, test cut on pine 25 × 150 × 500 mm with no findings). Production START. The first 40 oak boards 25 × 150 × 3000 mm from Wednesday's band-saw session go smoothly: single feed, push stick in the last 300 mm, 25 seconds per board. Marek stands 2 m behind the workstation, observing, saying nothing.

Board 41 comes out with a wavy edge (amplitude 1.5 mm, wavelength 20 mm). Oleksii sees it immediately: sign 2 from Lesson 3, waviness of the cut. Left hand on STOP. "Marek, please take a look: waviness." Marek checks the edge, then the blade: one tooth chipped, chip 3 × 2 mm. "Good catch. Blade change in two hours; for now we finish the batch slower, feed 15 m/min instead of 25."

**Lesson of the day**: signs of blade wear demand **the same discipline as signs of band-strap wear** (band saw, Lesson 3). Cracks, chipped teeth, blue patches on the body, waviness of the cut: every one of them means STOP plus a report. An unreported sign is amputation in 5 minutes, not in 5 weeks.

## Key terms

- **klin rozszczepiający** (pl) / **riving knife** (en) / **cuchillo divisor** (es) / **розклинюючий ніж** (uk): steel plate behind the blade of a rip saw, 0.1–0.2 mm thinner than the kerf, mandatory under Ministry of Economy Regulation § 10 item 3
- **popychacz** (pl) / **push stick** (en) / **empujador** (es) / **штовхач** (uk): wooden tool 400–600 mm long that replaces the hand in the last 300 mm of material or when the board is <150 mm wide
- **osłona nastawna** (pl) / **adjustable guard** (en) / **protección regulable** (es) / **регульований захисний кожух** (uk): guard lowered onto the material, gap ≤5 mm, Ministry of Economy Regulation § 11
- **pilarka tarczowa wzdłużna** (pl) / **rip circular saw** (en) / **sierra circular longitudinal** (es) / **поздовжня циркулярна пилка** (uk): cuts along the grain, kickback along the axis of the board, riving knife mandatory (edger, table saw, multi-rip)
- **pilarka tarczowa poprzeczna** (pl) / **cross-cut circular saw** (en) / **sierra circular transversal** (es) / **поперечна циркулярна пилка** (uk): cuts across the grain, kickback directed sideways and upward, riving knife does not apply (trimmers)
- **pilarka wielopiłowa** (pl) / **multi-rip saw** (en) / **sierra multihoja** (es) / **багатопильна пилка** (uk): 2–12 blades on a shared shaft, automatic feed 10–80 m/min, requires master authorization and UDT certification
- **odrzut** (pl) / **kickback** (en) / **rechazo** (es) / **відкид** (uk): ejection of material from the workstation at 30–40 m/s due to jamming on the blade, responsible for 85% of serious accidents on rip circular saws

## Check yourself

1. The blade of a rip circular saw has a kerf 3.2 mm wide. What should the riving-knife thickness be?
   - a) 3.0–3.1 mm (0.1–0.2 mm smaller)
   - b) 3.2 mm (identical)
   - c) 3.3–3.4 mm (0.1–0.2 mm larger)

2. You are cutting a pine board 100 mm wide. Do you need a push stick?
   - a) Yes, a narrow push stick: the board is <150 mm, the hand would end up in line with the blade
   - b) No, as long as the board is >300 mm long, because the hand is far from the blade
   - c) Only at the end of the cut, when <300 mm of material remain

3. A cross-cut circular saw (trimmer) has no riving knife. Why?
   - a) Because when cutting perpendicular to the grain the material does not jam longitudinally, so the knife is useless
   - b) Because the requirements of Ministry of Economy Regulation § 10 item 3 apply only to rip saws
   - c) Both answers above are correct

4. A multi-rip saw has 4 blades working simultaneously. What is the most important additional risk compared with a single rip saw?
   - a) Kickback from 4 blades at the same time, a domino effect of jammed boards
   - b) Higher blade rotation speed
   - c) Noisier operation

5. You notice waviness in the cut after 40 boards. What do you do?
   - a) Continue, because the batch has to be finished
   - b) STOP, left hand on the mushroom button, report to Marek
   - c) Drop the feed to minimum and keep cutting without reporting

**Answers**: 1a, 2a, 3c, 4a, 5b.

## Link to practice

After this lesson, in the circular-saw hall:

- Walk up to the edger (with Marek or a senior). Without starting the machine, identify: the riving knife (point to it), the fixed guard, the adjustable guard, the console with the STOP mushroom, the cabinet with push sticks.
- Measure the **distance of the knife from the blade** with a millimetre-scale ruler: it must be 2–10 mm. Write the result in your notebook.
- Walk up to the cross-cut saw (if the sawmill has one). Point out 3 design differences compared with the edger: direction of material feed, lower guard surrounding the blade on three sides, absence of the riving knife.
- Find the multi-rip saw (30–40% of sawmills have one). From a distance of 3 m identify: the infeed conveyor, the outfeed conveyor, the safety line hanging over the workstation, the console with the STOP mushroom. **Do not go closer than 2 m while the machine is running.**

This month you will assist at the single rip saw. In 4–6 months, after 100+ hours at the edger, Marek will introduce you to operating the console of the rip circular saw under direct supervision. The multi-rip: not before a year from now, after a UDT course.

## Trainer notes

**Delivery time**: 120 minutes (45 min theory in the classroom + 45 min hall walk-through + 30 min hands-on procedure).

**Resources**: sawmill edger (any design with 1–2 rip blades), 6 wooden push sticks of different lengths, steel ruler with a mm scale, a demo saw blade (can be worn), a demo riving knife, a printout of the Ministry of Economy Regulation of 14 April 2000, § 10 and § 11, a set of 5 pine boards 25 × 150 × 500 mm for the test cut.

**Two most common trainee mistakes**: (1) confusing the rip circular saw with the cross-cut: the trainee looks at the blade (round, spinning) and treats it as one workstation, ignoring the different kickback directions and the different knife obligations. Teach recognition by direction of material feed (long side = rip, short side = cross-cut), not by blade shape. (2) Failing to notice the riving knife: the knife is an unassuming plate standing 10 mm above the table; the trainee looks at the blade, fails to see the knife, and assumes it is not there. Teach "check behind the blade, not in front of it".

**Push-stick discipline and the multi-rip boundary**: the push stick is ALWAYS ready in the cabinet at the workstation, not in the toolbox. When the trainee does not see a push stick at hand, he stops cutting, finds a push stick, and resumes. "I'll just quickly finish this one board by hand" is the PIP amputation history of 2019–2023. For the multi-rip, do not teach operation; teach recognition. After this lesson the trainee is able to say: "That is a multi-rip, I do not operate it, I can feed a cant from 2 m away." Authorization for independent single rip-saw work: a minimum of 100 hours under supervision, 4–6 months. Multi-rip: a minimum of 6 months on the single saw + UDT + 20–30 cuts under supervision.

**Bridge to Lesson 5**: the next lesson (edger and planer, position and feeding) extends the topic of this area of the hall. Oleksii continues at the Storti and also works with the Weinig 4-sided planer: positioned to the side, not in the ejection axis.
