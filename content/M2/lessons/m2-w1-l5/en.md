## Introduction

The sixth day of week 1. Andrij closed Friday with a 5-point checklist (lesson 4). Monday, new week, new protagonist: **Oleh, a Ukrainian from Chernihiv, 27 years old**. In his previous workplace near Warsaw he was an assistant for a year and a half; now he is in his first month as a junior operator of a format circular saw. New sawmill, new machine, new rank.

On Monday morning, the foreman places an A4 printout on his table and says: **"Read this, underline what applies to your machine, and give it back to me tomorrow."** Oleh looks at the header:

> *Regulation of the Minister of Economy of 14 April 2000 on occupational health and safety in the operation of woodworking machinery. Dz.U. 2000 nr 36 poz. 409.*

Oleh thinks to himself: **"Why do I need a page from the ministry? I have a saw here, not a government office."** This is exactly the moment when most operators make the first mistake of week 2: **they confuse law with bureaucracy**. For the operator of a woodworking machine, the law is not bureaucracy. It is a **shield** that you can use when the machine is faulty and the foreman says, "run it, we will tighten it later".

In lesson 2 you learned the 5 PPE items and the ban on gloves near rotating parts; in lesson 3 the STOP > START principle and emergency switches; in lesson 4 the 5-point checklist. All three had one hidden source: this single regulation. Today we open it to see **what the law requires from your machine and from you**, and where to check the full text when in doubt.

## Objectives

After this lesson:

1. You know what Rozp. MG of 14.04.2000 is (Polish Ministry of Economy Regulation of 2000) and why it applies to every woodworking machine in Poland, regardless of year of manufacture.
2. You know the four requirements that your machine must meet: **brake** (coast-down up to 10 seconds), **guards** (fixed and adjustable), **anti-kickback** (riving knife), **no-glove rule** near rotating parts.
3. You can recognise in 30 seconds whether a specific saw meets these four requirements, and you know what to do if it does not.
4. You know the place where you can read the full text of the regulation free of charge (isap.sejm.gov.pl) and you know in which paragraph to look for each requirement.
5. You understand why Rozp. MG 2000 is **your shield**, not bureaucracy: art. 210 § 1 KP (Polish Labour Code) gives you the right to refuse to work on a machine that does not meet the requirements, and the regulation is the list of those requirements.

## Content

### Why one regulation instead of a hundred instructions

In 2000, the Polish legislator faced a simple question: **how to protect 120 thousand sawmill and carpentry workers from several dozen types of machines?** In use were circular saws, band saws, frame saws (gang saws), milling machines, 4-sided planers, jigsaws, wood lathes, and sanders. Each had a different design, different hazards, a different manufacturer.

The solution: **one regulation describing the requirements common to all of them**. It does not say "circular saw X from manufacturer Y must have a 2 mm sheet-metal guard". It says: **"every machine with a rotating cutting tool must have a guard protecting against contact with the tool during operation."** The manufacturer decides how to build that guard. The PIP inspector checks whether it is in place and whether it works.

This is the logic of **"functional, not constructional requirements"**. Advantage: one regulation for the entire industry. Disadvantage: you have to be able to recognise whether a specific guard fulfils its function, because the regulation will not show you a photo.

::: info
**Why the year 2000**: Poland was preparing at the time to join the European Union (2004). Rozp. MG 2000 implements the Machinery Directive 89/392/EEC (today 2006/42/EC). From 2004, every new machine on the EU market must bear the **CE** mark and a declaration of conformity. Older machines, produced before 2004, are legal in Poland, but they must meet the **minimum requirements** of Rozp. MG 2000. There are no "retired machines": a 40-year-old band saw working in a sawmill today must have a brake acting within 10 seconds, even if in 1984 it had none at all.
:::

### Four requirements you will learn this week

The regulation has more than a dozen paragraphs. Four are key for you. Each touches a different layer of risk: mechanical, spatial, kinetic, and personal.

**Requirement 1: brake and coast-down (§ 13)**

After STOP is pressed, the cutting tool must come to a halt **within no more than 10 seconds**. This time is called the **coast-down** (run-out time). On a circular saw without a brake, the coast-down is 40 to 60 seconds; on a band saw 30 to 50; on a 4-sided planer 20 to 40. Every second of coast-down is a second in which the blade or band can still cut off a finger. A brake (mechanical, electromagnetic, or electrical-reverse) shortens this time to 5-10 seconds.

What you check: **press STOP on a running machine, count "one thousand one, one thousand two..." until you hear silence**. More than 10 seconds = faulty machine. You go to the foreman. You do not start the blade again.

**Requirement 2: fixed and adjustable guards (§ 10, § 11)**

Two categories of guards:

- The **fixed guard** covers rotating parts the operator never needs to see (lower part of the blade under the table, the drive belt, band wheels below the worktop). Mounted on bolts or rivets, removable only with the machine off and power disconnected.
- The **adjustable guard** covers the cutting zone from above and moves with the material (for example the upper guard on a format circular saw). In working position it leaves only a gap the thickness of the board.

What you check: **fixed guard without bolts = STOP, foreman**. **Upper guard permanently raised (for example tied up with wire) = STOP, foreman**. Never start a machine that has its guard removed or locked open, not even "for a moment, to let a thicker board through".

**Requirement 3: anti-kickback and riving knife (§ 10 item 3)**

**Kickback** is the sudden throwing of the material back towards the operator at a speed of 30-40 m/s (that is, 100-140 km/h). It is the third most frequent cause of death in Polish sawmills, after the press and transport (approximately 15% of fatal accidents, PIP 2020-2023).

Two safeguards:

- The **riving knife** on a circular saw: a vertical metal plate behind the blade that separates the cutting slot and prevents the wood from clamping onto the blade. It must be thicker than the blade, thinner than the cutting slot, and 2-3 mm higher than the blade. Positioned 3-8 mm behind the blade.
- The **anti-kickback finger** (curved pawls) on planers and band saws: hooks that stop material moving back towards the operator.

What you check: on a circular saw, **look aside, touch the riving knife with your finger (machine switched off!), check whether it is there and whether it has the right height**. No riving knife = faulty machine.

**Requirement 4: no-glove rule near rotating parts (§ 5)**

We return to what was in lesson 2, but now with the paragraph in hand. The regulation says directly:

> *§ 5. It is not permitted to use protective gloves during the operation of machine tools if the rotating parts of the machine may catch the glove and draw the hand into the danger zone.*

This is not a recommendation. It is a statutory prohibition. An employer who orders you to work in gloves on a circular saw is breaking the law. You, when you put on gloves "to be more comfortable", are also breaking the law, but above all you are putting your own fingers at risk.

::: warning
**Remember from lesson 2**: gloves protect when **carrying lumber and logs** (splinters, resin, strapping). Gloves are forbidden when **in contact with a rotating tool** (blade, band, cutter, planer knife, drill bit). The switch: **wood = yes, machine = no**. You take off the gloves **before** you approach the saw table, not during the work.
:::

### Where to find the full text

The full regulation is freely available on the **Internet System of Legal Acts of the Sejm of the Republic of Poland**: `isap.sejm.gov.pl`. Type into the search box "Rozp. MG 2000 obrabiarki drewno" or directly **"Dz.U. 2000 nr 36 poz. 409"**. Click "tekst aktu" and you have the whole thing as a PDF.

::: tip
**30-second machine check in practice**: before you reach for START, run your eyes over the machine in the following order:

1. **Eyes down**: is the guard under the table on bolts (not on wire, not on adhesive tape)?
2. **Eyes up**: does the adjustable guard lower freely into working position?
3. **Finger (machine switched off!)**: is the riving knife in its place and does it protrude 2-3 mm above the blade?
4. **Ear**: press STOP at idle, count to 10. Silence by "ten" = brake OK. Not yet = brake to be replaced.
5. **Hands**: do you have your gloves off?

30 seconds, four paragraphs, one life.
:::

### Case Oleh: a saw without a riving knife

Oleh's second day in the new sawmill. He opens the format circular saw, looks behind the blade. **There is no riving knife**. There is the bolt where the knife should sit, but the knife is gone. Oleh goes to the foreman:

> **Oleh**: Mr Marek, there is no riving knife.
> **Foreman**: Yes, the last operator took it off, it got in the way when cutting wide boards. Run it normally, I have been cutting like this for two weeks without a problem.
> **Oleh**: I will not run it. Rozp. MG 2000 § 10 requires a riving knife. Without the knife, this is not a functional machine.
> **Foreman**: Then go to the manager.

Oleh goes to the manager. The manager calls the service. Within two hours the riving knife is back in place. The next day the foreman says nothing. A week later he assigns Oleh to a second machine. **The law worked. Oleh did not lose a finger, he did not lose his job, and he earned the reputation in the team of being "the one who knows".**

::: example
**Legal basis for refusal**: art. 210 § 1 of the Labour Code. "Where working conditions do not comply with occupational health and safety provisions and pose a direct threat to the health or life of the employee [...] the employee has the right to refrain from performing work, notifying the supervisor immediately." For such a refusal **the remuneration must not be reduced** (art. 210 § 3). This is not a revolt. It is a duty.
:::

## Check yourself

**Question 1**: You are working on a circular saw. You press STOP, you count "one thousand one, one thousand two..." and you hear silence only at "one thousand seventeen". What do you do?

*Good answer*: you do not start the blade again. You go to the foreman and say: "The brake does not meet § 13 of Rozp. MG 2000, the coast-down exceeds 10 seconds. The machine goes to service." You have the right to refuse work (art. 210 § 1 KP).

**Question 2**: The foreman tells you: "The lumber is thick, put on gloves so that you do not scrape your hand on the blade." What do you do?

*Good answer*: you refuse. Rozp. MG 2000 § 5 prohibits gloves near rotating parts. The alternative: a wooden push-stick or a guide for cutting wide pieces, not a glove.

**Question 3**: Where will you check the full text of Rozp. MG 2000 if the foreman claims something you do not believe?

*Good answer*: `isap.sejm.gov.pl`, search for "Dz.U. 2000 nr 36 poz. 409". The text is free and the PDF can be downloaded.

**Question 4**: Which four requirements does Rozp. MG 2000 place on every circular saw (list them and give the paragraph numbers)?

*Good answer*: (1) brake with coast-down up to 10 s (§ 13), (2) fixed and adjustable guard (§ 10, 11), (3) riving knife as anti-kickback (§ 10 item 3), (4) no-glove rule near rotating parts (§ 5).

## Trainer notes

A legal lesson for a non-lawyer: **the key is the change of tone from "we are learning the rules" to "we are being given a shield to defend ourselves"**. Migrant course participants often carry the model "the law is against me" (from experiences in their countries of origin, in dealings with migration offices, in informal employment). Showing that the Polish health-and-safety regulation is **on their side**, that the foreman is breaking the law when he orders them to work in gloves, is a turning point. Afterwards some ask for access to the full texts for their colleagues.

Do not require paragraph numbers to be memorised. The four requirements yes, the paragraph numbers as orientation. During the quiz allow them to consult the printout; this is a test of understanding, not of memory.

Oleh's case can be played as **a role-play in pairs**: one as the operator, the other as the foreman arguing "run it without the knife, I always did it that way". The operator has to refuse **citing a specific paragraph**. Ten minutes of exercise, ten years of payoff.

The ritual of week 1: so far we have had **check yourself** (l2 PPE), **check STOP** (l3 the mushroom button), **check the machine** (l4 the 5-point checklist). Lesson 5 adds a fourth layer: **check whether the machine complies with the law**. This is not the fifth item of the checklist; it is the **foundation** on which all the previous ones rest. If the machine does not meet Rozp. MG 2000, the checklist is meaningless. First compliance with the law, then the daily ritual.
