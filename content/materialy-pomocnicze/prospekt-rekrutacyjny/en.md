# Become a sawmill operator in Poland. Free "Sawmill Work" course

**EGIDA Foundation** invites labour migrants in Poland to take part in a 12-week free vocational course that leads to real work in a Polish sawmill or joinery shop. The course is free of charge for migrants and grew out of the integration capacity built by the EGIDA Foundation.

> "Before the course I did not know a single word of Polish beyond 'dzień dobry' (hello / good morning). After twelve weeks I was running an eight-hour shift on my own, with two junior operators under me. A sawmill in Małopolska hired me full time three days after I finished module three."
>
> **Mykoła K., Ukraine, graduate of a previous edition, autonomous operator at the "Buk-Drew" sawmill near Nowy Sącz**

---

## 1. Who is EGIDA Foundation

EGIDA Foundation is a Polish non-governmental organisation that has been operating since 2019 and specialises in the vocational integration of migrants from Ukraine, Uzbekistan, Moldova, Belarus, Georgia, Azerbaijan, Armenia, and third countries outside the European Union. Our mission: to give you a concrete trade, real work, and a stable life in Poland, not short-term relief.

Since 2022 we have been running vocational courses in three industrial areas: **sawmilling and joinery** (this course), **MAG welding** (a separate 8-week course), and **warehousing and logistics** (a separate 6-week course). Every course ends with employment at a specific employer from our partner network, not just with a paper certificate.

EGIDA Foundation's partner employers: about 30 sawmills in the Śląsk, Małopolska, Mazowsze, and Wielkopolska voivodeships, including BTM Drewno Sp. z o.o., Wood-Mizer Polska, Tartak Kowalski sp.j., and Drewex Małopolska. The full list of employers is available at the project office.

## 2. Who is this course for

We invite you to the course if:

- you are at least 18 years old (exceptionally 17 with the consent of a legal guardian and the Państwowa Inspekcja Pracy (National Labour Inspectorate)),
- you speak Polish at A2 level (you can handle basic communication: "dzień dobry" (hello), "proszę" (please), "dziękuję" (thank you), "nie rozumiem" (I don't understand), and answer a question about your name and your job),
- you have a residence or work permit in Poland (permanent residence card, temporary residence card with a work permit, work visa, refugee status),
- you are physically healthy (we require a current medical certificate of fitness for physical work),
- you want to learn a concrete trade and work in a Polish sawmill or joinery shop.

**We do not require technical education.** About 40% of our graduates worked in agriculture, construction, or retail before the course. What we require is motivation, diligence, and a willingness to learn professional Polish.

**We do not require sawmill experience.** Module one starts from zero: why a sawmill is different from any other workplace, what PPE (Personal Protective Equipment) is, how to recognise wood, where you are allowed to stand and where you are not.

We teach people who do not read technical Polish. All course materials are available in four languages: Polish, English, Spanish, and Ukrainian. Lessons are taught in Polish with a teaching assistant who translates into your language when needed.

## 3. What you get after the course

After twelve weeks you are an autonomous sawmill operator ready for full-time work. Specifically:

**After module one (4 weeks)**: you can work as a helper. You recognise wood, wear PPE correctly, feed material to the operator, clean your workstation, and report hazards to the foreman. In Poland you earn about 4300 zł gross per month (national minimum wage plus an internship bonus from the project of 1700 zł gross per week of internship).

**After module two (next 4 weeks)**: you can independently operate a band saw, a vertical sawframe, or a milling machine under a foreman's supervision. You read technical documents, maintain the workstation log, and react to faults. As a junior operator you earn about 5500-6500 zł gross per month.

**After module three (final 4 weeks)**: you are an autonomous operator with elements of supervision. You run an 8-hour shift, coordinate the work of 2-4 junior colleagues, handle customer complaints, and take part in FSC audits (our partner sawmills hold FSC certification under code FSC-C112233). You earn 7500-9500 zł gross per month. In sawmills exporting to Germany the figure can reach 11 000 zł gross.

In addition you receive:

- **Certificates of completion for M1, M2, M3** in line with the Regulation of the Minister of National Education of 19 March 2019 on continuing education, valid for life.
- **An EGIDA Foundation recommendation** for a specific employer from our partner network (after M2 and M3).
- **A manufacturer's certificate from BTM or Wood-Mizer** for selected graduates at the request of the M2 trainer (financed by the project, market value around 8500 zł).
- **A basis for further qualifications**: UDT (forklift, overhead crane), SEP E1/D1 (electrical), which are a valuable addition to a sawmill operator's CV.

## 4. What your week looks like

The course lasts 12 weeks. Each week consists of:

- 8 teaching lessons of 120 minutes each (Monday to Thursday, 2 lessons per day plus one on Friday morning),
- 1 block of practical exercises on Friday afternoon (4 hours in the partner sawmill hall),
- a consultation meeting with the teaching assistant on Saturday morning (1 hour, optional, for trainees who need extra help with Polish).

That is a total of 24-28 hours per week. The rest of your time is yours: for rest, for casual work (if you have any), for self-study.

After each module you have a one-week break during which you complete an informal internship at a sawmill partner, in a role matching the module just completed (after M1 as a helper, after M2 as a junior operator). The internship is mandatory and gives you your first contact with a real workplace, not just the training hall. For the internship you receive a stipend of about 1700 zł gross per week.

Lessons are taught by:

- **Lead trainer**: an experienced sawmill operator with at least 10 years of experience, UDT and SEP qualifications, and pedagogical training from EGIDA Foundation (around 80 hours of preparation for working with migrants).
- **Teaching assistant**: a person speaking your native language (Ukrainian, Russian, Spanish, or English), present during lessons and during the internship. Their role is to translate content you do not understand from Polish and to help you communicate with the trainer and the employer.

## 5. Three career paths after the course

After completing module three you can choose from three typical career paths. All of them are realistic, all of them have been tested by graduates of previous editions, including Mykoła (quoted at the start of this prospectus).

**Path A: Export operator**

You sign an employment contract with a sawmill exporting to Germany, Austria, or the Netherlands (about 30% of our partners). Starting pay is 9500-11 000 zł gross. After a year of work you can become a shift foreman. After three years the path leads to the position of production manager or sawmill master (pay 13 000-16 000 zł gross). This path requires a stable command of Polish (B1 after the course, B2 after a year of work) and additional UDT forklift qualifications (cost around 1500-2500 zł, may be reimbursed from the operator's programme).

**Path B: Specialist operator**

You choose one specific machine (BTM band saw, vertical sawframe, Wood-Mizer optimising line) and sign a contract with the machine manufacturer for specialist training (2-4 weeks, financed 100% by the EGIDA project for selected trainees, market cost around 8500 zł). After specialist training you earn 10 000-14 000 zł gross in the manufacturer's service department or in large export sawmills. This path requires strong technical skills and a willingness to keep learning (machine servicing is engineering work, not just operator work).

**Path C: Your own joinery or a family joinery**

You work 2-3 years as an autonomous operator, save money, complete additional UDT and SEP qualifications, and get to know wood suppliers. After a period of hired work you open your own small joinery (made-to-order furniture, garden gazebos, firewood) or take over the family joinery of your wife or husband (most often the choice of graduates from Ukraine). Entry threshold for a family joinery: 30 000-80 000 zł (used band saws, circular saw, milling machine, wood storage). This path requires entrepreneurship and knowledge of Polish business regulations.

## 6. Course costs and EFS+ financing

**Your participation in the course is completely free.** All costs are covered by the EGIDA Foundation from its own resources and dedicated partner donations. The cost package covers:

- wages of trainers and teaching assistants (around 180 000 zł per 3-month cycle, 12 trainers in total),
- teaching materials and access to the machines of sawmill partners (around 45 000 zł per cycle),
- PPE (Personal Protective Equipment) for every trainee (boots, helmet, goggles, gloves, hearing protection, masks, value around 450 zł),
- internship stipends during the breaks between modules (1700 zł gross per week, 3400 zł gross in total for 2 weeks of internship during the course),
- accident (NNW) and liability (OC) insurance for the period of the course and the internship,
- external BTM or Wood-Mizer certificates for selected trainees (around 8500 zł market value).

What is not financed:

- accommodation outside the course (you must have your own flat or a rental; we help to find housing during the first 2 weeks through EGIDA partners),
- transport to classes (we cover only collective transport to partner facilities under code ZLE-2026-XX-XXX),
- food (lunch at the partners' canteen around 18-22 zł per day, optional),
- UDT and SEP exams after the course (the trainee pays on their own or is reimbursed from their first salary at the employer).

## 7. When and where

We run the course in **three annual cycles**, each covering the full M1 + M2 + M3 sequence over 12 weeks. A group consists of 8-12 trainees.

Courses run in several cycles per year. The exact calendar of recruitment, cycle start and the final M3 exam is shared by the Fundacja EGIDA coordinator on candidate request.

**Course locations**:

- **Module 1 (theory and introductory practice)**: the EGIDA Foundation teaching room in Warsaw (ul. Grójecka) and the training hall at the "BTM Drewno" sawmill near Pruszków (30 km from the centre of Warsaw, collective transport provided).
- **Module 2 (machine practice)**: partner halls of BTM Drewno and Wood-Mizer Polska in Pruszków and Otwock.
- **Module 3 (solo on shift)**: a hall selected individually for each trainee according to the location of the future employer (Śląsk, Małopolska, Mazowsze, Wielkopolska).

## 8. Frequently asked questions (FAQ)

**Do I have to know Polish?**
Yes, at A2 level (basic everyday conversation). At entry we run a short conversational test. If your Polish is weaker, we invite you to our free preparatory course "Professional Polish A2" (4 weeks before the sawmill course starts).

**Can I work during the course?**
Yes, but only at the weekend or in the evenings. The course takes 24-28 hours per week spread from Monday to Saturday morning. You cannot take up work that clashes with the lessons.

**Will I get an employment contract in Poland?**
After completing M2 or M3 we recommend you to a specific employer from our network. The employer typically offers a fixed-term employment contract for 12 months with a guarantee of extension to an indefinite contract, subject to a positive evaluation. About 85% of our graduates get a full-time job within 30 days of finishing the course.

**What if I do not pass the exam?**
If you do not pass the M1 theoretical test (70% threshold), you have the right to one retake after a week. If you do not pass the M1 practical test, the teaching assistant together with the trainer will assess whether you need an additional week of internship or whether it is better to break off the course and come back in the next cycle. There is no financial penalty for dropping out.

**Can I bring my family to Poland?**
After signing a fixed-term 12-month employment contract you have the right to apply for a temporary residence permit for your spouse and children. We support you in this procedure through our lawyer.

**What about health insurance?**
During the course you are covered by the project's accident (NNW) insurance. Once you start work at a partner, the employer pays ZUS (social security) contributions, which gives you access to public healthcare (NFZ, National Health Fund) for you and your family.

## How to enrol

1. **Fill in the online form** on the EGIDA Foundation website (link available at the project office). It will take 15 minutes.
2. **Call the recruitment coordinator** (phone number at the project office) or send an e-mail. We reply within 48 working hours.
3. **Come to the recruitment interview** (about 45 minutes, at the Foundation office or online). The interview is conducted in your language (Polish, Ukrainian, Russian, English, Spanish).
4. **Take the Polish A2 test** (15 minutes, conversational).
5. **Submit a medical certificate** of fitness for physical work (we issue the form at the office, examinations are free of charge with our partner doctor).
6. **Sign the EGIDA trainee contract** (24 pages, in your language plus Polish). You receive the schedule, the name of your trainer, and the name of your assistant.
7. **Come to the first M1 lesson** on the day the cycle starts.

**Application deadline for the upcoming cycle** is set by Fundacja EGIDA and communicated during recruitment.
