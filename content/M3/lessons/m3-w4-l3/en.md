## Theory: FSC/PEFC audit, chain of custody, markings, operator conduct

### What FSC is

**FSC, Forest Stewardship Council**, an international non-profit organisation, founded in 1993 in **Toronto** (Canada), currently headquartered in **Bonn** (Germany). **Mission**: promoting responsible management of the world's forests. The FSC mark, a green tree-with-tick on a white background, is a **globally recognisable symbol** of sustainable wood.

**FSC Polska** (FSC Poland, a foundation seated in Warsaw), the Polish office, runs national audits, training, oversight of Polish certificates. Director: Leszek Rączka (as of 2026). An annual audit costs a sawmill ~2500-3500 zł net + VAT, depending on the size of the company.

**Three levels of FSC** for wood:

| Level | Meaning | The sawmill may sell as |
|---|---|---|
| **FSC 100%** | all wood from FSC-certified forests | full certification, the customer knows the harvesting is sustainable |
| **FSC Mix** (e.g. 70/30) | ≥70% FSC + rest from Controlled Wood | partial certification, accepted globally |
| **FSC Controlled Wood** | "non-problematic" wood (not from problematic forests), but without a full audit | the lowest level, rarely used on its own |

### What FSC Chain of Custody (CoC) is

**Chain of Custody** (supply chain, acronym CoC) is a **documentation system** that shows that the wood at every point of the chain **from the forest to the end customer** retains its certified origin. **The sawmill must hold its own CoC certificate** (e.g. EGIDA: FSC-C112233) in order to sell wood as "FSC 100%" or "FSC Mix".

**CoC requirements**:
1. **Raw material documentation**, invoices, log delivery receipt cards, batch numbers from the supplier.
2. **Batch separation**, physical separation of certified from non-certified on the yard and in the process.
3. **Stack markings**, plates with the certificate number, species, date.
4. **Transfer of the certificate number**, from the raw material invoice to the customer WZ (via KEP-001).
5. **Personnel training**, all employees have knowledge of FSC CoC (every year).
6. **Annual audit**, an external auditor (accredited by FSC Polska) checks compliance.

### What PEFC is

**PEFC, Programme for the Endorsement of Forest Certification**, an international organisation founded in 1999 in **Paris**, currently headquartered in **Geneva** (Switzerland). **An alternative to FSC**, popular especially in **Germany, Austria, Switzerland, France, Poland**, less strict in some respects (e.g. it recognises state certificates of national forestry units), therefore **easier for small sawmills**.

**FSC vs PEFC differences**:

| Aspect | FSC | PEFC |
|---|---|---|
| **Founded** | 1993 Toronto | 1999 Paris |
| **Headquarters** | Bonn (Germany) | Geneva (Switzerland) |
| **Popularity in Poland** | ~60% of sawmills | ~40% of sawmills |
| **Audit rigour** | higher (independent international) | lower (recognises national standards) |
| **Customer recognition** | higher globally | higher in DE/AT/CH |
| **Average sawmill audit cost** | 2500-3500 zł/year | 1500-2500 zł/year |
| **Typical customer requires** | IKEA, DIY chains, export to USA | Obi, Bauhaus (DE), export DE/AT |
| **Graphic mark** | green tree + tick | two green trees in a circle |

**EGIDA currently holds FSC only** (PEFC introduction planned for 2027, a business decision by director Paweł, the German customer Bauhaus Berlin requires PEFC, and EGIDA is considering entering the German market).

### 6 areas of an FSC CoC audit

An FSC audit always covers **6 core areas**:

| Area | What is checked | Typical non-conformances |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Raw material supply chain** | invoices, KOD, batch numbers from the forest district | missing date on KOD, mismatched numbers |
| **2. Batch separation** | log yard, markings, dividing lines | FSC batch next to a non-certified one without a gap |
| **3. Stack markings** | FSC plates, legibility, water resistance | plate washed off by rain (EGIDA's past 2025 non-conformance) |
| **4. Quality documentation** | KEP-001, WZ with FSC-C, complaint register | missing FSC-C on WZ, mismatched numbers |
| **5. Personnel training** | training list with certificate evidence | list not updated after a new hire |
| **6. Complaints and non-conformances** | complaint register, status of corrective actions | non-conformance not closed within 12 months |

### Types of non-conformance

**Major non-conformance**:
- directly violates the FSC CoC standard
- requires **corrective action within 30 days**
- **may lead to suspension of the certificate**
- examples: selling non-certified wood as FSC 100%; mixing FSC batches with non-certified ones without documentation; lack of a sawmill CoC certificate when trading

**Minor non-conformance**:
- indirectly violates the standard or is a procedural gap
- requires **corrective action within 12 months** (by the next audit)
- **does not affect certificate validity**
- examples: plate washed off by rain; training list out of date by 2 months; one missing date on a KOD

**Voluntary recommendation (observation)**:
- a suggestion from the auditor, non-binding
- **may be ignored** without consequence
- examples: "consider an electronic KEP-001"; "consider PEFC in addition"

### M3 operator conduct during an audit

**Rule 1: the M3 operator is a co-creator of the certificate, not a subject of control.**

The M3 operator, through everyday work, **builds CoC compliance**:
- carefully transcribes batch numbers from KOD to KEP-001
- maintains the visual markings on logs (blue crayon on the end face)
- reports irregularities (missing plate, washed-off markings, mixed-up batches)
- obtains and renews the FSC CoC operator certificate (every 12-24 months)

**Rule 2: answer substantively, do not improvise.**

The auditor wants to see **real everyday practice**, not a show. Answers such as:
- *"I do not know, but I will check with Mrs. Ania"*, **accepted**, shows the hierarchy
- *"it is always OK, no problems"*, **suspicious**, sounds like a lie
- *"I will be happy to show you the KEP-001, one moment"*, **model**, a document beats words

**Rule 3: know the 3 FSC levels (100%, Mix, Controlled Wood).**

The M3 operator **must** be able to explain:
- what FSC 100% means (the whole log from an audited forest)
- what FSC Mix means (at least 70% + the rest Controlled Wood)
- what FSC Controlled Wood means (not from problematic forests, but without a full audit)
- why **batch separation** is crucial (so that FSC 100% does not mix with a lower level)

**Rule 4: awareness of the 'batch mixing' risk.**

The most common M3 operator error: **transcribing the wrong batch number** from KOD to KEP-001. If, instead of "N-STR-06-042" (FSC 100% spruce), one enters "N-SRO-04-118" (FSC Mix 70/30 alder), **the batch is classified as Mix** and sold to the customer as FSC 100%. **That is a CoC violation = a major non-conformance in the worst case**. That is why Rustam checks **twice** on every entry.

### Pedagogy: the audit as a periodic quality review

**A common thinking mistake by M3 operators**: *"An audit is an external check, someone wants to catch me out."*

**The real meaning**: *"An audit is a periodic review that shows us where we stand in terms of quality."* The auditor **wants the sawmill to pass the audit**, his business goal is **supporting the sawmill in retaining the certificate**, not throwing it out of the system. That is why the auditor **helps the sawmill understand minor non-conformances** and gives 12 months to correct them.

**For EGIDA the audit brings 3 benefits**:
1. **FSC-C112233 certificate valid for another 12 months**, a requirement of key customers (MebelPro, DOM-STOL, Dom-Bud, Domy Drewniane Iława).
2. **An external point of view**, the auditor notices things that internal staff no longer see (e.g. a plate washed off by rain).
3. **Building the operational standard**, every closed non-conformance = a steady process improvement.

**An extra benefit for the operator**: "a well-passed audit" is **recorded in the employee's training card**. After 5 well-passed audits Rustam gets a **promotion bonus** on the EGIDA career path, priority in competitions for foreman or quality controller positions (see lesson l6 on professional development).

### Everyday relationship with FSC

**FSC is not an audit once a year**. FSC is **an everyday discipline** for the M3 operator:

- **morning**: checking the log batch plate before the first cut
- **during work**: blue crayon on the log's end face with batch number + FSC-C
- **at the KEP-001**: **double-checking** the transcription of the FSC number from KOD
- **on the WZ**: Mrs. Ania adds FSC-C112233 (EGIDA's number) to the customer's WZ
- **on dispatch**: a label on the pallet with the FSC-C number, automatic from CRM

Every one of these points is **a drop in the chain**. If even one drop is broken, **the FSC Chain of Custody cracks**, a major non-conformance in the next audit, potential loss of the certificate.

That is why Krzysztof Kowalski praised Rustam for **'batch mixing' risk awareness**, it is **a sign of an M3 operator's maturity**.
