## Introduction

Art. 283 § 1 of the Polish Labour Code: the petty-offence sanction for failing to carry it out. Art. 100 § 1 of the Polish Labour Code: to perform work diligently, in this case **the teaching work**.

## Content

### Art. 212 of the Polish Labour Code: your role as a person directing

In l3 you met the three articles that define your responsibility as a self-reliant operator: art. 100 § 1 (to perform work diligently), art. 210 (the right to refuse dangerous work), art. 211 (the worker's duties with regard to OSH). Today a fourth article comes back, which **was not in force** for you in l3 (because you were only a subordinate then), but begins to operate **today**, at the moment of signing Wasyl off.

**Art. 212 of the Polish Labour Code** (full text via IURA, as of 2026-05-04):

> "A person directing workers is obligated to:
>
> 1. organize workstations in accordance with occupational safety and health regulations and principles,
>
> 2. ensure the serviceability of personal protective equipment and its use in accordance with its intended purpose,
>
> 3. organize, prepare, and conduct work, taking into account the protection of workers against accidents at work, occupational diseases, and illnesses related to the conditions of the work environment,
>
> 4. ensure the safe and hygienic condition of work premises and technical equipment, as well as the serviceability of collective protection measures and their use in accordance with their intended purpose,
>
> 5. enforce workers' compliance with occupational safety and health regulations and principles,
>
> 6. ensure implementation of recommendations of the physician providing medical care over workers."

From the moment you sign Wasyl's training card (planned around 9:00 on Monday 4 May), you are, within the meaning of this article, the **person directing** for him. Not by title ("*brygadzista*", foreman), not by pay (you stay a self-reliant operator at the same rate), but **functionally**: in the scope of Wasyl's safety on P3, until the end of the shift.

What each of the six points means for you concretely:

- **Point 1 (organisation of the workstation):** workstation P3 is tidy, passageways free, the location of the emergency mushroom known, tools in their places. This is **your control** before Wasyl stands next to you.
- **Point 2 (PPE):** before the shift begins you check whether Wasyl has the full set and whether he puts it on correctly. An incorrectly worn helmet or goggles under the chin means you do not work until he fixes it.
- **Point 3 (organisation of work with a view to protection):** you lay out Wasyl's work so that he does not stand in the path of the blade, does not reach for sawn timber during cutting, does not distract your attention at key moments.
- **Point 4 (condition of premises and equipment):** this is your section (P3 and its surroundings up to 3 m). The rest of the hall is Marek and Jaroslaw, but P3 is you.
- **Point 5 (enforcing OSH compliance):** if you see that Wasyl has taken off his goggles because "he is bored", you **stop** work and correct him. You do not "let him adapt", you do not "cut him slack" because you know him from the cohort. You enforce.
- **Point 6 (physician's recommendations):** if Wasyl has medical limitations (e.g., the doctor wrote "work up to 8 hours a day without overtime", "breaks every 2 hours"), **you** observe them, not him. You ask Marek for Wasyl's medical card before the shift.

The sanction for violating art. 212: art. 283 § 1 of the Polish Labour Code (from l3), a fine of **1,000 to 30,000 PLN**. It concerns you as a person directing. The same as it concerns Marek as foreman and Jaroslaw as the OSH inspector. The only difference is in scope (you for Wasyl, Marek for everyone on the shift, Jaroslaw for the whole plant).

::: warning
Mykoła once said in the canteen after the third coffee: "in Poltava, in the old sawmill, nobody taught anybody. The younger one watched, the older one worked, sometimes he showed something without words, most often he shouted when something went wrong." This is not a unique story, it is a type of work organisation that disappeared from the Polish Labour Code after 1990. Today a helper **must** have a documented instruction, **must** have an individually assigned mentor, **must** have the right to escalate. If that is not there, the plant is breaking the law, and the mentor (you) takes on the criminal and civil consequences. This is the first difference between the "old" and the "new" sawmill, and it is in it that your entire difference in the work of a self-reliant operator lies.
:::

### Three sign-off conditions, without which you are not allowed

Before you fill in the training card and sign, you check three conditions. If **any** of them is not met, you refuse acceptance and escalate to Marek. This is not bureaucracy, this is your personal legal protection.

**Condition 1: the helper has completed initial general training.** This is the M1 training within the EGIDA programme (or an equivalent from a previous plant, confirmed by a document). Wasyl finished M1 in February 2026, the document is in the personnel file folder. Mykoła asks Marek for a copy of the M1 training card, checks the date, Jaroslaw's signature, the scope (ten key topics). It is valid **until February 2027**.

**Condition 2: current medical examinations.** In Poland a worker employed at an exposed workstation (sawmill cutting floor means exposure to noise, wood dust, mechanical vibration) has **annual** preventive examinations (some plants extend this to 2 years for healthy staff, but the standard is one year). Wasyl's examination of 10 February 2026 is valid until 10 February 2027. It includes: hearing capacity (audiometric test), vision (acuity, depth perception), grip strength, general history. Medical statement: "fit for work, without limitations". Mykoła checks the card, sees the physician's signature, the stamp of the Occupational Medicine Centre at 12 Borowa Street in Warsaw.

**Condition 3: a full PPE set.** Wasyl must have on him **all five elements** of personal protective equipment matching the P3 workstation risk assessment:

- **Protective helmet** with the EN 397 certificate, yellow for helpers (self-reliant operators have red, foremen green). The helmet is "personal", assigned to Wasyl for a year, with his name inside.
- **Protective goggles** with the EN 166 certificate, impact resistance to chips class B (medium-energy impact).
- **Hearing protectors** with the EN 352 certificate, SNR (Single Number Rating, the Polish norm for noise reduction) of **at least 30 dB**. In the P3 cutting floor the noise varies between 95 and 102 dB(A); 30 dB of reduction gives 65 to 72 dB in the ear, at the borderline of the permissible 85 dB over 8 hours.
- **Work-and-protection gloves** to EN 388, class A4 (medium-high cut resistance) plus class 2 for abrasion. **Not** the operator's gloves (he has class 5 anti-cut plus dexterity); the helper gets class A4 for the knife and the hand.
- **S3 protective footwear** with the EN ISO 20345 certificate, protection class S3 (steel toe cap 200 J, midsole puncture resistance, SRC anti-slip).

Mykoła checks Wasyl's PPE at 6:54. Wasyl stands in the changing room, Mykoła walks in, shakes his hand, goes through it systematically:

> **Mykoła:** "Helmet, Wasyl." Wasyl shows it, Mykoła reads the label inside: "EN 397, production date 2024-08, first-use date 2026-02-10, Melnyk Wasyl". Yellow helmet, no scratches, no cracks. OK.
>
> "Goggles." Wasyl hands them over from the case. Mykoła checks the glass (no scratches), the frame (intact), the elastic strap (flexible). EN 166 B. OK.
>
> "Hearing protectors." Wasyl shows 3M Peltor X5A earmuffs, SNR 37 dB. Better than the 30 dB minimum. OK.
>
> "Work gloves." Wasyl shows a pair, EN 388 with class A4. New, no wear. OK.
>
> "Boots." Wasyl shows protective boots, S3, the EN ISO 20345 label inside. No sole wear. OK.

Three conditions met. Mykoła can sign off.

::: info
If any condition is not met, for example Wasyl came without protective gloves ("I forgot them in the car") or with a damaged helmet ("Monday morning, the helmet got jammed in the car door"), Mykoła **does not sign off**. He sends Wasyl back: "Go back for the gloves" or "Go to the warehouse for a new helmet". Until the lack is made up, Wasyl **does not enter** P3. This is not over-zealousness. This is the only way in which art. 212 point 2 (ensure the serviceability of PPE and its use) has any meaning. A helper without gloves on the cutting floor is a helper whom you, the operator, let onto the workstation with incomplete PPE. Art. 283 § 1: your fine, not Marek's.
:::

### The ten-point structure of the instruction

The Regulation of the Minister of Economy and Labour of 27 July 2004 on training in occupational safety and health (Dz.U. No. 180, item 1860, as amended), § 8, sets out the scope of the workstation instruction. At EGIDA Jaroslaw wrote this out as **ten points**, which Mykoła has to go through with Wasyl in the first two hours of the shift. The points are ordered from general to specific, from theory to practice, from observation to exercise.

**Point 1: description of the workstation and basic hazards.** Mykoła shows P3 from a distance of 3 metres, without starting it. He names the parts: the band (sharp, cuts), the carriage (moves, crushes), the main motor (loud, hot), the control panel (Mykoła's place). He lists three key hazards: **cutting** (the band, 52 m/s when running), **crushing** (the carriage, 3.2 tons, hydraulic stop in 0.8 s), **noise** (95 to 102 dB(A) under full load, harmful after 15 min without protection).

**Point 2: work zones.** Mykoła draws in chalk on the floor (temporary, it washes off) three zones:

- **Green zone** (over 3 m from P3): Wasyl may stand here, walk, rest. Communication with Mykoła by gesture or by voice (with ear protectors on).
- **Yellow zone** (1.5 to 3 m from P3, marked with yellow tape on the floor): Wasyl works here (outfeed ramp, sorting, timber stacks), but **only** when Mykoła confirms the machine is in a safe state. The yellow zone is **working**, but not unconditionally safe.
- **Red zone** (within 1.5 m of the band and the carriage, marked with red tape on the floor): Wasyl **never** enters it if the machine is running. He enters only when Mykoła has performed LOTO (switch off, padlock on, tag with a name). Without LOTO means no entry, ever.

Mykoła tests: he shows Wasyl three positions on the floor and asks "zone?". Wasyl answers: "green, yellow, red". The first time he makes a mistake (the yellow zone at the ramp is not "red"). Mykoła repeats. The second time without error.

**Point 3: PPE in use.** Mykoła goes again with Wasyl through putting on each element of the PPE, checking the fit. Helmet: chinstrap tightened, the helmet seated on the crown of the head, not tilted. Goggles: on the eyes, not on the forehead, strap at the back of the head. Hearing protectors: inserted into the ear (earmuffs seating snugly against the temples), SNR 37 dB active. Gloves: on the hands, cuff pulled into the jacket sleeve (not the other way around, because chips fall into the sleeve). Boots: laced to the top, knee-high socks pulled up over the boots.

**Point 4: emergency procedures.** Mykoła leads Wasyl to the control panel, shows (without starting) the red emergency mushroom: "this is YOUR mushroom, if you see something is wrong. You strike it with your palm (the whole palm, not the finger), you shout STOP, only then you look at me." Wasyl tries striking the mushroom in the air (without the machine) three times, to train the reflex. The third time it is already automatic.

Mykoła continues: **where** the main switch is (the distribution cabinet 4 m from the panel), **how** the plant alarm is activated (the button on the post next to P3, it triggers a siren plus a phone call to reception), **which way** evacuation from the hall goes (two exits: through the loading gate onto the yard, or through the west doors to the office; Wasyl memorises both).

**Point 5: gestures and signals.** In the hall the noise of 95 to 102 dB(A) means voices are lost. Mykoła and Wasyl have to communicate through gestures. At EGIDA there are five basic ones:

- **STOP** (the word in Polish and English is the same): palm vertical forward, fingers together, a short movement like a policeman's "stop". Meaning: stop everything, immediately.
- **START** (the word in Polish and English is the same): palm down, lifted slightly, a soft motion. Meaning: you can go back to work.
- **SLOWER** (Polish: *WOLNIEJ*): palm down, a slow up-and-down rocking. Meaning: I am slowing the pace.
- **FASTER** (Polish: *SZYBCIEJ*): palm horizontal, a short motion forward. Meaning: I am speeding up. (Rarely used, because in a sawmill you rarely speed up a helper.)
- **ATTENTION** (Polish: *UWAGA*): index finger vertical upwards, a short motion like "listen". Meaning: something is about to change, pay attention.

Mykoła shows each gesture, Wasyl repeats. After three repetitions Wasyl responds correctly. **The key gesture**: STOP. If either of them sees a hazard, the STOP gesture stops both of them in mid-motion. No need for words.

**Point 6: observation of normal machine operation.** Mykoła starts P3 (after the morning warm-up procedure from l7, the machine is already warm at 6:45). Wasyl stands in the green zone (3 m from P3), observes. Mykoła cuts the first trial log, Wasyl watches the movement of the band, the carriage, the waste, the sorting. Mykoła stops after the log, approaches Wasyl (Wasyl in the green zone, this is a neutral space for conversation), asks "what did you see". Wasyl says: "band goes, log goes, planks fall out, sawdust flies." Mykoła refines: "notice that the band vibrates differently in a knot than in a straight grain. Can you hear the change in sound?" Wasyl listens to the second log, nods.

**Point 7: the helper's role in normal production.** Mykoła defines Wasyl's tasks:

- Receiving sawn timber from the outfeed ramp into the sorting zone.
- Visual sorting into three stacks (A class, B class, reject) according to Mykoła's guidelines.
- Maintenance of workstation cleanliness: sweeping sawdust once an hour, removing large waste from the ramp.
- Assistance during band changes (holding, passing tools, **not** touching the tooth of the band).
- Observation of the machine for unusual signals: strange sound, vibration, smell, smoke. Immediate reporting.

Wasyl listens, notes down in his notebook (the helper's notebook, EGIDA issues one to each helper, A5 hard-cover, Mykoła gave it to him this morning). Mykoła asks: "did you understand?" Wasyl reads his five points aloud, without mistakes.

**Point 8: criteria for escalation to you (the operator).** This is analogous to what Mykoła learned in l6 (when he himself escalated to Marek). For Wasyl there are three criteria:

- **I don't know what this is.** You see something you cannot name. An unusual sound, smell, vibration, stain. You come to me (Mykoła), show, ask. You do not guess on your own.
- **I know what this is, but I don't know if it is dangerous.** You see a crack in the sawn timber, a frost layer on the surface, the edge of a log sticking out. You recognise the phenomenon, but you cannot assess the scale. You come to me, describe, ask for a decision.
- **I know it is dangerous and I want to stop.** You see an acute situation (band break, fire, a shout from a colleague at another saw). **You strike STOP immediately**, then notify me. The decision to stop does not need my confirmation.

Mykoła tests Wasyl: "let's take a scenario. You see that light smoke is coming from the motor of P3. What do you do?" Wasyl answers: "criterion three, I stop." Mykoła: "good. And if the smoke were very faint, one centimetre high, and disappeared at once?" Wasyl: "criterion two, I describe it to Mykoła, I ask." Mykoła: "good. And if you smelt something you don't know, but you don't see smoke?" Wasyl: "criterion one, I show it to Mykoła."

Three scenarios, three correct answers. Wasyl understands the system. That is the value of this criterion: **the operator does not have to predict every scenario**, he gives the helper a frame under which new situations can be mapped.

**Point 9: breaks and hygiene rules.** Mykoła passes on the working-time rules: 8-hour shift (6:00 to 15:00 with an hour's break) for Wasyl (a helper, standard rate without overtime in the first three months). Two 15-minute breaks (9:30 and 13:30) plus one 30-minute one (12:00 lunch). Water intake: 1.5 L per shift mandatory, the bottle in the changing room, Wasyl takes it every 2 hours. Toilet: unrestricted access, but with a gesture-signal to Mykoła ("I am going", an open palm towards the door). Meal in the staff canteen, free for operators and helpers (Wasyl gets a voucher).

**Point 10: sign-off, signatures, archive.** Mykoła takes out the workstation training card (EGIDA form KSS-001, A4, two-sided). He enters: Wasyl's data, date 4 May 2026, workstation P3, operator-instructor Mykoła Hrycenko. He marks the ten programme points: "discussed, shown, understanding checked". Under each point a "yes/no" box and space for comments. Mykoła marks ten "yes", comments blank.

At the bottom of the card three signatures: **Wasyl** (the participant, confirms receipt of the training), **Mykoła** (the instructor, confirms having carried it out), **Marek** (the supervisor, confirms admission to the workstation). Mykoła and Wasyl sign at 8:58. Marek will sign at 14:00 (after reviewing the card). The card goes into Wasyl's personnel file (HR folder at EGIDA) plus a copy into Mykoła's "KSS" folder (so that, in case of a PIP inspection, Mykoła has proof that the instruction took place).

::: example
After signing, Mykoła says to Wasyl one sentence that he once received from Marek on his first day of work as a self-reliant operator: "From now, until 15:00, you are my responsibility, and I am your mentor. If something happens to you, it is my fault. If you do something good, it is your merit. In a week we start learning the work, today we are learning the rules. Agreed, Wasyl?" Wasyl repeats: "I agreed, Mykoła." They shake hands. This is the informal part of the ritual, but it is accepted at EGIDA: after the signature there is a handshake, not a "good, let's move on". The handshake says: we are now a pair, not operator plus helper.
:::

### Art. 283 § 1 of the Polish Labour Code comes back: your criminal liability

In l3 you read art. 283 § 1 of the Polish Labour Code: a fine of 1,000 to 30,000 PLN for the person responsible for the state of OSH in respect of failing to observe OSH regulations. Back then it applied to you **indirectly** (as a self-reliant operator, not formally a person directing). Today it applies **directly**: you are a person directing Wasyl within the meaning of art. 212, and so art. 283 § 1 concerns you in respect of Wasyl.

What concretely can be the subject of a fine:

- **Admitting a helper to work without PPE** (helmet, goggles, hearing protectors, gloves, boots). Sanction: a fine of 1,000 to 30,000 PLN, yours.
- **Admitting a helper to work without current medical examinations.** Sanction: a fine of 1,000 to 30,000 PLN, yours.
- **Admitting a helper to work without the required workstation training.** Sanction: a fine of 1,000 to 30,000 PLN, yours.
- **Failing to observe the work zones** (the helper in the red zone with the machine running). Sanction: a fine of 1,000 to 30,000 PLN, yours, plus potentially criminal liability under art. 220 § 1 of the Polish Penal Code (*Kodeks karny*) (exposure to direct danger, deprivation of liberty up to 3 years, if the helper suffers an injury).

In Polish practice a PIP inspector who discovers such a violation will issue a **ticket** (a fine in a summary procedure of 500 to 2,000 PLN), a **caution**, or a **motion to the court** (proceedings in petty-offence cases, the full amount up to 30,000 PLN). The decision depends on the gravity of the violation and on whether an accident occurred.

::: warning
For you as a self-reliant operator with an annual net income of around 60,000 PLN, a fine of 30,000 PLN means **six months of work**. A ticket of 2,000 PLN means **two days of work**. The regulations are real, not symbolic. Ten points of instruction and three sign-off conditions cost you two hours of work on Monday and one signature. This is the best protection against a fine that exists in a Polish sawmill.
:::

### What changes in the following days and weeks

For the first two weeks Mykoła is next to Wasyl **all the time**, six steps from him in the green or the yellow zone. Wasyl does not enter the yellow zone on his own without a signal from Mykoła. During this time Wasyl learns not only the things from the ten points, but the **rhythm** of the work: the pace of sorting, recognising timber classes, communicating with Mykoła.

In the third week Mykoła lets Wasyl work in the yellow zone on his own (sorting, cleaning), but comes back to him every 30 minutes to check. Wasyl escalates to Mykoła through the workstation phone, if Mykoła is further away (e.g., during a band change).

After the first month Mykoła fills in the **helper assessment card** (EGIDA form KOP-001), in which he marks Wasyl's progress in ten areas (the ten points of instruction plus a few operational ones). The card goes to Marek and Jaroslaw, and into Wasyl's folder as the basis for the decision on promotion to M2 (junior operator) after three months.

After three months (end of July 2026) Wasyl goes to the EGIDA M2 module, becomes a junior operator at the saw. Mykoła gets a new helper (Marek has a queue list of ten candidates). The cycle repeats.

**In this cycle you, the self-reliant operator, are responsible for introducing several generations of helpers into the profession over the course of your career.** Good instructions, carried out regularly, build the culture of the sawmill. Bad instructions, or their absence, destroy it in two years. This is your **long game** as a self-reliant operator.

## Instructor notes

- **120 min rhythm (proposed):** 10 min Introduction + Objectives (the morning scene of 4 May, Wasyl arriving, Murat announcing his presence); 15 min art. 212 of the Polish Labour Code (the full text, the six duties with commentary for the operator); 10 min the three sign-off conditions (M1, examinations, PPE, discussion: "what happens if the plant has no PPE?"); 30 min the ten points of instruction (board, brief overview, pausing at point 5 (gestures) and point 8 (escalation criteria)); 15 min the Murat scene at 8:30 (reading the dialogue aloud, discussion: "what did Murat say that he had not said before?"); 15 min art. 283 § 1 of the Polish Labour Code in your role (tickets, fines, potential criminal liability under art. 220 of the Polish Penal Code); 10 min the long-term perspective (KOP-001, promotion to M2 after three months, the cycle of successive helpers); 10 min Key terms, summary, preview of W2 M3 (materials science); 5 min reserve for emotional discussion (some trainees will have a reaction to the Murat scene).

- **Pedagogical trap no. 1: the trainee says "in my plant the instruction lasts five minutes".** A typical situation. Do not scold him for it. Explain the difference: five minutes is **not** an instruction, it is "saying something just so that it is said". The Regulation of 2004 sets the minimum hours of instruction at thirty minutes for low-risk workstations, eight hours for high-risk ones (in a sawmill the band saw is in the "high" category). Five minutes is **a violation of the Regulation**, a fine for the employer (1,000 to 30,000 PLN). An operator who knows this and still does five minutes loses his own legal protection. An operator who knows it and insists on a full instruction protects himself, the helper, and the plant. This is your position at EGIDA.

- **Pedagogical trap no. 2: the trainee says "Wasyl is already my friend from the M1 cohort, I don't need to baby him".** Stop there. Say: personal relationship and professional instruction are two different things. Friendship with Wasyl is a bonus (easier for him to remember, easier to escalate), but **it does not relieve you** of the formal duty. Sign-off, signatures, cards are your legal protections. If Wasyl suffers an accident and there is no filled-in card, art. 283 § 1 of the Polish Labour Code concerns you, regardless of the friendship. Friendship does not protect against PIP.

- **Pedagogical trap no. 3: the trainee says "this is too much, in a Polish sawmill nobody does that much".** I agree that in many Polish sawmills the instruction is ticked-box or only formal. EGIDA is aspirational. But: **you** will one day become foreman, shift master, manager. In your own plant you **will introduce** this standard. This is your generational change. You will come to the plant as a self-reliant operator with M3 EGIDA. After three years you will become foreman. After five, shift master. The pace and quality of the instruction in your plant will be **your** responsibility, not your predecessors'. What you learned today from Mykoła, tomorrow you will introduce at your own place.

- **Pedagogical trap no. 4: the Murat scene is emotionally strong.** The second time in M3 W1 (the first was in l5, the accident). Some trainees will have a reaction: tears, silence, personal questions. Do not force a comment. Reassure them that the lesson is not therapy: Murat is a narrative figure, his situation represents **a pattern** of many real situations in Polish sawmills. If a trainee wants to share his own story (his own accident, a colleague's accident), give him space, but do not require it. Listen, thank, return to the lesson. The group will remember above all your **behaviour** towards the trainee's story, not the words in the lesson.

- **Link with l3 (art. 212, 283, 100):** today the loop of the three articles started in l3 closes. In l3 they were your defences (before the foreman, in a refusal). Today they are your duties (towards the helper). The same legal text works in two directions. Draw the group's attention to this symmetry: the system of the Polish Labour Code is neither the defence of the worker nor of the employer, it is a **frame** that defines roles and responsibilities.

- **Link with l5 (Murat's accident):** Murat comes back in today's lesson, not as a production figure, but as a **commentator on the cycle**. His scene is the closing of the narrative arc from l5. Use this moment to show that the accident in l5 had **consequences** for the whole narrative of the module, it was not a "didactic insert". In a good training every scene has its echoes in later scenes.

- **Link with l6 (OR-OP card) and l7 (unusual conditions):** Wasyl does **not** fill in OR-OP cards during the instruction (the card is the operator's role, not the helper's). But Wasyl has the right to **know** that the card exists, and that Mykoła fills it in before a new operation. In point 7 of the instruction (the helper's tasks) you can add: "when Mykoła fills in the risk assessment card before a new operation, you work in the yellow zone calmly, you do not distract his attention". This is a subtle part of the sawmill culture: the helper respects the operator's time for reflection before the operation.

- **Wasyl's path onwards:** in week 2 Wasyl will be with Mykoła on the classification of sawn timber (l1, l2, l3), wood defects, moisture. In week 3 Wasyl helps in the kiln-drying campaign (l3, l4, l5). In week 4 Wasyl assists at outgoing goods, claims, the FSC audit. After four weeks of Mykoła's M3 Wasyl is ready for promotion to M2 in July. This path is in the syllabus, but it is worth emphasising it in the instructor notes, because it shows that l8 is not an isolated lesson, but an **opening** onto four weeks of cooperation. The trainer can refer to "Wasyl as the background hero" in the remaining M3 lessons.

- **Optional practical exercise:** if the group contains people with instruction experience (former foremen, shift masters, operators with long tenure), ask one of them to run a hypothetical instruction in front of the group (the group in the role of "helper"). The others observe, say what was done well, what was missing. Three such demonstrations in a group of twenty-five are more valuable than four hours of theory. If you do not have such people in the group, prepare a video recording of an instruction (15 minutes, a simple saw will do).

- **Closing of W1 M3:** today's lesson closes week 1 of Module 3. Do a retrospective with the group: what was the hardest (usually l3 law or l5 accident), what was the most interesting (usually l8 instruction or l6 card), what would they like more of (usually practical scenes, not theory). This gives you data to modify future cohorts and builds a sense of closure of the OSH block. In week 2 the tone changes (materials science), it is good to mark the transition with a pause.
