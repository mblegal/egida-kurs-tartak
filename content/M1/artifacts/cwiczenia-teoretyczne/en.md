# M1 theoretical exercises: 8 mini-cases

## Purpose

A set of **8 mini-cases** for group discussion, **between l7 (recap) and l8 (test)**. Goal: practice applying the 12 principles of M1 in open conversation, before the trainee faces closed questions in the test. The exercises build **networked thinking** (combining principles from several lessons), not just memory of isolated facts.

Format: the trainer reads a case aloud to the group, gives 2-3 minutes for individual reflection, then open discussion (10-15 min per case). Total 8 cases × 12 min ≈ 2 hours of discussion. Can be split into 2 blocks of 4 cases each (morning + afternoon).

## Case 1: „No gloves today"

**Situation**. Monday, 7:30, workstation prep at PT-02 (band saw). The helper opens the PPE cabinet: the anti-slip gloves were taken yesterday by a colleague from another shift and not returned. The foreman is busy briefing another crew. The operator is already coming and says: „hurry up, we're starting soon, bring the first log from the yard".

**Discussion questions**:
1. Which M1 principles activate in this situation?
2. What does the helper do before starting work?
3. Can he „make do" with thermal gloves or cotton work gloves?

**Analytical hint** (for the trainer). Activate: **principle 3 (PPE: 6 items with the glove exception)**, **principle 4 (Labour Code art. 210)**, **principle 6 (6-point list: item 1 PPE)**. The helper does not enter the workstation without the correct gloves: he goes to the PPE store; if closed, he calls the foreman (even during the other briefing, this is urgent). „Substitute" gloves (thermal, cotton) are NOT equivalent to anti-slip ones for log handling: different protection. At the saw itself (rotating machine) gloves come off anyway, but feeding a log from the stack to the saw requires gloves beforehand. The operator may grumble about the delay: not his decision. The helper cites Labour Code art. 210 § 1: „I am refraining until I receive the correct PPE; this is a direct hazard".

## Case 2: „Smoke from the planer side"

**Situation**. Wednesday, 10:20, after the coffee break. The helper returns to the hall and from 10 metres away sees **grey smoke** coming from planer S-04. Nobody has reacted yet; the S-04 operator is still on break, the rest of the crew scattered. The smell: „rubber and oil".

**Discussion questions**:
1. What is the helper's first move in the first 5 seconds?
2. Who does he notify and how?
3. What risks combine here?

**Hint**. Activate: **principle 2 (6 hazards: fire + mechanical + dust)**, **principle 11 (escalation pyramid)**, **principle 12 (Heinrich: do not ignore)**. First move: a **shout „UWAGA! POŻAR!"** (alarm command from l4) + **STOP gesture shown to everyone in line of sight** (l3). Next: **emergency stop on the planer** (if still running, switch off), **ABC extinguisher** (not water: electrical hazard), **112 in parallel** if the smoke thickens. Notify the foreman by phone (do not run looking for him: time matters). Risks combine: smoke = fire (T1 l4), explosive-dust risk (T1 l3 + T3 l7) in the extraction ducts, electric shock risk (cables near the machine). Evacuation: T1 l8, known routes.

## Case 3: „They delivered oak logs, we expected pine"

**Situation**. Tuesday, 8:45. A log delivery arrives at the yard; the paper delivery note says „Scots pine, 15 m³". The helper notices, however, that **the bark is darker than pine** and the cross-section of one trunk (already cut before) has **a dark heartwood typical of oak**. The foreman and storekeeper are absent (in a meeting).

**Discussion questions**:
1. What does the helper do with the delivery?
2. Why does the difference (pine vs oak) matter for subsequent work in the hall?
3. What PPE should be reconsidered?

**Hint**. Activate: **principle 2 (IARC 1 for oak)**, **principle 3 (PPE: FFP2 mask becomes FFP3)**, **principle 12 (Heinrich: report the discrepancy at once)**. The helper **does not unload** (not a storekeeper, no authority to verify). He **writes in the minor-incident log** with the 5W1H formula: „I see a discrepancy between paper and material, requires verification by the storekeeper". He calls or walks to the foreman (level 1). The difference matters because: oak = **carcinogenic dust IARC 1** (pine is not); saw operators must switch FFP2 → FFP3; oak dust in the extraction ducts may **not mix out with pine residues** (batch contamination). The helper does not make a commercial decision: he reports the fact.

## Case 4: „Forklift with no driver"

**Situation**. Friday, 13:50, after lunch. The helper walks onto the log yard and sees **a forklift standing alone, key in the ignition, engine off, but the handbrake not engaged**: the forklift rests on a slight slope. Two metres away, a large log stack. If the forklift moves, it will hit the stack. The driver is missing (apparently went to the toilet, left the forklift). Behind the stack a female worker (the break-room housekeeper, who came in with her 4-year-old son because school is over) is **playing with the child**.

**Discussion questions**:
1. What is the risk in the first 10 seconds?
2. What does the helper do immediately?
3. What log entries are needed after the situation is resolved?

**Hint**. Activate: **principle 5 (2-second decision)**, **principle 8 (log yard #1 fatality)**, **principle 1 (I help but do not operate: but here an emergency)**, **principle 11 (escalation + 112)**. Risk: forklift rolls → hits stack → stack tips → child behind stack dies. First move: a **shout „UWAGA! ODSUŃ SIĘ!"** to the housekeeper with the child (with a gesture showing direction away from the stack). Second move: **secure the forklift**. Here an exception to principle 1. In a direct life-threatening situation the helper MAY engage the handbrake (this is not „machine operation", it is a rescue move: Polish Penal Code art. 162 § 1 duty to help). Right after: report to the shift manager (foreman absent), **112 if the child has already approached**. After the situation: entry in the minor-incident log (5W1H), talk with the forklift driver, discussion of the „children on the yard" policy with management (absolutely forbidden, but it happens).

## Case 5: „Sign me in, mate"

**Situation**. Thursday, 7:45. The helper approaches the desk with the attendance log. A colleague from the crew (Belarusian, good acquaintance, they have worked together 3 months) comes up: „listen, I will be 40 minutes late today, dentist. Sign me in for 6:50, OK? Normally I would do it myself, but I want to avoid trouble with the foreman, just this once". The helper likes him, knows the colleague is in financial trouble and a late mark can mean a deduction.

**Discussion questions**:
1. What happens if the helper signs?
2. How to refuse while keeping the friendship?
3. Is this „a small matter" or a big one?

**Hint**. Activate: **principle 10 (4 documents: attendance log)**, **principle 12 (reporting culture: „trivial" is a myth)**. Document forgery = **Polish Penal Code art. 270** = fine or up to 3 years' prison. For a migrant: loss of work permit, deportation, 5-year entry ban. For the colleague: same + disciplinary dismissal. **Refusal**: „Listen, I understand, I know you are in a tough spot. But if I sign, we both lose our jobs in Poland: this is not a small thing, it is a criminal paragraph. Better call the foreman now, warn him about the delay; dentist is a human reason, he will not fire you. Even with 40 minutes docked, the loss is tiny compared to deportation. I can help with something else: I can take over your share of the work for the first hour, talk to the operator that you are coming. But I will not sign". **Key**: the migrant does not sign but **offers real alternatives**. Friendship is preserved through kindness, not through breaking the law.

## Case 6: „The emergency stop does not work"

**Situation**. Routine 6-point workstation list (7:10). The helper checks item 6: the emergency stop. He presses it: **the button latches, but the machine gets no stop signal** (in test mode, engine in „ready" position). He tests a second time: same thing. The operator is 5 metres away, warming up other equipment. The foreman will arrive in 15 minutes.

**Discussion questions**:
1. Can the helper start work?
2. Who does he notify and how urgently?
3. Does this go into the minor-incident log or the machine-failure log?

**Hint**. Activate: **principle 4 (Labour Code art. 210: red workstation)**, **principle 6 (6-point list)**, **principle 11 (escalation)**, **principle 12 (Heinrich: preventive near-miss)**. A broken emergency stop = **direct health hazard** (cannot stop the machine in an accident) = **„red workstation"**. The helper **does not start work**. He informs the operator immediately (STOP gesture + approach + Polish word „uwaga, wyłącznik"). He calls the foreman (does not wait 15 minutes: urgent). Entry: **machine-failure log** (foreman or maintenance writes, not the helper alone, since it is a technical matter). The helper **additionally** writes in the minor-incident log with the 5W1H formula (what: emergency stop does not work; when: 7:10; where: PT-02 item 6 of the list; who: me, witness operator Nowak; why: hypothesis: broken signal cable or bad contact; how: pressed 2× test, no signal). Work is halted until maintenance repair. Could be 2 hours or 2 days: decision of the foreman / mechanic.

## Case 7: „I cannot hear the operator through the ear protection"

**Situation**. Third week on the job, the helper knows the operator's rhythm well. Today, however, a problem: the operator calls him with the PODAJ gesture, the helper hands over, the operator suddenly makes **a new gesture the helper does not recognize**: a few quick hand motions, as if pushing away, but not the standard ODSUŃ SIĘ. The hall is noisy (100 dB), foam ear plugs, no words audible. The helper stands with the next log in his hands, unsure.

**Discussion questions**:
1. What does the helper do at the moment of uncertainty?
2. What is the general rule for an „unknown gesture"?
3. How to prevent this situation in the future?

**Hint**. Activate: **principle 5 (2-second decision)**, **principle 9 (5 gestures + eye contact)**, **principle 12 (reporting culture)**. General rule: **in case of uncertainty → treat as a warning gesture** (safe mode) → **STEP ASIDE and place yourself outside the working zone** → then establish eye contact and use the I NEED HELP gesture (opening palms) to show you did not understand. The operator will repeat slower or come over to explain. **Never assume „he probably meant X"**: wrong interpretation = accident. Prevention for the future: **at the first-day briefing on a new station ask the foreman about local gestures** (every crew has 2-3 non-standard ones); write them in a notebook. After a week on the job: with the operator in a break „listen, do you have any gestures of your own that are not standard? Show me, I want to know".

## Case 8: „I hid the finger cut"

**Situation**. The helper comes home after a shift with a visible injury on the right finger: a cut from a splinter while filing the hand saw (T3 l6). In the morning it did not hurt, now it is swollen, bleeding more than he thought. He did not report it at work: because „it is nothing", because the foreman did not see it, because he did not want to „waste a quarter of an hour on paperwork". After 3 days: **infection**: the finger is swollen, fever 38.5°C, poor condition. He goes to the doctor, who says: „this is a **work accident** if the wound happened at work, but it must be reported at the time of the accident. Do you have a report?".

**Discussion questions**:
1. What are the consequences for the helper?
2. What should he have done 3 days earlier?
3. Why is „a small thing" the most dangerous myth?

**Hint**. Activate: **principle 12 (Heinrich 1:30:300:3000)**, **principle 11 (escalation pyramid: level 1 foreman, entry in minor-incident log)**, **principle 4 (Labour Code art. 210: right to report a health hazard)**. Consequences: **no entry = no work accident** = treatment at the helper's own cost (not social insurance). Cost of infection treatment + sick leave without pay = PLN 2-3k loss. If an IV antibiotic pump is needed + 3-day hospitalization: PLN 8-15k. The insurer will refuse because „not reported in time". What to do 3 days earlier: **an entry in the minor-incident log** (5W1H: finger cut while filing the saw, witness operator X, dressing applied), a quarter of an hour of time, zero cost, full social-insurance coverage should it worsen. The „trivial" myth: every serious accident starts with a minor one (Heinrich), and on a personal level: every small wound has a chance of infection (statistically 2-5%, especially wood + oil + dust). Self-protection requires 2 minutes of paperwork.

## Summary of the 8 cases

Each case covers **at least 3 M1 principles** and requires **integrated thinking** (not „find the correct answer", but „assemble a response from elements"). After the 8 cases the trainee should have an ingrained reflex:

1. **Stop**, observe, assess.
2. **Immediate safety**: physical (step back, warn), then organizational.
3. **Escalation** per the pyramid, not alone.
4. **Documentation**: minor-incident log / failure log / accident protocol depending on severity.
5. **Team learning**: feedback, what the group takes away.

## Notes for the trainer

**Running the session**:
- 2 hours total (8 cases × 12 min = 96 min + 24 min buffer for discussions that outgrow the case).
- Each case: 2 min reading, 2 min individual reflection, 8 min group discussion.
- The trainer does not provide the answer up front. He lets the group reach it on its own. The analytical hint is the **final stage**: a check whether the group arrived at the same answer as EGIDA experts.

**Typical group mistakes**:
- Forgetting Labour Code art. 210 as a legal shield (trainees are afraid to refuse by default).
- Focus on „what to do physically" rather than „how to document" (migrants do not trust paper, you must reinforce).
- Mixing „trivial" with „not important" (principle 12 is the foundation, return to it often).

**After the 8 cases**: a short closing exercise: **each trainee picks one case** and tells the group „how I would do it differently from what the group suggested". If the answer matches the group's suggestion, let it and praise. If it differs, discuss why. This moment (the final 20 minutes) usually delivers more learning than all 8 cases together.

**Supporting materials**: printed cases A5 (one case per sheet, handed to the trainee). Note paper. Flipchart to record key group takeaways.

**Announcement of l8**: „tomorrow the test. These cases are the best training: if you made it through 8 today, you will make it through 3 in the test".
