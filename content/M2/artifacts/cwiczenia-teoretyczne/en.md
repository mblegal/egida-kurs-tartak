# M2 Theoretical Exercises – 15 mini-cases for individual study and group discussion

## Purpose

A set of **15 theoretical exercises** for use **between lesson l7 (M2 recap) and lesson l8 (theory test + practical preparation)**. Two modes of use:

1. **Self-study mode**: the trainee solves each case in writing in a notebook, or aloud in their head, 10 min per exercise. No grading. Goal: practice applying the knowledge from the 32 M2 lessons in open discussion, before encountering closed-ended questions in the test.
2. **Trainer-led group discussion mode**: the trainer reads the context aloud to the group, gives 2-3 minutes for individual reflection, then opens discussion (10-15 min per exercise). 15 × 12 min is roughly 3 hours of discussion time. Can be split into 3 blocks of 5 exercises (2 classroom days with 1 block each + 1 block on Friday after the test).

**Exercises are NOT graded**. They do not affect M2 completion. They are a **diagnostic** tool for the trainee (do I understand?) and for the trainer (who needs reinforcement?).

## Area 1: Material knowledge

### Exercise 1: "Grade mix-up"

**Context**. Wednesday 8:30, log yard. A supplier delivered a bundle of pine; the delivery paper says "Scots pine, visual grade A, 3.8 m³". You look at the bundle and notice: 3 boards with cracked knots larger than 15 mm (the grade A threshold is sound knots up to 12 mm), 2 boards with surface cracks 60 mm per linear meter (the grade A threshold is 50 mm/lm). The bundle carries the EGIDA label SO-SW-A-260420-019.

**Questions to think through**:
1. Is the bundle actually grade A?
2. What are your options for action?
3. Whom do you inform, and in what format?

**Supporting materials**:

Grade A thresholds for pine (extract from M2 T2 l5):
- Sound knots up to 12 mm, unsound knots up to 8 mm, maximum 2 per linear meter
- Surface cracks up to 50 mm per linear meter, no end cracks exceeding 1/4 of the width
- Grain deviation up to 5%, straightness deviation up to 4 mm/lm
- No mold, no blue stain, no heart darkening

**Analytical hint (for trainer)**. What activates: **A-D grading from l5 T2**, **re-grading from l8 T3**, **the "certification without evidence = no certification" rule from l3 T4 Block 1**. The bundle is NOT grade A (3 boards with cracked knots over 12 mm break the threshold, plus 2 boards with cracks over 50 mm/lm). Options: (a) accept as grade B plus entry in the logbook plus TartakERP update plus entry in the delivery register; (b) escalate to the foreman in 4W format before accepting because the price difference is 15-25 PLN per board, and 60 boards means 900-1500 PLN of value at stake. A junior operator does NOT resolve a grade difference on their own; they escalate. 4W format: "Marek, Hai here, log yard, 8:30, bundle SO-SW-A-260420-019 defects exceed grade A, please decide on acceptance".

### Exercise 2: "Moisture content after transport"

**Context**. Monday 7:15, an oak bundle SO-DĄB-FSC-260420-022 arrived overnight. You measured moisture content with a pin-type meter (Brookhuis): 4 measurement points give 24.5% / 26.1% / 23.8% / 25.2%. The dispatch slip says the oak is to go into the kiln today after 10:00. Zbigniew Majchrzak asks you, "Hai, what's the moisture content on the bundle? Do we set the kiln to the fresh program or the transport program?".

**Questions**:
1. What is the average MC and the spread?
2. Is the bundle in transport or fresh condition?
3. What answer do you give Zbigniew?

**Supporting materials**:

Moisture content classification for timber (extract from M2 T2 l3):
- Fresh: MC >30% (just felled)
- Transport: MC 18-30% (typical after summer delivery)
- Air-dry: MC 12-20% (after a season in storage)
- Kiln-dry: MC 6-12% (after a drying cycle)

Kiln programs for oak (from M2 T3 l7):
- "Fresh" program (MC >30%): 28-32 day cycle, 3-4 day warm-up
- "Transport" program (MC 18-30%): 22-25 day cycle, 2-3 day warm-up

**Hint**. Average 24.9% with a spread of about 1.15%, i.e. within the transport band. Answer to Zbigniew: "Zbyszek, average 24.9% with 2.3% spread, bundle is in transport condition, 22-25 day program, 2-3 day warm-up". What activates: **pin-type meter from l3 T2**, **three drying phases from l7 T3**, **loader-foreman responsibility split from l7 T3** (the operator reports measurements, the foreman programs the cycle). A common junior mistake: giving only the average without the spread. A spread over 3% requires additional measurement at 4 more points (because an uneven cycle will endanger the batch).

### Exercise 3: "Unreadable label"

**Context**. Thursday 10:45. You are receiving a bundle from Krzysztof (forklift operator) at the FSC field. The label on the side of the bundle has been partly washed out by overnight rain; only a fragment is visible: "SO-__B-FSC-26042_-0__". The dispatch paper says "SO-DĄB-FSC-260420-007, customer Bouwhout". You cannot see the last three digits.

**Questions**:
1. Do you accept the bundle?
2. What do you do if Krzysztof is in a hurry and says "take it, we'll sort it out later"?
3. What are the business risks if you accept?

**Hint**. You do NOT accept the bundle without a complete label. **The rule from l3 T4 Block 1**: "bundle without a label = warehouse keeper's error = escalation". A partial label is equivalent to a missing label, because it does not confirm a specific batch number. To Krzysztof: "Krzysiek, STOP, don't unload, the label is unreadable. We pause until this is clarified with the warehouse keeper or Marek". Risks: (1) loss of the FSC chain of custody if this bundle turns out to be a different species than ordered, (2) batch mix-up leading to the wrong customer leading to the loss of the export customer Bouwhout van Doorn, (3) certifier penalty of 150-300 thousand PLN if a NEPCon/SGS audit catches it. What activates: **FSC certification from l3 T4 Block 1**, **supply chain**, **IMMEDIATE urgency classification from l5 T4 Block 2** (uncontrolled risk grows).

## Area 2: Machines under supervision

### Exercise 4: "Blade tensioning"

**Context**. Tuesday 7:45, band saw 2, first cut of the day in oak. You check before starting: oil OK, blade tensioning pressure per the gauge 1900 PSI. The dispatch order is for oak 50×200×3000, class C30. Tadeusz Kowalski (senior) is observing the first settings.

**Questions**:
1. Is 1900 PSI correct for oak?
2. What is the full range for oak and for pine?
3. What do you say to Tadeusz?

**Supporting materials**:

Wood-Mizer LT40 tensioning pressure table (from M2 T3 l2):
- Pine / spruce 100-250 mm: 1800-2200 PSI
- Pine / spruce 250-400 mm: 2000-2400 PSI
- Oak / beech 100-250 mm: 2200-2600 PSI
- Oak / beech 250-400 mm: 2400-2800 PSI

**Hint**. 1900 PSI is TOO LOW for oak (minimum 2200 PSI for thicknesses 100-250 mm). Answer to Tadeusz: "Tadeusz, 1900 PSI is too low for oak, I'm raising it to 2300 PSI". What activates: **band saw setup from l2 T3**, **species vs. pressure**, **senior observation**. Pressure too low leads to blade vibration, leads to a wavy cut surface, leads to a grade drop plus faster dulling. Pressure too high leads to fatigue in the blade steel, leads to a risk of blade breakage, leads to replacement cost. The 300-400 PSI difference between ranges is not a nuance; it is a safety and quality parameter.

### Exercise 5: "Kiln alarm"

**Context**. Monday 7:15. The panel of kiln 3 shows: day 14 of the oak cycle, programmed temperature 55°C (matching the oak program), actual 61°C for 45 min (per the log). Yellow alarm. Zbigniew Majchrzak is on a coffee break, back in 10 min. Marek is in the morning briefing.

**Questions**:
1. Is 61°C in an oak cycle a critical problem?
2. Do you have authority to shut down the kiln?
3. What do you do in the next 3 minutes?

**Hint**. 61°C exceeds the oak limit (60°C) by only 1°C, but it has been 45 min, meaning potential damage to the medullary rays (radial cracking above 60°C). You cannot ignore it. You do NOT have authority to shut down the kiln (mantra "I load, I close, I report, I do NOT program cycles"). 3 minutes: (1) I run to Zbigniew in the break room (30 m), (2) if he's not there, phone call to Marek despite the briefing plus SMS, (3) if he doesn't answer, phone call to Piotr Czerwiński the shift supervisor plus SMS, (4) record the time and values in the logbook as an incident timeline. What activates: **species limits from l7 T3**, **loader-foreman responsibility split from l7 T3**, **3 urgency categories IMMEDIATE from l5 T4 Block 2**, **5-level escalation chain**.

### Exercise 6: "Strange noise from the band saw"

**Context**. Friday 11:30. You are cutting the 15th spruce beam in the day's cycle. The ammeter is normal, in the green zone. But for the last 2 minutes you have been hearing a strange metallic clang on every cut that was not there earlier. The operator Tadeusz reports that Marek has driven to the other sawmill. You are halfway through the 15th beam.

**Questions**:
1. Do you finish the cut or stop immediately?
2. What do you report and to whom?
3. What is the risk if you ignore it?

**Hint**. **FINISH THE CURRENT CUT** (safety: do not pull the blade out of the log, do not change parameters while running), then STOP. What activates: **controlled STOP from l2 T3**, **symptomatic description, not diagnostic, from l5 T4 Block 2**, **3 urgency categories**. Report to: **Piotr Czerwiński the shift supervisor** (the foreman Marek is off-site, a level skip is permitted plus SMS to Marek). 4W format: "Piotr, Hai here, band saw 2, 11:30, new metallic clang on every cut for the last 2 min, ammeter normal, STOP after the 15th beam, please arrange mechanic diagnosis". I do NOT write "damaged bearing" because that is a diagnosis requiring experience. Risks of ignoring: (a) damage to the drive wheel or the guide bearing equals repair 8-15 thousand PLN plus downtime 1-2 days, (b) potential blade rupture in motion equals threat to the operator (blade speed 28-32 m/s, a broken blade flies like a projectile).

### Exercise 7: "Sorting under time pressure"

**Context**. Thursday 14:30, the last hour of the shift. Piotr Czerwiński comes over and says, "Hai, Marek changed the plan, 30 grade A boards need to ship to a customer on Friday morning, sort them faster now". 40 boards remain to be sorted. Your average sorting speed: 3 minutes per board, including moisture measurement and defect assessment. 40 boards × 3 min = 120 min = 2 hours. The shift ends in 1.5 h. Piotr: "no way around it, we're doing it".

**Questions**:
1. Do you agree to speed up?
2. What are the risks of technically cutting corners on the procedure?
3. What reporting format do you use?

**Hint**. **I do NOT speed up at the cost of procedure**. What activates: **art. 210 KP right to refuse**, **Piotr's 5 criteria from l8 T4 Block 3** (procedural accuracy outweighs tempo), **4W format**, **the rule that the operator in the crew proposes understanding**. Answer to Piotr (assertive, respectful): "Piotr, the 3 minutes per board is because of the full MC measurement plus defect assessment. Cutting it short risks a re-grading that costs more than overtime. I propose: (a) I stay on overtime 30 min until 15:30, finish 30 A boards properly, and leave the remaining 10 for the Monday shift, (b) Marek authorizes a second operator now, (c) renegotiation with the customer for Monday morning". Report to Marek (I do not bypass him, because the plan change came from him): SMS plus phone call in 4W format. Entry in the minor-events register with description. Risks of cutting corners: (a) MC unmeasured leads to a 16% board shipping as 12% leads to a complaint a month later, (b) defect overlooked leads to grade A instead of B leads to a customer penalty of 150 PLN per board, (c) dimensions not verified lead to a 197 mm board instead of 200 mm lead to the rejection of the whole batch.

## Area 3: Operational documentation

### Exercise 8: "Missing field on the dispatch slip"

**Context**. Tuesday 6:20, you receive dispatch slip DP-2026-052. Marek hands it over and walks off. You read the fields: customer, order, species, dimensions, quantity, priority, deadline, special remarks, machine parameters, operator signatures. You notice that **the "dimensional tolerance" field is empty** (it should contain a value, either ±1 mm or ±2 mm).

**Questions**:
1. Why is tolerance important?
2. Do you sign the slip without this field?
3. How do you phrase the question to Marek?

**Supporting materials**:

Dispatch slip fields (extract from M2 T4 Block 1 l1):

| Field | Example | Criticality |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Customer | Dębowy Dom Wieliczka | High |
| 2. Order | SK-2026-024 | High |
| 3. Species | Scots pine C24 | CRITICAL |
| 4. Dimensions | 100×200×4000 mm | CRITICAL |
| 5. Tolerance | ±1 mm / ±2 mm | CRITICAL |
| 6. Quantity | 45 pcs / 10.8 m³ | High |
| 7. Priority | A / B / C | High |
| 8. Deadline | 26.04.2026 15:00 | High |
| 9. Remarks | No unsound knots | Medium |
| 10. Parameters | 2200 PSI, 50 mm blade | High |

**Hint**. The "tolerance" field is CRITICAL, because ±1 mm requires a different saw setup and tighter dimensional control than ±2 mm. Joinery customers often require ±1 mm, structural customers ±2 mm. You do NOT sign the slip without this field. You stop Marek with the "I see X, I understand Y, is that correct?" schema: "Marek, I see the tolerance field is empty, I understand the default is ±2 mm construction-grade, is that correct?". If Marek confirms, he should write it in with a pencil and initial it. If he says "I don't know, I'll call the customer", you put the cut on hold until the value is received. PIP statistic: 4% of sawmill accidents are caused by verbal instructions inconsistent with the state of the stock, leading to re-grading risk plus customer loss.

### Exercise 9: "Logbook after a complicated shift"

**Context**. Friday 13:50, 5 min to the end of the shift. You are filling in the green logbook ZMI-2026-094-01. The shift was turbulent: (a) 3 blade stoppages (replacement, dulling, jam), (b) 1 priority change from Marek, first verbally then in writing, (c) cracked beam reported to Piotr, (d) rotation with Oleksii for half an hour (Oleksii on the band saw, you on sorting). Maricel will be on the Monday cycle.

**Questions**:
1. What are the 8 main fields that must be filled in?
2. What information do you include for the replacement operator in the faults field and the blades table?
3. What additional entries go beyond the 8 fields?

**Hint**. **Eight main fields** (from l2 T4 Block 1): number, date, operator, machine, motor-hours, m³ in, m³ out, efficiency indicator. Plus **tables**: stoppages T/M/O/P with owner, blades with dullings. Plus **faults field** in a 3-part format of what/when/what I did (not a diagnosis). Key information for Maricel: (a) blade 11 left on the band saw after about 180 cuts of dulling, replace on Monday, (b) cracked beam 4 from bundle SO-DĄB-FSC-003 reported to Piotr at 9:15, quarantined in the east field, (c) the rotation from sorting to the band saw was good practice, Oleksii willing, can be repeated. Additional entries: (a) priority change verbal at 10:20 from B to A, written confirmation in pencil by Marek at 10:23, initialed "M.N.", (b) cracked beam moved to quarantine plus report number to Piotr. **The A6 notebook habit**: all exact times from the notebook, not from memory. **The logbook has two audiences**: the white one to Marek to the dispatcher, the green one to Maricel to operational knowledge.

### Exercise 10: "FSC certification in practice"

**Context**. Tuesday 10:15. A new dispatch slip DP-2026-055 from the customer Kingfisher Polska, order KF-2026-003. Oak 50×200×3000 mm, class C24, 100 pcs, FSC 100% marking. Kingfisher requires **complete supply chain documentation**: bundle label, receipt register, sender's certificate (FSC-C012345 EGIDA), document confirming the FSC storage field. Marek asks: "Hai, list the things to keep under control for this customer?".

**Questions**:
1. What is the "chain-of-custody" for this bundle?
2. What are 3 typical junior mistakes with FSC?
3. What are the business risks if you miss something?

**Hint**. What activates: **PN-EN 1611-1 + PN-EN 14081-1 + FSC from l3 T4 Block 1**, **three FSC markings (100% / Mix / Recycled) from l3**, **mixing equals loss of certification**. Chain-of-custody list: (a) bundle collected from a specific FSC field (not a regular one), label with code SO-DĄB-FSC-date-number visible, (b) receipt register with date, time, operator, forklift operator Krzysztof, bundle number, (c) entry in the logbook "FSC bundle, east field, label verified", (d) cutting under Tadeusz's supervision with entry in the logbook, (e) after sorting, a new finished-product label with origin code FSC-C012345, (f) TartakERP entries linking the source bundle to the final product. Three typical junior mistakes from l3 T4 Block 1: (1) "it's probably certified" without verifying the label, (2) confusing visual classes S0-S4 with strength classes C14-C50, (3) substituting FSC for PEFC (Kingfisher accepts ONLY FSC, PEFC is not permitted). Risks: (a) loss of EGIDA certification at the annual NEPCon audit, (b) penalty 150-300 thousand PLN, (c) loss of the Kingfisher customer (annual contract 800 thousand PLN).

## Area 4: Crew relations and self-regulation

### Exercise 11: "Hand signals with the forklift operator in noise"

**Context**. Thursday 8:30, log yard, noise from the Toyota forklift plus band saw 2 in the background. Krzysztof is maneuvering bundle SO-FSC-260420-024 toward your position. Shouting is pointless, he cannot hear you. The bundle is 3 m from your position. You want him to stop, because another operator is coming out of the hall with another bundle; a collision is possible.

**Questions**:
1. What signal do you use, and exactly how?
2. Is eye contact before or after?
3. What do you do if Krzysztof does not respond?

**Hint**. What activates: **3 standard hand signals from l4 T4 Block 2**, **mandatory eye contact**, **3 m maneuvering zone**, **no entry between machine and load**. **STOP signal**: right arm raised straight above the head, open palm facing the forklift operator. Hold for at least 3 seconds. Eye contact **BEFORE** (make sure he is looking at you) leads to the signal leads to eye contact **AFTER** (make sure he understood and stopped). If Krzysztof does not respond: (a) step back out of the 3 m zone, (b) repeat the STOP signal with both hands plus body movement signaling "stop", (c) if there is still no response, shout "STOP, STOP!" plus possibly run into the line of sight of the other operator to warn him. **NEVER step between the forklift and the bundle**. After the incident, conversation with Krzysztof plus Marek in 4W format plus entry in the logbook as a near-miss.

### Exercise 12: "Colleague dozing at the band saw"

**Context**. Wednesday 14:45, hour 7 of the shift. Your colleague Emeka (operator on band saw 3) nods off for a few seconds standing at his station between cuts. You see this from band saw 2, 8 m away. Band saw 3 is just finishing a cut, and Emeka is about to start another cycle. No foreman on the floor, Piotr Czerwiński is in the office.

**Questions**:
1. Is this a problem, or does it happen to everyone?
2. What do you do in the next 30 seconds?
3. What is the objective risk?

**Hint**. **It happens to everyone**, but **hour 7 is the peak accident risk hour, 24%** (CIOP-PIB 2019-2023, l6 T4 Block 2). Dozing next to a machine with a blade running at 28-32 m/s is immediate risk. 30 seconds: (a) shout "Emeka! Emeka!" to wake him before the next cycle, (b) STOP signal in his direction, (c) approach after securing band saw 2 (stopped), (d) short conversation "hey, I saw you, do you need a micro-break?", (e) if yes, stop his saw plus phone call to Piotr in 4W format "Piotr, Hai here, band saw 3, 14:45, Emeka 3 fatigue signs plus dozing, please arrange rotation or a 10 min micro-break" plus SMS to Marek. **What activates**: **self-regulation from l6 T4 Block 2**, **the operator monitors fatigue in colleagues** (operator-junior distinction, point 5), **5-level escalation**. Objective risk: hand in the blade zone, amputation or death, PIP statistic 41 fatalities per year in the wood processing sector.

### Exercise 13: "Conflict with Krzysztof"

**Context**. Friday 11:00. Krzysztof Zagrodny tells you, irritated: "Hai, how many times will I wait for your READY signal? I've got 12 bundles today, I can't wait 2 min on each". You need 2 min because you only move to a new bundle after verifying the label and making an entry in the logbook for the previous one.

**Questions**:
1. Is Krzysztof right?
2. How do you respond and keep the relationship intact?
3. Who do you bring in if the conflict continues?

**Hint**. Krzysztof is **NOT right** on the safety side, but he **is right** operationally (12 bundles × 2 min = 24 min lost per hour). Relationship-preserving answer: "Krzysiek, I understand the time pressure, but the READY signal is the safety moment for you and for me. The 2 min is the label check plus the entry. I propose a three-way conversation with Marek, maybe there's a way to shorten the procedure on my side without risk. Until that conversation I'll keep the 2 min". **What activates**: **communication with the foreman in 4W format from l5 T4 Block 2**, **assertiveness with respect**, **5-level escalation**. You bring in **Marek** (level 1), not Piotr (level 2), because an operational conflict within the crew is the foreman's area. If Marek does not resolve it, it escalates to Piotr. A three-way conversation gives Marek the chance to decide: (a) verify whether 2 min is necessary (maybe 90 s is enough), (b) task rotation (someone else does the entries), (c) procedure change signed off in writing by management. After the conversation: logbook entry on both sides plus a potential addendum to the workstation instruction.

## Area 5: Integrative (2+ areas combined)

### Exercise 14: "Monday from zero"

**Context**. Monday 6:15, the first day after the weekend, the first full shift of the month. Your to-do list by 7:00: (a) take delivery of an oak bundle from the night warehouse keeper, (b) check kiln 3 after the weekend cycle, (c) receive dispatch slip DP-2026-060 from Marek, (d) prepare band saw 2 for the first pine cut for the customer Dębowy Dom. Weather: wet from the weekend, labels may be crumpled. You have **45 minutes**.

**Questions**:
1. In what order do you do the tasks?
2. What risks do you anticipate?
3. Which step do you escalate if something goes wrong?

**Hint**. Prioritization from the integration of 3 areas (material + machines + documents): **by descending criticality**. Order: (1) dispatch slip from Marek first, because without it I don't know what to do, 5 min (check 10 fields plus "I see X, I understand Y, is that correct" questions), (2) kiln 3 urgent because the cycle may need Zbigniew's intervention, 10 min (panel check, MC at 4 positions if the door is already open, report to Zbigniew), (3) oak bundle 15 min (label check, SO-DĄB-FSC-number code, delivery paper, MC with pin meter), (4) band saw 2 last, 10 min (oil, pressure, guards, emergency stop, 30 s idle run). Risks: (a) incomplete dispatch slip leads to delay pending clarification, (b) kiln 3 with a weekend temperature anomaly leads to batch loss 15-25 m³, (c) bundle label unreadable from moisture leads to refusal and delay, (d) band saw 2 with low pressure or an oil leak leads to a delayed cycle start. Escalation: any block not finished in its time window equals 4W to Marek plus SMS plus continuation of the rest of the list.

### Exercise 15: "Full re-grading chain"

**Context**. Friday 8:00, you and Zbigniew are opening kiln 2 after the pine cycle SO-SW-A-260420-015 (you loaded it 10 days ago). First measurements: average MC 11.4%, spread 2.8% (above the standard of <2%). You open boards from position D (lowest middle), you see 2 boards with end cracks at 1/3 of the width (the A/B borderline). Zbigniew asks, "Hai, your assessment?". The customer Dębowy Dom ordered 30 grade A boards from this batch.

**Questions**:
1. What is the comprehensive diagnosis of the batch?
2. What documents are generated by this process?
3. Who is informed and when?

**Hint**. Integration of 4 areas: **material + machines + documents + communication**. Diagnosis: (a) spread 2.8% > standard 2% equals a slightly uneven cycle, acceptable with a warning in the report (not critical), (b) 2 boards with cracks at the A/B border equals re-grading to grade B, (c) initial batch 32 grade A boards, after re-grading 30 A + 2 B, which MATCHES the customer order of 30 A, (d) final batch 30A (for the customer) + 2B (to stock). Documents: (a) kiln 2 control report with 4 MC readings plus spread plus assessment, (b) new label SO-B-260420-015-r for the 2 boards (with the "r" re-grading suffix), (c) entry in the kiln register A4, 5-year archive, (d) TartakERP update of batch 015 with the note "30A originally, 2 re-graded to B-r", (e) operator logbook with cycle report, (f) potential customer report if this batch was tied to a specific order. Answer to Zbigniew: "Zbyszek, spread 2.8% slightly above the standard, 2 boards with end cracks go to B-r, the remaining 30 meet grade A, batch ready for Dębowy Dom". Communication: (a) Marek informed in 4W format within 30 min, (b) documentation in TartakERP by the end of the shift, (c) customer NOT informed about the re-grading because they receive the ordered 30 A (re-grading is an internal quality assurance process). **Week 3 full mantra**: "I start up, I adjust, I cut, I sort, I load, I measure, I re-grade, I update, but always under supervision".

## For the trainer: how to run this

**Preparation**. Print each exercise on a separate A4 sheet (15 sheets). Also have printed **supporting tables** ready for exercises 1, 2, 4, 8 (pine grades, moisture content, blade pressures, dispatch slip fields). Print the analytical hints for yourself; show them to the trainees only after the discussion.

**In the classroom**. Split into 3 blocks of 5 exercises, 3 × 1 h. Block 1 (ex. 1-5, areas 1 + 2): Tuesday after l7. Block 2 (ex. 6-10, areas 2 + 3): Wednesday. Block 3 (ex. 11-15, areas 4 + 5): Thursday. Friday l8 test.

**Discussion**. Read the context aloud (2 min), 3 min of silence for individual reflection, open the discussion: "who has an idea?". Migrants at A0-A1 Polish need encouragement, call on them by name. If someone speaks their mother tongue to a neighbor, allow 30 s, then "in Polish for everyone".

**Feedback**. After each exercise, read the analytical hint aloud. Compare with the group's answers: "Maricel said A, Oleksii said B, the hint says B, why?". The goal is **conscious gaps**, not a binary right/wrong result.

**Diagnostics**. Note in a private sheet who has trouble with which area. Before the Friday l8 test, run 15 min of individual consultation with the 2-3 trainees with the largest gaps.

**Mixing areas**. Integrative exercises 14 + 15 must be done (after blocks 1-3), because they reveal whether the trainee can **connect** 4 areas, not just know each separately. In the Tuesday practical test (Piotr's 5 criteria) integration is the key.

**Cultures**. Ukrainians and Belarusians are eager to discuss rule-breaking (they have that culture), Vietnamese and Filipinos rarely (culture of respect for the superior). Ask them direct questions, then they speak. A Georgian may push back against everyone in the discussion, give him space, he is verifying his identity.
