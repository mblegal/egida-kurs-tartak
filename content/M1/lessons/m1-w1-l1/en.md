## Introduction

Welcome to the course "Work in a Sawmill". If you are reading these words, you are probably starting a job as a **helper** in a Polish sawmill. The helper is the first step. The helper does not operate machines. The helper learns, watches, listens, passes the material, and cleans up. This is important work, without a helper, the sawmill does not run.

But before you touch a board, before you walk up to a machine, you must understand one thing: **a sawmill is not an ordinary workplace**. Things happen in a sawmill that do not happen in a shop, a kitchen, an office, or even on a construction site. That is why this course begins with **OHS** (Occupational Health and Safety) and not with learning how to operate a saw.

In this lesson you will learn **why a sawmill is different** and why the first four weeks of the course are mostly about safety. Once you understand "why", it is easier to remember "how".

## Objectives

After this lesson:

- You understand how a sawmill differs from other workplaces in terms of hazards.
- You know the three main differences: **machines**, **invisible hazards**, and **the weight of the material**.
- You know why the course starts with OHS and not with machine operation.
- You remember the helper's first rule: **"I do not do what I do not understand"**.

## Content

### What is a sawmill?

A **sawmill** (in Polish: *tartak*) is a plant where trees are turned into **lumber**: boards, beams, and battens. **Logs** (large pieces of wood) arrive at the sawmill. Boards leave the sawmill, and later become houses, furniture, and floors.

It sounds simple. But inside there are three things which, together, make this a dangerous place:

1. **Large, sharp machines**: saws, bands, and blades that cut wood in a fraction of a second.
2. **Hazards you cannot see**: wood dust and noise.
3. **Heavy material in motion**: logs, boards, and stacks that weigh hundreds of kilograms.

Each of these three things exists somewhere else too. But **together, in one place, on one production floor, only in a sawmill**. That is why a sawmill is different.

### Difference 1: machines that do not forgive

In a kitchen the knife is sharp, but small. If you cut yourself, it hurts for three days. In an office the biggest risk is back pain from the chair.

In a sawmill the **saw** (in Polish: *pilarka*, the machine that cuts wood) cuts a log 40 cm thick in a few seconds. The same machine cuts a human hand in one second. The blade **does not stop by itself**. The motor is 10, 20, even 50 times stronger than you are.

::: warning
A sawmill machine takes about 3 seconds to stop after it is switched off. A human hand disappears into the blade in less than 1 second. That is exactly the kind of math you cannot cheat.
:::

That is why in a sawmill we do not say "be careful", we say **"stand clear"**. The **danger zone** (the area around a machine where you must not stand) is painted on the floor. The line on the floor is not decoration. It is the border between health and the hospital.

### Difference 2: hazards you cannot see

This is the hardest part to grasp, because **it does not hurt right away**. Two hazards in a sawmill are silent:

**Wood dust.** When the saw cuts wood, dust flies into the air. You breathe it in all day. You do not see it clearly, but it settles in your lungs.

::: info
The European Union (Directive 2017/2398) confirms: dust from oak and beech is **carcinogenic**: that is, it causes cancer. After 10–20 years of working without a mask, the risk of nose and lung disease is many times higher than for other people.
:::

**Noise.** Saws are loud, 90–100 decibels. That is as loud as a busy street for 8 hours straight. Noise does not hurt, but **it destroys your hearing**. A sawmill worker without **hearing protection** (earmuffs or earplugs) hears less after a few years. After a dozen years, they go deaf.

A mask and hearing protection are not "just in case". They are tools that **protect your health in retirement**.

### Difference 3: weight and movement

A 4-metre pine board can weigh 25–30 kg. A log, 300, 500, even 1000 kg. A **lumber stack** (boards piled on top of each other) can weigh a ton.

That weight is in motion. A board rolls along the conveyor. A log is carried by a **forklift**. A stack stands still, but sometimes it falls over.

If something like that lands on a foot without **safety boots** (boots with a steel toe), it breaks bone. If a stack falls on a person, it can kill them. That is why on the sawmill floor **you always know where you are standing** and **you never stand under a lifted load**.

### Why OHS first?

Now you know. In a sawmill one mistake often does not give you a second chance.

- In a shop you got the price wrong, you correct it.
- In a kitchen you burned the meal, you cook a new one.
- In a sawmill you were not careful at the saw, **you lose fingers** or **your life**.

That is why we do not start with "how to operate a machine". We start with **"how not to die"**. The next lessons of week one (Week 1, lessons 2–8) will show you specific hazards: mechanical, dust, noise, fire. You will learn what you wear (PPE), what the floor looks like, what to do if someone is injured, how to evacuate when the alarm goes off.

::: example
In 2023, Polish wood-processing plants recorded several hundred workplace accidents registered by the National Labour Inspectorate (PIP, Państwowa Inspekcja Pracy). The three most common causes: **being caught by a machine**, **material kickback** (a piece of wood thrown from the saw), and **a lumber stack falling over**. All three could have been avoided if the worker had been standing where they were supposed to stand.
:::

### The helper's first rule

Through this whole course one rule comes back again and again. Remember it today, on day one:

> **I do not do what I do not understand. If I do not know, I ask. If nobody tells me what to do, I stop and wait.**

This is not weakness. This is not stupidity. This is **professional maturity**. The best helpers in a sawmill are the ones who ask ten questions a day. The worst accidents happen to those who "know better by themselves".

::: tip
Better to ask the foreman ten times "can I come closer?" than to lose a finger once. Always. In a sawmill there are no stupid questions, only questions asked before, not after, an accident.
:::

## Link to practice

On your first day on the sawmill floor you will see something interesting. The foreman **will not show you first** how to start a saw or how to carry boards. First, he will ask whether you have a **hard hat**, **safety boots**, **safety goggles**, and **hearing protection**. Then he will show you where the **emergency STOP button** on each machine is, and where the **first-aid kit** hangs. Only after that will you walk into the production area.

Notice the order. Safety first. Rescue equipment next. Work last. This is not a coincidence. This is the standard you learned today in this lesson.

## Trainer notes

**Pedagogical goal:** build an **attitude**, not deliver technical knowledge. The learner usually already knows that a sawmill is dangerous, but does not understand *why* the course starts with four weeks of safety. This lesson gives them that "why".

**120-minute rhythm:** about 15 min for introduction and group discussion (each participant points to a place on a map of Poland where they come from, builds group rapport), 60 min of reading the content with a facilitator, 25 min on terms and self-check, 20 min on a conversation about "the helper's first rule" (each participant shares an example of a situation when they would rather ask than guess).

**Note on mixed groups:** even if some learners have experience from construction or from a factory, stress that **a sawmill has specifics that are not found elsewhere**. However, avoid comparisons like "a sawmill is worse than X", this is not a contest. Every trade has its own hazards. A sawmill has these three together.

**Supporting materials:** on screen, show a photo of a large circular saw (from the PIP handbook "Safe Work in a Sawmill", pp. 8–12). Optional: a short film (2–3 min) from the PIP YouTube channel showing a sawmill production line. Do not show injury photos in the first lesson, those will appear later, in the first-aid lesson (m1-w1-l7).

**Common learner mistake:** "I already know this, I have worked in construction". Trainer's answer: "Good that you have experience. That is exactly why you need to hear what makes a sawmill different, so that an old habit does not become a new hazard here".

**Link to the next lesson (m1-w1-l2 "Mechanical Hazards Around You"):** "Today we said that machines do not forgive. Tomorrow we will see exactly which parts of machines are most dangerous and where the danger zone runs."
