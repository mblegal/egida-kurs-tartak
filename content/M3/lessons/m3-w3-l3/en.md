## Content

### 1. What is wood drying and why

**Freshly felled wood** (pine, oak, beech, birch) typically contains **35-55% moisture** (percentage of water mass in the total wood mass). This water is in two forms: 1) **free water** in the lumen of the vessel cells, 2) **bound water** in the cell walls (chemically bonded to cellulose and hemicellulose). Free water leaves faster (the first 8-20 days of drying), bound water more slowly (the following weeks).

**Why we dry**:

1. **Dimensional stability.** Fresh wood shrinks after felling as it loses water. Shrinkage is non-uniform: radial (up to 12-20% at maximum for oak), tangential (8-12% for oak), longitudinal (<1%). If the board is not dried before use in a building, **it shrinks in the structure** (cracks, gaps between boards, sagging ceilings).

2. **Mechanical strength.** Wood dried to 12% has **bending strength 40-60% higher** than fresh wood. Strength classes (C16, C24, C30) are **calibrated for 12% moisture**. Wood dried to a different target (e.g. 18%) has a different strength, and a conversion factor is needed.

3. **Protection against fungi and mould.** Wood with moisture below 20% **is not a growth medium** for mould fungi. Fresh wood is ideal for mould (water + cellulose + actual room temperature).

4. **Processability.** Fresh wood **does not plane well** (it slides, planes unevenly), **does not glue well** (glues require 8-12% moisture), **does not take varnish** (varnish does not hold on wet wood). For **furniture joinery** the requirement is 8-10%, for **construction joinery and flooring** 12%, for **roof truss timber** 18%.

5. **Weight and transport-cost reduction.** 1 m³ of fresh pine at 35% moisture weighs **~720 kg**. The same pine at 12% moisture weighs **~520 kg**. A difference of 200 kg/m³, which for 10 m³ means 2000 kg less to transport. EGIDA -> Warsaw transport cost averages 35-45 zł/km for a 15 t truck: real savings.

### 2. Two basic drying methods

**Natural drying (air drying, seasoning)**: wood stored on a roofed yard without insulation or heating, water evaporates to the atmosphere in cold air. Time: **6-12 months for a 25 mm pine board**, **24-36 months for a 28 mm oak board**. Advantages: cheap (no energy, only time), natural drying quality (slow, without stresses). Disadvantages: **very slow** (EGIDA's throughput would be ~30% of current if only seasoning was used), **seasonal** (in winter moisture drops very slowly, in spring there is a mould risk), **no target control** (final moisture depends on climate, not requirement).

EGIDA uses seasoning for **low-priority items** (birch, oak leftovers, batches without a specific client), on an **800 m² storage yard** behind the hall, 4-6 stacks (each 15-25 m³), continuous rotation.

**Kiln drying**: wood in a closed chamber, controlled heating, regulated air humidity, forced ventilation. Time: **7-30 days** depending on species and thickness. Advantages: **fast, controlled, repeatable** (the same program 5 times gives the same result 5 times), accurate target moisture (±1%). Disadvantages: **energy-costly** (EGIDA's BH-50 uses 800-1200 kWh for a 20-day cycle plus ~400 kg of gas), requires a **specialist** (kiln master), risk of cracks with a wrong program.

EGIDA uses the chamber for **main production** (pine C24, oak flooring, beech furniture), planned customer orders, every batch with a deadline.

### 3. When to use combined drying

In EGIDA practice there are three combined strategies:

**Pre-seasoning plus chamber.** For fresh oak (35-38% moisture) we apply **2-3 weeks of seasoning on the yard** (drops to ~28-30% moisture), then **the chamber for 20-24 days** (from 28% to 14%). Total 5-6 weeks. Advantages: saves chamber energy (the first 10% of moisture is given up "for free" on the yard), the chamber operates in a range in which it is efficient.

**Chamber plus final seasoning** (rarely, for special clients). Chamber to 14%, then seasoning 2-3 months, the wood "matures" in a stable climate, stresses settle down to equilibrium. Used for exports to regions with a different climate (Germany, the Netherlands), where the wood has "settled" and does not crack in transit.

**Two-stage in the chamber.** Very fresh wood (fresh pine 32%+), chamber program for warm-up and initial drying phase (7 days), then **a pause in a climate-controlled hall** (3-5 days, the wood stabilises), then **the chamber for a second program** of main drying and conditioning (5-8 days). Total 15-20 days. Used for large batches where shortening the cycle is not critical.

### 4. Drying program for fresh 28 mm pine

An example of program **SOS-STAND-28** (standard pine 28 mm boards, fresh to 12%), used by EGIDA in the BH-50 (and also used by Drew-Sus because of a similar Brunner controller):

```
DAY 1 (warm-up):
  Temperature: 20 -> 35°C (slowly, ~0.5°C/h)
  Humidity RH: 95% (very high, wood does not dry)
  Aim: equalise the temperature of the whole wood mass
  End of day 1: wood at 35°C, wood moisture unchanged (32%)

DAY 2-3 (initial drying):
  Temperature: 35 -> 50°C
  Humidity RH: 95% -> 70%
  Aim: remove free water (the easiest)
  End of day 3: wood moisture ~20-22% (from 32%, drop 10-12%)

DAY 4-7 (main drying):
  Temperature: 50 -> 70°C (peak)
  Humidity RH: 70% -> 40%
  Aim: wood moisture down to 14-15%
  End of day 7: wood 70°C, wood moisture 14%

DAY 8 (conditioning):
  Temperature: 70 -> 60°C
  Humidity RH: 40% -> 70%
  Aim: equalise internal stresses, no internal cracks
  End of day 8: wood 60°C, moisture 12-13%

DAY 9 (cooling):
  Temperature: 60 -> 25°C (naturally, fans without heating)
  Humidity RH: 70% (stable)
  Aim: wood ready for discharge without thermal shock
  End of day 9: wood 25°C, moisture 12%, DISCHARGE.
```

**Total time**: 9 days (nominal cycle). In practice ±1-2 days depending on initial moisture and chamber parameters.

**Controller parameters**:
- Temperature sensors: 4 (chamber corners plus centre)
- Relative-humidity sensors: 2 (fan inlet plus outlet)
- Wood-moisture sensors: 3 (probes driven into boards in 3 different stacks: front, centre, rear of the chamber)
- Permissible deviation: temperature ±2 °C from setpoint, RH ±5% from setpoint
- Controller alarm: the permissible deviation exceeded by 50% (4 °C temp, 8% RH)

**Energy consumption**: ~180 kWh/day (fan electricity), ~25 kg of gas/day (heating), together **~85 zł/m³ per cycle** for a full 38 m³ charge.

### 5. Drying program for fresh 28 mm oak

Oak requires **a much slower cycle**, because it has a higher risk of cracking (tangential-radial structure with a large shrinkage difference, the core of the wood is sensitive to thermal shock):

```
DAY 1-2 (slow warm-up):
  Temperature: 20 -> 30°C (very slowly, 0.2°C/h)
  Humidity RH: 95%
  Aim: wood mass temperature without shock

DAY 3-10 (initial drying):
  Temperature: 30 -> 40°C (slowly)
  Humidity RH: 90% -> 75%
  Aim: wood moisture 38% -> 28%

DAY 11-18 (main drying):
  Temperature: 40 -> 60°C (oak peak, not higher!)
  Humidity RH: 75% -> 45%
  Aim: wood moisture 28% -> 16%

DAY 19-21 (long conditioning):
  Temperature: 60 -> 55°C
  Humidity RH: 45% -> 75%
  Aim: equalise stresses (oak is particularly sensitive, risk of core cracks)

DAY 22 (cooling):
  Temperature: 55 -> 25°C
  Humidity RH: 75% stable
  End: wood 25°C, moisture 14%, DISCHARGE.
```

**Total time**: **22 nominal days**, often 24-25 days in practice for large batches.

**Key differences vs. pine**:
- Peak temperature **60 °C** (vs. 70 °C pine), higher and the oak cracks
- Conditioning **3 days** (vs. 1 day pine), oak requires long equalisation
- Target moisture **14%** (vs. 12% pine), oak cannot reach 12% without brittleness

**Energy consumption**: ~180 kWh/day × 22 days = 3960 kWh plus ~400 kg of gas = ~**80-90 zł/m³** (similar to pine, because slower but with lower temperatures).

### 6. What the M3 operator sees on the controller panel

The BH-50 control panel in the kiln control room shows **in real time**:

**Main screen**:
- Program number (e.g. SOS-STAND-28)
- Cycle day (e.g. 5 of 9) and phase (e.g. "Main drying")
- Chamber air temperature (in °C, from four sensors plus average)
- Relative air humidity (RH in %, from two sensors plus average)
- Wood moisture (from three probes, plus average, plus spread)
- EMC (calculated from air temperature and RH, compared to current wood moisture)
- Fan state (RPM, current A)
- Heater state (current power in kW, percentage of maximum)

**Alarm screen** (if any sensor is out of range):
- Alarm type (temperature, humidity, air flow, sensor)
- Time of detection (hour, minute)
- Reading vs. expected value
- Automatic recommendation (e.g. "increase ventilation", "stop cycle")

**What the M3 operator understands** (without intervening):
- If temperature is within ±2 °C of setpoint, **OK**, the program holds.
- If wood moisture falls monotonically (each day by 1-2%), **OK**, drying is on track.
- If the spread between the three wood-moisture probes is <2%, **OK**, the charge is uniform.
- If the spread is >3%, something is off (stacking issue, uneven air flow, report to the master).
- If there is a red alarm on screen, **report to the master immediately** (do not try to solve it yourself).

**What the M3 operator does NOT do**:
- Does not change program parameters (temperature, RH, phase duration)
- Does not switch the program to another one mid-cycle
- Does not open the chamber doors during a cycle (lets heat out, thermal shock to the wood)
- Does not turn off fans or heaters
- Does not reset an alarm without the master's approval

### 7. Charge uniformity, why it is critical

The kiln dries **the whole charge at once under the same program**. If the charge is **non-uniform** (different species, different thicknesses, different moistures), the program that is optimal for part of the charge is **wrong for the rest**. Examples:

**A. Pine 25 mm plus oak 25 mm together.** Pine program 9 days to 12%. Oak after 9 days on this program: temperature 70 °C (too high for oak, it cracks), oak moisture still 22% (not dried). Oak program 22 days to 14%. Pine after 22 days: moisture 8% (too low, brittle wood), temperature 60 °C (OK), but 2.5 times longer than needed (energy loss). **Do not mix species in a single charge.**

**B. Pine 25 mm and pine 45 mm together.** Same species, different thickness. Program 25 mm 9 days. 45 mm in 9 days: moisture 20% (under-dried), the cycle has to be extended by 3-5 days. Program 45 mm 15 days. 25 mm in 15 days: moisture 9% (over-dried), brittle. **Different thicknesses means a compromise or two charges.**

**C. Fresh pine 32% plus seasoned pine 22% together.** Same species, same thickness, but different starting moisture. Different behaviour in the initial phases: the fresh gives up a lot of water (saturates the air), the seasoned gives up little (does not saturate). In the main phase the fresh is still wet, the seasoned is already over-dried. **Mixing starting moistures is permissible only within ±5% (e.g. 28-33% together, 20-25% together).**

**Uniformity assessment before a charge** (kiln master's procedure):
1. Measure moisture **in at least 10 boards** from different batches in the planned charge.
2. Calculate the spread. If <±5% from the average, the charge is uniform.
3. Record in KS-001 section "Planned charge" (average, minimum, maximum).
4. If the spread is larger, split into two charges or set aside part of the material for another charge.

### 8. Conditioning and internal stresses

**Conditioning** (phase 4 of the cycle, after main drying) is the **key quality phase**, often underestimated by a beginner operator. Physically: after main drying the wood has an **internal moisture gradient**. The outer layer of the board (the surface) is dried (8-10%), the core of the board is wetter (14-15%). The gradient generates **internal stresses** (compression of the outer layers, tension in the core).

If the board leaves the chamber in this state and goes to processing (planer, dimension saw), **the stresses are revealed**: after cutting the board "cracks" (internal micro-cracks), after planing it warps, after a few weeks in the building the client sees **core cracks (*internal checks*)**.

Conditioning **partially reverses the gradient**: by raising air humidity (RH from 40% to 70%) at a slightly lower temperature (60 °C from 70 °C), the outer layers of the board **absorb a bit of moisture** from the air (surface moisture rises from 8% to 11%), while the core still equalises downwards (15% -> 13%). After 2-3 days the gradient **drops to 2-3%** (from 6-7%), stresses are dissolved.

**In practice**: the M3 operator looks at the spread indicator between the moisture probes after conditioning. If the three probes show 11.8 / 12.0 / 12.3% (spread 0.5%), conditioning is successful. If 10.5 / 12.0 / 13.8% (spread 3.3%), conditioning is incomplete, the master extends the phase by a day.

::: info
**Skipping conditioning** is the most common kiln drying shortcut "to save money". An untrained operator sees that the wood "is already dry" after the main phase (moisture 12% on the probes) and wants to take it out. The master replies: **"you see the average, you do not see the stresses". Wood without conditioning looks identical from the outside, but cracks within 2-4 weeks at the client's site**, a complaint is unavoidable. The cost of a complaint (replacing timber plus transport costs plus a client visit) will always exceed the saving from 2 days of conditioning (40-60 zł of electricity).
:::

### 9. Equilibrium moisture EMC and the program

**EMC (equilibrium moisture content)** is the moisture **that the wood tends towards** in a given environment (temperature, air RH). It is a fundamental concept in kiln drying. EMC depends on temperature and RH, not on wood species (small differences between species exist, but in EGIDA practice the tables are simplified).

**EMC table** (simplified, for pine and oak at 20-70 °C):

| Temperature | RH 30% | RH 50% | RH 70% | RH 90% |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 20°C | 6.3% | 9.2% | 13.1% | 20.5% |
| 40°C | 5.8% | 8.5% | 12.2% | 19.3% |
| 60°C | 5.2% | 7.7% | 11.2% | 18.0% |
| 70°C | 5.0% | 7.4% | 10.8% | 17.5% |

**How the controller uses EMC**: in each phase of the cycle the controller knows the setpoint temperature and RH, calculates the current EMC, compares with the target for that phase. If the wood is above the EMC target for the moment, **drying continues** (water evaporates). If the wood is below EMC (dried too fast), the controller **raises the RH** (injects steam or shuts off ventilation) so the wood slows drying down.

**Key to understanding pine 28 mm to 12%**: the final program requires **60 °C and 50% RH**, because then EMC ~7.7% (table). The wood tends towards 7.7%, but because of the board thickness and diffusion, in the 2-3 days of the last phase it will not reach 7.7%, it will stop at ~12%. **That is where the controller ends the cycle** (target achieved). If the cycle lasted another 5 days, the wood would drop to 8%, it would be too dry.

**The kiln master** understands this dynamic intuitively. **The M3 operator** understands the principle (EMC, why 60 °C and 50% RH give 12% in 28 mm). They do not have to compute EMC in their head, but they see on the panel "current EMC 7.7% vs. wood moisture 12%" and know what it means.

### 10. Typical problems in a cycle and how the M3 operator signals them

**Problem 1. Temperature does not reach setpoint.** The program sets 70 °C, the controller shows 62 °C. Causes:
- Heater damaged (service)
- Thermostat dirty (service)
- Charge too big, heater cannot cope (operator reports, master lowers target)
- Doors not sealed (master checks, possibly replaces seals)

**M3 operator signal**: report to Mr. Henryk after 2 hours of deviation. "Chamber at 62 instead of 70, for 2 hours."

**Problem 2. Wood moisture drops unevenly across the probes.** Probes 1/2/3 show 18/14/21%. Spread 7%. Causes:
- Probes fitted in different species (loading error, master corrects)
- Charge stacked unevenly, non-uniform air flow (master decides on the correction)
- Probe damaged (master checks after the cycle)

**M3 operator signal**: report a >3% spread. "Probes 18/14/21, spread 7%, since the morning."

**Problem 3. Ventilation alarm.** The controller shows "alarm: air flow below 80%". Causes:
- Fan damaged (master stops the cycle, calls service)
- Blockage in the ventilation ducts (master checks)
- Flow sensor damaged (master switches to manual control)

**M3 operator signal**: **immediate**, phone call to the master. "Ventilation alarm on the chamber, red LED."

**Problem 4. Smell of mould in the control room.** A subtle signal that inside the chamber there are conditions favourable to mould (too high humidity in the initial drying phase, usually from underheating). Not an alarm on the controller, requires an experienced nose.

**M3 operator signal**: report to the master, "the hall smells musty, something is going on in the chamber". Master checks, decides.

## Key Terms

**conventional kiln dryer** (*komora suszarnicza konwencjonalna*, EN *conventional kiln dryer*, ES *cámara de secado convencional*, UK *конвенційна сушильна камера*): a closed room with hot-air heating, axial fans and a moisture exhaust; typical net capacity 30-80 m³ of sawn timber, drying cycle 7-25 days depending on species.

**drying program** (*program suszarniczy*, EN *drying schedule / program*, ES *programa de secado*, UK *програма сушіння*): a programmed sequence of phases (warm-up, initial drying, main drying, conditioning, cooling) with specified temperatures and RH for a particular species and thickness, e.g. SOS-STAND-28 for 28 mm pine (9 days), DUB-STAND-28 for 28 mm oak (22 days).

**EMC (equilibrium moisture content)** (*wilgotność równowagowa*, EN *equilibrium moisture content EMC*, ES *contenido de humedad de equilibrio*, UK *рівноважна вологість*): the moisture the wood tends towards in a given environment, depending on temperature and relative air humidity; a theoretical value governing the drying program.

**conditioning** (*kondycjonowanie*, EN *conditioning*, ES *acondicionamiento*, UK *кондиціонування*): the penultimate phase of the drying cycle (2-4 days), during which air humidity is raised again (from 40-50% to 70-80%) at a slightly lower temperature, in order to equalise the internal moisture gradient of the board and to dissolve stresses.

**kiln charge** (*wsadka suszarnicza*, EN *kiln charge / load*, ES *carga del secadero*, UK *завантаження сушарні*): the full content of the kiln during a single cycle, typical size 30-38 m³ net for the BH-50; uniformity of species, thickness and starting moisture is required.

**charge uniformity** (*jednorodność wsadki*, EN *charge uniformity*, ES *homogeneidad de la carga*, UK *однорідність завантаження*): the requirement that all boards of the charge share the same species, a similar thickness (±3 mm) and a similar starting moisture (±5%); a condition for the program to work correctly.

**wood moisture probes** (*sondy wilgotności drewna*, EN *wood moisture probes*, ES *sondas de humedad de la madera*, UK *зонди вологості деревини*): electrical sensors (usually 2 electrodes driven into the board) measuring wood moisture during the cycle, three units in the BH-50 (front, centre, rear of the chamber), operating reading spread <3%.

**BH-50** (*Brunner-Hildebrand BH-50*, EN *Brunner-Hildebrand BH-50*, ES *Brunner-Hildebrand BH-50*, UK *Brunner-Hildebrand BH-50*): a kiln model by the Austrian-German manufacturer, capacity 50 m³ gross and 38 m³ net of sawn timber; in use at EGIDA since 2011, Polish distribution by BTM.

**air drying / seasoning** (*sezonowanie*, EN *air drying / seasoning*, ES *secado al aire / oreo*, UK *природне висихання / сезонування*): natural drying of wood on a roofed yard without heating, time 6-36 months; used at EGIDA for low-priority batches or as a pre-drying step before the chamber.

**external drying service** (*suszarnia zewnętrzna*, EN *external drying service*, ES *secadero externo*, UK *зовнішня сушарня*): a commercial entity providing a drying service for timber from other sawmills; EGIDA example: Drew-Sus in Mrągowo, rate 180 zł/m³ plus 350 zł flat rate transport per run.

**kiln master** (*mistrz suszarni*, EN *kiln master*, ES *maestro del secadero*, UK *майстер сушарні*): an employee with BTM or equivalent authorisations to operate the kiln, deciding on program, charge and mid-cycle corrections; at EGIDA it is Mr. Henryk (seasonal retiree).

**kiln log card KS-001** (*karta suszarni*, EN *kiln log card*, ES *ficha del secadero*, UK *картка сушарні*): an A4 form filled in by the kiln master for each charge decision, four sections (planning, next charge, notes, signature), retention 3 years on paper plus 5 years as a scan.

**target moisture content** (*target wilgotności*, EN *target moisture content*, ES *humedad objetivo*, UK *цільова вологість*): the target wood moisture after drying, depending on application: 8-10% furniture joinery, 12% construction joinery and flooring, 14% structural oak, 18% roof truss timber.

**moisture gradient** (*gradient wilgotności*, EN *moisture gradient*, ES *gradiente de humedad*, UK *градієнт вологості*): the moisture difference between the surface of the board (drier) and its core (wetter) after main drying; dissolved in the conditioning phase; if not maintained, it causes internal cracks at the client's site.
