## Introduction

In lesson 1 you heard two numbers: **two hundred and ten** and **two hundred and twenty**. We said they were two articles of the Polish *Kodeks pracy* (Labour Code) and that we would come back to them with full content today. It is Wednesday, 6:50 in the morning, Mykoła is standing in front of sawbench P3, and in a moment he will face the first situation in his life in which those numbers stop being numbers from a slide and become his own words spoken to the foreman.

One editorial note first. In lesson 1 we said "art. 220 KP – the duty to perform an order". That number was a simplification. The duty to perform an order arises from **art. 100 § 1 of the Polish Labour Code**, and the basic OSH duties of the worker (including the duty to immediately notify of a threat) sit in **art. 211 KP**. Today you learn three correct articles, not two. Numbers in an operator's working memory are like emergency phone numbers: they must be exact.

The lesson runs two hours and is entirely legal. By the end you will be able to write a service memo (*notatka służbowa*) and hand it to the foreman in a form that, in a dispute, carries evidentiary weight before a labour court or a PIP (*Państwowa Inspekcja Pracy*, State Labour Inspectorate) inspector. You did not have this skill in M2. In M3 it is one of the axes of the position.

## Objectives

After this lesson:

1. You know the wording of the three articles of the Labour Code that govern the dispute between a foreman's order and your safety: **art. 100 § 1**, **art. 210 § 1 and 2**, **art. 211**. You know which of them is a worker's duty and which is a right.
2. You understand the three cumulative preconditions that must all be met for your right to refrain from dangerous work to be a protected right and not an arbitrary refusal.
3. You know that immediate notification of the superior is a **condition** of protection, not an add-on to it. You know two Supreme Court rulings that have confirmed this.
4. You can write a service memo in a format that a labour court will accept as evidence: date, time, addressee, description of conditions, reference to the OSH provision, demand, signature.
5. You recognise the line beyond which your right ends: subjective fear without an objective threat, no notification, persistent refusal of secondary orders unrelated to the threat itself.
6. You know that on the other side of the dispute stands art. 283 § 1 KP, which may reach the foreman and, in narrow circumstances, you as an operator leading a helper.

## Content

### Three articles that intersect

In the Polish Labour Code the duty to perform an order and the right of refusal do not sit in one article. They sit in three that talk to each other. Start with the texts, because in law the text is everything. A paraphrase is a trap.

**Art. 100 § 1 KP**, full wording:

> "An employee is obliged to perform work conscientiously and diligently and to comply with the orders of superiors that relate to the work, provided that they are not contrary to legal regulations or to the employment contract."

Two words here matter more than the rest. First: *obliged*. Polish labour law treats a superior's order seriously. Failing to carry out an order without grounds is usually a grave breach of employee duties, justifying disciplinary termination (art. 52 § 1 pt 1 KP). Second: *provided that*. The duty has a limit: "provided that they are not contrary to legal regulations". An order that breaches OSH is contrary to legal regulations. It is not a binding order.

**Art. 210 § 1 KP**, full wording:

> "In the event that working conditions do not comply with occupational safety and health regulations and create a direct threat to the health or life of the employee, or when the work performed by the employee threatens such danger to other persons, the employee has the right to refrain from performing the work, notifying the superior thereof without delay."

Three preconditions must be met **cumulatively**:

1. working conditions **do not comply** with OSH regulations,
2. these conditions **create a direct threat** to health or life (yours or a third party's),
3. the employee **notifies** the superior **without delay**.

Missing any one precondition removes the protection. Machine lacks a CE certificate but operates without danger: no right. Danger but no notification: no right. Subjective fear without objective fact: no right. On each of these three the Supreme Court has written a separate ruling, you will see them shortly.

**Art. 210 § 2 KP** (escalation, when refraining does not remove the threat):

> "If refraining from the performance of work does not remove the threat referred to in § 1, the employee has the right to withdraw from the place of danger, notifying the superior thereof without delay."

**Art. 210, further paragraphs** (worker protection):

> "The employee shall not bear any adverse consequences as a result of refraining from work or withdrawing from the place of danger in the cases referred to in § 1 and 2.
> For the time spent refraining from the performance of work or withdrawing from the place of danger in the cases referred to in § 1 and 2, the employee retains the right to remuneration."

Two things to carry into the sawmill. First: "any adverse consequences" means no reprimand, no disciplinary penalty, no bonus cut, no notice. Second: remuneration for the time spent refraining is full, not reduced. Labour law does not punish you for exercising a right.

**Art. 211 KP**, the worker's basic OSH duties, full wording:

> "Compliance with occupational safety and health regulations and rules is a basic duty of the employee. In particular, the employee is obliged to:
> 1. know the occupational safety and health regulations and rules, take part in training and instruction on this subject and undergo the required verifying examinations;
> 2. perform work in accordance with the occupational safety and health regulations and rules and comply with the orders and instructions issued by superiors in this respect;
> 3. take care of the proper condition of machinery, equipment, tools and gear, and of order and tidiness at the workplace;
> 4. apply collective protection measures and also use the assigned personal protective equipment and working clothes and footwear in accordance with their intended purpose;
> 5. undergo initial, periodic and control medical examinations, as well as other recommended ones, and follow medical recommendations;
> 6. immediately notify the superior of an accident at the workplace or a threat to human life or health noticed at the workplace, and warn co-workers as well as other persons present in the area of the threat of the danger facing them;
> 7. cooperate with the employer and superiors in fulfilling duties concerning occupational safety and health."

Art. 211 is vast. For this lesson three points matter: point 2 (work in accordance with OSH), point 4 (apply PPE), point 6 (immediately notify of a threat). Point 6 is **your personal defence**. If you noticed a threat and did not notify, you breached art. 211, not just ignored a machine.

::: info
Note the symmetry of art. 210 § 1 and art. 211 pt 6. Both speak of the same act: **immediate notification of the superior about a threat**. In art. 210 it is the condition of your protection. In art. 211 it is your duty. Polish law leaves no room for "I forgot". Notification is a duty, and if it leads to refraining from work, it also becomes your right to protection. Two provisions, one act. A written service memo is that act in a form which does not disappear once emotions cool.
:::

### Art. 212 KP: what hangs on Marek

Marek is not a party to this dispute, he is its frame. Polish law places on him a load often heavier than yours. **Art. 212 KP** reads:

> "A person directing employees is obliged to:
> 1. organise workstations in accordance with the occupational safety and health regulations and rules;
> 2. take care of the serviceability of personal protective equipment and its use in accordance with its intended purpose;
> 3. organise, prepare and conduct the work, taking into account the protection of employees against occupational accidents, occupational diseases and other diseases connected with the conditions of the work environment;
> 4. take care of the safe and hygienic condition of the work premises and technical equipment, as well as the serviceability of collective protection measures and their use in accordance with their intended purpose;
> 5. enforce the observance by employees of occupational safety and health regulations and rules;
> 6. ensure the implementation of the recommendations of the physician providing health care over the employees."

Your refusal to carry out an order that breaches OSH **is not** hostile to Marek. It helps Marek fulfil his duty under points 3 and 5. If Marek's order were defective and you carried it out and an accident occurred, legal liability would fall first on Marek as the person directing workers (art. 283 § 1 KP, a fine up to 30,000 PLN, plus civil liability). Your refusal closes that path before it opens.

Good foremen understand this at once. Bad ones learn it after their first PIP visit. Marek is in the first group.

### Mykoła's third day: Wednesday, 8:42 in the morning

Mykoła is in the hall at half past six. He ran the OSH briefing on Monday and Tuesday, five minutes each, Tuesday's topic: slippery feed frame after overnight rain. Today Marek ran the briefing to go over the oak campaign starting tomorrow. Mykoła stood in the second row, listened, nodded. A normal day.

At 7:40 Mykoła starts batch 51: pine, 22 logs, diameter 30–38 cm, entry moisture 44%. Kerf 3.5 mm, timber 50 × 150, a client's order for roof truss. Next to him his helper is Wasyl, 19 years old, M1 helper from Ukraine (Ternopil), third week at the sawmill. Wasyl feeds logs from the ramp and takes timber off the other side. His Polish is still weak, but he knows gestures, the STOP signal, and the 3-metre zone around the saw. Mykoła instructed him personally on Monday (you will see that instruction in lesson 8).

At 8:42 the internal phone rings. Marek picks up, listens 40 seconds, nods, puts down the receiver and walks straight to P3.

> **Marek:** "Mykoła, listen. The client for batch 49, the oak one from last week, arrived early. Truck at the gate, can't wait, traffic slot in Warsaw in two hours. I have to unload. Wasyl, come with me to ramp 2 to help load. Mykoła, keep going alone for 30 minutes, can you manage?"

Mykoła looks at Marek. Looks at Wasyl. Looks at the machine. First reaction: "of course I can manage". Second, three seconds later: "Wasyl feeds the logs from the ramp. Without me there, the nearest person will be the P2 operator, Andrij. But Andrij has his own batch, he won't be watching my ramp." Third, another five seconds: **if I feed a log with nobody watching the authorised log-entry zone, there is a risk of unauthorised entry into the P3 zone**. EGIDA workstation instruction, point 7: "at the P3 band saw during the cycle there must be an assistant observing the feed ramp, or the ramp area must be closed off by a barrier".

Mykoła steps closer to Marek, lowers his voice so Wasyl cannot hear, speaks Polish slowly but clearly:

> **Mykoła:** "Marek, one moment. Without Wasyl at the ramp I have an open ramp and the band running. The workstation instruction says I can't. Can we do it differently?"

Marek stops. Looks at his watch. Looks at Mykoła. Says nothing for four long seconds. Then:

> **Marek:** "All right. Stop the band, close the cycle on this log, Wasyl closes the ramp barrier and comes with me. Five minutes of downtime is not the apocalypse. Here, write this downtime in the log: reason, time."

Mykoła stops the cycle, waits for the band to slow, switches off the green, presses the red. Wasyl closes the ramp barrier (slides it from open to closed, the click of the latch audible three metres away). Marek and Wasyl head to ramp 2. Mykoła opens the machine log:

> **8:47. Downtime, batch 51. Reason: assistant (Wasyl Melnyk, M1) reassigned to unloading at ramp 2 by foreman M. Kowalski. Without assistant, cycle stopped in accordance with workstation instruction P3 point 7. Planned resumption upon assistant's return, approximately 9:15.**

He saves. Shuts the machine down to full stop. Sits on the stool next to P3 and waits. Not nervous. Surprised that Marek changed the plan so quickly. Half an hour ago he would not have known he had the right to say "one moment".

::: example
Notice three things in this scene. First, Mykoła **did not** quote art. 210 KP to Marek. He stated a concrete operational objection: "the workstation instruction says I can't". That is the right tactic. The Labour Code stands behind you in the background, but in the first conversation with the foreman you speak about a specific plant-level norm, not a code article. Second, Mykoła **proposed an alternative**: "can we do it differently?". Common courts treat the proposal of an alternative as evidence of the employee's good faith (judgment X P 1138/14, below). Third, **he logged the reason for the downtime**. The machine log is objective evidence, it does not start emotions, it records facts. All three elements together close the dispute before it opens.
:::

### If Marek had not given way: the service memo

The scene above ended well because Marek is a good foreman. Imagine the alternative. Marek is under stress (traffic slot, client, liquidated damages for delay), he says:

> **Marek (hypothetically):** "Mykoła, no time. Five minutes is nothing. Wasyl just helps me unload, we're back. Go."

Two options. First: start the machine without an assistant, counting on five minutes going fine. If it does not, art. 52 § 1 pt 1 KP (disciplinary termination) is the employer's friend, and the labour court will see you as someone who **knowingly took on the risk while having the right to refrain from work** (judgment VIII Pa 217/18, shortly). Path one is a defeat at the labour court, even if Marek also bears liability.

Second: a written service memo. You take a sheet of paper (or open the machine log in memo mode). You write it up. You hand it to Marek to sign. You do not need to explain. Marek already knows what a signed sheet at 8:42 means. This is the moment when Marek either returns to the original proposal ("all right, stop the band") or signs and accepts his own responsibility, which PIP will then take note of.

The service memo is not a legal institution defined in the Labour Code. It is an **evidentiary practice** that grew out of art. 210 § 1 (which requires "notification without delay") and out of a court's need ("what exactly was said to whom and when"). Written form survives a dispute. Oral form vanishes when emotions cool.

**Format of a service memo that a labour court will accept as evidence:**

1. **Date and time.** Precise. "2026-04-22, 8:42". Not "Tuesday morning".
2. **Author's full name.** Yours.
3. **Addressee's full name.** Your superior's.
4. **Position and place.** Band saw P3, EGIDA sawmill, main hall, ul. X.
5. **Description of conditions.** Concise, factual. "Order to continue the cycle of batch 51 without an assistant at the feed ramp, contrary to workstation instruction P3 point 7, on the date and at the time indicated above."
6. **Reference to the OSH provision.** "Workstation instruction P3 point 7 requires an assistant observing the feed ramp during the cycle. The absence creates a risk of unauthorised entry into the saw zone. Art. 210 § 1 KP."
7. **Demand.** "I request that the cycle be stopped until the assistant's return, or that closure of the ramp barrier and stopping of the cycle be authorised."
8. **Declaration of notification.** "By this memo I notify the superior of the threat in accordance with art. 210 § 1 and art. 211 pt 6 of the Labour Code."
9. **Signature and date.** Your signature and full name in print.
10. **Copies.** Two. One for the addressee, one for your file. Both signed and dated. If the addressee refuses their copy, write on yours: "Copy for [name] refused by the addressee, date and time".

::: tip
In a sawmill with established practice (EGIDA has it), the memo usually fits into two paragraphs, not ten rigid points. But **ten points is the minimum content** that must appear so a labour court or PIP inspector can understand the situation without talking to you. Write for a reader who will read the paper six months from now, was not there that day, and will try to reconstruct what happened. That reader is a labour-court judge or an inspector.
:::

### What the courts say: SN I PK 191/05

The Supreme Court, in its judgment of 11 May 2006, case signature **I PK 191/05**, decided a case of a worker who refrained from work invoking the lack of a conformity certificate for equipment. The Supreme Court held that the right under art. 210 § 1 KP applies "**only in the situation of the cumulative occurrence of two preconditions**: working conditions that do not comply with occupational safety and health regulations **and** those conditions creating a direct threat to the health or life of the employee".

Key quotation from the reasoning:

> "A mere breach of formal requirements, such as the lack of a conformity certificate for equipment, does not entitle refraining from work unless it is accompanied by a proven direct threat to health or life."

What does this mean for you in the sawmill? Missing a CE certificate on a saw guard is **not enough in itself** to refuse work. But the complete absence of a guard, or a guard temporarily fastened with wire instead of bolts so that it may come loose, is a direct threat. You must point to an **objective fact**, not just a missing piece of paper.

The same judgment added that "the **mistaken assumption** that the claimant was not entitled to the right to refrain from work results in the dismissal without notice under art. 52 § 1 pt 1 k.p. being deemed **justified**". In other words: if you tell yourself you have the right, and then the court says you did not, losing the job is justified, even if no actual danger arose. The right of refusal is real, but narrow.

### SN I PKN 619/99: why notification is a condition, not an add-on

The Supreme Court, in its judgment of 9 May 2000, case signature **I PKN 619/99**, decided a case in which a worker refrained from work **without notifying the superior**. The worker held a managerial position, had years of tenure, and a reasonable assessment of conditions. The Court held clearly:

> "A worker's breach of the duty to render work, regardless of the reasons for refraining from performing it, constitutes a grave breach of basic employee duties justifying the termination of the employment contract without notice on the basis of art. 52 § 1 pt 1 of the Labour Code (KP), if the worker did not notify the superior of this fact without delay, in accordance with art. 210 § 1 KP (...). The principle of the prohibition of abuse of a subjective right (art. 5 KC in conjunction with art. 8 KP) cannot be applied in favour of a worker who himself breaches the law."

This is, for you in the sawmill, the sharpest judgment in this whole lesson. The message: **no notification, no protection**. Even if you are objectively right, even if the machine really is dangerous, even if you have worked in the sawmill for fifteen years, the lack of immediate notification turns your refusal into unauthorised conduct, which the labour court treats as grounds for disciplinary termination.

What does "without delay" mean? In sawmill terms: "before the log leaves the ramp, before the band starts moving, before you leave the hall". A few minutes is acceptable. Half an hour is already bad. Two hours is catastrophic.

::: warning
Remember this signature: **I PKN 619/99**. It is your most frequent trap. An operator who feels a machine is dangerous has an instinct to step aside, go for a cigarette, wait it out. The legal instinct is the opposite: first notify, then step aside. Two minutes of conversation with the foreman, or two minutes of a memo, save your contract. Silence does not.
:::

### SN II PSK 76/22: the line between fear and threat

The Supreme Court, in its judgment of 26 January 2023, case signature **II PSK 76/22**, decided a case of a worker who refused to wear protective masks during the COVID pandemic, invoking art. 210 § 1 KP. The worker said: "the mask threatens me". The Court held:

> "The claimant, who has not proven that the use of protective masks at the workplace constituted a direct threat to his health or life within the meaning of art. 210 § 1 k.p., cannot effectively invoke that provision as a basis for refraining from work."

For you in the sawmill: **the burden of proving an objective threat rests with you**. "It seems dangerous to me" is not enough. Point to a fact: a missing guard, a damaged band, a wet electric floor, a broken alarm lanyard. Fear is a signal, only the objective fact is evidence.

When you call the shift master and say "I want to exercise art. 210", prepare for the question: "what specifically are you pointing to". "It seems to me" is not enough. "The blade guard is not fastened, I see a 4 mm gap" is always enough.

### SP VIII Pa 138/14: an analogy close to your work

One common-court judgment is especially close to a sawmill situation. The Regional Court, in case **VIII Pa 138/14 of 27 November 2014**, decided the case of a driver who refused to transport materials in a technically unserviceable vehicle. The gearbox was defective, the equipment not compliant with road-traffic regulations. The employer dismissed the driver on disciplinary grounds. The court held:

> "An employee's refusal to carry out a work order concerning the transport of materials in a technically unserviceable vehicle, creating a direct threat to the health or life of the employee (...), does not constitute a grave breach of basic employee duties within the meaning of art. 52 § 1 pt 1 of the Labour Code justifying termination of the employment contract without notice, because the employee's action in such a situation is consistent with art. 100 and art. 210 § 1 of the Labour Code (...)."

You see the analogy. Unserviceable vehicle = unserviceable saw. Defective gearbox = damaged blade guard. Road-traffic regulations = Polish standard PN-EN 1807 for sawmill machinery, or the manufacturer's workstation instruction. The court said: the refusal is lawful, the dismissal is unjustified, the worker is entitled to compensation.

In practice: if your refusal under art. 210 § 1 later turns out to be mistaken as to the threat (there was none in reality), but was sincere, based on visible facts and notified, Polish courts usually protect the worker. **Sincerity of assessment has legal value**, not only moral.

### SP X P 1138/14: propose an alternative

One more common-court judgment, **X P 1138/14 of 3 November 2015**, reinforces a technique Mykoła applied in the morning: proposing an alternative.

> "If the employee (...) proposed to use an alternative, seemingly safer measure (...) and obtained oral consent to it from a superior holding the authority to grant such consent, and the employer did not provide the employee with proper training, instructions for the use of the equipment and proper supervision, then the employee's action does not constitute a grave breach of basic employee duties."

In other words: proposed alternative + oral consent of the superior = legal protection, even if the alternative was unusual. Mykoła proposed "closing the ramp barrier + stopping the cycle". Marek accepted orally. If something had gone wrong during the five-minute downtime, Mykoła would have had evidence: proposal + oral consent + log entry. All three together.

### SP VIII Pa 217/18: the warning on the other side

The right of refusal protects you, but it also has another face. The Regional Court, in judgment **VIII Pa 217/18 of 16 January 2019**, held:

> "(...) in the event of improper performance by an employee of duties relating to occupational safety and health, including breach of the rules of safe machine operation and of operating instructions, resulting in a workplace accident or a threat to life and health, the employer has the right to terminate the employment contract without notice, even if there were shortcomings on the employer's side in the organisation of work or enforcement of OSH regulations, **provided that the employee knowingly accepted the risk and did not exercise the right available to him to refrain from work** in a situation creating a direct threat (...)."

In sawmill language: if you knew the machine was dangerous, knowingly accepted the risk, and an accident happened, the employer may dismiss you on disciplinary grounds, **even** if they made organisational errors. **Not exercising art. 210 is a mistake the courts notice**. Silence does not save, silence burdens.

VIII Pa 217/18 is the mirror of VIII Pa 138/14. Together they give the full picture: art. 210 protects you if you use it; it does not protect you if you merely knew about it.

### Art. 283 § 1 KP: the penalty on the other side

The last provision you must know. **Art. 283 § 1 KP**:

> "Whoever, being responsible for the state of occupational safety and health or directing workers or other natural persons, fails to comply with occupational safety and health regulations or rules, shall be subject to a fine of between 1,000 PLN and 30,000 PLN."

This is a penalty on the **directing person's** side, not yours. Marek falls under it at all times. Shift master Piotr falls under it. The plant manager falls under it. PIP issues a fine up to 2,000 PLN under a fine notice (art. 96 § 1a of the Code of Procedure in Petty Offences Cases), up to 5,000 PLN for a repeat offence within two years (§ 1b). Fines up to 30,000 PLN require court proceedings.

**Can it concern you?** In narrow circumstances: when you direct a helper (e.g. Wasyl) and do not observe OSH towards them. Not frequent in a sawmill, but possible. If you instruct Wasyl defectively and he is injured, art. 283 § 1 KP comes into play. That is why workstation instruction must be solid (lesson 8).

**Art. 283 § 2 pt 6 KP** is the second layer, important for lesson 5 (post-incident procedure): failing to notify PIP of a fatal, serious or collective accident carries the same fine (1,000 to 30,000 PLN). Back to this on Friday.

### Five situations in which a service memo is the right response

Not every safety dispute ends with a service memo. Most end the way Mykoła's did this morning: conversation, change of plan, log entry. The memo is an escalation you use when conversation does not work. Five typical situations from a Polish sawmill:

1. **The foreman issues an order breaching the workstation instruction and does not withdraw it after your objection.** Example: working without hearing protectors when emission exceeds 85 dB(A).
2. **The machine has a visible safety defect and the foreman tells you to keep working "until the end of shift".** Example: cracked blade guard, foreman says "another three hours, I'll report it to maintenance at night".
3. **PPE is not provided at the required standard.** Example: scratched goggles, no replacement, foreman says "wear what you have". The Labour Code (art. 2376 and following) requires serviceable protective equipment.
4. **A helper or junior operator is pulled from the workstation without your knowledge and returns with an order you do not know.** Example: Wasyl returns saying "the master told me to stand in the no-go zone". You ask the master; he confirms the order against the instruction.
5. **An event on your line is not recorded.** Example: a minor cut to the helper, the foreman says "let's not write it down, paperwork", and you know the Labour Code and the internal procedure require it.

In all five, the memo is an **act of notification under art. 210 § 1 and art. 211 pt 6 KP**, protecting you legally and helping the superior decide in writing, not orally.

### When a memo reaches PIP

If you wrote a memo and the dispute is not resolved within the plant (the foreman signed and nothing changed, the shift master did nothing, the manager ignored it), you have three external paths. Each is lawful, each foreseen by the Polish labour system.

**Path 1: PIP.** Report online through www.pip.gov.pl or in person at the regional inspectorate (the Warsaw inspectorate for sawmills in the Mazowieckie voivodeship is at ul. Lindleya 16). The memo is the first attachment. PIP has 30 days to initiate an inspection. It may fine the foreman up to 2,000 PLN or refer the case to court (up to 30,000 PLN). Your report may be anonymous vis-à-vis the employer, but PIP knows your identity.

**Path 2: Trade unions.** In EGIDA there is NSZZ Solidarność (you will meet its representatives in lesson 3 of week 4 of M1, migrant rights). The union can represent you vis-à-vis the employer free of charge and usually resolves a case faster than PIP.

**Path 3: The labour court.** If a reprimand, notice, or disciplinary termination has been imposed because of your refusal protected by art. 210, you have **14 days from delivery of the employer's decision** to appeal to the labour court. The cost for the worker in Labour Code cases is symbolic (a 30 PLN filing fee). The memo is evidence.

::: warning
The PIP path and the labour-court path are not mutually exclusive. You may report to PIP **and** go to the labour court at the same time. In serious cases this is routine. A migrant in the Polish labour system has the same rights to labour-court proceedings as a Polish citizen. An instruction on this is always included in the final notice at termination, but many migrants do not read it. You will.
:::

### What about this Wednesday's OSH briefing

Marek ran Wednesday's briefing to cover the oak campaign. After the ramp incident, resolved orally, neither Marek nor Mykoła had to add anything. If Mykoła had written a memo, the duty to discuss it at Thursday's briefing would have fallen on Marek or shift master Piotr. In a Polish sawmill the OSH briefing **closes the event loop**: every safety-related event comes back to the briefing as a reminder or a procedure correction.

You have seen three layers of the loop: **oral** (the 8:42 Mykoła – Marek conversation), **documentary** (log entries at 8:47 and 10:34), **escalatory** (the memo, not needed today). Three layers on one event, depending on how it develops.

### Summary of lesson three

Three articles of the Labour Code hold the whole system together: art. 100 § 1 (the duty to perform, limited by "not contrary to the law"), art. 210 § 1 and 2 (the right to refrain from dangerous work, conditional on immediate notification), art. 211 (basic OSH duties, including point 6 – notification of a threat). Around them stand two more: art. 212 (Marek's duties) and art. 283 § 1 (the penalty on the directing person).

Supreme Court case law (I PK 191/05, I PKN 619/99, II PSK 76/22) and common-court case law (VIII Pa 138/14, X P 1138/14, VIII Pa 217/18) give you three pillars of defence: **objective fact**, **immediate notification**, **proposal of an alternative**. All three together, not separately.

The service memo is notification in a form that survives a dispute. Ten content points, two copies, a signature. Not needed daily, needed when conversation does not work.

Mykoła this morning exercised the right of refusal without citing the Code, but with a concrete plant norm and a proposed alternative. Marek responded properly. The log entries are in place. The dispute did not develop. That is what a good sawmill looks like. Tomorrow, in lesson 4, you will see what to do when a dispute does develop: the "STOP, secure, report, document" procedure. The service memo will then be closer.

## Notes for the Trainer

- **120-min rhythm (proposed):** 10 min Introduction + Objectives + art. 220 → art. 211 correction; 25 min "Three articles that intersect" (read arts. 100, 210, 211 aloud in pairs, with pauses); 15 min "Art. 212 KP – Marek's burden" (discussion); 15 min "Mykoła's third day" (read the scene, stop at the alternative); 15 min "Service memo – format" (each trainee writes a memo for a hypothetical situation, 10 content points); 15 min case law (five judgments, one signature per trainee); 10 min external paths (PIP, unions, labour court); 10 min homework + summary.
- **Pedagogical trap no. 1:** migrant trainees may believe "the law protects only Polish citizens" or "a migrant who goes to PIP will be dismissed". Both are false. Polish labour law **does not differentiate** workers by citizenship (art. 113 of the Labour Code, prohibition of discrimination). Access to PIP and the labour court is identical. On the real-risk question: "in a sawmill with FSC certification or an export client, the plant culture does not allow dismissal for using PIP, because that would hit certification". For a small uncertified sawmill the answer is more cautious, but still: the provisions are on the worker's side, and the memo as evidence is crucial.
- **Pedagogical trap no. 2:** trainees may start treating art. 210 as a "get-out-of-work card". Careful. Judgment SN II PSK 76/22 shows subjective fear without objective fact is a trap. Practise "visible fact" vs. "impression". Fact: a gap in the guard, measured noise above the norm, a missing signal lamp. Impression: "it seems to be overheating", "this band sounds strange", "I don't trust this log". Impressions are a diagnostic signal, not a legal basis.
- **Pedagogical trap no. 3:** the memo-format section may provoke "is this snitching". It is not. A memo is a **notification to the superior about a threat** required by art. 210 § 1 and art. 211 pt 6. In some trainees' cultures (former USSR, Middle East) written notification of a superior is treated as hostile. Dismantle this consciously: in Poland the memo is a standard element of working culture, foremen expect it as proof of operator diligence.
- **Required reading before lesson 4:** the PIP handbook entry "STOP, secure, report, document" from www.pip.gov.pl, if available. Lesson 4 transfers the legal content from lesson 3 onto operational procedure.
- **A note on the art. 220 → art. 211 correction:** the correction is **conscious and necessary**. Art. 220 appeared in lesson 1 as a simplification; it is not an article about a worker's OSH duties. Correct it without drama, as an act of professional precision. If a trainee spots it, praise them: "good that you remember. In law the number counts, not intuition".
