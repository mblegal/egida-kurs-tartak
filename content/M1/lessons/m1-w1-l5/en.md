## Introduction

For four lessons we talked about **what** you protect yourself from: machines, dust, noise, fire. Today we will show **what you protect yourself with**. This is your **PPE** — personal protective equipment. Helmet, goggles, footwear, gloves, mask, hearing protection, sometimes a coverall and a harness.

PPE is not decoration and not a uniform. It is the **last line of defence** between your body and the hazard. Everything before — machine guards, ventilation, procedures — should work, but **when something fails**, it is what is left for you. That is why PPE must be **functional, fitted, and put on correctly** — otherwise it gives a false sense of safety instead of protection.

In this lesson you will learn the **seven basic PPE items** for a helper in a sawmill, when you put them on, when you **do NOT use them** (yes, that matters), and why missing one item can be as dangerous as missing all.

## Objectives

After this lesson:

- You recognize and name the **seven PPE items** for a helper: helmet, goggles, mask, hearing protection, gloves, footwear, clothing.
- You know **when gloves must NOT be worn** — this is not a technicality, this is life.
- You understand how to **check the PPE state** before a shift (expiry date, cracks, dirt).
- You know what to do when one item is missing (and the answer is not "work without").

## Content

### PPE is the last line of defence

In occupational safety there is a **hierarchy of protection measures** — from best to weakest:

1. **Elimination** — remove the hazard. E.g. buy a machine that does not spark.
2. **Substitution** — replace the hazard with a milder one. E.g. an odour-free grease instead of an irritating one.
3. **Engineering controls** — guards, dust extraction, soundproofing. They protect everyone.
4. **Procedures and training** — you know how to work. They protect only when followed.
5. **PPE** — the last step. Protects you only when you put it on.

::: info
PPE is the **weakest** protective measure, because it depends on the human: you must have it, put it on, put it on well, and maintain it. Each step — a chance to miss. That is why the employer has a duty to do everything higher on the list (1–4) *before* relying on PPE.
:::

### The seven PPE items for a helper

**1. Safety helmet** 🪖
Protects the head from impact (a falling object, hitting a beam, kickback). Standard EN 397. The **expiry date** is on the inside — a helmet loses its properties 3–5 years after manufacture (UV light, temperature). Cracked, deeply scratched, after an impact — **not usable**, even if it looks fine. Throw it out and take a new one.

**2. Safety goggles** 👓
Protect the eyes from chips, dust, and splinters. In a sawmill you put them on ALWAYS when you are in a zone where something can fly — essentially the whole production floor when machines are running. Standard EN 166. Scratched goggles are replaced — scratches scatter light and tire the eyes.

**3. Dust mask** 😷
From yesterday's lesson: **FFP2** minimum, **FFP3** for oak, beech, sanding. Disposable — thrown out at end of shift. Reusable (half-mask with a replaceable filter) — clean and keep in a closed box.

**4. Hearing protection** 🎧
Earmuffs or earplugs. You put them on **when entering the floor** where the saws run and take them off **only when leaving**. Earplugs are yours personally — and so are the earmuffs (hygiene, comfort).

**5. Gloves** 🧤
This is **the trickiest PPE item in a sawmill**. You use gloves **when carrying loads, wood, sharp materials**. But:

::: warning
**Do NOT wear gloves near rotating machines** — saws, drills, lathes, planers. A glove caught by a blade pulls your hand into the machine faster than you can let go. Without a glove — the hand slips free; with a glove — it does not. This is the exception to the "PPE always" rule. Mind it.
:::

Glove types:
- **Cut-resistant** (Kevlar, class 5 to EN 388) — for carrying rough boards with splinters.
- **Grippy rubber/latex** — wet material, work with bark.
- **Thick work gloves** — general, for most tasks.
- **Damaged gloves** (torn, worn fingers) — thrown out.

**6. Safety footwear** 👟
Boots with a **steel or composite toe cap** (protects against falling weight) and a **puncture-resistant sole** (protects against a nail in a plank). Standard EN ISO 20345 — marking **S3** is the minimum in a sawmill (water-resistant, slip-resistant, puncture-resistant, with a toe cap).

Footwear has a date of manufacture and wears out — **a sole older than 2 years of daily use** loses elasticity and grip. If you see that the boots have a smooth sole — request a replacement.

**7. Work clothing** 👔
Not baggy (entanglement), not revealing (sparks, dust), with long sleeves and trousers. **Sleeveless shirt, shorts, sandals on the floor — absolute ban**. In winter, an insulated jacket must be fitted, not hanging.

A fall-arrest harness (work at height) and a disposable coverall (drying-kiln work, intense dust) are special cases you will learn when the need arises.

### Pre-shift PPE check — a 30-second ritual

Every morning, before entering the floor:

1. **Helmet** — check the date inside, look from above for cracks.
2. **Goggles** — no scratches, elastic firm, nose rests well.
3. **Mask** — fresh (disposable) or clean (reusable), in factory wrapping/box.
4. **Hearing protection** — earmuffs undamaged, headband elastic. Foam plugs — a new pair.
5. **Gloves** — a full pair, no holes or worn spots, matching the day's task.
6. **Footwear** — laced, sole without wear.
7. **Clothing** — buttoned, fitted, no loose fabric around the hands.

::: tip
The "30-second check" ritual you do **before entering the floor**, not during work. If you notice something wrong — go back to the locker room, get another item, or report to the foreman. Do not walk onto the floor thinking "I'll fix it at break" — at the break it may already be too late.
:::

### What to do when an item is missing

Sometimes the locker is empty. Sometimes you lost something. Sometimes the foreman forgot to order. **You do not enter the floor without a full PPE set**. This is not your decision — it is the employer's duty and your criminal liability if an accident happens.

The procedure:
1. Report the missing item to the foreman.
2. Ask for a replacement from the PPE store (every plant has a stock).
3. If no replacement — **you do not enter the floor**. You work in zones that do not require that item (store, office) or you wait.
4. You record it in the **PPE register** (there is one at the workstation). If the next day the same item is missing — it is a systemic signal for PIP.

::: example
From a PIP report, 2022: a helper without safety goggles stood at a circular saw because "the locker was empty and the foreman said to work". A small chip hit the eye. Result: damaged cornea, 3 months of sick leave, partial loss of vision. The helper won the court case (one cannot be forced to work without PPE), but the eye did not fully recover. **The "missing PPE in the register" entry for that day** was the key evidence in court.
:::

### PPE does not replace thinking

The last rule: **having full PPE, you do not stop thinking**. A helmet will not protect you from a 500-kg log. Goggles will not stop a board flying at 100 km/h. An FFP3 mask will not help if you walk into an explosive dust cloud.

PPE is a **cushion for residual risk** — the risk left after elimination, engineering controls, and procedures. If you step into high risk "because I have PPE", you are misusing PPE. A good worker treats PPE as **a complement to the procedure**, not as a licence to risk.

## Key terms

- **ŚOI** (środki ochrony indywidualnej) 🥽 — PPE (Personal Protective Equipment) / EPI (Equipos de Protección Individual) / ЗІЗ (засоби індивідуального захисту)
- **hełm ochronny** 🪖 — safety helmet (hard hat) / casco de seguridad / захисна каска
- **okulary ochronne** 👓 — safety goggles / gafas de protección / захисні окуляри
- **rękawice robocze** 🧤 — work gloves / guantes de trabajo / робочі рукавиці
- **rękawice antyprzecięciowe** ✂️ — cut-resistant gloves / guantes anticorte / рукавиці, стійкі до порізу
- **obuwie ochronne** 👟 — safety footwear / calzado de seguridad / захисне взуття
- **podnosek stalowy** 🔩 — steel toe cap / puntera de acero / металевий носок
- **podeszwa antyprzebiciowa** 🛡️ — puncture-resistant sole / suela antiperforación / підошва, стійка до проколу
- **odzież robocza** 👔 — work clothing / ropa de trabajo / робочий одяг
- **uprząż asekuracyjna** 🪢 — safety harness (fall arrest) / arnés de seguridad / страхувальна система
- **rejestr ŚOI** 📋 — PPE register / registro de EPI / журнал ЗІЗ

## Check yourself

1. You work at a circular saw and hold a short piece of board in your hand. Do you wear gloves?
   - A) Yes — without gloves splinters may hurt.
   - B) No — with rotating machines gloves can be caught by the blade.
   - C) Only cut-resistant ones.

   → **B**

2. Your helmet is 7 years old (the date of manufacture is inside). No cracks, looks fine. Do you use it?
   - A) Yes, since there are no cracks.
   - B) No — a helmet loses its properties after 3–5 years regardless of looks. Replace it.
   - C) I ask the foreman and he will say "use it anyway".

   → **B**

3. The PPE locker is empty — no FFP3 masks, and today you work with oak. What do you do?
   - A) I put on FFP2 because it is on the shelf.
   - B) I work without a mask — just for today.
   - C) I report the shortage to the foreman, record it in the PPE register, and do not enter the floor without the proper mask. I work in a zone that does not require the mask, or I wait.

   → **C**

## Link to practice

In the first week the foreman will walk you through the **full PPE inventory** in the cabinet. You will try on the helmet (adjustable inside — must sit stable, not slip), goggles (three sizes — fit them), gloves (different types — take a starter pair of each type). Footwear is fitted to size — sometimes it takes 2–3 days; until then you work in your own.

During the first month you will also do a **personal PPE inventory** — find and mark everything you received with the date of receipt. This record becomes your personal document — keep a copy if you change employer.

## Trainer notes

**Pedagogical goal:** the move from the abstraction of hazards (l1-l4) to the **materiality of protection**. The learner should **touch** every item — not just see a photo. Touch builds procedural memory.

**120-minute rhythm:** 10 min revision of the week (the triad + fire + dust), 15 min on the hierarchy of protection measures (PPE is last, not first!), 60 min **physical fitting of the seven items** (split learners into pairs — one puts on, one checks; each item 7–8 minutes), 15 min on gloves and the "rotating machines" exception (the most important rule of the lesson), 10 min on the missing-PPE procedure (the register), 10 min on terms and self-check.

**Key demonstration:** take a glove and hook it with one finger onto a rotating roller (a simulation — use a scrap board on a roller, for exercise safety). Show how easily the glove "catches" — a visual argument stronger than a thousand words.

**Common mistake:** the learner treats PPE as "extra work gear", not as "the last line of defence". Explain the hierarchy (1–5): that PPE is where everything else failed. This changes the respect for the gear.

**Control question at the end:** "Which PPE item do you least often put on at home when doing something dangerous?" Most will say: goggles. And goggles are exactly what is most neglected on the floor — because they are uncomfortable and require a moment to fit. That is the moment to realize the PPE habit starts with the uncomfortable items.
