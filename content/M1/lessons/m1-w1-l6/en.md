## Introduction

You already know what you protect yourself with (PPE from yesterday). Today you will learn **where you are allowed to be** and where not. In a sawmill, everything is shown on the floor and on the walls, you just have to **know how to read it**.

A sawmill floor is not a free space. It is a **map**, in which every line, every pictogram, and every sign has a meaning. Yellow lines, red signs, green arrows, blue markings, this is the language in which the hall speaks to you, even when no foreman is next to you. Once you learn to read these signs, the hall stops being chaos and becomes a **predictable place**.

In this lesson you will learn **safety colours** to ISO 3864, the four types of **safety pictograms**, the typical **zones of a sawmill** (production, traffic, storage, sanitary, evacuation), and what you do when you **see a broken marking rule**.

## Objectives

After this lesson:

- You recognize four safety colours: **red** (prohibition/fire), **yellow** (warning), **blue** (mandatory), **green** (information/evacuation).
- You read the four types of **pictograms**: prohibition, warning, mandatory, informational.
- You recognize the typical sawmill zones: **production**, **traffic**, **storage**, **sanitary**, **evacuation**.
- You know what to do when you see a **blocked evacuation route** or a missing sign.

## Content

### The four safety colours: the ISO 3864 system

Around the world, four colours are used for safety marking:

**🔴 Red, prohibition and fire.**
- No smoking, no entry, no mobile phones.
- Everything about fire protection: extinguishers, fire alarms, hydrants.
- Note: the **emergency stop button** is red-and-yellow (a red button on a yellow background).

**🟡 Yellow, warning.**
- Watch out for falling objects, watch for forklifts, watch for high voltage.
- Everywhere there is a potential hazard, but not a prohibition.
- **Yellow lines on the floor**: boundaries of working zones and traffic lanes.

**🔵 Blue, mandatory.**
- PPE must be worn: "helmet required", "goggles required", "hearing protection required".
- Flow directions, operating rules.
- Mandatory signs apply to everyone entering the zone, not only workers.

**🟢 Green, safety and evacuation.**
- Evacuation routes (green arrows).
- Emergency exits.
- First-aid kits, assembly point.
- Safety information ("first-aid kit here", "emergency shower here").

::: info
The safety-colour system is **international**. A learner from Ukraine, Colombia, or Venezuela, each recognizes the same red sign as "prohibition" without translation. That is why pictograms matter more than text, the text is in Polish, but the picture speaks universally.
:::

### The four types of pictograms

The geometric shape tells the message type:

**Prohibition sign**: 🚫
A circle with a red border and a diagonal slash. A black image inside.
Examples: "no smoking" (a cigarette crossed out), "no entry" (a figure crossed out), "no mobile phones".

**Warning sign**: ⚠️
A triangle with a yellow background and a black border. A black image inside.
Examples: "watch for forklift" (a forklift silhouette), "high voltage" (a lightning bolt), "flammable material" (a flame).

**Mandatory sign**: ✅
A circle with a blue background. A white image inside.
Examples: "helmet required" (a helmet), "hearing protection required" (headphones on ears), "mask required" (a mask on a face).

**Information sign**: 🟩
A rectangle or square with a green background. A white image inside.
Examples: "evacuation direction" (a running figure + arrow), "emergency exit" (a door + arrow), "first-aid point" (a white cross).

### Zones of a sawmill floor

A typical floor is divided into five zones:

**1. Production zone.** The machines stand here and the operators work. Yellow lines around the machines. You enter **only on the operator's signal**, and only when the machine is stopped. Full PPE required.

**2. Traffic zone.** Wide strips painted with yellow lines on the floor. **Forklifts** and people move here. **Do not sit or stand there without reason**: a forklift can arrive at any moment.

::: warning
A sawmill forklift weighs 3–5 tonnes and moves quietly (electric or gas motor). The operator often **cannot see a pedestrian** hidden behind a stack, that is why the traffic lane is only for walking through, never for standing. In doubt, shout, wave, step aside. Better to look silly than to be hit.
:::

**3. Storage zone.** Stacks of lumber, racks of logs, containers of chips. You enter when you have a task (take a board, clean). **Do not walk between stacks** without the foreman's permission, stacks can tip over.

**4. Sanitary zone.** Locker room, toilets, break room, kitchen with a microwave. **Here you remove PPE**: do not enter the locker room in dusty clothes (dust from under the helmet into the room where you eat is pointless contamination). The sanitary zone usually has a separate "dirty" area (a rack for work clothing) and a "clean" area (a locker for home clothing).

**5. Evacuation zone.** Evacuation routes, emergency exits, the outdoor assembly point. **Always clear.** No materials may be stored there. If you see a crate on the evacuation route, report to the foreman.

### Reading the floor

The colour and shape of floor lines matter:

- **Solid yellow line**: the boundary of a machine's working zone. You do not cross without permission.
- **Dashed yellow line**: warning: change of level / ramp / threshold.
- **White lines**: boundaries of traffic routes (pedestrian lane, forklift lane).
- **Red-and-white hatched area (zebra)**: a hazardous area, entry forbidden.
- **Green arrows**: direction to the emergency exit.

::: example
On your first day in a sawmill you notice how **very colourful** the floor is. This is not aesthetics, it is a system. In a sawmill in Opole, where PIP inspected in 2021, they found **faded yellow lines** under the saw's working zone. Helpers were crossing them "because they couldn't see them". Two accidents in half a year. After the inspection, the markings were repainted, accidents in the next half year: zero. Colour on the floor **changes behaviour** even when no one is watching.
:::

### What to do when a marking rule is broken

Scenarios where you react:

1. **Blocked evacuation route** (a crate, a trolley, material). → Report to the foreman. If light, move it yourself (a box or a crate only, not a machine!).
2. **Missing sign** (e.g. no evacuation sign over a door). → Report to the foreman; he orders a new one.
3. **Sign damaged, unreadable, painted over**. → Report to the foreman.
4. **Someone else breaks the marking** (smoking in a no-smoking zone, entering a working zone). → Remind politely ("hey, no smoking here"). If that doesn't work, report to the foreman. **Do not enter a physical confrontation.**
5. **Worn-out floor line**. → Report to the foreman.

### Special situations

**A visitor in the sawmill (inspector, courier, school visit).** Visitors are given a **high-visibility vest** and move **only along designated routes**. If you see a visitor leaving the route, direct them to the foreman or to the correct path. A visitor without a vest in a production zone is a serious problem, report immediately.

**Night shift and emergency lighting.** At night the zones are dimmed except for work stations. The evacuation route has **emergency lighting** (green lamps above doors, battery for about 1 hour). If the power fails during the day, emergency lighting turns on automatically.

## Check yourself

1. You see a solid yellow line painted around a machine. What does it mean?
   - A) An area to clean.
   - B) Boundary of the working zone, do not cross without the operator's permission.
   - C) A place to put tools.

   → **B**

2. A green arrow on the wall with a running figure. What is it?
   - A) Route to the locker room.
   - B) Evacuation route, direction to the emergency exit.
   - C) A reminder for physical exercise.

   → **B**

3. You see someone has placed a crate on the evacuation route. What do you do?
   - A) I leave it, not my business.
   - B) I move the crate (if light), report to the foreman, and make sure the route is clear.
   - C) I call my brother.

   → **B**

## Link to practice

On the first day in the sawmill, the foreman takes you on a **walk around the floor**: he shows you the boundaries of your work zone, the route to the locker room, the evacuation route, the outdoor assembly point. Pay attention to what is painted on the floor **under your feet**: the yellow lines are your boundaries.

In the locker room you get a **high-visibility vest** if your role includes moving through the traffic zone. The vest is **mandatory when crossing a forklift lane**: the forklift operator must see you.

## Trainer notes

**Pedagogical goal:** the learner **reads the floor** like a map. After this lesson, when entering for the first time, they should recognize the zones themselves and know where they are. This reduces first-day anxiety and speeds up adaptation.

**120-minute rhythm:** 10 min recap (l5: what you protect yourself with, today: where you are allowed to be), 15 min on the ISO 3864 colour system (a handout with the 4 colours, read each), 20 min on the four pictogram types (show at least 5 examples of each type, best as a "detective" activity: the learner gets a set of sign photos and sorts them into 4 piles), 20 min on the floor zones with a drawing (draw a typical hall with 5 zones, learners add where they do what), 15 min on floor lines and colours, 15 min on reaction scenarios (what do you do when…), 15 min on terms and self-check, 10 min of wrap-up.

**"Pictogram detective" exercise:** prepare 20 pictogram photos from various facilities (also outside the sawmill, e.g. swimming pool, construction, hospital). Learners sort them into 4 piles: prohibition/warning/mandatory/information. Check the answers. The exercise shows that the **system is universal**: learned once, it works everywhere.

**Common mistake:** learners mix up the warning sign (triangle, yellow) with the prohibition sign (circle with a slash, red). Explain: warning = "watch out, it's dangerous here", prohibition = "this must not be done". The difference in behaviour is huge.
