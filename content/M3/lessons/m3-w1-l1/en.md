## Introduction

Eight weeks ago you walked into the *tartak* (sawmill) as a **pomocnik** (helper). Four weeks ago you passed the Tuesday M2 exam and became an **operator junior** (junior operator). Today, on the door of the changing room, a new shift list is hanging. Your name is on it for the first time **without an asterisk**. In the *tartak* the asterisk meant one thing: "works under the *brygadzista's* (foreman's) authorization, each start-up separately". Today there is no asterisk. You are on the list in plain type, like Marek, like Tadeusz, like Andrij three months ago. You are an **operator samodzielny** (independent operator).

In M2 Marek, the *brygadzista*, stood at your machine. He saw your checklist. He spoke the word *"ruszasz"* (you go / you run it) before every cycle. You were responsible, but his eye was the last filter between your hand and the green button. In M3 Marek no longer stands next to you. He is at the foreman's workstation, two stations away. You **alone** make the decision, you alone press the button, you alone report the result. **The report goes to the foreman after the fact, not before the operation.**

This one change weighs more than all the other changes in M3. In a moment we will introduce you to Mykoła, who today stood on the list without an asterisk for the first time. Mykoła went through M1 and M2 together with you, in the same cohort of *Fundacja EGIDA* (the EGIDA Foundation). He came from Ukraine two years ago. His Polish sounds soft, but his hand already speaks the language of the band saw. Today is his first day of M3, just like yours. We will stay with him for the next four weeks.

## Objectives

After this lesson:

1. You understand what exactly changes in your duties when you move from the role of *operator junior* (M2) to the role of *operator samodzielny* (M3).
2. You can name the three new fields of responsibility of the *operator samodzielny*: batch quality, the team on the line, communication with the *brygadzista* after the fact.
3. You know why "after-the-fact report" does not mean "report without witnesses" and which workstation papers you keep from today on.
4. You recognize the moment at which a decision stops being yours and must return to the *brygadzista*, the *mistrz zmiany* (shift master) or the *kierownik utrzymania ruchu* (maintenance department manager).
5. You can see your place on the updated career map and you know what lies on the fourth rung, which you may reach after M3.

## Content

### What exactly changes in M3

The *pomocnik* (M1) did not operate the machine. The *operator junior* (M2) operated it **with authorization**. The *operator samodzielny* (M3) operates it **with responsibility**. Three different positions, three different legal statuses.

In M1 the *brygadzista's* eye was constantly present. In M2 the *brygadzista's* eye was present **before** every cycle (the word *"ruszasz"*). In M3 the *brygadzista's* eye is present **after** the cycle (shift report, signature on the batch card, entry in the machine log). Between these two moments you stand, alone, with your judgement and your hand on the button.

::: info
The Polish legal system recognises three worker statuses at woodworking machines and assigns to them different scopes of responsibility. The helper is responsible for his own body and for orders. The junior operator is additionally responsible for the machine he operates with authorization. The independent operator is responsible for **the outcome of the batch** and for **the safety of the team on the line**, if he has one (a helper, a more junior operator). This is not a cosmetic difference. This is a difference that is paid at a higher rate and that the Polish labour courts punish more severely.
:::

### Three new fields of responsibility

Once you cross the M3 threshold, three areas enter the picture which you did not formally have in M2.

**The first field: batch quality.** In M2 you sorted *tarcica* (sawn timber) into three stacks (A, B, reject) according to the *brygadzista's* instructions. In M3 you **decide yourself** which stack a board goes onto, and you **sign yourself** the *karta partii* (batch card) handed over to the *klasyfikator* (timber grader). Your signature on the batch card is a working document. If a client files a complaint about a batch, the batch card with your signature is the first piece of paper that the *mistrz suszarni* (kiln master) or the quality manager picks up. You are not obliged to defend yourself on your own (there is a complaint procedure for that, you will learn it in T4), but you are obliged to **document conscientiously** what you did.

**The second field: the team on the line.** From today a *pomocnik* M1 works next to you (a new cohort has just come in) or an *operator junior* M2 (you are replacing someone, someone is learning). You are **their first safety filter**. If the helper approaches the band saw closer than 3 metres, you pull him back, you write a note to the *brygadzista*. If the junior operator starts the machine without your authorization (now you are that authorization for him, within the scope set by the *brygadzista*), you stop the cycle. Legally you are not his superior, but from an OHS point of view you are his *instruktor stanowiskowy* (workstation instructor) (more on this in l8 of this week).

**The third field: communication after the fact.** In M2 you used to say *"Sprawdzone, Marek"* (Checked, Marek) before the start. In M3 you say "Batch 47 closed, 12 m³ of class A timber, 3 m³ of B, one board reject with end split, band to be changed tomorrow" after the end. This is not a casual conversation. This is a **workstation report** which the *brygadzista* acknowledges with a signature in the machine log. You will learn its format in l4 T4. For now remember: short form, facts in numbers, exactly what happened, without guesses about causes.

::: warning
The biggest trap for operators entering M3: **the illusion of freedom**. When Marek no longer stands next to you, it is easy to decide that "nobody is watching". Nobody is watching in a given second, but everybody is watching after the fact. CCTV records for 30 days (the standard setting in most plants). The machine log stays for 5 years. The batch card with your signature, if it concerns structural timber, stays for 10 years in the client's archive. In the morning nobody sees. Two months later everybody sees, because the client files a complaint about a batch that had an end split, and somebody opens the card.
:::

### Who Mykoła is

Mykoła Hrycenko came to Poland two years ago from Poltava. He was 24 years old, he had finished a mechanical *technikum* (technical secondary school) in Ukraine and had learned Russian at school. He did not know Polish at all. For the first eight months he worked in a Lidl warehouse near Warsaw, he carried pallets, he learned the words *wózek* (truck / trolley), *paleta* (pallet), *stawka godzinowa* (hourly rate), *umowa zlecenie* (service contract, a Polish civil-law contract). He lived with three fellow Ukrainians in one room in Praga, for 900 PLN per person. He earned 3800 PLN net. He called his wife once a week.

He was signed up for the *Fundacja EGIDA* course by a colleague from Wołyń, who had seen a poster at the Orthodox church on Pułtuska street. "Mykoła, go to the sawmill. In the sawmill there is air, not a warehouse. In the sawmill they pay better. In the sawmill they keep you longer than a service contract." Mykoła went. He finished M1 with 81%. M2 with 84%. He is not the star of the cohort, the star is Andrij from Lviv, who passed M2 with 94%. But Mykoła is **solid**. Marek the *brygadzista* writes in his workstation card one Polish word that Mykoła already understands: *konsekwentny* (consistent).

Today Mykoła stands in front of the band saw P3 in the EGIDA sawmill hall. For the first time without Marek next to him. Marek is 40 metres away, at the circular saw P1, where a new *pomocnik* M1 from Ukraine starts today. Mykoła knows that Marek will not come to him until two hours later, with the machine log in hand. Until then Mykoła makes every decision on P3 by himself.

::: example
Mykoła is not "a new hero instead of Andrij". Andrij is still working in the same sawmill. He is three months ahead of Mykoła, he has already been an *operator samodzielny* for a quarter of a year, he operates the circular saw P2. You will meet him in T4 of this module, at the lesson on client complaints. Andrij has twice in his life taken part in a complaint visit, he knows how to behave. Mykoła does not know yet. That is why you, together with Mykoła, will go to Andrij for advice. That is how promotion works in a Polish sawmill: every rung learns from the rung one above it.
:::

### Mykoła's first Monday

It is 6:47 in the morning, 18 minutes before the start of the shift. Mykoła enters the hall, takes off his jacket in the changing room, takes his helmet, goggles, hearing protectors, cotton gloves from the locker. He walks past the board with the shift list. He runs his finger along his name. There is no asterisk. His heart races for three seconds, then returns to normal.

Five minutes before the start, at the band saw P3, he does what he used to do in M2: five points of the checklist. Guards in place. Band tensioned (2300 PSI, within the range 2200–2400). Dust extraction humming. Idle run smooth, no wheel wobble. Nobody within 3 metres of the machine. In M2 at this moment he would have said: *"Sprawdzone, Marek"*. Today there is nobody to say it to.

Mykoła holds his breath for a moment. He looks at the green button. In M2 the authorization was the foreman's word. In M3 the authorization is **the state of the checklist**. Five points passed. The state is positive. The authorization to start came out of his own head, not from Marek's mouth. Mykoła presses the button. The band starts. For the first 10 seconds he watches the machine, not the log. The sound is steady. The band does not wobble. He takes the first *kłoda* (log).

Thirty minutes later Mykoła wipes his forehead with his sleeve. He has processed six pine logs, batch 47. He walks up to the machine log (a tablet in a case on a stand next to P3), opens it, taps with his finger:

> **6 pine logs, diameter 28–34 cm, entry moisture 42% (measurement on 2 logs).**
> **Sawn into timber 50 × 150 mm, kerf 3.5 mm. Raw material yield approximately 58%.**
> **Band fine, temperature warm in the hand but not hot.**
> **Batch 47 in progress, 6 of 20 logs.**

He saves. Marek walks up, reads the tablet, looks at Mykoła. He says briefly:

> **Marek:** "Czytelnie. Dalej." [Clear. Keep going.]

And walks away. This is the first after-the-fact report that Mykoła wrote on his own. Marek did not ask "how is it going". He asked **what you wrote**. This is the difference between M2 and M3. Paper, not conversation.

::: tip
Notice three things in Mykoła's report. First, **numbers**: 6 logs, 28–34 cm, 42%, 50 × 150 mm, 3.5 mm, 58%. The *brygadzista* does not need descriptions, he needs data. Second, **the boundary of knowledge**: "temperature warm in the hand but not hot" instead of "band overheated". Mykoła does not diagnose what he does not measure. Third, **the state of the batch**: "6 of 20 logs" tells Marek that in 100 minutes the batch will be closed and a space for the stack needs to be prepared. The report is a planning tool for the *brygadzista*, not a description of the operator's emotions.
:::

### After-the-fact report does not mean a report without witnesses

The most common misreading of the "after-the-fact report" rule: thinking that between the start of the cycle and the report you are alone. You are not. During those two hours four things are watching you, which in M1 and M2 you did not see clearly.

**CCTV cameras.** The EGIDA sawmill has 11 cameras in the main hall. They record for 30 days, but the *mistrz zmiany* can pull the footage whenever he wants. An accident, a client complaint, a dispute with a colleague: each of these reasons opens the archive. The camera sees your hands, your posture, the distance of the helper from the machine, whether you put your goggles back on after the breakfast break.

**The machine log.** The tablet at P3 does not only receive your entries. It also records data from the machine: cycle count, motor temperature every 15 minutes, torque, alarms. If your entry says "band warm" and the log shows an overheating alarm at 10:43, the *brygadzista* will see a mismatch. A mismatch between the entry and the data is the first signal that the operator was "painting reality".

**The batch card.** Every batch has a paper card signed by the operator, the *mistrz suszarni* (after drying) and the *klasyfikator* (after grading). Your signature on the card means that you confirm: species, dimensions, entry moisture, number of pieces. If a complaint concerns species non-conformity, the batch card with your signature is the first piece of paper the plant's lawyer picks up.

**The team on the line.** The helper sees your decisions. The junior operator sees your decisions. The forklift driver who comes for the stack sees your decisions. A sawmill is not a mine, here the hall is open, you can see everything from every spot. In M3 the audience for your work grows, not shrinks.

::: warning
You do not keep the report for yourself. You keep it **for three audiences**: the *brygadzista* (operational decisions), the *mistrz zmiany* (weekly summary report), and the client or quality auditor (complaints, FSC audit). If any of these three audiences looks at your report six months from now and does not understand what you did on that day, the report was written badly. The rule: **write as if you are being read by someone who was not there with you**. Because that is how it is.
:::

### When a decision returns to the *brygadzista*

In M3 you have your own decisions, but not **all** decisions. Five situations send the decision back to the *brygadzista*, the *mistrz zmiany* or the *kierownik utrzymania ruchu*. Remember them like a formula.

1. **Atypical raw material.** A log outside of your order for the day: a different species, a different diameter, a marking you do not know. You set it aside, you call the *brygadzista*. You do not improvise.
2. **A machine alarm you do not know.** The machine log flashes code E-07. In the workstation manual there is E-01 to E-05. You do not know E-07. **Stop, switch off, report to UR** (the maintenance department). Never guess the meaning of an alarm.
3. **A colleague's injury, however small.** The helper cut himself with a splinter. Even a small wound. **First-aid kit, entry in the accident log, *brygadzista* notified.** In Poland every injury at the workplace has a paper trail (you will learn it in l5 of this week).
4. **A conflict on the line.** A colleague shouts at you, or you at him, about a procedure. **Stop, separate, call the *brygadzista*.** Resolving personal conflicts at a working machine is the leading cause of serious accidents in Polish sawmills (PIP data from 2022; PIP is the Polish State Labour Inspectorate).
5. **A visit from outside.** A client, an auditor, a supplier. Somebody enters the hall whom you do not know. **Stop, ask for an ID, call the *brygadzista*.** The *operator samodzielny* does not represent the plant to outsiders. That is the role of the *mistrz zmiany* or the manager.

::: info
Point five (a visit from outside) concerns you doubly if the auditor is from FSC or PEFC. These audits have a strict protocol. If an operator answers an auditor's question incompletely or imprecisely, it may block the certification of the entire sawmill for another year. That is not your role and not your responsibility. You will learn the audit visit procedure in l3 T4.
:::

### Art. 210 and 220 of the *Kodeks pracy* – a preview

Two articles of the Polish *Kodeks pracy* (Labour Code) define your legal status in M3. You will learn them in detail in l3 of this week, because they deserve a separate lesson. Here we will only name them, so that you know where we are going.

**Art. 210 KP**: the employee has the **right** to refrain from work if the working conditions do not comply with OHS regulations and create a direct threat to health or life. You already had this right in M2. In M3 it has a second face: as the *operator samodzielny* you are **the only person** at the machine who can exercise this right at a given moment. Your refusal of dangerous work stops the line, not only your workstation.

**Art. 220 KP**: the employee is **obliged** to follow the superiors' orders concerning work, if they do not conflict with legal regulations. In M3 this is a duty set against art. 210: an order that breaches OHS is not a binding order. But the judgement "does it breach" rests with you, in the first moment, before the dispute reaches PIP (the State Labour Inspectorate).

::: tip
These two articles come back in everyday conversations with the *brygadzista*, sometimes under other names ("the right to stop", "the duty to perform"). In l3 you will see what the full texts say and what the service memo looks like that you write if you disagree with the foreman's order. For now remember only the numbers: **two hundred and ten** (the right to refuse), **two hundred and twenty** (the duty to perform). In two days we will come back to them with full content.
:::

### The career map – the third rung and what lies beyond

The four rungs of the sawmill path which you learned in M2 now look like this:

**Rung 1. Pomocnik (helper)** (completed, M1). OHS, PPE, the 2-second rule, the six hazards. Work under constant supervision, without machine operation.

**Rung 2. Operator junior (junior operator)** (completed, M2). Operation of the band saw and the circular saw under authorization, moisture measurement, sorting, assistance with band changes. You worked for four months, with the last two already involving an ever smaller share of the *brygadzista*.

**Rung 3. Operator samodzielny (independent operator)** (you start today, M3). Independent start-ups, cutting parameter selection, band change without assistance, planning of the drying campaign, supervision of the helper and the junior operator, after-the-fact report, responsibility for the batch. After M3 and after 12–18 months of tenure you have access to the courses of the next rung.

**Rung 4. Mistrz zmiany (shift master)** (a perspective, 2–4 years from today). You lead a crew of 4–8 people. You receive the raw material from the supplier, you talk to the client about quality, you run the shift briefing. Requires: M3, the UDT licence (forklift, issued by the Polish *Urząd Dozoru Technicznego*, technical inspection authority), SEP up to 1 kV (Polish electrical qualification), and a master craftsman examination in the *izba rzemieślnicza* (chamber of crafts) for structural timber.

Additional development paths after M3 that come into play today:

- **Mistrz suszarni (kiln master)** (1–2 years of tenure in M3 + the ITB kiln master course; ITB is the *Instytut Techniki Budowlanej*, the Polish Building Research Institute). Responsible for the drying chambers, the campaign schedule, the final quality of the timber. You will meet his role in T3 of this module.
- **Klasyfikator tarcicy (timber grader)** (2–3 years of tenure + accredited ITB training, PN-EN 14081). He independently assigns the strength classes C16/C24/C35. His hourly rate is higher than the operator's. You will learn the scope in l3 T2.
- **CNC machine operator in a joinery** (after completing the joiner course, the CAD path). A way out of the sawmill towards furniture or construction joinery. Cleaner work, lower noise, higher precision.

::: info
Rung 4 and the three additional paths are a map for 2–4 years. You do not choose today. During M3 you have four weeks to observe: which of these rungs pulls you? People strong in decisions and communication become *mistrzowie zmiany* (shift masters). People patient and precise with numbers become *mistrzowie suszarni* (kiln masters). People with a good eye and a memory for standards become *klasyfikatorzy* (graders). Every profile is possible. Observe which role pulls you during these four weeks, and at the end of M3 talk to Marek about the next step.
:::

## Trainer notes

- **120-min rhythm (suggested):** 15 min Introduction + Objectives; 20 min "What exactly changes in M3" + "Three new fields of responsibility"; 15 min Mykoła narrative (reading with pauses, stop at the moment "his heart races for three seconds"); 15 min "After-the-fact report does not mean without witnesses" (pair work: list the 4 audiences of the report); 15 min "When a decision returns" (brainstorm, examples from the trainees' own plants); 10 min preview of art. 210/220 KP (for now only numbers and intuition); 20 min Career map (each trainee marks on the board which fourth rung pulls them) + Key terms; 10 min homework reminder.
- **Pedagogical trap no. 1:** the introduction of Mykoła as a new protagonist (after 24 lessons of Andrij) may provoke the question from trainees: "what about Andrij?". Short answer: **Andrij keeps working, he was promoted faster, you will see him in T4**. Do not go into details, because Mykoła's arc has priority. If the question comes back, redirect: "in a sawmill promotion is not a race, everyone has their own pace".
- **Pedagogical trap no. 2:** the section "Three new fields of responsibility" carries real legal weight. Watch out for trainees who out of fear may raise the question: "I do not want this responsibility, I would rather stay in M2". Answer: **in a Polish plant the junior operator is a transitional stage, not a target position**. Rates, access to courses, contract stability: everything grows with entry into M3. If the trainee is afraid, go back to l6 M1 (risk assessment) and remind them that fear is a signal, not a verdict.
- **Pedagogical trap no. 3:** Mykoła as a character should be presented **with respect for his migrant background** (Ukraine, Poltava, the Lidl warehouse, the room for 900 PLN). Do not dramatize, do not pity him. Mykoła is a competent adult who made the decision to take the course and does his work. If there are trainees in the group with a similar path, this narrative will open a conversation about their own first days in Poland. Allow it, but do not force it.
- **Preview of l3 (art. 210/220 KP):** you consciously do not enter the content of the articles today. Lesson l3 is a legal one and requires its own rhythm. If a trainee asks for details, answer: "on Wednesday, with the text in front of you". You are building expectation, not extinguishing it.
- **Time of return to this lesson:** in every lesson of T1 come back to the "after-the-fact report" rule and the "five situations of decision return". These two grids are the axis of the whole first week of M3, not only of the introduction.
