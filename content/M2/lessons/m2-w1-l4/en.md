## Introduction

Hai's fifth day as an operator. He walks up to the band saw, dressed like an operator (lesson 2), with his left hand already reaching instinctively for the E-stop mushroom button (lesson 3). The foreman comes over, hands him an A5 sheet with five checkboxes, and says: **"From today, before you press START, you tick off five points. Five out of five or zero."**

Hai looks at the sheet:

- [ ] Guards
- [ ] Weld / blade
- [ ] Dust extraction
- [ ] Idle run
- [ ] Clear surroundings

Five words, two minutes of work, one rule: **there are no important and less important points**. If you cannot tick any one of them, you do not start the machine and you go fetch the foreman.

This lesson explains why a checklist is not bureaucracy but the **operator's fifth limb**. Why the **order** of points is rigid (from the most physical to the most spatial). Why the "5 out of 5 or zero" rule is stricter than "4 out of 5 is enough, I'll check the fifth while running". And why Hai, after a month of such sheets, will start completing the checklist **in his head**, without paper, in 90 seconds.

This is the last lesson of Week 1 of M2. The **check – start – report** ritual closes here: five points is "check" in full expansion. After them comes START. After start, in lesson 6, we return to "report".

## Objectives

After this lesson:

1. You know the five points of the pre-start checklist (guards, weld/blade, dust extraction, idle run, clear surroundings) and you understand why their order is rigid.
2. You can perform each point in 15-20 seconds and you recognise exactly what you are assessing (what is OK, what is not OK, what requires the foreman).
3. You understand the "5 out of 5 or zero" rule and you know why the shortcut "4 out of 5 is enough" is the most common trap of an operator's second month on the job.
4. You can fill out the checklist on paper in 2 minutes and you know when you transition to the "in the head" version (after 20-30 days of daily repetition).
5. You close the Week 1 ritual: check yourself (PPE) → check STOP (mushroom button) → check the machine (5 points) → start (START).

## Content

### Why a checklist and not "the operator's eye"

An operator with 10 years of experience knows what to check without a sheet. An operator with 10 days of experience does not. The difference: **2200 repetitions of the ritual**. During that time every operator has missed something at least once. PIP (Polish Labour Inspectorate) statistics for 2020-2024 for accidents on woodworking saws:

- **39%** of accidents are the result of **skipping the guard check** (unscrewed, blocked, removed "for a moment").
- **24%** are **skipping the blade check** (cracked disc, torn band weld).
- **16%** are **skipping the surroundings check** (someone entered the kickback zone).
- **12%** are **dust extraction failure** (dust ignites from a bearing spark).
- **9%** other.

The first four categories are in your checklist. Together **91%**. The checklist does not eliminate risk, but it turns "I remember / I don't remember" into "ticked / not ticked".

::: info
**History of the checklist in aviation**: after a series of Boeing B-17 crashes in 1935, the USAAF introduced the "pilot's checklist" for engine start-up. Pilot fatalities fell by 60% within 18 months. In the wood industry, the pre-start checklist became permanent after Machinery Directive 89/392/EEC (1989), amended by 2006/42/EC.
:::

### Five points: from physical to spatial

::: warning
**The order of points is not accidental. You start with what you hold in your hand (guard), you end with what you see in the hall (surroundings). You do not change this.**
:::

**Point 1: Guards**

- **What you check**: fixed guard (motor housing, flywheel cover), movable guard (hood over the band, flap over the disc), adjustable guard (band guide, riving knife).
- **How you check**: visually and by touch. All bolts tightened. Hood closed. Riving knife set 3-8 mm behind the disc, without play.
- **Common mistake**: "I'll make just one cut, why close the hood". The hood stops the kickback fragment before it reaches you. Without the hood, the fragment flies at your face at 30-40 m/s.

**Point 2: Weld / blade**

- **Band saw**: you check the **band weld** (the place where the band loop was welded together). Visible as a darker transverse line. It must be **straight, without cracks, without delamination**. A cracked weld = the band will tear under rotation and fly out in the shape of a spring at 25-30 m/s.
- **Circular saw**: you check the **disc**. No cracks, no broken teeth, correct diameter (matching the housing). A disc with three broken teeth next to each other = imbalance, it will crack after 20-40 minutes of work.
- **How you check**: visually at close range (10-20 cm), under good lighting (500 lux or a phone flashlight). With a gloved finger (anti-vibration glove, not bare – splinters!) along the edge of the blade.

**Point 3: Dust extraction**

- **What you check**: extraction duct connected to the machine without gaps, bag / cyclone / filter not filled above 70%. Damper open.
- **How you check**: visually (duct in its place), with a manometer (if there is one) or by hand (suction noticeable 10 cm from the inlet).
- **Common mistake**: "the extractor pulls weakly anyway, it has always been like that". Weak extraction = dust settles on the floor and bearings. Oak and beech are IARC group 1 (carcinogen). Wood dust plus a spark = ignition in 150 ms.

**Point 4: Idle run**

- **What you check**: start the machine **without load**, let it run for 30-60 seconds, **listen**.
- **What you hear correctly**: a steady, even motor sound. The band sings quietly on the wheels. The disc rotates without sideways "wobble".
- **What you hear incorrectly**: bearing knocking (dry bearing, it will seize any moment), band whistle (tension too high or too low), disc vibration (imbalance, crack). Alarm sounds signal a failure **before** you start cutting.
- **Common mistake**: "the machine always sounds like that, it's normal". It is not normal. You report it to the foreman.

**Point 5: Clear surroundings**

- **What you check**: **two metres around the machine** and **the entire kickback corridor** (6-10 metres behind the machine). No people, no materials, no tools on the floor.
- **How you check**: you turn 360°, you look. If someone is in the zone, **you say loudly "watch out, starting up"**, you wait for confirmation by gesture or word.
- **Common mistake**: "I see my colleague out of the corner of my eye, he probably knows I'm starting". Out of the corner of your eye is not eye contact. Eye contact is the one you learned about in M1 Week 1 lesson 3.

::: example
**Hai's case (fifth day of M2)**: Hai has ticked four points and is moving to the fifth. He hears the foreman behind him talking to a supplier. Hai turns around: the supplier is standing **in the kickback corridor, 4 metres behind the machine**. Hai does not press START. He calls the foreman with the word "foreman, the zone!". The foreman apologetically walks the supplier out of the zone, returns, nods. Now Hai ticks the fifth point. **5 out of 5**. He presses START.
:::

### The "5 out of 5 or zero" rule

::: warning
**There is no "4 out of 5 is enough" option.** There is no "I'll check the fifth while running" option. There is no "I know the guard is OK because it was yesterday" option. Five points means five independent checks. If any one of them is "not OK" or "I don't know", **you stop before START and you go fetch the foreman**.
:::

This is the heaviest trap of the operator's second month. After 20-30 days the checklist becomes a rhythm, the hand reaches for the sheet by itself. That is when the shortcut begins: "4 out of 5, the fifth I can see is fine, I'm starting". Six months later: "3 out of 5, the last two are always OK". A year later: an accident.

The defence against the shortcut is one: **each point requires physical confirmation**. Touch the bolt. Run your finger along the weld. Place your palm on the extraction duct. Listen to the idle run to the end of the 30 seconds (not "sounds fine after 3 seconds"). Turn 360°. Tick it off. Then you press START.

### Closing the Week 1 ritual

Week 1 of M2 built the **check – start – report** ritual from three layers:

- **Lesson 2 (PPE)**: **check yourself** before you reach the machine.
- **Lesson 3 (E-stop)**: **check STOP** before you touch START.
- **Lesson 4 (checklist)**: **check the machine** in five points before you start it.

After these three layers of checking comes **start**. That is exactly how a junior operator works in a Polish sawmill: you go from helper to operator in a month, from "I press START" to the full 3 × check ritual in a week.

The "report" step we return to in lesson 6 (fault reporting) and lesson 8 (first aid). Week 2 of M2 will introduce the second ritual: **cut with the guide, not with the hand**.

## Check yourself

**Question 1.** You have ticked 4 out of 5 checklist points. The fifth (clear surroundings) you did not check carefully, because "my colleague probably walked away". What do you do?

A) I start the machine, because 4 out of 5 is almost everything.
B) I tick off the fifth point "on trust".
C) **I do not start the machine. I turn 360°, I check visually, if anyone is in the zone I call out. Without confirmation I do not press START.**
D) I press START and rely on reflex, that I will stop the band with the E-stop if anything happens.

**Question 2.** You are at the band saw. While checking the idle run you hear bearing knocking. The machine still works, but the sound is troubling. What do you do?

A) I tick off the point, because the machine is spinning.
B) I wait until the knocking goes away on its own.
C) **I do not tick off the point. I stop the machine, I report to the foreman, I do not begin work. Bearing knocking = dry bearing = failure before the end of the shift.**
D) I increase the band tension, because that helps.

**Question 3.** Which order of checks is correct?

A) Surroundings → guards → extraction → idle run → weld.
B) **Guards → weld/blade → extraction → idle run → surroundings** (from physical to spatial).
C) Idle run → guards → weld → extraction → surroundings.
D) Any order is OK, what matters is that you tick off five.

**Reflection**: Tonight, before you leave the sawmill, pull your checklist sheet out of your pocket and check **how many times today you ticked off a point without physically checking it**. The first few days it is normal that filling it out takes 3-4 minutes and you cut corners. But awareness of the shortcut is the beginning of its elimination. In a month the sheet will no longer be necessary, because the five points will be in your head in a muscular sequence.

## Link to practice

**Tomorrow morning in the sawmill:**

1. **Take a sheet** (the foreman has a stock) or print a template with five boxes. Keep it in the pocket of your overalls. At every start-up (at least 3-5 times during a shift at the saw) tick off the five points. Write on the back: how many times today you really checked all five.
2. **Ask the foreman** whether there exists in the workplace a **written workstation instruction** for your saw (the pre-start section). If so, compare the five universal points with the company list. The company may have additional ones (e.g. a bearing lubrication test) or more detailed requirements.
3. **Meet Hai** inside your own head: on the fourth day instead of the fifth, on the third instead of the tenth. Try out the ritual **with the sheet for 20-30 days**. Only then move the checklist "into your head". Skipping the paper stage (from day one without a sheet) = guaranteed shortcuts by the third week.

## Trainer notes

**Lesson accents:**

- The third step of the **check – start – report** ritual. Together with lessons 2 and 3, it closes "check" in three layers (yourself, STOP, machine). Emphasise: the checklist is not an addition to the ritual, but **its thickest floor**.
- Hai closes the arc of the week. In the pilot he was "first time alone". In l2 he dressed like an operator. In l3 he placed his hand on STOP. In l4 he holds the sheet and stops the machine before the 5th point because the supplier entered the zone. **This is a complete junior M2 operator after a week.**
- The order of the 5 points is not negotiable. Pedagogically, you sell it as **"from physical to spatial"**: first what you hold in your hand (guard), at the end what you see in the hall (surroundings). This order has its basis in ergonomics research: hand closer to the head, eye further. A person more easily focuses attention first on close objects.

**Migrant traps:**

- In many countries (Vietnam, Moldova, Georgia) a pre-start checklist is rare in craft sawmills. The operator learns "by eye". Transferred to a Polish plant (compliant with 2006/42/EC) he treats the checklist as bureaucracy. Show him the PIP statistic: 91% of accidents are the skipping of one of the 5 points. Not "bad luck" – skipping.
- The "5 out of 5 or zero" rule sounds like an ultimatum. Explain that it is not a threat, but **mathematics**. 4 out of 5 = 20% of risk unaddressed. With 5 start-ups a day × 5 days a week × 50 weeks = 1250 start-ups a year × 20% = **250 times a year we play roulette with one unchecked element**. Sooner or later the element comes up.

**Link to M1 and M2:**

- M1 Week 1 lesson 2 (preparing the workstation) is the **helper's** checklist (6 points: PPE, cleanliness, walkways, lighting, tools, main switch). The operator in M2 l4 has his own **operator's checklist** (5 points on the machine). These are two different lists, two different layers of work. Show them side by side.
- M1 Week 1 lesson 3 (communication by gestures) returns in point 5 (clear surroundings): if anyone is in the zone, you use the "watch out" command or the gesture from M1.
- M2 lesson 3 (E-stop) and M2 lesson 2 (PPE) are a **prerequisite** for l4. The checklist does not work without PPE and without a functional STOP.

**Hard questions:**

- "What if the foreman orders me to start the machine, and I see that one of the 5 points is 'not OK'?" You have the right to refuse on the basis of Art. 210 § 1 of the Polish Labour Code. You say: "point X is 'not OK', I'm reporting it, I'm waiting for the fault to be removed". A foreman who applies pressure **is breaking the law** and can be reported to PIP (Polish Labour Inspectorate).
- "How many minutes does the checklist take in practice?" First week: 3-4 minutes. Second week: 2 minutes. After a month: 90-120 seconds with the sheet, 60-90 seconds without the sheet. Longer than 5 minutes: learning has stalled, go back to one point at a time.
- "Can I fill out the checklist once a day, at the start of the shift, instead of at every start-up?" No. The state of the machine changes during the shift (the disc dulls, dust builds up in the filter, someone may enter the zone). Checklist at **every** start-up.

- A print run of 50 A5 "Pre-start saw checklist" sheets (5 checkbox fields, space for signature and time) to hand out to every participant.
- An A3 poster with the 5 points on the wall next to every saw in the workplace.
- PIP 2020-2024 statistics on the 5 main accident categories as a table on a slide.
- A list of 5 saws in the workplace with written workstation instructions (if they exist) for comparison against the universal list of 5 points.
