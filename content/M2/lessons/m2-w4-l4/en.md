## Introduction

Thursday, 21.04.2026, 7:05. Andrij is finishing the morning briefing with Marek in the foreman's office. Cutting disposition DP-2026-045 from yesterday is on the board: pedunculate oak C24 FSC, 60 pieces to cut by the end of the week. The raw material is to come from the FSC field (field no. 4 on the yard, fenced, sign "FSC 100% – no mixing"). Marek wraps up briefly: "Andrij, now you go with Krzysiek for the oak package from the FSC field. You don't move the forklift yourself, you don't get near the forks, you don't stand behind the forklift when it reverses. Krzysiek will show you the signals. You listen to him, he has UDT permits, I don't. When you come back, he'll set the package down for you at saw 2. Then you open it and cut".

Andrij goes out into the yard. At 7:10 a **Toyota 8FG25** forklift pulls up (load capacity 2500 kg, LPG propane-butane, 2022). Behind the wheel is **Krzysztof Zagrodny**: 48 years old, 20 years at EGIDA, forklift operator with UDT permits category I WJO (powered lift trucks with a seated operator). Work shirt with EGIDA logo, reflective vest, helmet, safety-toe boots. Krzysztof stops the forklift **three meters** in front of Andrij, lowers the window, waves. "Andrij, come up from the side, not from the front and not from the rear: always from the side, so I see you in the mirror and you see me through the window. Today I'll show you how we work together. Marek said this is your first time around a forklift as an operator, not a helper, so now you don't just carry things, now you also **give and receive signals**. Let's start with three things: how to position yourself, how to show me you're ready, and when I should stop".

This lesson does not teach you to drive a forklift. For that you need a UDT course (35 hours + exam, 5-year validity). It teaches you to **work together** with a forklift operator when he delivers raw material or picks up a finished batch. A crew operator does not drive the forklift, but **every day is 2–3 m from a moving forklift**, receives packages, hands batches over, directs the maneuver when the forklift reverses to a workstation. Poor coordination between the crew operator and the forklift driver is **the most frequent cause of fatal accidents in a sawmill**. According to PIP data (2019–2023), 22% of accidents involving forklifts in sawmills are pedestrian strikes or crushings, and 60% of those victims were machine operators at their workstations.

## Objectives

After this lesson:

1. You know the **eye-contact rule**: you do not approach a forklift that is moving or whose engine is running until the forklift operator has seen you in a mirror or through the glass and confirmed with a gesture ("I see you, wait" or "I see you, come up"). Your eye contact = your safety, not the operator's duty.
2. You know the **3 m maneuver zone** around a moving forklift: the forklift stops at least 3 m before the operator or the package, the operator never enters this zone until the forklift is on the parking brake (signal: the operator raises a hand, "stop, I'm standing"). The zone covers the entire length of the forklift plus the forks plus the package on the forks.
3. You know the **three standard gestures** per the industry OSH norm (based on PN-ISO 7010 and the 2000 Minister of Economy OSH regulation on the operation of powered trucks): **STOP** (open hand raised above the head, fingers together), **REVERSE** (arm bent at the elbow, hand vertical at chest level, moving toward yourself, slowly), **READY / RECEIVING** (hands crossed in front of the chest in an X for 1–2 seconds, then both hands down). Every gesture clear, 2–3 seconds, directly in front of the cab.
4. You know the **no-go rule between machine and load**: you never stand between the forklift and a wall, between the forklift and a stack, between two forklifts. "Blind" spots are places where the forklift operator will not see you in time, even in the mirror. If you must cross from one side of the forklift to the other, you go around from the front at a visible distance of 3 m, not from behind.
5. You know **package weight vs. forklift load capacity**: the package you hand to the forklift cannot exceed the forklift's load capacity at that position (the forklift has different capacity depending on lift height, shown on the nameplate on the mast upright). A crew operator can roughly estimate package weight (a pine board 45 × 150 × 3000 mm = 13–14 kg, a package of 100 pieces = 1300–1400 kg; an oak package 50 × 200 × 3000 mm = 28 kg/piece, 60 pieces = 1680 kg) and compares it with the forklift's capacity before signaling "lift".

## Content

::: info
**The forklift is the biggest OSH risk in a sawmill.** PIP statistics 2019–2023 for the wood-processing sector: 312 serious accidents and 41 fatal accidents involving forklifts. Three dominant scenarios: (1) pedestrian strike of an operator standing at the workstation, 48% (the forklift reverses, the operator doesn't hear the backup buzzer because the saw at 90 dB drowns out the signal), (2) crushing by a package sliding off the forks, 22%, (3) running over the foot of an operator standing too close to the drive wheels, 18%. The only defense is iron discipline on three rules: eye contact, 3 m zone, standard gestures.
:::

### Eye contact and the 3 m maneuver zone

**The eye-contact rule** is simple: **you do not exist for the forklift operator until he has seen you in a mirror or through the cab glass and confirmed with a hand movement**. Merely "standing next to the forklift" does not count. While driving, the forklift operator watches the package, the route, the side and rear mirrors: his direct field of view in front of the forklift's hood is about 5 m straight ahead, but with a package on the forks the field shrinks to 2 m (because the package blocks the front window of the cab). Krzysztof's Toyota 8FG25 has two side mirrors and one rear mirror, plus a reversing camera on a screen in the cab. Even so, blind zones remain: **directly behind the forklift 2 m, directly in front of the forklift under load 1.5 m**.

In practice:

- You see a forklift in motion: **stand still**. Don't wave, don't shout, don't try to "chase" the forklift, don't run in front of the cab.
- Wait until the operator looks your way. He normally checks his mirrors every 2–4 seconds, so the wait is at most 5 s.
- When the operator sees you, **he raises a hand to the window or nods his head**: that is the "I see you" signal. From that moment you are on his mental map of the situation.
- Only now can you **signal what you want**: come up for instructions, receive a package, hand over a batch to be taken away.

**The 3 m maneuver zone** is the minimum distance at which the forklift stops in front of the operator or the package before making the final precise movement (lowering, reversing to a stack, positioning at a workstation). Three meters is not a "polite distance"; it is the calculated braking distance of a forklift with a 1500 kg package moving at 5 km/h on a concrete hall floor: 2.1–2.4 m + safety buffer. If you stand closer than 3 m from the front of a moving forklift, an emergency brake will still cause contact. Krzysztof brakes earlier (4–5 m), but not always: if the package is unevenly balanced, shorter braking means the package falls off the forks. That is why **3 m is the standard for both sides**.

### Three standard gestures

There are three gestures, and that is a deliberate industry choice. More than three and people confuse them, fewer than three and you lack the basic "ready". Every gesture has **three common features**: performed with an extended arm at chest height or above, held for 2–3 seconds (not waving), performed directly in front of the forklift cab, not from the side or behind.

**STOP**: open hand raised above the head, fingers together and straight, palm facing the cab. One upward movement, held for 3 seconds. Meaning: "stop the forklift immediately, something is wrong". You use it when: you see an obstacle on the forklift's route, you see the package wobbling, you see someone entering the maneuver zone, you yourself tripped and need a moment to stand still. STOP is a **master** gesture: anyone in the hall, not only the operator at the destination workstation, can use it, and the forklift operator must stop the forklift before asking why. Never shout "STOP!" instead of the gesture: saw noise drowns out the voice, the gesture is always visible.

**REVERSE**: arm bent at the elbow at 90 degrees, hand vertical (fingers pointing up), movement toward yourself that bends the elbow, slowly, 2–3 times. Meaning: "reverse the forklift toward me, the path is clear". You use it when the forklift operator is to reverse the truck to a loading station and you are standing on the station side and can see that nothing obstructs the area behind the forklift (no packages, no other forklift, no person passing). You are responsible **for the reversing path being clear**: if you give the REVERSE signal, you have checked the space behind the forklift with your own eyes, you do not "assume it's empty". A mistake means the forklift hits a stack or a person, and it is **your** pedagogical responsibility even if formal legal responsibility rests with the operator.

**READY / RECEIVING**: both hands crossed in front of the chest in the shape of the letter X for 1–2 seconds, then both hands lowered along the torso. Meaning: "package is positioned, the forklift can leave" or "position is good, don't move any more". You use it when the forklift has set the package at the workstation and is waiting for confirmation that it stands well (won't shift, doesn't hit the saw's legs, is level). Alternatively it means: "I'm receiving the package, I'll start handling it now, you can go for the next one". **Without the READY gesture the forklift operator does not leave**: it is the agreement with Krzysztof and with all EGIDA forklift operators that the forklift stands with the package until the operator confirms readiness.

### No-go rule between machine and load

This is the simplest rule of week 4 and the most often broken. **You never stand between the forklift and anything solid**: a hall wall, a stack on the yard, another forklift, a saw, an entrance gate. The "blind" spot between the forklift and an object is the spot where **the forklift operator has nowhere to pull back in an emergency**. If the operator hits the brake and the package slides off the forks, the package goes toward the object behind you, and you are in the way. If the operator brakes hard and the forklift shifts by 30 cm, you have a 30 cm buffer instead of 3 m.

Three situations in which operators fall into this trap:

- **"I'll just grab it from the ground"**: a board has slid off the forks, the operator bends between the forklift and the stack. No. You step back, give STOP, the forklift leaves, and only then you pick it up.
- **"I'll help position the package"**: the operator grabs the package from the side, trying to correct its position. No. The forks move 50–100 mm even during lowering; a hand on the package at the moment the forks move means crushing. The forklift operator corrects the position; you give STOP or READY.
- **"He'll see me"**: the operator assumes eye contact is constant. No. The forklift operator looks at different places every 2–4 seconds; between "he sees me" and "he looks again" up to 5 seconds pass, and in that window he does not see you at all.

::: warning
**The three most common crew-operator mistakes when working with a forklift.** Mistake one: **approaching without eye contact**. The operator sees the forklift coming from a distance, walks toward it to "meet halfway". The forklift operator, focused on the package, does not notice the operator entering the 3 m zone. Rule: **the forklift's first step toward the workstation = your last step toward it**. You stand and wait. Mistake two: **using non-standard gestures**. The operator nods his head, makes circles with a finger, shows "OK" (thumbs up), calls "good, good". The forklift operator interprets it ambiguously: "OK" can mean "ready" or "I see you" or "understood". Only the three standard gestures (STOP, REVERSE, READY) are unambiguous between all forklift operators and crew operators at EGIDA. Other gestures mean interpretation risk. Mistake three: **crossing the 3 m zone with a "small" request**. The operator enters the zone to ask "which saw?", "which side do I stand on?". No. You ask questions when the forklift is on the parking brake, engine at idle, the operator has opened the window and looked at you. Until that moment you stand 3 m away and wait. Every zone crossing is a new risk point in the PIP statistics.
:::

### Package weight vs. forklift load capacity

Krzysztof has a **Toyota 8FG25**: the "25" in the designation is a nominal capacity of 2500 kg with the package's center of gravity 500 mm from the fork face. But capacity **drops with lift height**. The nameplate on the mast upright shows the capacity curve: 2500 kg up to 3.3 m height, 2000 kg up to 4.5 m, 1600 kg up to 5.5 m. If a package weighs 1800 kg and the forklift has to lift it to 5.5 m onto a tall stack, **it exceeds the capacity, the forklift will tip or the mast will be damaged**. That is why before every lift the operator checks two things: the package weight (roughly) and the target height.

The crew operator learns to estimate package weight. **Volumetric rule** (the most frequent cases at EGIDA):

- **Pine/spruce (dry, 15–20% moisture)**: density 450–500 kg/m³. A package of 100 boards 45 × 150 × 3000 mm ≈ 1000 kg. Fresh (moisture 40–60%) is 40–60% heavier for the same volume: this is the first trap because "it looks the same".
- **Oak (dry)**: density 680–750 kg/m³. A package of 60 boards 50 × 200 × 3000 mm ≈ 1200–1330 kg. Fresh oak (1000–1100 kg/m³) for the same batch = 1800–2000 kg.

Before the READY signal the operator checks the **package label** (at EGIDA the weight is written next to the batch code, e.g. "SO-A-180420-012 – 980 kg"). If the label is illegible, he asks the forklift operator. Only then does he know whether the package fits within the capacity at **that height** which the forklift must reach.

**Disposition DP-2026-045**: 60 oak pieces 50 × 200 × 3000 mm. If the wood is fresh (moisture 30–40%, just arrived from the forest), the package weighs 1800 kg. The Toyota 8FG25's capacity at a lift height of 3.3 m is 2500 kg: 28% margin. Safe.

### Forklift operator permits and scope of responsibility

Krzysztof has a **UDT permit category I WJO** (powered lift trucks with a seated operator): 35-hour course + exam, 5-year validity, renewed each cycle. A plate with the permit number hangs in the forklift cab; the list of all forklift operators with expiration dates is updated monthly by HR on the board in the foremen's room. Legal responsibility for the maneuver rests with the forklift operator (paperwork, decision on speed), but the crew operator is **co-responsible** for his own position and for the signals he gives. In a PIP report on a "pedestrian strike in a blind zone" accident, the prosecutor assigns 20–40% of the fault to the operator, even if the forklift driver was at the wheel. Pedagogical rule: **the forklift operator has the paper, you have the legs**. Another person's formal responsibility does not protect you from a physical outcome; what protects you is behavior according to the 3 m zone and the three gestures.

::: tip
**Where to check whether the forklift operator has current permits.** Two sources: the placard in the forklift cab (photocopy of the permit + expiration date, on every EGIDA forklift) and the board in the foremen's room (list of forklift operators, monthly update). If you see someone at the wheel without a placard or not on the list, you signal STOP and call the foreman. A forklift operator without permits means uninsured operation; in the event of an accident the sawmill pays the treatment costs.
:::

::: example
**Case Andrij + Krzysztof: first oak package from the FSC field.** 7:15, yard. Krzysztof briefs: "I'm going to the FSC field for the oak package, 60 beams, roughly 1500–1700 kg. You stand here, on the ZP-2 marking (loading zone at saw 2). I'll pull up in reverse and stop 3 m from you. You wait until I see you in the mirror and raise my hand. Then REVERSE: arm bent, hand vertical, moving toward yourself. I reverse along the yellow line. When the package lands, look whether it stands straight and whether the spacers are intact. If everything is fine, READY. If something is off, STOP. Rule: if in doubt, STOP. One unnecessary STOP is better than one missing STOP when it was needed". At 7:21 Krzysztof returns with package SO-FSC-210420-003 (1670 kg, labeled). He passes Andrij at a distance of 8 m, stops at 4 m, looks in the rear mirror, raises a hand. Andrij gives REVERSE: three movements toward himself. Krzysztof reverses slowly along the yellow line, 3 km/h, in stages: two meters, stop, one meter, stop, 50 cm. The package sits on the ZP-2 line, spacers intact. READY: hands crossed in an X, 2 seconds, then down. Krzysztof raises the forks out of the package, backs off by a meter, waves from the cab and drives away. The package stands straight. First joint maneuver: 3 minutes, no incident, by the three gestures.
:::

## Key terms

Po polsku / In English / En español / Українською:

- **Kontakt wzrokowy z wózkowym / Eye contact with forklift operator / Contacto visual con el operador de la carretilla / Візуальний контакт з оператором навантажувача**: a mandatory gesture confirmation that the forklift operator sees you; without confirmation you do not "exist" for him in the safety sense.
- **Strefa manewru 3 m / 3 m maneuver zone / Zona de maniobra de 3 m / Зона маневру 3 м**: the minimum distance from a moving forklift; covers the length of the forklift plus the forks plus the package; you must not enter the zone until the forklift is on the parking brake.
- **Gest STOP / STOP gesture / Gesto de ALTO / Жест СТОП**: open hand raised above the head; a one-sided signal to stop the forklift immediately; anyone in the hall may use it, and the forklift operator must stop without discussion.
- **Gest COFAJ / REVERSE gesture / Gesto de RETROCEDER / Жест НАЗАД**: arm bent, hand vertical, movement toward yourself; the signal "the path behind the forklift is clear, you may reverse"; responsibility for the clearness of the reversing path lies with the person giving the signal.
- **Gest GOTOWE / READY gesture / Gesto de LISTO / Жест ГОТОВО**: both hands crossed in an X in front of the chest; confirmation "the package is set correctly, the forklift may leave"; without this gesture the forklift operator does not leave the station.
- **Udźwig wózka / Forklift load capacity / Capacidad de carga de la carretilla / Вантажопідйомність навантажувача**: the maximum weight the forklift can lift to a given height; drops with lift height; nameplate on the mast upright; the operator estimates the package weight before the READY signal.
- **Uprawnienia UDT kategoria I WJO / UDT permit category I WJO / Permiso UDT categoría I WJO / Дозвіл UDT категорія I WJO**: a permit from UDT (Polish Office of Technical Inspection) to operate powered lift trucks with a seated operator; 35-hour course + exam; 5-year validity; the crew operator checks whether the forklift operator has current permits (foremen's board + cab placard).

## Link to practice

At the training site you will do three exercises in the classroom (no real forklift, simulation only). First: **standard gestures**. The trainer stands 3 m from you, plays the forklift operator role, gives you 12 short scenes ("forklift is moving", "package is wobbling", "package is placed correctly", "a board fell on the reversing path", "another operator enters the zone"). Your task is to show the correct gesture in each scene (STOP / REVERSE / READY / no gesture) within 2 seconds. Pass threshold: 10 of 12 correct. Second: **package-weight estimation**. You receive 8 cards describing packages (species, board dimensions, count, moisture) and 8 cards with weights. You must match them in pairs within a tolerance of plus/minus 15%. Threshold: 6 of 8. Third: **recognizing 3 m zone violations**. You watch 5 short clips (30 s each) from real sawmill incidents (anonymized, from PIP). In each you point out where the operator breached the zone and which gesture should have been used. Threshold: 4 of 5.

## Trainer notes

This lesson is **the first lesson of Block 2 T4** and introduces the narrative of **relationships between people** (after Block 1 "documents in the sawmill"). Andrij meets Krzysztof for the first time: a fourth type of senior figure (after Tadeusz the operator, Marek the foreman, Zbigniew the drying-kiln master). Krzysztof represents **formal competence in a different trade**: not a saw operator but a forklift operator, with his own UDT paper and his own permit hierarchy for categories I/II/III. This matters pedagogically: the crew operator learns that a sawmill has **different trades with different papers**, not just "operators" and "helpers". Building respect for the forklift operator's competence is the key to working together without incidents.

Three typical learner difficulties in this lesson:

1. **Underestimating the 3 m zone**. Migrant learners sometimes come from cultures where "normal distance" at work is closer (East Asia, Latin America). 3 m can feel "cold" or "excessive". The trainer explains that this is **not a cultural issue** but the physics of braking a loaded forklift, and shows a clip of a 3 m breach (PIP has published training materials).
2. **Using one's own gestures**. Learners with experience from another sector (construction, commercial warehouse) know different gesture sets. The trainer shows **only the three official EGIDA gestures** and drills them repeatedly until they "enter the body", gestures made without thought, mechanically, as a reflex. In the first 2 weeks the operator performs the gestures consciously (thinks), then they become automatic.
3. **Overestimating the forklift operator's responsibility**. "Krzysiek has the paper, he's responsible, I just walk". The trainer corrects: **the forklift operator is responsible for the forklift, you are responsible for your legs and your signals**. The physical effect of an accident hits the operator, not the forklift operator: an operator cannot "rely" on another person's formal responsibility. Exercise: the trainer asks each learner to name 3 situations in which **only the operator**, not the forklift driver, could have prevented an accident.

Closing exercise (20 minutes): **full cooperation simulation**. The trainer plays the forklift operator (chair placed 3 m from the learner, simulating the cab), the learner plays the crew operator. Scenario: "deliver a package to saw 2, lift a finished-batch package for takeaway". Full cycle: eye contact to REVERSE to READY to unloading to REVERSE to LOADING the new package to READY to departure. No gesture errors, no 3 m zone breaches, no stepping between the forklift and an obstacle. Total cycle time: 5–7 minutes. Threshold: one clean cycle without trainer intervention. Failed = another day of simulation training; we never release a learner to a real forklift before he masters the gestures.
