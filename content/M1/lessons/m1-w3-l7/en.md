## Introduction

Seventh lesson of Week 3. All the previous ones were about **doing the work**. Today about what **is left after the work**: **sawdust, chips, wood scrap**. And why tidying is **NOT ungrateful helper drudgery**: but **one of the most important OHS tasks** in the whole sawmill.

Sounds overstated? Look at the facts:

**Fires in Polish sawmills 2020–2024** (data from the State Fire Service): **on average 45 fires per year**, of which **70% start with ignition of wood dust or sawdust** in contact with **an overheated machine / a spark / a cigarette**. Wood dust is a **pyrotechnic material**: a small stream of sparks is **enough to detonate the dust**, which destroys the building in **3–5 minutes**.

**Slips and falls** (PIP data): **15–20% of sawmill injuries** come from **slipping on sawdust**. Your foot slides, back on your back. In the best case a bruise. In the worst, **head hitting concrete** = concussion, coma, death.

**Moulds and allergies**: sawdust lying **for weeks in moisture** develops **mould colonies** (Aspergillus, Penicillium). Inhaling spores = **chronic asthma**, in allergic people, **acute reaction**. This is not theoretical, chronic respiratory disease in sawmill workers is **documented ZUS (social insurance) statistics**.

That is why **workstation tidiness** = **not coincidence, not drudgery**, but **the third layer of OHS** after PPE and work technique. And this is **your job** as a helper.

In this lesson you'll learn **three types of scrap** (sawdust, chips, large scrap), **three hazards** from them (fire, slip, health), **cleaning techniques** (broom, shovel, extraction, container), **five tidying moments** during the day and the rule **"whatever leaves the machine, must go somewhere"**.

## Objectives

After this lesson:

- You understand that **wood dust = a pyrotechnic material**: a small stream of sparks is enough for an explosion that **destroys the building in 5 minutes**.
- You know the **five tidying moments** during the day: end of shift, before the break, after every cutting operation, after a finished task, before changing tasks.
- You know what **wood waste fractions** are in a sawmill: **fine sawdust** (up to 1 mm), **chips** (1–20 mm), **large scrap** (pieces >20 mm), **bark**, **end cuts** (up to 50 cm).
- You can **secure the workstation against fire**: dust extraction on, no piles of sawdust under the machine, no cigarettes/open flames within 10 m.
- You know **5 tidying rules** and can apply them anywhere in the sawmill.

## Content

### Three types of wood waste

Cutting and planing produce **not one** type of waste. There are **three fractions**, each with a different hazard:

**Fraction 1: Wood dust (up to 1 mm).**

The most dangerous. Formed during **fast cutting** (disc with high rpm), during **sanding**, during **milling**. A single particle is **invisible**, but in large quantities forms a **fog** in the air. Key features:

- **Flammability**: wood dust **explodes** on contact with a spark if the concentration in the air exceeds **30–50 g/m³** (**a lower concentration** than a petrol mist). That explosion **is not a fire**: it is a **detonation**, a pressure wave of **0.5–2 bar**.
- **Health**: oak and beech wood dust is **classified as a GROUP 1 CARCINOGEN** (lung cancer) by IARC. Other species: possible carcinogen.
- **Slipperiness**: **very** slippery (like flour under the feet).

**Fraction 2: Chips (1–20 mm).**

From hand cutting, the band saw, the planer. They look like **small spirals** or **strips**. Features:

- **Flammability**: burn **slowly**, but **intensely** (lots of heat). Don't explode like dust.
- **Health**: less harmful than dust, but **splinters in the eye** are **real**.
- **Slipperiness**: **moderately** slippery, especially when damp.
- **Visibility issue**: **clog** the dust extraction, filters, pipes, you must **clean daily**.

**Fraction 3: Large scrap (>20 mm, pieces, offcuts).**

Board ends, leftovers from cutting to size, defective pieces, removed knots. Features:

- **Flammability**: burn **normally** like firewood. Don't explode.
- **Health**: low risk (only when they are **with nails / protruding screws** from old materials).
- **Tripping**: **main hazard**: you trip over a 30 cm piece, you fall.
- **Useful recycling**: **most sawmills sell it** (briquettes, fuel, chips for farmers). You don't throw it away like rubbish.

::: info
**Waste economics**: a large sawmill (100 000 m³ per year) produces **about 15 000–20 000 m³ of sawdust + 10 000 m³ of chips + 5000 m³ of large scrap**. Sale of briquettes/pellets = **additional revenue of 2–5 million zł per year**. That's why scrap is **NOT "rubbish"**: it is a **by-product**. Tidying = you don't throw into a bin, you **sort into the right place**.
:::

### Three hazards: where accidents come from

**Hazard 1: Fire and dust explosion.**

Physics: wood dust in the air **explodes** when:
- Dust concentration: **30–50 g/m³** (a visible "fog" in lamp light)
- Ignition source: a **spark** from the disc, a **spark** from an overheated bearing, an **electrical spark**, a **cigarette**
- Oxygen: normal (air)

The explosion is **fast**: **10–20 metres per second** pressure wave. Destroys windows, doors, machines. Human reaction time to an explosion = **zero**. Meaning: there is no "escape" once it starts, you are in it.

**Prevention**:
- **Dust extraction** (suction ventilation system) **ON** during machine operation.
- **Tidy up after every operation**: don't let dust accumulate on the floor, machines, shelves.
- **No smoking** within **10 metres** of the workstation. A cigarette = **a spark = explosion**. Going for a cigarette, you go to the smoking zone 20 m away.
- **Fire extinguishers** at the workstation, **CO₂** or **dry powder ABC** (not water, water disperses dust into the air, speeds up the explosion).

**Hazard 2: Slip and fall.**

Sawdust + chips under your boots = **like ice skates on ice**. Warm floor (concrete), moist dust, **friction coefficient drops from 0.6 to 0.15**. The same as walking on icy stairs.

**Prevention**:
- **Cleaning every 2–3 hours** of sawdust/chips from walkways, even if nobody asked.
- **Boots with proper soles**: EN ISO 20345 S3 (with **anti-slip rubber** SRC). Asphalt-rubber boots have worse grip.
- **Walking zones** clearly marked (floor stripes), help to avoid shortcuts through unswept areas.

**Hazard 3: Health (respiratory, allergy, cancer).**

Oak and beech dust = **IARC group 1, carcinogen** (proven carcinogenicity in humans). Pine, spruce, larch dust = **group 2B, probable carcinogen**.

Inhaling dust daily = after **20–30 years** you increase the risk of **nasopharyngeal cancer** **8-fold** compared to the general population.

**Prevention**:
- **P2 or P3 half-mask** (FFP2/FFP3), ALWAYS when cleaning dust or during dust-generating work.
- **Dust extraction** (stationary), extracts particles before they reach your lungs.
- **Safety glasses**: even the smallest splinter in the eye = **a serious corneal injury**.
- **Work clothes changed** after each shift (dust settles in the fabric, you inhale at home).

### Five tidying moments during the day

Tidying is **not** "at the end of the day". It is **during the day**, at five moments:

**Moment 1: End of shift (mandatory, 15–30 min).**

The last 15–30 minutes of the shift (typically 15:30–16:00) = **tidy up the workstation**. All sawdust collected, large scrap taken to the container, tools in their places, PPE in theirs. The next shift (if any) **comes to a clean workstation**.

**Moment 2: Before the break (5–10 min).**

Before the 30-minute lunch break (e.g. 12:30), **quick sweep** of the main workstation. Why? Because during the break **nobody is watching**, and if a fire starts, **there's no one to react**. A clean workstation = a safe break.

**Moment 3: After every cutting operation (2–3 min).**

You cut a large log, 15 boards came out. **Don't move to the next log** without **quickly** collecting the large scrap (end cuts). Takes **1–2 minutes**, but **prevents** the scrap from piling up for an hour and **you tripping**.

**Moment 4: After a finished task (5–10 min).**

You finished stacking. **Before moving to a new task**: **sweep the stack area**. There remains sawdust under the stack, fallen boards, unused stickers. A new task without tidying = **you repeat the mistake**.

**Moment 5: Before changing tasks (3–5 min).**

The foreman says: "leave the sorting, go to unloading". **You don't leave a mess** at the sorting station, you **precede** the new task with a quick tidying of the previous one.

::: tip
**The "5S" rule**: a Japanese method (Sort, Set in order, Shine, Standardize, Sustain), widely used in Polish industry. "Shine" (clean, wipe down) is part of **the daily routine**, not "special circumstances". A helper at a sawmill using 5S, **always** tidies between tasks.
:::

### Cleaning techniques: from dust to container

**Fine dust:**

- **Industrial vacuum cleaner with HEPA filter**: ideal, but 500–5000 zł. Large sawmills have them.
- **Dust extraction (stationary)**: built into the building. Switches on with the machine. You **don't operate it**: it works automatically.
- **Do NOT sweep dust with a broom!** It **stirs** the dust into the air, **increases** the risk of explosion and inhalation. **Forbidden!**
- If you must collect residual dust, **a damp cloth** (dust settles) or a **special antistatic broom** (captures dust, doesn't stir).

**Chips:**

- **Broom** with stiff bristles (not feathers), you collect into a pile.
- **Shovel**: you transfer the pile to the **sawdust/chips container**.
- **Aluminium scoop**: for larger chips, faster than a shovel.

**Large scrap:**

- **Hand in a glove**: you grab a piece, throw into the **wood-waste container**.
- **Transport cart**: if you tidy up after cutting a larger batch (50+ pieces).

**Containers:**

- **Sawdust + chips**: **one container** (extraction for briquettes).
- **Large scrap**: **second container** (extraction for fuel).
- **Bark**: **third container** (extraction for garden mulch).
- **Metal scrap** (nails, screws from scrap beams), **a special container** ("metal"). These things **do not go into briquettes**: **they destroy the mill**.

::: warning
**Forbidden in sawmill waste**: paints, glues, plastics, glass, chemicals. These substances ruin the **briquetting process** or **poison** the compost. If you find a tin of paint in waste, **go to the foreman**, don't throw it in. Could have come from a building refurbishment.
:::

### Five tidying rules: concise

**1. "Whatever leaves the machine, must go somewhere."** It doesn't stay under the machine. **During the day**, not "at the end".

**2. "Sweep dust with a damp cloth, not a broom."** A dry broom = you stir it into the air = you inhale + risk of explosion.

**3. "Before changing tasks, tidy the previous one."** You don't leave a messed-up area.

**4. "Metal does not enter the wood container."** A nail in the briquetter = broken machine, cost 20 000 zł.

**5. "If you see someone smoking in the production zone, call the foreman."** Don't fight personally (conflict), but **don't ignore it**. Fire from a cigarette = a catastrophe.

### When fire breaks out anyway

Despite tidying, a fire can happen (a spark from the disc, an electrical short, self-ignition of overheated dust). You have **10 seconds to react** before a small fire becomes a **hall fire**:

**Seconds 1–3**: **Shout "FIRE!"** and the **STOP gesture** towards the nearest machine. Workers react.

**Seconds 3–6**: **Fire extinguisher** (powder ABC or CO₂), **aim at the base of the fire**, not at the top. Direct the stream **at the base of the flames**.

**Seconds 6–10**: If the fire **doesn't go out** after one extinguisher, **evacuate**, don't fight. A dust fire spreads **in seconds**. Your extinguisher = one attempt. If it's not enough, **you flee**, **you shout** to everyone, **you call the Fire Service (112)**.

Evacuation details are in **M1-W1-L8** (evacuation and the STOP system). Here enough: **tidying is prevention, the extinguisher is a last resort, evacuation has priority over fighting**.

## Summary

Today you understood **why tidying is OHS**:

- **Three types of waste**: dust (up to 1 mm, explosive, carcinogen), chips (1–20 mm, flammable, clog), large scrap (>20 mm, trip).
- **Three hazards**: **fire/explosion** (wood dust detonates at 30–50 g/m³), **slip** (15–20% of injuries from sawdust), **health** (nasopharyngeal cancer from oak/beech dust, IARC group 1).
- **Five tidying moments**: end of shift, before break, after every operation, after a task, before changing tasks. **Not "at the end"**.
- **Techniques**: dust = extraction / damp cloth (NOT broom!), chips = broom + shovel, large scrap = hand + container. **Segregation**: sawdust/chips, large scrap, bark, metal, **4 separate containers**.
- **Five rules**: what comes out → goes somewhere. Dust = damp. Before change → tidy. Metal ≠ wood. Smoker → foreman.
- **10-second reaction** to fire: shout + STOP → extinguisher to the base → evacuation.

Tidying **is not a punishment**, **is not drudgery**. It is the **third layer of OHS** after PPE and technique. A sawmill that **doesn't tidy**: burns down **in 5 minutes**. A sawmill that **tidies**: stands **30 years**. The difference: **your broom**.

## Key terms

- 💥 **Wybuch pyłu drzewnego** (pl), wood dust explosion (en), explosión de polvo de madera (es), вибух деревного пилу (uk). Detonation of dust at 30–50 g/m³ concentration with a spark. Wave 0.5–2 bar, destroys the building in 5 minutes. 70% of sawmill fires in Poland.

- 🫁 **Pył dębowy/bukowy, kancerogen IARC 1** (pl), oak/beech dust, IARC group 1 carcinogen (en), polvo de roble/haya, cancerígeno IARC 1 (es), дубовий/буковий пил, канцероген IARC 1 (uk). Proven nasopharyngeal cancer after 20–30 years of exposure. Mandatory P2/P3 half-mask when working with these species.

- 🧹 **Odciąg pyłu** (pl), dust extraction (en), aspiración de polvo (es), пиловідвід (uk). Suction installation, captures dust at the source (machine). Switches on with the machine. A safety condition against explosion.

- 💨 **Pył / wióry / odpad duży** (pl), dust / chips / large scrap (en), polvo / virutas / desecho grande (es), пил / стружка / великий відхід (uk). Three fractions of wood waste. Each goes to a **separate** container. Each has a different hazard.

- 🦺 **Półmaska P2/P3 (FFP2/FFP3)** (pl), P2/P3 half-mask (FFP2/FFP3) (en), mascarilla P2/P3 (es), півмаска P2/P3 (uk). Filters dust particles. Mandatory when working with oak/beech dust, when cleaning dust.

- 🔥 **Zasady pożarowe, strefa 10 m bez iskier** (pl), 10 m spark-free zone (en), zona sin chispas de 10 m (es), зона 10 м без іскор (uk). Within 10 m of a dust-generating machine: no cigarettes, no open flames, no sparks. A dust explosion = ignition without borders.

- 🛁 **Mokra szmata zamiast miotły** (pl), wet cloth instead of broom (en), trapo húmedo en lugar de escoba (es), мокра ганчірка замість мітли (uk). Dust cleaning rule: a dry broom stirs dust into the air (explosion + inhalation). A damp cloth captures dust.

- ♻️ **Segregacja odpadu drzewnego** (pl), wood waste segregation (en), segregación del residuo (es), сортування відходу (uk). 4 containers: sawdust/chips (briquettes), large scrap (fuel), bark (mulch), metal scrap (separate, so as not to destroy the mill).

- 🧯 **Gaśnica ABC (proszkowa) / CO2** (pl), ABC powder / CO₂ fire extinguisher (en), extintor ABC / CO₂ (es), вогнегасник ABC / CO₂ (uk). The only appropriate for wood dust. NEVER water, water disperses the dust, speeds up the explosion.

- 🎯 **Pięć momentów porządku** (pl), five tidying moments (en), cinco momentos de orden (es), п'ять моментів порядку (uk). End of shift, before break, after operation, after task, before changing tasks. "Not at the end", but **during the day**.

## Trainer's notes

**Pedagogical goal**: to **change the perception** of tidying from "ungrateful helper work" to "a key OHS task". After the lesson the learner should **willingly** tidy up, **understand why**, and **react** to a dirty floor as to no hard hat, as a serious OHS breach.

**120-minute rhythm**:
- **0–20 min**: opening, **demonstration "little dust experiment"**. The trainer takes a handful of sawdust, **releases it in the air** over a lit candle (at a safe distance, with an extinguisher ready). **The dust reaction**: a flash, sometimes a small explosion. Learners **see** the hazard. "Imagine this in the building."
- **20–40 min**: **three types of waste**: samples on the table (a bag of dust, a bag of chips, pieces of large scrap). Learners **touch**, assess slipperiness on tile/concrete. "Which slips more?", they try, they'll see.
- **40–60 min**: **fire and cancer statistics**. Show a PIP graphic of 45 fires/year, 70% from dust. IARC group 1 statistic. Photos of burnt sawmills (from the internet or from an archive).
- **60–80 min**: **five tidying moments**: analysis of a typical working day (recap from l1). Identify **when** it is swept. A timeline drawing 7:00–16:00 with five points.
- **80–100 min**: **tidying practice** (if a shop floor is available). Each learner sweeps a dusty workstation. Trainer corrects: "not a dry broom for dust", "damp cloth instead of a broom". Demonstration of containers (segregation).
- **100–115 min**: **fire, extinguisher, evacuation**: an extinguisher course. Demonstration: powder ABC, "at the base of the fire" technique. If there's a training extinguisher (inactive powder), learners **try**. Three-second stream, controlled movements.
- **115–120 min**: key terms, quiz ("which fraction of waste is most dangerous?"), announcement of l8 (marking and identification of material, **the day ends with marking and a report**).

**Frequent learner mistake**: "tidying isn't my job, I'm a helper". **Trainer's response**: "Tidying is **part of the helper's work**, just like passing a board. Written into the job description. If you don't tidy, you breach your duties. But not only, **you put others at risk** of fire, slip, lung cancer. A foreman sees a helper who tidies well = **values** more than one who cuts fast. Because fast cutting without tidying = catastrophe in a year."

**Practical recommendation**: introduce the **"5-step tidying rule"**. Every learner in the first week learns: before leaving the workstation (even for 2 minutes), make **five steps**: (1) look at the floor, (2) pick up large pieces, (3) quick sweep of chips, (4) throw into the right container, (5) look whether everything is in. **3 minutes**, automatism after a week.

**Link to the next lesson**: l8 is **marking and identification of material**: chalk/marker on logs and timber. The last lesson of Week 3, **closes the day's loop**: in the morning you come to the workstation, you do the work, you tidy (l7), and **before leaving** you mark what you did, and **report to the foreman**. A full day from "entry" to "report".
